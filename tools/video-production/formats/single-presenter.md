# Format · single presenter

**Shape.** One person talking to camera, cut across several locations and
moments. The UGC shape — the workhorse.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Cast** | One role, `{LEAD}`. One trained identity. If it is a contracted creator, train from her own footage. |
| **Places** | Several, deliberately — a kitchen, a car, a bathroom, outside. Variety of place is what stops it reading as one long take. |
| **Scenes** | One scene per line being said; the scene runs as long as the line takes to say and land. Length is never a number here. |
| **Sound** | One voice throughout. Consistency of voice matters more than consistency of room. |

**What a scene contains.** One person to camera, one line, one of several
locations.

**The beat shape.** Hook (the strongest line, to camera, first) → the
problem in her words → what she tried → what changed → the product as the
thing that changed it → offer. Hook variants are cheap: generate several
openings, pick at the edit.

**Stations used.** cast · still · motion · voice. Restyle only when the
piece is not photoreal. No audio station — music is the editor's.

**The references it needs.** Her cast sheet in every frame, without
exception — this is the format that most needs it. The packshot in the
frames where the product is held or shown.

**The sections that carry the weight.** 04 the grade and 06 wardrobe. With
one person across many rooms, the two things that betray a cut are her
outfit changing and the colour shifting. Section 08 barely matters — there
is no blocking to protect.

**Ledgers that apply.** Wardrobe, object, atmosphere. **Not** the entrance
or position ledgers — nobody enters anything.

**Post (edit-time).** Captions, the music, any push-in. Nothing else — the
format's realism is in the raw take.

**Gates it adds.** None — `RUN-PROTOCOL.md` carries them.

**The failure this format invites.** The same person reading as a slightly
different person from location to location, because each new room re-rolls
her. A trained identity is the answer, and it is why this format is the
one that most needs the character built properly first
(`../building-the-cast.md`).

**Structural notes.** Wardrobe may change between scenes if the piece
implies days passing — but then it must change on a schedule written in the
document, never at the model's discretion.

**The run it was proven on.** Not yet run end to end on this machine.

**What still fails.** Unknown until a run is filed.
