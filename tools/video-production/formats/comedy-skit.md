# Format · Comedy skit

Ruled a format by the owner, 2026-10-23: *"it is a format, comedy skits operate similarly"* — first worked on the pastor
barber body (the Druski mega church pastor skit). The shared moves below come from eleven viral pastor and barber
skits broken down shot by shot (`runs/video-writing/<brand>/pastor-barber-body/swipes/SWIPES.md`).

**The bank row** — in `bank.json`:

     "name": "Comedy skit",

     "profile": "comedy-skit.md",
     "status": "draft", "signed": null,

**Shape.** One character played big (a pastor, a barber, a coach) inside a world that looks real, preaching or
performing at a room; the room and one reactor carry the laugh; the product is answered inside the bit, never
pitched outside it.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Cast** | The lead (the character), one reactor (the person the bit lands on — sits in the chair, in the front pew), a silent sidekick at the lead's side, and the crowd. Every face from the brand's AI cast. |
| **Places** | A few sets in one world (the stage, the pews, the chair close) plus one place away from it for the flip (the parking lot, the office). |
| **Scenes** | A set-up per scene (V35), a new payoff every 3–5 seconds, a reaction cut every 10–15 seconds. |
| **Sound** | Dialogue on camera in the lead's voice; the crowd's shouts as a layered track; a music bed under the room. |

**What a scene contains.** a character played big in a real-looking world, every line answered by a reaction, ending on a flip

**The beat shape.** Open mid-action (no intro) → call someone out → say the line that matters three times, louder →
a hard cut to the evidence after every claim → the product arrives inside the bit → the proof → the ask → the flip
that turns the whole skit over.

**Stations used.** cast · still · motion · voice (dialogue and crowd). No song.

**The references it needs.** The swipe's own frames per set-up; every person's banked casting sheet (the reactor's
face especially — a close-up of the reactor starts from HIS sheet, never from the swipe's face); the set plates; the
real product photo and real in-use footage.

**The sections that carry the weight.** The call-out, the turn (the mechanism, said as the line the room shouts
back), the demo inside the bit, and the close (the flip).

**Ledgers that apply.** All six; the claims ledger matters most — a skit makes claims fast and every one needs its
receipt and its evidence shot.

**Post (edit-time).** One bold caption per line, the crowd track, the music bed, the end card. No dead air between
lines.

**Gates it adds.** None — `RUN-PROTOCOL.md` carries them. The face gate (`machine/face_gate.py`) is the one this
format leans on hardest: the reactor appears in the most shots.

**The failure this format invites.** The reactor's face drifting between close-ups (23 Oct: a different man in
Marcus's chair in four scenes). The fix: the reactor's full sheet goes in behind the swipe frame on every close-up,
and no clip is made before the face gate passes.

**The run it was proven on.** Not yet — first run: the pastor barber body (`brand_a_flex_brief-0075`).
