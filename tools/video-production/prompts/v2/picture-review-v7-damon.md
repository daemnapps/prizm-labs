## Five hard fails — check each one, every picture, before anything else

Any one of these is a problem, whatever else is right. Name it plainly and give the fix.

A. **The swipe's things, not ours.** Every object in the picture must be one the brief below names (its props, its set's dressing) or plain background the set would hold. Anything else — an ingredient the brief does not list (peppercorns, chilli, paprika, garlic, lemon, honey…), a tool or object nobody asked for (a sponge, a loofah, a brush), a flag, a sign, a poster, decoration the set description does not have — is the swipe's or invented. Fix: replace it with the brief's own prop, or remove it.
B. **Any words.** ANY readable text drawn in the picture — captions, headlines, a sign, a label, letters on a jar, a sticker — is a problem. Pictures carry NO words: words are laid on in the edit. (Real calligraphy art the set description names is part of the room, not a caption.) Fix: remove the words, fill with what is behind them.
C. **Not the real product.** When the brief puts the product in a person's hands, it must be the brand's REAL product, exactly as the "real product" reference shows it — the same shape, colour, cap and label, at its real size against the hand. A different product, a generic jar or tube, a redrawn label, garbled print or the wrong size is a problem. Any other product, jar, bottle or packaging the brief does not put in the picture is a problem. Where the brief has no product in the scene, hands hold nothing product-like. Fix: the real product in the hands (it is pasted from the real photograph), or remove the stranger.
   Any held object the brief does not define — a sponge, a loofah, a brush, a towel — is a problem (see A).

D. **Not the cast member.** A person must be the SAME person as the cast master reference: same age, face shape, eyes, nose, mouth, skin, wrinkles, hair. Younger, smoother, a different face or a different build is a problem even if she looks similar. Fix: make her the cast master's face and body exactly.
E. **The set is ours.** Decoration our set does NOT describe — a flag our set does not have, a poster, a sign, an ornament, any object from the swipe's room — is a problem (see A). A described item being out of view, a different layout of shelves or window, the counter's shade, or the camera angle are NOT problems: the frame keeps the swipe's own framing and layout on purpose.

F. **The tone drifts.** The person's skin and clothes must hold the cast master's tone (the master reference): a yellow, orange, green or grey cast on the skin, skin much darker or lighter than the master, or a shirt of a different shade than her look is a problem. The light of the room may differ; the person's colour may not. Fix: say which way it drifts (e.g. "skin too yellow, shirt too grey") — the grade is corrected, not the picture redrawn.

## Rules you judge by

1. **A banked look is correct.** Hair, clothes, headband, robe, jewellery, makeup that match the references are RIGHT — never send a picture back for matching its references. Only send it back if it DIFFERS from them.
2. **Same person.** Face shape, eyes, skin, age and marks match the master picture.
3. **The scene says what the brief says** (below): the action, the place, what is in hand.
4. **Product and props** match their references: shape, colour, label words, and real size against the hand. A prop that appears in more than one picture is the same object at the same size every time.
5. **No broken anatomy** (extra or fused fingers, melted faces), **no fake writing** (letters that are not words; real writing in any language, like a calligraphy print or a jar label, is fine).
7. **Nothing physically impossible:** a book open and shut at once, an object floating, a tool held by nothing, two of something that should be one, one object passing through another, a step the brief describes that is not happening.
6. **Safe area:** every face, hand and product sits inside the middle box — 20–80% of the width, 22–78% of the height.

8. **Absurd on purpose is not slop.** When the brief asks for it — an oversized, gross or hyper-detailed thing (a grapefruit-sized pore, a model of skin ten times life size, ooze, flakes, a dramatic over-the-top demo) — that picture is RIGHT, and the more vivid and specific it is, the better. Never send a picture back for being too much, too gross, too big or too strange if that is what the brief says. Send it back only when the brief's own words are NOT shown at their full size, texture, colour and action (a softened, shrunk or generic version of what was asked is a problem).
9. **Slop is still slop.** A malformed hand, fused fingers, a prop that changes size or shape from its reference, an object nobody asked for, letters that are not words — these fail whatever the style.

Do not judge taste, brand politeness, lighting choices or camera style unless they break the brief's look line. Never soften a depiction for brand taste (VIDEO-RULINGS V24).
