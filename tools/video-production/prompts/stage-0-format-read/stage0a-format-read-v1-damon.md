## FORMAT

Name the profile this brief is. One of the profiles above, by its exact
name. Then one paragraph on WHY — pointing at what is in the brief that
decides it: how many people speak, whether they share a space, whether
anyone talks to camera, whether the product does the work.

If the brief genuinely sits between two profiles, say so and name the one
you are ruling for, with the reason. **Never invent a profile that does
not exist in the list** — if nothing fits, say that plainly and describe
the shape you actually see, because that is a new profile someone has to
write before this can run.

## SCENE COUNT AND SHAPE

How many scenes this piece needs, and roughly how long each runs. Take the
count from the brief's own beats where it has them; where it does not,
derive it from the profile's usual range and say you did.

Then a one-line-per-scene skeleton — the function of each scene, not its
content:

    1 · <function>  ~<n>s
    2 · <function>  ~<n>s

Functions are things like: hook, problem, escalation, turn, proof, offer.
Name the function this brief actually uses, not a template's.

## THE CAST

Every person who appears, as a ROLE. Use the profile's role vocabulary
where it fits — LEAD, FOIL, WITNESS, AUTHORITY — and coin a role name only
where the brief needs one those do not cover. For each: what they do in
the piece, whether they speak, and how many scenes they appear in.

If the brief names real people (a contracted creator, a founder), say so
here — that changes how their identity gets built.

## WHAT THE PIECE NEEDS THAT IT DOES NOT HAVE

The gap list. Be specific and be blunt; this is the section that stops
money being spent on a production that was never fully decided. Cover at
least:

- **Cast identities** — which faces need to exist before generation, and
  whether any of them already exist.
- **Product truth** — is the product's real packaging available, and does
  any scene put its label at hero size?
- **The place** — one continuous set, several locations, or unspecified.
  If the brief has not decided, say it has not.
- **The look** — has anyone chosen the grade? If not, this is a decision
  someone must make once, because it gets repeated in every scene.
- **Claim boundaries** — anything the brief implies that the product
  cannot legally or honestly claim. Quote the brief's line and name the
  boundary. If you see none, say so explicitly rather than staying silent.
- **Anything the brief assumes a reader already knows.** These are the
  decisions that otherwise get made silently by whoever generates first.

## READY OR NOT

One line: `READY` if the production document can be written from this
brief plus the gaps you listed being filled, or `NOT READY` with the one
thing that must be answered by a person before anything else is worth
doing.
