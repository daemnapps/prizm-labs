# Stage 4 · Offer cards — the price and the guarantee, on screen

The brief's TYPE C scenes. Short, silent, product-only, with the offer as
on-screen type. Worked example — `ser-01`, a Brand B run, where the brief
asked for three and none were made:

| scene | on screen |
|---|---|
| 16 | `$__` |
| 31 | `90 days. If your skin doesn't transform, full refund. You keep the device™.` + *Plus 1-year device warranty* |
| 33 | `$__` + `the brand's site/products/<product>` |

**She says the offer out loud and the viewer never sees it.** [the price], ninety days, full refund, link below — all spoken, none readable.
A direct-response ad shows its price and its guarantee.

```shape
{product}

{camera}

{look} The product is the only thing in frame. Nothing is held, nothing enters \
frame, and no hand, face or body appears. The only writing anywhere is the \
real label the product itself carries; no other text, no invented lettering, \
no price tags, no signage, no captions burnt into the picture. \
No filming equipment in shot.
```

## The type is NOT generated

The card's words are composited afterwards, over the clip, by the editor.
A generator cannot render six correct characters — it has produced "bloodmark",
"BUMP PATROL", "aesthete" and "douxcds" on request, and a price rendered wrong
is worse than no price. So the shape **forbids all lettering except the
product's own label**, and the `on_screen` text is carried on the row as data
for whoever lays it in.

Generate the picture. Composite the type. The same rule that got the wordmark
onto the bottle.

## `{product}` and `{camera}` come from the brief

TYPE C scenes carry their own **Product:** and **Camera:** lines — the device on
its charging base on the bathroom counter, a slow push ending square on the
label, a slow rotation. Paste them. These shots exist to be still and legible,
so the camera move is deliberate and small, and "ending square on the label"
means the last frame is the one the type sits over.

## Duration

The brief writes 1s. Cinema Studio's floor is 4s. Generate at 4 and let the
editor hold the frame he wants — a card that has to sit under a price for two
seconds is better cut from four seconds of usable material than stretched
from one.
