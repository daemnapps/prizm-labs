# Stage 4 · Offer cards — the price and the guarantee, on screen

The brief's TYPE C scenes. Short, silent, product-only, with the offer as
on-screen type. The shape of a worked example — a run whose brief asked for
three cards and none were made:

| scene | on screen |
|---|---|
| an early scene | `<price from the brief>` |
| a late scene | `<guarantee line from the brief, verbatim>` + *`<warranty line from the brief, if it has one>`* |
| the last scene | `<price from the brief>` + `<the brief's link>` |

**She says the offer out loud and the viewer never sees it.** The price, the
guarantee window, the refund, the link — all spoken, none readable. A
direct-response ad shows its price and its guarantee.

The `on_screen` text is COPIED from the brief, character for character. It is
never recalled, rounded, reworded or borrowed from another run. A card whose
brief names no price carries no price.

```shape
{product}

{camera}

{look} The product is the only thing in frame. Nothing is held, nothing enters \
frame, and no hand, face or body appears. The only writing anywhere is the \
real label the product itself carries; no other text, no invented lettering, \
no price tags, no signage, no captions burnt into the picture. \
No filming equipment in shot.
```

## The type is NOT generated

The card's words are composited afterwards, over the clip, by the editor.
A generator cannot render six correct characters — asked for a brand name on a
bottle it has returned invented wordmarks and near-miss spellings every time,
and a price rendered wrong is worse than no price. So the shape **forbids all
lettering except the product's own label**, and the `on_screen` text is
carried on the row as data for whoever lays it in.

Generate the picture. Composite the type. The same rule that got the wordmark
onto the bottle.

## `{product}` and `{camera}` come from the brief

TYPE C scenes carry their own **Product:** and **Camera:** lines — shaped like
*`<the product, where the brief stands it>`*, *a slow push ending square on the
label*, *a slow rotation*. Paste the brief's own lines. These shots exist to
be still and legible, so the camera move is deliberate and small, and "ending
square on the label" means the last frame is the one the type sits over.

## Duration

The brief writes 1s. Cinema Studio's floor is 4s. Generate at 4 and let the
editor hold the frame he wants — a card that has to sit under a price for two
seconds is better cut from four seconds of usable material than stretched
from one.
