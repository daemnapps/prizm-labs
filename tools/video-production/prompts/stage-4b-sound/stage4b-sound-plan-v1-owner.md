## THE BED

One music bed for the whole piece unless the document's arc calls for a
change, in which case say exactly where it changes and why.

- **The brief, in one sentence** — written as a description of the finished
  music, ready to hand to a generator: instrumentation, tempo feel, mood,
  and what it must NOT do (pull focus, resolve too early, sound like stock).
- **Length** — the piece's full runtime, plus a tail.
- **Where it sits** — under everything, or entering at a named beat. If it
  enters late, say what the opening runs on instead.
- **The duck points** — the moments where the bed drops back so a line
  lands. Name them by scene.

If the format profile says this piece carries no bed, write
`NO BED — <the reason from the profile>` and move on. Silence chosen on
purpose is a decision; silence by omission is a defect.

## IS THIS PIECE SUNG?

One line: `SPOKEN` or `SUNG`.

If the format is a song, the script IS the lyrics, and this section carries
them instead of a bed brief:

- **The lyrics**, verbatim from the production document, laid out with
  `[verse]` / `[chorus]` structure markers.
- **The style tags** — the genre, the vocal (range, gender if the cast
  fixes it, delivery), the energy.
- **The hook line** — the one line that must be intelligible even to
  someone half-listening, and where it repeats.

A sung piece has no separate voice casting for its lines; the song carries
them. Say so explicitly here so nobody generates both.

## THE EFFECTS

A table, one row per effect the piece needs. Take them from the scene
prompts' own Audio lines and from the mechanics — a lid coming off, water
running, a door, a phone buzzing.

| Scene | At | The sound, described for a generator | Length |
|---|---|---|---|

Describe each effect as the recording you want, not as the event: "a plastic
screw cap turning and clicking free, close, dry room" rather than "opens the
tube." A generator makes what you describe.

Only list effects that the clip cannot produce on its own. A clip generated
with audio already carries room tone and most on-camera action; adding a
second version of the same sound doubles it. Where you are unsure whether
the clip will carry it, say `CHECK` in the length column rather than
generating over the top.

## ROOM TONE

One line per distinct location: what the space sounds like with nobody
speaking. This is what fills the gaps between lines, and its absence is what
makes a cut feel like it snapped.

## THE MIX NOTES

Three or four lines for whoever assembles: what sits on top, what ducks
under what, where the bed comes up, and the one moment in the piece that
should be the loudest. Written for a person, not a machine.
