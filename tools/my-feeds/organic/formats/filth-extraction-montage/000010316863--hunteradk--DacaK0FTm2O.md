# Filth-extraction montage — rebuild sheet

A 30-second hands-only body-scrub demo: one black finger-strap scrub pad worked over shoulders, a leg, an arm and a thigh across two bathrooms, with the lifted dead skin shown to camera — 10,316,863 views, 20,386 likes, by hunteradk.

**Why it works**
The pad is turned face-up to camera loaded with a grey mat of lifted dead skin, and a clump of it is sitting on the floor by the foot in the next beat — that debris is unfakeable and physically satisfying, so it does all the persuading before any claim is made. Repeating the same tool on four different body parts, in two different rooms, turns one demo into a verdict on the product rather than a single lucky use.

**What carries the value**
The debris. The grey mat of dead skin on the face of the pad at 7s, and the clump shed onto the floor at 12s. Everything after that is repetition — arm, back, thigh, foam, running water — proving the tool works anywhere on the body and rinses clean.

**Shoot it**
- **Camera:** phone, vertical, handheld throughout, held close to the contact point. No tripod, no zoom, no ramps. One cut per body part / room change. Frame tight enough that faces stay out — the performer is a pair of hands.
- **Light:** whatever the bathroom has. Flat, even daylight in the dry room; the shower beats are lit by the room itself with water in frame. No lamps, no colour, no filter.
- **Wardrobe:** bare skin plus a plain towel. The person holding the tool is only a hand — nails visible, no jewellery competing with the product.
- **The one thing you must get:** dry, un-rinsed skin for the first two beats, so there is real debris to lift, and a beat where the tool is turned face-up to the lens with that debris on it. Shoot the debris beat before anything gets wet; once you move to water and lather, the evidence is gone and you're only showing foam.
- **Order:** dry extraction first (the proof), other body parts second (the range), water and lather last (the clean close).
