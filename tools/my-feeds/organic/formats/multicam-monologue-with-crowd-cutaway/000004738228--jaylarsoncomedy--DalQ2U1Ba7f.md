**What carries the value**

The real crowd. The laugh cutaway at 12.87s is the proof the line landed, and covering one live take from four positions is what makes the room read as genuine rather than staged. The running caption is the delivery mechanism, not the value — it just keeps the speech legible on mute so the laugh has something to be a laugh at.

**Shoot it**

- **Camera:** three or four positions on one continuous take, all rolling at once — a tight close-up on a long lens, a full-body wide from the room, one pointed back at the audience, one from behind the crowd over their heads. Every camera is near-locked: the frame breathes, it never reframes or zooms. All the movement in the edit comes from changing angle, never from moving a camera.
- **Light:** whatever the venue has. A warm front key on the performer, heavy falloff into a dark room, red spill from the house lights on the audience side. The angles here aren't colour-matched to each other and it doesn't hurt — the 16.55s shot is visibly darker than the rest. Don't relight to fix that.
- **Wardrobe:** plain and unstyled — a crew-neck sweater over a tee, chinos. Nothing that reads as costume, nothing that dates the clip.
- **The cutaway is the shot you cannot skip.** Point one camera at the audience and use it where the laugh is. If there's no real audience, this format doesn't work — a staged reaction is the one thing viewers catch instantly.
- **Captions:** burn them in, word-synced, all-caps, white with the live phrase in bright green, centre-low, one or two lines at a time, running under every angle without a break.

**Or generate it**

The close-up delivery beat is the one to generate — it's where the caption sits and where the rebuild starts:

```
vertical 9:16 photograph, tight close-up chest-up of {SUBJECT} in a plain
navy crew-neck sweater over a white tee, holding a wired handheld microphone
up to their mouth mid-word, standing on a small club stage; behind them a
soft pink-mauve wall with framed pictures thrown fully out of focus, warm
stage key light from the front and slightly above, dark room falloff at the
edges, video-camera look with a shallow depth of field, no on-screen text
```

**Motion:** near-locked handheld on a long lens — the frame breathes but never reframes; only the performer moves inside it, and the edit changes angle rather than the camera moving.

**Text overlay:** burned-in word-synced captions, centre-low, heavy all-caps sans, white with the currently spoken words in bright green, one or two lines at a time, running continuously under every angle — `"{SPOKEN PHRASE}" / "{SPOKEN PHRASE}"`.

One caution on generating this format: the crowd-reaction beat is what carries it, and a generated audience is the part that will read as fake. Shoot that one for real even if everything else is generated.
