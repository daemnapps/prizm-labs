**What carries the value**

The cut-open cross-section. The build is opaque by nature — once the first layer rolls, you cannot see inside it — so the knife is the only moment that pays off the watch, and the layered cut face is simultaneously the reward and the proof that nothing was faked or sped past.

**Shoot it**

- **Camera:** phone, vertical, handheld throughout — no tripod. Start at counter height, roughly chest-level on the maker, and get progressively closer with each beat: whole-room wide → medium at the work surface → hands-only over the pan → macro on the cut. The tightening is the structure; do not shoot it all at one distance.
- **Face:** in frame for the first half, cropped out for the second. Nobody talks to camera and nobody performs — there is no line to deliver.
- **Light:** whatever the kitchen has. In the reference it is recessed ceiling lights plus daylight from a window; nothing added, no fill, no reflector.
- **Room:** do not clear the counter. Oil bottles, a metal tray, a used measuring cup and a pink trivet are all visible and all of it reads as real. A styled surface kills this.
- **Wardrobe:** real home clothes. The embroidered apron is competence signalling, not styling — pick something the person would genuinely wear to cook, not a costume.
- **Final beat:** shoot the cut slow and close and let it run. Cut through, move the hand back, hold on the exposed faces. That shot is the whole video.
- **Casting:** the structure needs no performer, but it needs a real person with real hands doing a real thing. A model miming it will not survive the macro.
