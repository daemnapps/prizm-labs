# Word-per-beat internal reveal — rebuild sheet

A 63.5s fully rendered anatomy piece by science__pulse explaining why an eyelid twitches — 1,405,887 views, 30,291 likes. Not a single frame is filmed: every beat is a 3D medical render of the inside of a face, with a running caption that never finishes its sentence.

**Why it works**
It shows you the inside of your own body doing the thing you already felt this morning, so a claim you would argue with becomes something you appear to be watching happen. The caption is one clause per beat that is still mid-sentence at the last frame, so there is no clean place to leave — and it escalates from a symptom you recognise to a word you're frightened of ("NEUROLOGICAL").

**The beats**

| 55.91s | Black frame: a brain and brainstem at the top, a glowing red nerve tree branching down over the face of an older man grimacing with teeth bared | IT CAN BE A **NEUROLOGICAL** |

One word per clause is bright green; the rest is white all-caps with a heavy black outline, centre-low, same position every beat.

Two things the frames do not show, so they are not claimed here: what happens in the roughly 10 seconds between each sampled beat, and any product — no product, bottle, hand or brand appears in any of the six frames, and the sentence is still unfinished at 55.91s, so how it lands is unknown.

**What carries the value**
The interior view. A mechanism painted in glowing colour inside a body no real camera could enter, held under a sentence that never closes — you watch to see inside and read to finish the sentence at the same time. Nothing else is doing any persuading: no person talks, no product is shown, no before/after is offered.

**Shoot it**
Say plainly: you cannot shoot this. Five of the six beats are 3D renders of the inside of a head and no phone can get there — this format is generated or it is not made. The one filmable element in the whole piece is the closing face: an older subject in tight close-up against a black background, grimacing, lit from one side only, shot on a phone with the exposure pulled down so the background goes to black — the glowing nerve tree is then composited over it. Wardrobe is irrelevant; nothing below the neck is ever in frame. If you want a live-action version of this structure, the honest move is to film that one face and generate everything else.
