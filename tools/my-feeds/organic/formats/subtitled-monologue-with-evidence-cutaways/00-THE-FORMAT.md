# Subtitled monologue with evidence cutaways

**2,812,071 views proved it** · 23 of Damon's swipes use this format

## What it is

The speaker's own unchanging frame carries most of the runtime while one continuous narration is burned in as per-beat captions that never close a sentence; every few beats the frame is handed to the thing the speech has just named — an object held up beside the face, a full-frame macro insert, a borrowed diagram or a stack of screenshots pasted into the same frame. The speaker's camera never changes; the evidence is the only thing that ever moves. Where the borrowed material instead holds the full frame for most of the runtime and the creator is reduced to a pinned panel, that is running-caption-borrowed-cutaways, not this.

## Why it works

The caption is always mid-sentence, so there is no clean place to leave, and each insert arrives as a receipt for a claim the viewer has just heard rather than as a change of subject. Because the speaker's frame is identical every time it returns, a scrapbook of borrowed proof reads as one person's argument with exhibits.

## The beats, in order

1. the speaker's frame opens already mid-sentence, first caption clause running
2. the claim is named — the object, the credential or the product held up beside the face
3. cut or paste to the evidence: a full-frame macro, a labelled diagram, someone else's before close-ups
4. back to the identical speaker frame, the sentence one clause further on
5. a second piece of evidence — the mechanism, the printed panel, the competing listings
6. close on the speaker frame, or on the product doing the thing that was claimed

## On-screen text

one continuous narration burned in as speech captions — one clause, or one spoken word, per beat in the identical position, cut off mid-sentence every time; sometimes a hook line over the opening beat that never returns, but nothing is ever pinned across the runtime

## Camera

one fixed frame on the speaker — arm's-length selfie or a locked chest-up — identical every time it comes back, cut against full-frame evidence inserts or panels pasted into the top third or one side of that same frame; no second location and no second angle on the speaker
