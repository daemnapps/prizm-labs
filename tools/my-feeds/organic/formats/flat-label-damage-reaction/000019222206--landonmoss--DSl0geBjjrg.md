# Flat label → damage → reaction — rebuild sheet

A 9.5s POV clip of charcoal-heated slippers being loaded, worn, and burning a hole
through the wearer's sock, closing on a face reacting — 19,222,206 views, 758,072 likes,
by landonmoss, captioned "Last time shopping on temu 🤣".

**Why it works** — A flat, unexcited label ("FEET WARMERS FROM TEMU") sits over a
straight product demonstration, so for four seconds the viewer is watching an ordinary
unboxing and has no reason to brace. Then the demo turns on itself: the label disappears
at the exact beat things go wrong, the payoff shot is the damage instead of the product
working, and a face arrives at the end to tell you it's allowed to be funny.

**Shoot it**

- **Camera** — a phone held in your own hand, looking down at the floor, vertical. Beats
  1–3 are point-of-view: your feet, your hands, no face. Beat 5 is the same POV with the
  ruined thing lifted in toward the lens until it fills half the frame. Beat 6 is a
  separate selfie in a different room.
- **Light** — plain overhead indoor light, whatever the room has. Nothing lit, nothing
  bounced. The tile in the source is glossy and the frames are slightly blown out in
  places; that's fine and reads as real.
- **Room** — an ordinary hard-floored room with furniture legs and clutter in shot. Do not
  clear the frame. The stool legs, the bowl, the basket edge are what make it not an ad.
- **Wardrobe** — house clothes only, and one detail matters: put the product on over
  something plain and light, because the damage has to be legible against it. The white
  sock is the reason the burn reads at all.
- **Talent** — beats 1–5 need nobody on camera, just hands and feet. Beat 6 needs one
  person willing to hold a wince to camera for about a second. No lines are visible in the
  stills, so the expression alone may be carrying it.
- **The one rule** — the label goes on at frame one and comes off the instant the thing
  goes wrong. Don't caption the damage. The damage doesn't need help.
