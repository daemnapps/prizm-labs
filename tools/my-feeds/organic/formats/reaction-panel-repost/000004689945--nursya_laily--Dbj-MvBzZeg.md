**What carries the value**

The borrowed clip's slow deform-to-reveal. The pinned line front-loads a premise and the clip pays it off physically, one beat at a time. The reaction panel carries none of the payoff — it gives the viewer a face to laugh with, and it is why the caption can vanish at the reveal without the joke collapsing.

**Shoot it**

You don't shoot the main frame. This format is a repost: the whole persuasion is in a clip you did not make, and the only thing you produce is the corner panel and the line.

- **Camera (panel):** phone front camera, locked on a desk or stand, chest-up, one continuous take of you actually watching the clip. It is never cut to and it never moves — it sits bottom-right at about a quarter of the frame width, hard rectangular edge, no border, no zoom.
- **Light (panel):** screen light on the face is enough and is what the original uses. Plain room, no setup.
- **Wardrobe:** plain and dark, nothing that competes with the main frame. No branding on you.
- **Rules:** one line of text, pinned unchanged, black rounded box in the top third, removed before the payoff beat. Don't add a second line, don't animate it, don't caption over the reveal.
- **What the main clip has to do:** start ordinary and end absurd, with the change arriving gradually enough that four beats pass before anyone knows where it's going. If your clip reveals itself in the first two seconds, this format has nothing to hold.
