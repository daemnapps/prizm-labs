**What carries the value**

The stranger's unpaid laugh. The bag is only funny once it's worn at gut height inside an ordinary transaction and a real cashier reacts — the reaction *is* the demo. The venue switch at 8.23s is the second proof point.

Two honest gaps: there's no audio in what I read, so whether music, a voiceover or the room's own sound is doing work is unknown. And the comment-bait — "Comment 'BAG' if you NEED one 😂😂 #funny #belly #husband #gift" — lives entirely in the caption, never on screen; don't put it in the video.

**Shoot it**

- **Camera:** phone, vertical, handheld, chest height, a few steps back from the counter — far enough that both the wearer and the staff member are in frame. No zoom, no cuts inside a reaction; let it run.
- **Light:** whatever the shop has. Overhead store fluorescents in the checkout beats, daylight through the window in the shop beat. Nothing added.
- **Wardrobe:** plain and unstyled — dark tee, cargo shorts or joggers, white sneakers. The product sits at the waistband under the shirt hem so it reads as the body, not as a bag. Nothing else on the wearer competes.
- **The rule that makes it work:** the wearer never speaks, never mugs, never looks at camera. He does a normal errand. Everything funny comes from the object and from the person behind the counter.
- **Locations:** one transactional public place (checkout, counter, till) plus a second, different one for the last beat — different venue, and a different wearer is fine.
- **Cost:** no talent, no script, no crew. What it does need is a real public place and a real bystander, and that part can't be faked or generated — a staged laugh reads as staged instantly.
