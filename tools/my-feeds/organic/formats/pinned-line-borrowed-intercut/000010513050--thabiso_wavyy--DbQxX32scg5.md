**What the frames don't show — don't guess at these**

- **No audio evidence.** Six stills can't tell you whether a track, a sound or a voiceover is setting the cut rhythm. Assume there is one and pick your own.

**What carries the value**

The rhyme between two clips that were never shot together — an animal up on its hind legs moving like a person, cut against a man's heavy walk — held under one caption that never changes and never finishes its sentence. Not the wardrobe, not the location, not a product: the pairing plus the open colon.

**Shoot it**

- **Camera:** phone, vertical, held at chest height about three or four metres out, subject full body in frame. Walk backwards with them so they stay the same size and centred. No zoom, no cut inside the shot, no tripod — the small handheld drift is part of the look.
- **Light:** whatever the sky is giving you. Shoot the street half at dusk while it's still gold and open — no lamps, no reflector, nothing bounced. The borrowed clip will be a different light entirely and that's fine; the mismatch reads as two different worlds, which is the joke.
- **Location:** an empty street with a plain wall behind. Nothing in frame competes — no crowd, no signage, no traffic.
- **Wardrobe:** everyday street clothes, unbranded, one bulky layer plus loose pants. The volume is doing work — a heavy jacket and loose trousers make a gait legible from four metres away where a fitted outfit wouldn't. Same outfit in every shot of your half.
- **The other half:** find a clip that rhymes physically with what you shot — same movement, different creature or object. Cut A/B roughly every three seconds. Both halves take the same pinned caption, unchanged, all the way through.
