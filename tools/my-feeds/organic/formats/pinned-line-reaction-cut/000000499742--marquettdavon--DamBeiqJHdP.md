**What carries the value** — The pinned line. It is the whole joke; the alligator footage only illustrates it, and the cut to a face is the line finally being cashed. Nothing is demonstrated, nothing is claimed, and no word ever completes the sentence.

**Shoot it**

- **Act one (five-sixths of the runtime):** one wide, locked or near-locked, shot from where a spectator would stand — the audience visibly in frame is doing work, because it proves the thing is really happening in front of people. No zoom, no reframe, no cut for the whole act. This is found or borrowed footage in the original; if you shoot it, shoot something genuinely impressive being done in public and leave the camera alone.
- **Act two (one hard cut, the last few seconds):** phone front camera at arm's length, chest-up, square to the lens, in an ordinary room. No set, no props. The person does nothing — flat expression, holds the lens. The cut is hard; no transition, no music change needed.
- **Light:** act one is flat outdoor daylight under shade; act two is soft daylight from a window behind and to the side. Neither is lit.
- **Wardrobe:** everyday clothes in both acts. The two acts must look like two different worlds — different person, different room, different camera. That gap is the joke's mechanism, so do not match them.
- **Text:** one line, upper-middle, same words and same position on every beat including the cut. Never change it, never finish it, and keep the colon.
