**What carries the value**
The borrowed clip's own admission — a man saying "Slept in my car." inside a paid conference ballroom — sitting under a headline that names the absurdity for you. The poster never argues it. He cuts to his own face and reacts to a fact somebody else already filmed.

**Shoot it**

*Act one — you don't shoot this, you find it.* Source a clip where somebody says the incriminating thing out loud on their own footage. Keep it letterboxed on a black field with the source watermark and the source's own subtitles left visible — the visible seams are what make it read as found rather than made. Write one headline, set it across the top third in the black field above the clip, colour two or three loaded words inside it, and never change a character of it for the whole act.

*Act two — phone front camera, parked car, one take.* Sit in the driver's seat, hold the phone at arm's length just below eye level so the headrest and ceiling fill the frame behind you and daylight comes through the side or rear window. Don't mount it — the small drift and re-tilt between moments is what keeps it reading as a real reaction. Never cut to a second angle and never leave the car.

*Wardrobe:* one plain top, nothing branded, nothing styled. He is in a white ribbed tank; the rule is that act two must look like you did not get ready for it.

*The two hard rules:* every word of on-screen text lives in act one, and act two has none at all. And act two runs long — roughly half the runtime on one unbroken face.
