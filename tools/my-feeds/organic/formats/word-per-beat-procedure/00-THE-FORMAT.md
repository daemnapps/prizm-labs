# Word-per-beat procedure

**75,994,238 views proved it** · 4 of Damon's swipes use this format

## What it is

A camera that never moves, held on the thing being treated. ONE WORD per beat builds a sentence across the whole video, one step of the procedure per word. The operator is hands only — or a swapped-in element standing in for them; the problem is the subject and stays in frame the entire time.

## Why it works

The sentence is unfinished until the last beat, so the read is the retention device — you cannot leave mid-sentence. The unmoving frame on the problem means the before-state never leaves the screen while the fix happens.

## The beats, in order

1. word 1 — the problem, raw and close (bumps, spots, texture visible)
2. word 2 — the product going on, colour change across the whole area
3. word 3 — the jar or tube held to camera, label readable
4. word 4 — the working step (scrub, sponge, massage)
5. word 5 — the wipe / removal, clean skin appearing
6. word 6 — hands finishing, the result held for a beat

## On-screen text

one word per beat, centre-low, all caps or lowercase consistently, one word in a colour for emphasis; the words read as a sentence in order and are still unfinished at the last sampled beat

## Camera

zero camera movement — usually locked overhead on a reclined or seated subject, sometimes a locked frontal frame with the problem held at the edge of shot; even indoor light

## Higgsfield — image prompt

```
vertical 9:16 top-down photograph of {body area with the problem, described exactly}, {practitioner's hands} applying {product exactly as described}, {room} floor and chair edges in frame, even indoor light, clinical but unstyled, phone-camera look, no on-screen text, no retouching of the skin
```

## Higgsfield — motion

```
camera perfectly still, only the hands move, one continuous step
```
