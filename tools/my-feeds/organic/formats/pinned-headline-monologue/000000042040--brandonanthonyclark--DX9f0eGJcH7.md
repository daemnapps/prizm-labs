**What carries the value**

The unchanging headline read against a caption that is mid-sentence every single time. No product, no cutaway, no second person, no demonstration — the persuasion is entirely the speech, and the pinned line is what makes each fragment land as part of one argument instead of a stray remark.

**Shoot it**

- **Camera:** front camera, phone in hand at arm's length, held *below* chin height and pointed up. That upward angle is what puts empty sky behind the head and kills the background completely — no room to dress, no location to scout. Let the frame drift and roll naturally as the arm tires; don't stabilise it and don't put it on a tripod. Never cut away, never show a second angle, never show what you're talking about.
- **Light:** overcast daylight, outdoors. Flat white sky, no direct sun, no shadow direction on the face. An overcast day is the whole lighting setup — bright sun would blow out the sky and put hard shadows on the face, which reads as staged.
- **Wardrobe:** one plain garment, worn for the entire runtime, no change and nothing that competes with the face. In the source it's a grey hoodie with drawstrings and a small chest patch, plus a visible earbud — the earbud reads as "caught between things", not as a set-up shoot.
- **Talent:** this format does not work without someone who can hold an argument to camera for two minutes with no visual support. There is nothing to fall back on. **Not replicable without talent.**
- **Text:** burn one headline into the top third and never change it — it has to be the strongest line you have, because the viewer reads it against every sentence. Run auto-captions underneath and leave them as fragments; don't clean them into complete sentences.
