The pinned line is a flat claim ("Success is the default") that the talk never restates and never resolves early — it sits there as an open argument the viewer keeps waiting to see paid off, so leaving means leaving mid-argument. **What carries the value**

The claim and the face. There is no product on screen, no demo, no debris, no cutaway — the persuasion is one person arguing a single line for the full runtime while that line stays visible above their head. **This is not replicable without talent**: you need someone who can hold a camera and talk for two minutes. Everything else here is free.

Honest gaps in the evidence:

**Shoot it**

- **Camera:** phone front camera, held at arm's length, roughly eye height, vertical. Head fills most of the frame — chest-up at the widest. Handheld and drifting, never locked. No cuts to anything but the face.
- **Light:** one soft frontal source at screen level — a phone screen, a laptop, a small panel on the dash. Nothing behind. The background wants to fall to near-black so the face is the only lit thing; a row of warm out-of-focus lights back there gives it depth and costs nothing.
- **Room:** a dark, confined interior at night. A parked car does it. Confined and dark is the whole requirement — it stops the eye going anywhere but the face.
- **Wardrobe:** plain heather-grey crew-neck tee, brand logo small on the chest. No styling, no jacket, no set dressing.
- **Text:** write the claim first and pin it top-centre, plain white sans-serif, unchanged from frame one to the end. Turn on auto-captions, centre-low, small, one-word highlight. Do not animate the pinned line — the point is that it doesn't move.
- **Script rule:** the claim goes on screen; the talk argues it and never repeats it verbatim. Land the payoff at the end, not in the first ten seconds.
