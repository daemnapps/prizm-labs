**Why it works**

The video withholds the mechanism, not the product — in daylight the panel is a black rectangle that means nothing, so the only way to find out what it does is to stay for the cut to darkness. **Shoot it**

You can do all of this on a phone. No face, no dialogue, no performer.

- **Camera, act one:** vertical, handheld, held at about waist height so the frame runs from the chest down to the shoes. Keep the face out of frame entirely — that removes any need for someone who can perform.
- **Camera, act two:** get down level with the bed and close, so the open book fills most of the frame. Keep it still. Expose for the glow, not the room — the room going pure black is the point, so let everything outside the light fall away.
- **Light:** act one is plain daylight through a window, nothing added. Act two has no lighting at all besides the product itself. Kill every lamp; a faint blue night window in the background is a bonus, not a requirement.
- **Wardrobe and room:** deliberately ordinary — a plain sweatshirt, joggers, everyday sneakers, a real room with a real side table. Nothing styled. The whole format reads as a person showing you a thing, and dressing it up breaks that.
- **The one shot you cannot skip:** the close-up on the control (5.57s) followed by the same wide frame twice with the effect changed (7.16s → 8.75s). That pairing is the proof — press, then see. Shoot the two wides without moving the camera between them.
- **Text:** one line pinned top-third from frame one, unchanged through the whole daylight act. It swaps on the exact frame of the cut to darkness and holds to the end. Do not animate it, do not add a third line.
