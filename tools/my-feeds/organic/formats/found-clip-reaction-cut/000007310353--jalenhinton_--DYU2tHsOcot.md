**Shoot it**

*Act one — the borrowed clip (or your own ground-level capture)*

- **Camera:** phone laid flat on the ground, lens at gravel height, wide lens, recording. Do not touch it while it rolls — all three frames are pixel-identical in framing, and that stillness is what makes the foreground read as real rather than shot.
- **Position:** the interesting thing happens in the mid-ground and the near foreground stays in sharp focus. People run *past and away from* the lens, never toward a mark.
- **Light:** late-afternoon overcast daylight only. Broken cloud fills the top two thirds of the frame; shadows are soft and directionless. No lights, no reflector.
- **Wardrobe:** whatever people already own. The source shows t-shirts, gym shorts, trainers, rubber clogs, one man shirtless. Nothing matched, nothing styled — styling would kill the only thing this act has.
- **Direction:** none. Nobody looks at the lens in any frame.

*Act two — the take*

- **Camera:** front-facing, handheld at arm's length, held slightly *below* eye line and tilted up so the ceiling corner sits in the top of frame. Let it drift — it's visibly handheld across all three frames, not locked.
- **Room:** a bare wall corner. No furniture, no props, no window in shot.
- **Light:** flat, soft, slightly warm indoor light. No visible source.
- **Wardrobe:** plain black t-shirt. Nothing branded, nothing patterned.
- **Rule:** hard cut into it, no transition, and then stay on the face — the source never cuts away for the rest of the runtime we can see.
