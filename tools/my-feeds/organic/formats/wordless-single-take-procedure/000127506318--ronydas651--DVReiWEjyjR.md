**What carries the value**

The peeled sheet with the extracted hair still on it, lifted into the lens. Not the client, not the practitioner, not the room. The repeating structural move is *contact point → lift the debris to camera → back to the contact point*, and that lift is the whole format. Strip it out and you have a procedure video that nobody finishes.

**Shoot it**

- **Camera.** Phone, vertical, handheld, no tripod. Start tight enough that the contact point fills the frame, then let the camera drift back and slightly up as the debris is lifted, so the room opens up behind it. Move around the subject during the take — this video works from beside the head, then front-on, then from behind the shoulder. Motion blur on the lifted hand is fine and reads as real.
- **Light.** Whatever the room has. This is warm mixed overhead light — chandeliers and small ceiling spotlights — with no window daylight and no fill. Do not light it. The unlit, slightly orange look is part of why it reads as a real appointment.
- **Wardrobe.** Practitioner: plain dark uniform shirt, bare forearms, hands in frame far more than the face. Subject: covered by a cape or gown so there is no personal styling to look at. Nobody is dressed for camera.
- **Rules.** No text. No music cue you have to cut to. No talking. Never let the debris leave frame without being shown to the lens first — lift it at least twice. Keep the subject's eyes closed and their face unperformed; the moment they play to camera, the evidence turns into a bit.

Two things the frames cannot tell you, so decide them yourself: whether this is one unbroken take or a few cuts — the framing jumps between 4.59s and 6.42s, which reads either as the operator walking around or as a cut — and whether there is talking or ambient salon sound under it, since stills carry no audio.
