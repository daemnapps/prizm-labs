**What carries the value**

The face that refuses to react — held in close-up for the entire last third while she is still laughing off-camera. Not the cake smash, not the laugh. The empty plate in the wide beats is the only prop doing work, and it does it silently.

**Shoot it**

- **Camera:** phone, vertical, handheld at eye height, no tripod. Two setups only — a waist-up two-shot where the reactor is in front and the deadpan person is behind and slightly to the side, then a tight chest-up close-up of the deadpan person alone. Let the handheld drift; do not stabilise, do not zoom.
- **Light:** a real room with daylight — the source is a kitchen with windows and ceiling downlights. Nothing added. The close-up is warmer and softer than the two-shot, so shoot it standing closer to the window side of the room and let the background fall out of focus.
- **Wardrobe:** plain t-shirts, one white and one black, no logos, no styling. The room and the clothes have to be boring so the face is the only thing to look at.
- **Performance rules — this is where it lives:** the reactor over-plays it (real laugh, clapping, fist up, doubled over) and never looks at the deadpan person. The deadpan person does nothing: no smile, no eyebrow, no blink-and-look-away gag. One small eye move toward the lens at the very end is the entire performance.
- **Structure:** stimulus clip first (~3s), reaction pair in the middle (~10s), then give the last third — a full 6+ seconds — to the close-up of the blank face and do not cut away early. Hold it longer than feels right.
- **Honest note:** this needs two people who can perform, and one of them has to hold a still face on camera. It is not shootable with props alone.
