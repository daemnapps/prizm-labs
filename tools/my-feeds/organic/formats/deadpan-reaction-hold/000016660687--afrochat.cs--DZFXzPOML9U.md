**Shoot it.** Two people, two phones, no set.

- *Camera.* Both halves are handheld front-camera selfies at arm's length, eye height, vertical. Half one closes in beat by beat — start chest-up with the product visible, end tight enough that the frame cuts at the shoulders; do it by moving the phone in, not by cropping in the edit. Half two never moves: one static frame, front-on, centred, held to the end.
- *Light.* Nothing is lit. Half one is soft ambient indoor light on a plain wall with a faint shadow behind the head; half two is bare overhead work lighting with no fill, and it is allowed to look worse. The mismatch between the two rooms is the point of the cut — do not grade them to match.
- *Wardrobe.* Ordinary clothes, no costume: half one is a plain white ribbed tank and a thin chain; half two is real workwear — hi-vis vest, plain top under it, lanyard. Nothing branded, nothing styled.
- *Rooms.* Half one: a bare, featureless interior wall — anything on it competes. Half two: a real working space with depth behind the head (ceiling structure, pipework, racking).
- *Performance.* The second person must not react. No smile, no nod, no line — the whole payoff is that the frame refuses to move.
- *Product.* It appears once, held to the lens in the opening beat, then leaves and never comes back. It is not in the payoff frame.

This one is **not replicable without talent**. The structure is free, but the value sits entirely in two performances — a monologue worth 11 seconds and a face that can hold a dead stare for six. Rebuild it with people who can do that, or don't rebuild it.
