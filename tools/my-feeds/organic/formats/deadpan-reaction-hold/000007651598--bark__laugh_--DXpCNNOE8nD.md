**Shoot it**

- **Camera:** phone on a tripod, propped book, or shelf — set it and do not touch it. Vertical, at roughly the subject's height and a hair above. One continuous take, no cuts, no zoom, no reframe. The source runs 10 seconds; that is enough.
- **Light:** kill every light in the room. The subject's face is lit by a screen and nothing else — hold a phone just inside the bottom-right corner of frame with its back to camera, screen aimed at the face. Let the background go black. Play something on that screen that changes colour partway through (the source goes cold blue → warm → green); the colour turn is what sells that the content on the screen changed.
- **Subject:** a person or a pet held in one position, doing nothing but looking at the screen. No dialogue, no gesture, no product handling. The performance is eyes and mouth only — half-lidded to start, narrowing to a stare, lip tightening at the end. Slow. Nothing snaps.
- **Wardrobe / set:** whatever is already there. The source is a couch back and a bare wall, unstyled and underlit. Resist dressing it — the plainness is why the face is the only thing to look at.
- **Text:** one line, top third, white rounded comic-style type with a black outline, broken over three or four short lines. Burn it in at 0s and never touch it again. The rule that matters: the line states what you did for someone and what you are now looking at — and the video never shows what you are looking at.
