**Why it works**
The camera never moves, so the only thing changing in the frame is the horse's head dropping and snapping back — and because nothing competes with it, every bob reads instantly as an animal fighting sleep. The caption never explains the joke, it just keeps score ("Fighting" → "Stillll fighting"), which turns a 29-second static shot into a running gag you stay for to see whether the horse loses.

**What carries the value**
The horse's head bobbing down onto the sleeping man's face and jerking back up, again and again, inside a frame that never changes. It's an animal losing its own fight with sleep — unfakeable, and legible only because the camera holds absolutely still.

**Shoot it**
- **Camera:** one position, locked, and it does not move for the whole runtime. Set it at the reclined subject's height, side-on, far enough back that the standing subject's head fills the top half and the reclined one sits low in the opposite corner. That empty diagonal between them is the frame — don't recompose to "improve" it. If you want a tighter beat like 11.7s, shoot it as a second pass from the same side, and use it once, in the middle only.
- **Light:** flat, bright, overcast daylight outdoors. No fill, no reflector, no grade. Blown-out white sky is fine and reads as real.
- **Wardrobe:** plain and unstyled — white ribbed tank, head wrap, nothing branded, nothing that dates it.
- **Performance:** the human does nothing. Reclined, still, eyes closed, whole runtime. Every bit of movement in the frame has to come from the other subject. Resist the urge to react to camera.
- **Text:** small white sans, low centre, same position every time. No text on the opening or closing beat — the caption enters at beat 2 and leaves before the end.
- **Length:** ~29s, which only works because the change is slow. Don't cut it tighter; the waiting is the joke.
