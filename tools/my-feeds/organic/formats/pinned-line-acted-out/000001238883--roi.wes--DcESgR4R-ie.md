**Shoot it**
- **Camera:** one phone, vertical, handheld at the subject's own eye level, roaming with him through the room. Reframe with the performance rather than locking off; go wider when he moves, tighter when he corners someone. No tripod, no B-roll, no cutaway to a talking head.
- **Location and extras:** a real venue with real people in it at night. The crowd is not set dressing you can skip — the bystanders standing close and reacting are what makes the bit read as a scene rather than a sketch.
- **Light:** whatever the room has. Warm interior lamps, windows going black behind, no added light, no colour grade. The phone-camera look is part of the credibility.
- **Wardrobe:** the subject is dressed *slightly wrong for the room but not costumed* — white dress shirt, black trousers, glasses, a bit of gold. Everyone around him is in dark eveningwear. The contrast does the work; do not dress him as a character.
- **Text rule:** write the line once, lowercase, in a lower-third strip, and never touch it again. It states a premise and is never completed or answered on screen. The punchline goes in the post caption, not on the video.
- **Escalation rule:** each beat has to go one notch further than the last, and the physical prop arrives late — roughly the two-thirds mark, as the plate does here.
- **Talent:** this one needs a performer who will commit in front of strangers, plus a room full of them. It is not replicable without talent.
