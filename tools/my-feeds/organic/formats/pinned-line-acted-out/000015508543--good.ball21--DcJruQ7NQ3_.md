**What carries the value**

The escalation, not any single gag. One room, one afternoon, a chain of things that each go further than the last, held together by a caption that is never finished. Nothing here is a performance to camera — nobody talks to the lens, nobody is styled, and the camera is visibly a participant who is late to every moment. That lateness is the credibility.

**Shoot it**

- **Camera:** one phone, vertical, handheld, held at chest height at the edge of the action by someone who is part of the group. It pans, whips and loses the subject — do not stabilise, do not tripod, do not choreograph the framing. Motion blur is a feature. Shoot from the sideline for the collisions and from directly underneath for the finish.
- **Light:** whatever the room has. This is flat overhead fluorescent with no fill and no colour correction. Do not add lights.
- **Wardrobe:** street gym clothes, mismatched, some shirtless — plain long-sleeves, black shorts, caps, whatever they showed up in. No brand styling, no matching kit, nothing that looks issued.
- **Location:** one room for the whole runtime. Every beat is the same gym; changing location breaks the "this is one afternoon" read that makes it credible.
- **Casting:** a real group who are comfortable enough with each other to do this. This is the one hard requirement — a hired creator cannot fake a friend group, and the format has no on-camera talent to hide behind.
- **Structure rule:** order the clips so each one goes further than the last, and put your worst one last. The one calm beat at 8.4s matters — it makes the next escalation land.
- **Text rule:** write one unfinished fragment, put it top-left, and leave it there untouched for the whole video. Do not complete it, do not change it, do not add a second line. The caption on the post does the completing.
