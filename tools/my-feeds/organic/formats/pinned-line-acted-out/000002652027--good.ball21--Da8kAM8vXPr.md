**What carries the value**

The unfaked physical chaos. Real bodies hitting a real floor in a real gym, escalating shot to shot, with one pinned word framing it and asking nothing of the viewer. No product, no dialogue, no claim — the spectacle is the whole argument, and the word is only the doorway into it.

**Shoot it**

- **Camera:** a phone, vertical, held at standing height on the sideline — a bystander's position, not a filmmaker's. Roughly still, allowed to flinch toward the action. Move the camera between beats, never during one: sideline for most of it, one wide from behind the baseline, one close in under the hoop for the last beat.
- **Light:** whatever the room has. Overhead gym lights plus daylight from high windows. Do not add any.
- **Wardrobe:** street clothes people actually wore that day — a beanie, a plain tee, sweats. One person in a colour that stays readable across every beat (the pink beanie here) so the viewer can track who the bit is happening to. Nothing styled, nothing branded.
- **Rules:** one location for the whole runtime. Escalate physically, not verbally — each beat's stunt is bigger than the last, ending on the biggest. Keep the text word identical and in the same spot from first frame to last. No voiceover, no talking to camera.
- **Casting:** this one needs bodies willing to take real falls, and at least four of them. It is not replicable with a single presenter.
