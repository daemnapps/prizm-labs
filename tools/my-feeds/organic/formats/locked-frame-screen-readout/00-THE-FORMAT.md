# Locked frame, screen readout

**1,894,115 views proved it** · 2 of Damon's swipes use this format

## What it is

One absolutely locked frame with a person in it who never turns to camera or leaves their seat, a caption pinned unchanged for the entire runtime, and a screen inside the frame that is the only thing that changes — a document, a dashboard, a monitor rig — walked through section by section while the viewer reads it over their shoulder.

## Why it works

It is the inverse of a demo: because the frame and the caption are both constants, the screen is the only moving part, so the deck becomes the argument. Reading something over someone's shoulder feels like access rather than a pitch.

## The beats, in order

1. the locked frame opens with the caption pinned and the screen on a title or a single word
2. the first section arrives on screen — a heading, a block of text, a small grid of evidence
3. section two, the person gesturing at the screen without ever turning to camera
4. section three, the stack now visibly longer than the runtime
5. back to a bare title screen or a final readout, the frame and the caption unchanged

## On-screen text

one short line pinned in the identical position from first frame to last, never changed and never argued; everything else that gets read belongs to the document inside the frame

## Camera

absolutely locked for the whole runtime — same framing, same background, same light — with the subject filling one side of frame and the screen the other; no cut, no reframe, no cutaway
