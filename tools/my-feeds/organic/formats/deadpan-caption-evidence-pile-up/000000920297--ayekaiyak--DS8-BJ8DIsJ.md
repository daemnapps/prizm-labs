**What carries the value**

The pile-up itself. Knockout after knockout, from footage the creator did not shoot, each from a visibly different matchup — the body hitting the floor is the payoff and nothing on screen has to sell it. The line does no persuading; it just names what you are about to watch and then gets out of the way.

**Shoot it**

There is no shoot. This format's whole cost is sourcing and cutting:

- **Camera:** none of your own. Every frame is captured from a source you did not make — screen recording, archive, borrowed clips. Do not add a camera move, a zoom, or a transition; hard cut in, hard cut out.
- **Selection is the craft:** find 5–8 instances of the same payoff moment that are visibly unconnected — different people, different rooms, different light. If they look like they came from one session, the pile-up stops reading as evidence and starts reading as staged.
- **Order:** open on the tightest, most physical instance you have. Put your single best payoff around two-thirds through, not last. End on a fresh instance with no summary, no wrap-up, no call to action.
- **Text:** one all-caps line, top third, set once and never touched again. It names the payoff — it does not argue, escalate, or ask a question.
- **Pinned card:** a small identity card in the bottom-left for the whole runtime, so a viewer who arrives at any second knows what they are looking at.
- **No face, no voice, no product handling, no wardrobe.** Nothing here needs a person on camera.
