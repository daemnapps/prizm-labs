## What carries the value

The debris of the joke. Ordinary kitchen objects abandoned in wrong places, one per shot, each a new exhibit for the one pinned line — with the unattended pan sitting in frame at the start and the end as the running gag underneath it. Nobody speaks. Nothing is explained. No product is sold in the original; the slot where a product would go is the misplaced object itself.

## Shoot it

- **Camera:** phone, vertical, handheld. One establishing shot at subject height, then drop to counter level and stay there — five of the six frames are low, close and object-first. Every beat is a separate static-ish shot, not one continuous take; a small handheld drift is fine, no zooms, no whip transitions.
- **Light:** whatever the kitchen has. Warm overhead, no window daylight, visible shadows on the granite. Do not light it — the unlit look is what makes the shots read as evidence rather than a set.
- **Wardrobe:** plain and normal — fitted cream tee, grey sweatpants. Only visible in the opening shot; after that the performer is a forearm and a hand.
- **Casting:** none needed. No dialogue, no face after the opener, no timing to land. One person and a phone on a tripod or propped on the counter.
- **The rule that matters:** the caption never changes and never moves. Set it once, keep it on every frame. And leave one thing cooking, visibly ignored, in the first and last shot.
- **Beat count:** shoot 6–8 evidence shots, each a different corner of the same kitchen, and order them worst-last.
