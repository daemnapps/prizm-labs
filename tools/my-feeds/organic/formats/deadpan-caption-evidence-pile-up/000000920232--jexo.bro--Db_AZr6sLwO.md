**Why it works** — The line makes a flat, unarguable claim once and then never defends it, so every clip after it arrives as fresh evidence for a case the viewer already accepted in the first second. ## What carries the value

The pile-up. No single clip is the payoff — the argument is that six worlds sharing nothing but the subject all contain the same behaviour. Take any one beat out of context and it is a throwaway; run six of them under one unchanged line and it becomes a claim about a category. The unconnectedness is load-bearing: if two beats shared a room, the whole thing would read as staged and the claim would collapse.

## Shoot it

- **Camera.** One phone, one short self-contained shot per instance. Static or lightly handheld — no push, no pan, no move inside a shot. Deliberately give each beat a *different* camera relationship: one square-on at desk height, one long lens from far away, one shot through a doorway or window frame, one at ground level. The mismatch is the format.
- **Light.** Whatever that location has. Do not match them. Overcast outdoors, room light indoors, sea haze, car-park grey — the inconsistency is evidence that these are separate occasions.
- **Wardrobe / styling.** None. Nothing is styled, nothing is set-dressed, nothing is cleaned up. The two composited beats here work precisely because the *background* is boring and true — a real gravel path, a real cockpit, a real bedroom desk.
- **Locations.** Six that share nothing. Aim for at least one indoor, one outdoors-mundane, one high-production borrowed-looking, one caught-through-glass. Never shoot two instances in the same place, and never cut two similar-looking beats next to each other.
- **The line.** Write it once, before you shoot anything, and never touch it again. Centre frame, white sans-serif with a soft shadow, wrapping to two lines. Same pixel position every beat. If you find yourself wanting to change the wording between beats, this is the wrong format.
- **Talent.** None needed — no person appears in any frame and nobody speaks. Three of the six beats here are visibly composited or generated rather than shot; a phone-only rebuild substitutes real found or shot instances for those.
