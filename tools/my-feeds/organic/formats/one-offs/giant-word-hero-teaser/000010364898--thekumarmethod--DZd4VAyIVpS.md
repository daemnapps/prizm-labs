The unfinished announcement. One giant word per beat, ending on **SOON** without ever saying what's coming. ## Shoot it

A phone can do all of this except the landmark.

- **Camera.** Two setups only. (1) Studio wide, locked off at full-body distance, eye level, subject centred with headroom above and floor visible below — no zoom, no drift. (2) Close-ups: face chest-up, and one extreme close on an open hand reaching toward the lens. Vertical 9:16 throughout.
- **Light.** One soft source from camera-left, no fill. Let the right side fall into deep shadow and the backdrop go near-black at the edges. The whole piece is under-lit on purpose — the studio frames are dark and low-contrast, and that darkness is what makes the red type read.
- **Backdrop.** Plain mid-grey seamless. Nothing else in frame — no props, no set dressing.
- **Wardrobe.** Head to toe in one colour, matte, textured. Here: black ribbed knit turtleneck, black trousers, black shoes, worn identically in every shot including the exterior. The single-colour rule is what makes a studio frame and a street frame read as the same film.
- **The one held object.** The subject carries the same object in every beat — at his side, at his waist, at his chest. It's a prop, not a product being demonstrated; it never gets used.
- **Location beat.** One recognisable exterior at night, shot chest-up so the sign behind is legible but small. If you don't have a landmark, this beat is the one that costs you — the format needs a place that means something, not a pretty street.
- **The finish.** Blow the last beat out to white. The image and the type wash together to nearly nothing as the final word lands.
- **Talent.** None required. He stands, turns, holds and extends a hand — no performance, no skill on camera, no lines visible. You need a body and a face, not a performer.
