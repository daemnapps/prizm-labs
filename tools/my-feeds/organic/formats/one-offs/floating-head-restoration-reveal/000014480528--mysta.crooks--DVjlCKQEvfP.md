**Why it works**

The camera never moves, so the only thing that changes on screen is the colour of the object — which makes the transformation impossible to miss and impossible to fake, and gives the viewer a reason to stay until the pattern is fully revealed. The floating head turns satisfying footage into an argument: the caption picks a fight about the neglect ("why did you let it go so far?"), so people stay to watch the rug and then come back to fight in the comments.

**What carries the value**

The surface changing colour under a camera that never moves. Because nothing else in the frame shifts — same wall, same watering can, same cable — the colour coming back is the only event on screen, and that reads as proof rather than editing. The head carries the opinion; the object carries the persuasion. Do not swap those roles.

**Shoot it**

- **Camera:** one position, high on a wall or ceiling, wide-angle, pointing down so the object fills the lower two-thirds of the frame with the blank wall above it. Lock it and never touch it — not a nudge, not a zoom, not a reframe for the whole runtime. The empty upper third is not wasted space; it is where the head goes.
- **Light:** flat, even, overhead, no direction, no shadow. White floor, white wall. The room should be visually boring so the only value in the frame is the object's colour.
- **Wardrobe:** the worker is deliberately anonymous — cap pulled low, sunglasses, plain black t-shirt, khaki shorts, white boot covers. No face, no personality, no logo on the person. He is a pair of hands with a machine. Nobody should be watching him.
- **The object:** start it genuinely ruined — dirty enough that it reads as one flat grey shape with no pattern at all. The whole payoff is that a viewer could not have guessed what was under there.
- **Pacing:** one continuous restoration across the full runtime, one state per beat, always moving toward clean. Do not cut away to another surface or another product; the single object is the format.
- **The head:** a rectangular or oval cutout of a person talking, pinned over the frame's dead space and moved between beats so it never covers the action — bottom-left when the object is far, top-left when the object is near. It stays on screen the entire time.
- **The caption does the arguing.** Pick the judgement the footage invites and put it in the caption, not on screen.
