## What carries the value

The scale jump between beats. Loose unbranded samples → artwork on a screen → a room with your name on the wall → a freight pallet → cases → stock at a store. The unchanging line is what welds six visually unrelated clips into one timeline; if the line changed, each clip would read on its own and the ladder would disappear.

Ordering rule for a rebuild: every beat must be plainly bigger or later than the one before it. Six beats at roughly 5 seconds each. No beat may repeat the stage of another — that's the one thing that kills it.

## Shoot it

- **Camera:** phone, vertical, handheld throughout, no tripod, no stabiliser. Each beat is one shot with no cut inside it. Shot distance varies beat to beat — close on hands for the samples, wide for the room, medium for the pallet — and that variety is fine, because the line is what holds it together, not the framing.
- **Light:** whatever the real place has. Overhead fluorescent in the stockroom, daylight at the loading bay, window light in the office. Do not light anything.
- **Wardrobe:** ordinary work clothes, the same person visible in the later beats so the timeline has one owner. No styling, no change of outfit for effect.
- **What to actually shoot:** the boring milestones you already pass through. Samples arriving. Your screen with the artwork open. Your desk. The delivery. The first case opened. The product where it's sold. Shoot them as they happen over weeks and cut them in order later — this format cannot be staged in an afternoon, and it doesn't need talent, a script or a performance.
- **Text:** one line, white with a soft shadow, top third, two lines centred. Set it once and leave it on every beat, word for word.
