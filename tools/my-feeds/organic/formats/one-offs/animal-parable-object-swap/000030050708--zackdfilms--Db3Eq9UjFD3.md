## Why it works

The whole video happens inside one unchanged set, so when the jar's contents change from animal food to human comforts the viewer reads it instantly as the same jar — the metaphor becomes a physical object instead of an idea. The caption only ever shows a fragment of one spoken sentence at a time, so the sentence is unfinished for the entire runtime and there is no clean point to leave.

## What carries the value

The substitution inside one unchanged set. The jar is established for 23 seconds as the rat's food supply, then refilled with a person's comforts and a person lying in them — so the last two shots don't argue anything, they just show the viewer their own container. The animal earns the attention; the swap is what does the persuading.

**No product appears anywhere in this video.** It is an organic post, not an ad, so there is no product beat to copy. A rebuild has to decide what goes in the jar at beat 5 and beat 6, and that decision is the whole ad.

## Shoot it

**Camera.** Phone on a tripod, vertical, macro or 2x lens, held at the animal's eye level for the first four beats — the camera is low and the jar is tall, which is what makes the jar read as huge. Beat 6 is the one hard rig: phone clamped directly above the jar's mouth pointing straight down the axis.

**Light.** One hard key from above, and a plain cyan-blue seamless behind everything — paper roll or a painted board, no room, no props outside the set. The blue never changes across all six beats and that is what welds the shots together. Table is pale grey.

**Set.** One large clear jar with a gold lid, a bag of brown pellets, and a second smaller jar for depth at the frame edge. Shallow focus throughout: one thing sharp, everything else soft.

**Wardrobe.** The miniature person is a separate still — plain clothes, plain t-shirt and jeans, shot flat against a white wall and cut out, then composited into the jar at scale. It does not need a performer; it needs one clean photo of one person in two poses (standing with a phone; lying on their back looking up).

**What I can't tell you from the frames.** Whether the rat is a live animal, CGI, or a hybrid — nothing in the stills settles it. Whether any of these shots move, or are locked — six stills cannot show camera movement. Whether there is a voiceover — the caption fragments read like a transcript of narration, but the frames are no evidence of audio. In beat 5, the green bottle, the striped cup and the chair are clearly legible; the red soft shape and the stack of grey rings are not, so don't copy them literally. The red ring inside the jar neck at beat 6 could be a lining or a light — the frame doesn't say.
