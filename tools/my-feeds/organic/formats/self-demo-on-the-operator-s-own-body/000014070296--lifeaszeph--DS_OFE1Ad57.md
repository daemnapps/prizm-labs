**What carries the value**
The untreated before-state, offered up as the opening image under a stranger's dismissal — then the same head, same room, same shirt, same fixed camera arriving at the result with nothing cut away to hide behind. The continuity is the proof; the quoted comment is what turns watching it into settling an argument.

**Shoot it**

- **Camera.** Phone on a tripod, vertical, roughly chest height, and it never moves for the whole runtime. The background elements sit in identical positions across every frame — the size changes come from the subject leaning toward and away from the lens, not from a zoom. That is the trick: he moves, the camera doesn't.
- **Room.** One dressed but lived-in corner that stays frozen all runtime — here, dark botanical wallpaper, a vertical wood slat panel at left, a floating shelf with a small plant and a brass watering can, a rust-coloured armchair at right. The room is the continuity proof, so nothing in it may shift between beats.
- **Light.** A warm wall sconce glows in the background, but it is a set dressing practical, not the key — his face is evenly lit from the front by a source that is off-frame in every still, so I can't say what it is. Put a window or a soft lamp in front of the subject and let the warm practical sit behind.
- **Wardrobe.** One plain dark long-sleeve top, no logo, worn from the first frame to the last. Metal watch and dark beaded bracelet stay on throughout — continuity details like these are what make the single-take reading hold.
- **Casting.** No dialogue to deliver and no performance beyond looking at the lens. What this format actually needs is a real before-and-after on a real body: someone whose untreated state is genuinely unflattering and who is willing to open on it. Without that, there is nothing to prove.
- **The comment.** Use the platform's own reply-to-comment sticker on a real comment. A typed-out fake caption loses the borrowed conflict that makes the hook work.
