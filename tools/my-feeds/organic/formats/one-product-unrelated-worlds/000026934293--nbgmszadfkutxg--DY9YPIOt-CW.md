**What carries the value**

The disproportion, and nothing else. An entire room's worth of stuff on the floor, one bag that takes all of it, and a clear panel that lets you watch it fill. There is no face doing the selling, no line to read, no demonstration of a feature — the room being empty in the second shot *is* the argument.

**Shoot it**

- **Camera:** phone on a tripod or propped on something solid, vertical, standing height, in the doorway so the whole floor is in frame. Locked — no pan, no push, no handheld. The before and after must be shot from *exactly* the same spot; if the camera moves, the trick dies. One static wide per location, no cuts inside a beat.
- **The pile is the shoot.** Spend the effort here, not on the camera. It has to spill to the edges of the frame and look like a real household's stuff, not props — mixed sizes, mixed colours, unglamorous items (paper towels, cleaning bottles, a mower, toys). If the pile looks arrangeable, the shot is worthless.
- **Light:** whatever daylight the room has — one window, or the garage door up. No lamps, no fill, no colour correction. Shoot midday to late afternoon; the long window shadows in the third location are just sun, and they're free.
- **Wardrobe:** plain everyday clothes — tank or tee, leggings or shorts, bare feet or trainers. No styling, no makeup pass. Faces are incidental; several performers here never look at the camera at all. Anyone who can lift can be in it.
- **Repeat it.** Four locations minimum, genuinely different rooms with genuinely different categories of stuff (household supplies / clothes / camping gear / kids' toys). The repetition is the format — one location is a demo, four is a capacity claim.
