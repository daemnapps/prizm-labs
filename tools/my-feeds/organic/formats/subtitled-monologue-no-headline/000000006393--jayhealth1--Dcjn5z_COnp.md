**Shoot it**

- **Camera.** One phone on a tripod at counter height, vertical, chest-up on the speaker behind a kitchen island or table. Lock it and never touch it — same height, same distance, same background from first frame to last. No second angle, no cutaway, no B-roll. The counter should fill the bottom third so props can be set down inside the frame.
- **Light.** Big windows behind, bright daylight outside. The speaker in the reference is evenly exposed against those windows, so there is fill coming from the front — a window or a lamp on the camera side, or a soft light just off-lens. Do not shoot windows-behind with no front fill or you get a silhouette. Warm ceiling light in the background adds the "someone's home" read.
- **Wardrobe.** Plain, one colour, no logo doing any talking — a solid t-shirt. Nothing that reads as a uniform or a set.
- **Props.** Three, brought in and out of the same frame at intervals, never cut to: the ingredient being prepared, the raw source material held up in both hands, the finished product held beside the face. Everything gets raised into the shot, not shown in a separate insert.
- **Performance.** One unbroken piece to camera; this format does not work without someone who can hold a monologue. Forearms on the counter between props, leaning in slightly.
- **Text.** Burn the speech in as two-line all-caps cards in a fixed lower-third position, one word per card in yellow. Cut every card mid-sentence — never let a card be a complete thought.
