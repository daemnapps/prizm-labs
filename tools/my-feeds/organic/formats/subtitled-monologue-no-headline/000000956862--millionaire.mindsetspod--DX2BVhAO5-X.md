## What carries the value

The headline card. It is a claim against something the viewer already wants, held unchanged long enough to be read three or four times, and it is never answered in text — the answer is the talking. After it drops, the retention device is the subtitle itself: two to four words per card, cut mid-sentence every time, so the sentence is never finished on screen.

## Shoot it

- **Camera:** one phone on a tripod, locked, at seated eye height, slightly off to the subject's side so the mic is in frame. Shoot the whole take on the tight framing **and** a wide of the same seat if you have a second phone — otherwise crop the one take: the wide is the master, the tight is a punch-in from it. That crop change at ~1/3 in is the only edit in the picture.
- **Light:** flat, warm, even indoor light with no window in shot. No hard shadows on the wall behind.
- **Set:** a plain textured wall (slats, panels, brick) — it has to be busy enough to read as a room and plain enough to leave the frame quiet. A couch or armchair, a real mic on a boom in frame. The mic is what makes it read as an interview rather than a selfie.
- **Wardrobe:** one plain knit polo or plain shirt, no logo, nothing that dates it. He does not change clothes and nothing in the room moves for the whole runtime.
- **Second source:** one continuous clip of the world you are talking about — someone actually working, shot somewhere real — pasted into the lower half of the frame for the hook act only. It must be one take advancing, not a montage. Use footage you own or have the right to use.
- **Talent:** this one needs a speaker. Two and a half minutes of unbroken argument with no demonstration to hide behind — nothing else in the format carries it.
