# Locked-frame subtitled monologue — rebuild sheet

A 63s affiliate post by @jayhealth1 — one man squatting on the floor of his shower, talking straight to a fixed phone through a running rainfall head, rolling speech captions the whole way — 20,484 views, 722 likes.

**Why it works**
The caption is cut off mid-sentence at every single beat, so there is never a clean place to leave; the yellow word is the only thing the eye has to chase in a frame that otherwise never changes. And the speaker is physically inside the routine he is selling — barefoot, seated, being rained on — so the setting does the proving that a talking head in a car would have to argue for.

**What carries the value**
The never-finished caption chain — three or four words a beat, exactly one word burned yellow — read against a man who is visibly doing the thing he is describing rather than describing it from a desk. Nothing is pinned, nothing completes, and the frame never rewards you for looking anywhere else.

**Shoot it**
- **Camera:** one phone on a stand or shelf at squat height, square-on, set once and never touched. Same distance, same angle, first frame to last. No cutaways, no B-roll, no second angle — the stillness is the format.
- **Location:** a real tiled shower or wet room, door open enough to shoot in, with something soft and out of focus in the bottom foreground (a towel heap works) to give the frame depth.
- **Light:** whatever the bathroom has, even and warm. No lamps, no fill, no colour.
- **Wardrobe:** plain black fitted tee and shorts, barefoot. Nothing branded, nothing patterned — it has to read as "I was going to do this anyway".
- **Performance:** talk continuously to the lens for the full minute. Hold the product up once, in the first five seconds, then put it down and never pick it back up.
- **Get wet.** Turn the water on partway through and keep talking. Doing it wet is the credential; a dry person in a dry shower is a set.
- **Captions:** burn in rolling speech subtitles, 3–4 words at a time, centre-low, white all-caps with a black outline, one word per card in yellow. Never let a card complete a sentence.
