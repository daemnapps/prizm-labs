# Subtitled monologue, angle cuts — rebuild sheet

One performer doing a 56-second bit straight to an arm's-length phone in an industrial office, carried by a hook headline that lands once and then nothing but rolling speech captions — 538,733 views, 16,609 likes (@austinnasso).

**Why it works**

The hook headline makes a claim about a named person in the first beat and then gets out of the way, so the viewer has accepted a premise before the performance starts. After that the only text on screen is a speech caption that is never allowed to finish a sentence — every single sampled beat is a mid-sentence fragment ("and each time", "with branches", "into an \"s\"", "lost custody", "realized I", "I learned") — so there is no clean place to leave, and the face doing the impression is the only thing left to watch.

What the frames do **not** show: whether the framing changes are hard cuts between angles or just an arm drifting through one continuous take — six stills nine seconds apart cannot tell you. They also do not show the audio, so what he actually says between fragments is unknown; the caption is our only record of the script. No product appears at any beat, and nothing is ever held to camera.

**What carries the value**

The never-completing subtitle chain, riding on one performer's escalating impression. Every caption is a scrap of a sentence, so the retention device is the read; the persuasion is the performance, and there is nothing else in frame competing with either.

**Shoot it**

- **Camera:** your phone, front camera, held at arm's length, chest-up, at roughly eye level. Do not put it on a tripod — the slight drift and the change in distance beat to beat is part of the look. Reframe on the performance: closer on the big moments, back off between them. One location, no cutaways, no second source, no B-roll.
- **Light:** whatever the room has overhead. This one is flat fluorescent tube light in an office, and it is not flattering or corrected — that's fine, it reads as unstaged.
- **Room:** one busy but real interior — this one is exposed brick, a dark green wall panel, ceiling ducts. It never changes. It just needs to look like a real place, not a set.
- **Wardrobe:** one plain solid top for the whole runtime, nothing branded, nothing that pulls the eye. His is a cream heavyweight mock-neck tee.
- **Text:** hook headline in a white box, black bold, top third, on the opening beat only — then drop it and never bring it back. Everything after is burned-in speech subtitles in a small white box at bottom centre, same position every time. Cut each caption card so it ends mid-sentence.
- **Casting:** this one needs a performer. The whole 56 seconds is one person carrying a bit with their face and voice; there is no product, no demo and no debris to fall back on.
