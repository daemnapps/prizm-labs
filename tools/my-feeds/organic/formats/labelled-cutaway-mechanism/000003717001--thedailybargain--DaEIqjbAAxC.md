**Why it works** — The borrowed graphics do the arguing, so the talker only has to point: an anatomical diagram makes the problem a fact rather than an opinion, and a before/after pair makes the stakes cosmetic as well as medical. Then the product arrives by substitution, not by introduction — same bed, same angle, same shirt, only the object under his head has changed, which reads as a fix instead of an ad.

**What carries the value** — The in-place swap. Same head, same bed, same camera, wrong pillow then right one. The borrowed labelled diagram is what makes the swap land, because it arrives a beat before and tells you what to look at.

**Shoot it** — Phone on a tripod, side-on to the bed at mattress height, roughly two metres out, vertical. Do not move it: shoot every wrong-version beat and every product beat from the identical position so the substitution is the only thing that changes. Plain soft daylight from the window side, no lamps on, no hard shadows. Plain wall behind, one sconce, one plant, dark headboard — a made-up, uncluttered bedroom. Wardrobe rule: one plain solid t-shirt, worn for the whole shoot, wrong-version and right-version alike — if the shirt changes, the swap stops reading. He lies down and talks straight to lens; no performance needed, so anyone who can hold a script can do this. The graphics go on in the edit: leave the upper third of frame empty and boring on purpose, because that's where they land.
