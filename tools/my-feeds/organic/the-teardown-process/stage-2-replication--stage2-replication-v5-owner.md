## THE CULTURE READ — what the audience recognizes (v5, 2026-10-05)

Here is the culture read of the source — every film, show, song, character, catchphrase, meme or format it borrows, with receipts and a confidence, who recognizes it, why it lands, what must be kept, and the borrowed authority it trades on: {culture_read}

How the spec uses it:

- A reference read `CONFIRMED` or `LIKELY` is an ELEMENT of the inventory in its own right: the borrowed recognition. Its MANDATE says what the viewer must recognize and which load-bearing items carry it (the look, the line, the manner, the pairing, the moment of the reveal), written generically like every other mandate; its SOURCE INSTANCE names the reference exactly as the culture read names it, with its receipt; its INJECTION SLOT is FIXED unless the read says the recognition does not rest on that exact reference.
- Every item on the read's KEEP lines goes in section 6, LOAD-BEARING, with the read's reason. A replication that keeps the pacing and drops the reference has copied nothing.
- Where the teardown record names a person or element generically and the read's WHAT THE TEARDOWN RECORD CALLED THEM says who the audience sees, the SOURCE INSTANCE uses the read's name.
- `POSSIBLE` and `UNIDENTIFIED` references are noted in the SOURCE INSTANCE, never built on.
- If the read says `CULTURE LOAD: NONE` or `NO CULTURE READ`, nothing here changes the spec.

Turn it into a replication spec: the discrete, reusable elements that make
this asset work, abstracted from its brand, product and subject so it can
be executed for any brand in any category.

Two rules govern everything below:

**Abstract, never copy.** No mandate may carry the source's brand, product,
or subject. Convert every specific into its generic function — "positions
itself against a category-leading incumbent the audience already trusts,"
not the incumbent's name.

**A style reference is a descriptor, not a brand.** "Dolce & Gabbana-style
baroque" or "athleisure" names an aesthetic precisely, the way "brutalist"
or "art deco" does, and a stylist in any category can act on it. Keep those
— a vague mood word in their place makes the mandate weaker. The rule is
about the brand being sold, not about vocabulary for describing a look.

**Every element is discrete and injectable.** One element is one thing to
build. Each one names where a new brand's material enters.

Give me these seven sections.

**1. FORMAT**

What kind of asset this is, in one line.

**2. THE STRUCTURAL SKELETON**

A table: `Phase | Function | Duration | Fixed position? | Fixed or flexes?`

Duration in seconds, from the reference. For **fixed position**, say
whether the phase must stay in this order. For **fixed or flexes**, say
whether its length is fixed by what the phase does — a scroll-stopping
hook takes about the same few seconds whether the video runs 30 seconds or
90 — or whether it stretches and compresses with total runtime. Give the
fixed ones their ceiling in seconds. No percentages.

**3. THE ELEMENT INVENTORY**

The core section. Number every discrete element. For each, give all four
of these as separate labeled lines:

- **ELEMENT** — a name for the thing.
- **MANDATE** — what must be true. Written generically, executable without
  a judgment call, numbers where numbers apply. No brand, product, or
  subject from the source.
- **SOURCE INSTANCE** — how the reference did it, quoted exactly, so the
  standard is visible.
- **INJECTION SLOT** — what brand-side material fills this: customer
  language, product truth, the offer, identity/character, or FIXED if the
  element is structural and nothing gets injected.

**Read the VERBATIM TRANSCRIPT at the end of the teardown record, not only
the shot table.** The shot table keeps a fragment of speech per shot; the
transcript has every word, one numbered line per speaker turn. When people in
the video talk TO each other — replies, reactions, interruptions, a question
and its answer ("That's scary." / "Why?", "Mm-hmm.") — that exchange is an
element in its own right: list it, with a MANDATE saying who speaks to whom,
roughly how many turns, and that the short replies stay (v4, 2026-09-24,
the owner: "all that dialogue and back-and-forth … that's literally what makes this
thing real and work"). And in THE SCRIPT ARCHITECTURE, a beat that is an
exchange is written as the exchange — each turn on its own line — never
collapsed into one speaker's summary.

**4. THE SCRIPT ARCHITECTURE**

The script as a slot-based template, beat by beat. Write each beat as a
fill-in line with its slots marked in brackets — for example: "If you
[SPECIFIC BEHAVIOUR THE CUSTOMER RECOGNISES], do not [ACTION] because
[REVERSE-PSYCHOLOGY REASON]." Under each beat, state its job in one line
and quote the source's version.

**5. SOPHISTICATION SIGNATURE**

Which stage-move this asset runs, named from the five stages supplied above
and spelled exactly as that list spells it: the plain claim · the claim
enlarged · a mechanism · the mechanism enlarged · identification only.

**Carry the signature forward from the doctrine read above — it has already
been read off the source and cited, so do not re-derive it here.** Print it
with the beat the doctrine read cited. A spec that reaches a different
signature than the banked read has silently forked the record; if the
teardown genuinely contradicts the read, print the doctrine read's answer
and say where the contradiction is, and let a human settle it.

This is a reading of the SOURCE, not a decision about our version. An asset
that states a benefit and stops is not running a mechanism because it
mentions how something works later on; the signature is what the asset leads
with, and where the weight of its argument sits.

Then one line — and it is the reason this section exists: **what the
signature makes load-bearing.** A mechanism asset's load-bearing part is the
explanation, so a replication that keeps the pacing and drops the how has
copied nothing. A claim-enlargement asset's load-bearing part is the scale of
the claim itself. An identification asset carries neither, and its
load-bearing part is who the viewer is being shown as. Section 6 must agree
with this line; if it does not, one of the two is wrong and you say so here.

**6. LOAD-BEARING**

The elements that carry the performance — change them and the asset stops
working. One line of why for each.

**7. SWAPPABLE**

The elements that can change freely per brand at no cost. One line of why
for each.

Output rules: mandates only, no principles and no "consider." Leave out
anything that does not control this format. Never name the source's brand
or product in a mandate. Say nothing about how the asset gets produced —
the same spec must be executable as a generated video, a creator shoot, or
a studio shoot.
