# The two-minute swap

**805 ads across 2 of the 9 brands — but they are two of the four most
concentrated accounts in the sweep.**

| Market | Ads on this shape |
|---|---|
| Men's hair-thinning shampoo | 475 |
| Italian men's face & neck treatment | 330 |

Two brands only, so treat it as a strong shape rather than a universal one. But
look at what it is carrying: it is the Italian brand's **entire top pitch**
(259 ads, plus 71 more across near-identical variants — 71% of everything they
run), and the shampoo brand's second-biggest (475 ads, 25% of their account).

Both brands sell to men who have never bought anything in their category before.
That is not a coincidence, and it is the single most useful thing to know about
this shape.

---

## The shape

**Headline** — names a visible problem in three or four words, then refuses it
immediately. A question and a dismissal in the same breath. It is light, almost
throwaway. It does not sound like a health warning.

**First line of body** — acknowledges that the problem is real and gets worse,
in one sentence. One. Any longer and the shape breaks, because the whole promise
is that this is *not* a big deal.

**The turn** — a specific, tiny amount of effort. Twenty seconds before bed. Two
minutes in the shower, four times a week. The number is always small and always
exact, and it always attaches to something the person **already does anyway**.
That is the mechanism of the whole shape: you are not adding a routine, you are
replacing one item inside a routine that already exists.

**Proof** — either a country or a crowd, in one clause. Best-selling in
somewhere. This many men switched. It is a badge, not an argument, and it goes
past fast.

**Close** — an identity line rather than a discount. For guys who do X. Your
hair isn't done yet. The reader is being invited to be a certain kind of person
who does this small thing, not to buy a treatment for a condition.

---

## The template

```
HEADLINE:
{VISIBLE PROBLEM}? {FLAT REFUSAL — "Not anymore", "Not with X"}

OPENING LINE:
{ONE SENTENCE: the problem is real and it compounds}. But what do you
actually do about it?

THE TURN:
{EXACT SMALL NUMBER — seconds or minutes} {ATTACHED TO SOMETHING THEY
ALREADY DO DAILY}. That's it. That's the whole thing.

WHAT IT REPLACES:
You're not adding anything to your day. You're swapping {THE THING THEY
ALREADY USE} for {YOUR PRODUCT}.

PROOF (one clause):
{THE #1 SELLER IN COUNTRY / N PEOPLE MADE THE SWITCH}.

CLOSE:
{IDENTITY LINE — the kind of person who does this} 👇
```

## Why it works

The reason most people with a slow-moving problem do nothing about it is not
that they do not care. It is that every solution they have seen demands a
*routine* — a new twelve-step thing to remember, a daily commitment, a whole new
identity as someone who does skincare. That is a much bigger ask than the
problem feels like it justifies, so they keep putting it off.

This shape shrinks the ask below the threshold where a person needs to decide
anything. You already shower. You already go to bed. Change one bottle.

Three reasons it lands especially hard with a first-time buyer in a category:

1. **It removes the identity cost.** Buying a face cream means becoming someone
   who buys face creams, and a lot of men will not pay that. Swapping a bottle
   costs nothing in self-image.
2. **The exact number does the reassuring.** "A few minutes" sounds like a lie.
   "Twenty seconds before bed" sounds like a measurement, and people believe
   measurements.
3. **It has no failure mode the reader can imagine.** There is nothing to forget
   and nothing to keep up, because the habit already exists and is already stuck.

Note what these ads do *not* do: they do not explain the mechanism, they do not
run a study, and they barely discount. The Italian brand's top pitch is about
forty words long. Keep it that light.

## Worked example

**Made-up brand: Halden — a gum-health mouth rinse.**

> **Gums pulling back? Not with Halden**
>
> Receding gums don't hurt, which is exactly why people leave them for ten years
> and then get told it's too late. So what do you actually do about it?
>
> Thirty seconds. After you brush. Same as you already do.
>
> You're not adding a routine — you're swapping the blue mouthwash you've been
> buying out of habit for one built to stay on the gumline instead of rinsing
> straight past it.
>
> The number one gum rinse in Sweden. Now here.
>
> For men who'd rather deal with it at forty than at sixty. 👇

Roughly seventy words. That is the right size for this shape.

## Make it a video

**Image prompt**

> A man in his late forties in a small, real bathroom at night — warm overhead
> light, a slightly fogged mirror, an ordinary towel on the rail. He is mid-routine,
> unposed, not looking at camera, caught rather than shot. Nothing styled, no
> marble, no set. Phone-camera realism with a little sensor noise in the shadows.
> Vertical 9:16.

**Motion note**

Twelve to eighteen seconds. Short. The length of the film is itself part of the
promise — if the ad takes thirty seconds the "thirty seconds" claim reads false.

- **0–2s:** handheld, over the shoulder, the mirror. He glances at the problem
  and looks away. That glance is the hook and it should be so quick it almost
  reads as an accident.
- **2–4s:** headline on screen. Plain type, bottom third, no animation flourish.
- **4–10s:** the swap, shown literally. Old bottle picked up and set down. New
  bottle picked up and used. One continuous handheld take if you can — cutting
  makes it look longer and more complicated than it is, which is the one thing
  this shape cannot survive.
- **10–12s:** an on-screen timer or a counted number appears over the action. The
  exact figure, large. Let the viewer see the smallness of it.
- **12–15s:** morning. Same bathroom, daylight, he's fine, he's leaving. No
  before-and-after comparison — this shape never promises transformation, it
  promises easiness.
- **15–18s:** country or crowd badge, then the identity line.

For variants: change the man, the bathroom, the time of day, the exact wording of
the identity close. Keep the single continuous swap shot and keep the runtime
under twenty seconds.
