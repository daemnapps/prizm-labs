# Start here

This pack is built from a sweep of nine competitor brands' **live paid ads** —
6,269 ads running right now, spread across 600 different pitches.

You do not need to have run an ad before to use it. Read this page, then the
strategy page, then pick a shape and build.

---

## What a paid swipe is

A paid swipe is a collection of ads that companies are **currently paying to
show people**. Not their best posts. Not what went viral. The ads they are
buying space for, today, with money.

## Why that is different from an organic swipe

An organic swipe — the videos and posts people share and comment on — tells you
**what people choose to watch**. That is genuinely useful. It tells you what
holds attention, what feels native, what a person will stop for when nothing is
pushing it at them.

A paid swipe tells you something else: **what someone is paying to put in front
of a person who did not ask for it.** That is a harder test, and it is the test
your ads will actually face.

Here is why the difference matters so much.

Ad budgets do not spread out. They concentrate, and they concentrate fast. A
company launches fifteen pitches, two of them make money, and within weeks
almost all of the budget is behind those two. Everything else gets switched off.
Nobody keeps paying to run an ad that loses money — there is no sentiment in it,
and there is no ego in it, because the numbers come in every single day.

So when you look at a brand's live ads and see that one pitch is carrying 835 of
their 1,901 ads, you are not looking at a creative preference. You are looking
at **a survivor**. That pitch beat everything else they tried, and they kept
putting money behind it because it kept paying them back.

That is the whole trick of a paid swipe: **the angles carrying the most ads are
the angles converting.** You do not have to guess which of their ideas worked.
They already spent the money finding out, and the shape of their account tells
you the answer.

One honest limit, stated up front: we can count their ads, not their bank
statement. A big pile of ads on one pitch is strong evidence it is winning, not
proof. The strategy page says more about where that reasoning stops.

## What is in the pack

**`what-the-spend-says.md`** — the strategy lesson, read straight off these
nine brands' numbers. How hard they concentrate, how many versions they build of
a winner, how much is video versus still images, and what all of that says about
how they sell. Every claim carries its number.

**`angle-shapes/`** — ten files, one per **angle shape** that showed up again
and again across these brands.

An angle shape is **the structure of the pitch, not the subject**. "Hair loss"
is a subject. "Name the two solutions everyone already knows, disqualify both on
a specific cost, then present yours as the third one" is a shape — and a men's
shampoo brand, a testosterone supplement, a skincare brand and a probiotic brand
are all running it right now about four completely different products.

That is why shapes are the useful unit. A subject only helps you if you sell
that thing. A shape works on anything.

Each shape file gives you:

- what the headline does, what the first line does, what the proof is, what the
  close asks for
- a fill-in-the-blank template with `{PLACEHOLDER}` slots
- why it works on a person
- how many ads across how many brands are sitting on it
- a worked example, written out for a made-up brand
- an image prompt and a motion note, so you can make a video version

No brand is named anywhere in this pack. Each one is described by its market
only. No body copy is copied — every template is a rewrite of the structure.

## How to use it

**1. Read `what-the-spend-says.md` once.** It is short. It tells you how much to
concentrate and how many versions to build, and those two decisions matter more
than which shape you pick.

**2. Pick two or three shapes, not ten.** Start with the ones carrying the most
ads across the most brands — `the-spec-sheet`, `the-third-option`,
`the-hidden-culprit`, `the-deadline`. Those four are running in five to seven of
the nine brands. They travel.

**3. Fill the slots from the brand folder.** Every `{PLACEHOLDER}` in a template
is a question about your brand — who the customer is, what they already tried,
what the thing actually does, what the offer is. The brand folder has those
answers already written down. Do not invent them at the keyboard; go get them.
If a slot has no answer in the brand folder, that is a gap worth naming, not a
gap to paper over with a guess.

**4. Hand it to the teardown kit to build.** The shapes here are the *pitch*.
The teardown kit turns a pitch into a shot-ready piece: the hook, the beats, the
on-screen text, the shot list. Each shape file ends with an image prompt and a
motion note written to feed straight into that step.

**5. Build several versions of the one that works.** This is the single biggest
lesson in the numbers, and it is on the strategy page in detail. These brands do
not find a winner and move on. They find a winner and build it another two
hundred times.
