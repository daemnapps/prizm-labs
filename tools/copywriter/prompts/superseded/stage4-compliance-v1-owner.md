Pass every variation through compliance before anything reaches a media
buyer. This stage fixes, it doesn't just flag — the output is the same
copy, corrected, plus a short note of what changed.

Check each variation against four things:

1. **Banned words and approved alternatives**, per the language bank. Swap
   silently to the approved phrasing — this is mechanical, not a judgment
   call, so it doesn't need discussion in the note.
2. **Claims the product and offer files don't support.** A result, a
   timeframe, a comparison, a "clinically" or "proven" — if the supplied
   files don't say it, soften it to what they do say, or cut it. Never
   invent a hedge that still implies the same claim in softer words
   ("many people notice a difference" is the same overreach wearing a hat).
3. **Meta ad-policy shapes**, independent of brand rules: no "you" language
   that implies a personal attribute or diagnosis about the viewer ("you
   have wrinkles" vs. "wrinkles happen"), no before/after implying a
   guaranteed result, no unrealistic or exaggerated outcome, no personal
   health/financial assumption about the reader.
4. **Price and guarantee accuracy** against the offer file — a stale price
   or a guarantee stated looser or tighter than the file's exact wording
   gets corrected to match it exactly.
5. **Anything that dates the copy.** Today's date is at the top. A named
   month, season, or holiday only survives if it is true now and still true
   across the flight. A month carried over from the source video is the
   common case and it gets cut or rewritten — the ad runs when it runs, and
   copy that argues with the calendar reads as a rerun. Say what you changed
   and to what.
6. **Anything the chosen brand context contradicts.** The selected files are
   the ground this was written on: a spent hook that got rebuilt on anyway,
   an objection answered differently here than in the objection bank, a
   creator detail that isn't hers. These are correctness failures, not style
   notes, and they get fixed the same as a bad claim.

**Fix in place, then report separately.** The corrected copy stands on its
own, exactly as it should run — no strikethrough, no inline notes, nothing
that would confuse a media buyer pasting it straight into Ads Manager. The
list of what changed and why goes in its own section afterward, one line
per fix, naming the variation it touched.

If a variation needed no changes, say so in the note rather than leaving it
unmentioned — a media buyer reading the note should be able to tell "checked
and clean" from "wasn't checked."

Give me the corrected copy first, the change log after.
