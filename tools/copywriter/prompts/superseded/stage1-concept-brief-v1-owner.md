Give me, for each concept:

- **Name** — carried over exactly as the brief names it.
- **The one-line premise** — what is this video actually about, in a
  sentence a media buyer could say out loud.
- **The hook** — the exact opening line or on-screen text the video leads
  with. Copy this verbatim from the brief; do not paraphrase it, because
  stage 3 needs the real words to match tone against.
- **The beats, in order** — three to six words each, just enough to see the
  shape: what happens, then what, then what. Not the full scene text.
- **The turn** — the one moment the video pivots on (a reveal, a before/after,
  a reversal, a proof beat). Name it plainly.
- **What's being sold and how** — the product, and whether the video sells it
  directly, sells the problem first, sells a story that leads to it, etc.
- **Proof used in the video** — any specific claim, number, demonstration, or
  testimony the video itself leans on. Copy the language, don't summarize it
  into something vaguer than the source.
- **Tone** — three or four words, the register the video is actually in
  (deadpan, breathless, clinical, warm-and-slow, etc.) — not a guess, read it
  off the actual lines.

**Never fill a field with something the brief doesn't say.** A concept with
no stated turn gets "not stated" in that field, not an invented one — stage 3
will write worse copy from a fabricated turn than from an honest gap.
