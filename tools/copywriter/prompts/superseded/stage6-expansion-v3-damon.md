**The language above is queried, not dumped.** These rows were
chosen because of what they ARE — the kind of thing this stage needs — and
ranked by how loud they were and how well sourced. They are real sentences
real people said, each carrying who said it and where it came from.

Use their words. A row marked `ATTRIBUTION ASSUMED` is weaker evidence and a
row from a paid panel is not a customer speaking; weigh them accordingly and
never present either as something a customer said. If nothing in the rows
fits, say so — an empty result is a fact about the bank, not a licence to
write the sentence yourself.

**This stage only runs on an organic source.** If triage said the lane is
`ALREADY AN AD`, the runner skips it: that source's commercial structure
already exists and was inherited at injection, and adding moves to it would be
building a second argument on top of a working one. If you are reading this,
the source was organic and there is no commercial structure yet — it has to be
built from nothing.

An organic post was not made to sell. It has attention and it has credibility
and it has no argument. Your job is to add the smallest number of moves that
turn it into one, **without disturbing what earned the attention.**

**Every addition is gated.** For each move below, decide NEEDED or NOT NEEDED,
and NOT NEEDED is the honest answer most of the time. A move that is not
needed and gets added anyway costs length, dilutes the source's shape, and
makes the piece read like an ad wearing a post's clothes — which is the exact
failure an organic swipe exists to avoid.

Consider, in this order:

1. **The problem, named.** Gate: does the source leave the reader's problem
   implicit in a way that stops the product from meaning anything? Organic
   posts often assume it. If the reader would not know what this solves,
   name it — in the speaker's own terms, never in category language.
2. **Why it works.** Gate: is the reader going to think "sure, but why would
   that do anything?" Add the mechanism only if that doubt is live, and only
   as much of it as the doubt requires.
3. **Proof.** Gate: is there a claim here a stranger has no reason to believe?
   Add proof the files actually carry — a customer's words, a duration, a
   number. Never manufacture credibility with vague volume ("thousands love
   it").
4. **The objection.** Gate: is there a specific reason this reader would not
   act, and does the objection bank or brand context carry our real answer?
   Handle one, at the place the reader would flinch. Not a list.
5. **The ask.** Gate: the source had none. Does this piece need one, and what
   is the smallest version that works for this format? A `caption` may need
   nothing more than the product existing in the frame.

For each move, give: **NEEDED / NOT NEEDED**, the gate's answer in one line,
where it goes (after which move), what it costs in words, and — if needed —
the actual lines, written in the construct's voice rules.

**THE DEPTH PASS.** An option, reached for only where it genuinely makes
sense — never a rule forced on every piece. For each of Damon's nine
sections this piece already carries (named above), the technique options
that build it, from the table above: intensification's fresh angles,

camouflage for the format's native register. **Use an option only where a
language-bank row or a research receipt supports it — cite the row or
receipt, or leave the section as the gated moves above already wrote it.**
Depth is never added to a hook; this pass runs on the body only.

**What you may not do:**

- **Never trim a source beat to pay for an addition.** The budget comes from

  being replicated.
- **Never change the voice.** These moves are the same speaker. An organic
  source whose commercial beats arrive in brand voice is two pieces stapled
  together and reads exactly like it.
