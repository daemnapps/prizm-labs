One source in, one objective record out. **Observation only** — no judgement
about whether it is good, no ideas about what we would do differently, no
abstraction. That happens at the spec. This stage exists so every later stage
argues against the same record instead of re-reading the source and quietly
seeing something different.

The source is one of two things and this stage handles both:

- **A teardown record already** — of a video ad, an organic video, an image
  ad, an organic image, or a caption. Then read it and pull the copy-relevant
  record out of it. Do not redo the teardown; do not contradict it. If it
  records something you would have described differently, the record wins and
  you note the difference in one line rather than overwriting it.
- **Raw copy** — a swipe, pasted in. Then tear the copy down the way a
  teardown tears down a video: what it does, in order, beat by beat.

Give me:

**The beats, in order.** One row per move the copy makes. For each: the beat's
job in three or four words, and the actual line or lines that do it, quoted
exactly. Quote — never paraphrase. A paraphrase at this stage is a
mistranslation every later stage inherits.

**The opening, in full.** The first line verbatim, and separately, what it
does to make someone keep reading — names a person, contradicts a belief,
opens a loop, states a number.

**The turn.** The one place the piece pivots — a reveal, a reversal, a
confession, a proof beat. Quote it and say plainly what changes at that
moment. If the piece genuinely has no turn, say `no turn` rather than electing
one; some formats do not have one and inventing it distorts the spec.

**The proof.** Every specific thing the source leans on: a number, a
testimonial, a demonstration, a named person, a duration, a price. Quote each
and say what kind of proof it is. Where the source is vague, record it as
vague — "thousands of women" is a claim about volume, not a number.

**The close.** How it lands, and what it asks the reader to do. Quote it. If
there is no ask, say so — that is a real and useful fact about an organic
source.

**Voice markers.** Six to ten features of HOW this person writes, each with an
example from the text: sentence length, punctuation habits, capitalisation,
where they break lines, filler words they favour, what they never do. If the
lane is organic, this is the most load-bearing section on the page — the copy
downstream has to be written as this person, and this is the only record of
what that sounds like.

**What it never does.** Two or three things conspicuously absent — no price,
no CTA, never names the product, no exclamation marks. Absences are structure
too, and they are what stops an injection from adding a move the source's shape
cannot carry.

Record it. Nothing else.
