Two rules govern everything below:

**You are injecting, not rewriting.** The source format's spine — its
opening, its sequence, its device, its rhythm — survives this pass intact.
Every move adds a beat. None reorganise what is already there. If you find
yourself moving the source's beats around, stop: that is a different job and
it destroys the reason the format worked.

**The runtime roughly doubles, and that is correct.** The original section
stays whole as the thing that stops the scroll; the added length is earned
because she is already invested by the time the product appears. Never
compress the source to make room.

The four moves.

**Add social proof.** Nothing in the baseline tells her anyone else bought
this. Add it where the product is introduced — a real number and the real
reasons people buy, from the product and offer files. Never invent a number. If none is substantiated, write
`[SLOT: substantiated customer count]` and carry on.

This applies to **every word you output, not only the script**. Do not name
an example figure in your commentary, your reasoning, or your moves table. A
number written anywhere will be read downstream as a fact.

**Describe the product, do not just name it.** The baseline names something
the viewer knows nothing about. From this beat forward we are talking about
the product: what it is, what is in it, how it differs from what she has
already tried.

**Give every component a benefit tied to the buying reason.** Components
listed as a recipe are inert. Each one gets what it does, and that job ties
back to why she is buying — the outcome she wants, in her words from the
language bank. The differentiator runs through the component list, not as a
line at the end.

**Fix the mechanics the source got wrong.** The borrowed format describes a
usage that belongs to its own product, not ours — frequency, method,
duration, what the product visibly does. Take the documented usage from the
product file exactly as written and correct every mechanic carried across.
This is the easiest move to miss, because the script reads fine either way.

Two things apply throughout. **The added beats must read as one continuous
thought**, not a format with a pitch bolted on — write the connective tissue
in the speaker's own rhythm, finishing sentences the language bank shows she
already starts. And **every message beat gets a shot**; a message with no
scene is a line the editor has to invent a picture for. Take shots from what
the source already does where you can.
