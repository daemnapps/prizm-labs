Write the opening. Six versions, then stop — a human picks one before
anything else is written, because every line after it has to match its
register.

**A hook is a line and a scroll stopper, together.** The line is what she
hears or reads; the scroll stopper is the first-frame visual event that
arrests the thumb before a single word lands. Neither is optional and
neither is the other's caption — they are the same thought in two
channels, show and tell at once. "Selfie walk-and-talk at the entrance"
is a camera setup, not a scroll stopper. A scroll stopper is a thing
*happening*: an act mid-motion, an object where it should not be, a
texture close enough to feel, a before mid-transformation. If the frame
would read as ordinary with the sound off, it stops nobody. Take the
visual vocabulary from what the source already does where it serves;
build inside the format's world where it does not.

**The placement plan names the opening beat by timestamp.** Rewrite that
beat and no other. Do not rewrite a later line because it looks more like a
hook.

**First, name the foundational hook.** Before writing anything, read the
source's opening beat in the teardown record and pin three things:

- **Context** — where the opening drops the viewer: the place, the moment
  mid-happening, whose company she is suddenly in.
- **Structure** — the sentence's shape and device: a stated rule, a
  warning, a question, a confession, a boast, a complaint, a flat
  observation. **That function is the format's, and it stays.**
- **Awareness** — what the viewer must already know or believe for the
  line to land, straight from the placement plan's entry rung.

This is the foundational hook. Every variation is a deliberate move
against it along a named axis — a different research moment filling the
same device, the same moment in a different context, the same context
entered at a different awareness — never five random remixes of one
sentence. **The function is not the sentence.** If the source says
"Always read the reviews before buying skincare," the function is *a
warning stated flatly to camera* — not the words "always read the
reviews before." Four warnings can be four completely different
sentences. Reusing the source's exact frame and changing only its last
few words is copying, not keeping the device.

**Version 0 is the straight swipe, and it is the control.**

Take the source's opening beat exactly as it is and change only the words
that must change — the brand, the product, the category. Nothing else.
Same sentence, same rhythm, same length, same punctuation. Its scroll
stopper is the source's own first frame, held.

This is the version that already performed, wearing our name. Every variation
you write afterwards is a bet against it, and if none of them beat it in
testing that is a real finding rather than a failure. **Label it clearly as
the control and never improve it.**

Then write five variations. The rest of this prompt is about those.

**The ledger is spent ground.** Before writing, read the hook ledger. A
verbatim that already carries a hook there is **spent** — you do not
build another hook on it, however strong it is, unless the ledger marks
its hook `validated`. A validated hook returns only as itself — the
control of its own next test — never re-worded. The research is deeper
than its five most quotable lines, and reaching for a quote the ledger
shows twice already is the signal to mine a fresher seam: a body part
the scripts underweight, a behaviour nobody has used, a phrase from the
bank's edges. If the bank genuinely has nothing fresh left for this
format, say so plainly instead of repeating — that is a real finding,
and it routes to a research pass, not to a sixth cardigan.

Five rules govern what replaces the source's words:

**Every hook traces to a verbatim.** Pull the moment from the language bank
and the avatar — a behaviour, a time of day, an object, a thing she does
with her hands. The strongest hooks name a private moment she thought nobody
had noticed. A hook that cannot cite its source is invented and gets cut,
however well it reads. Reaching for a phrase that is not in the bank is the
signal you are writing at her instead of from her.

**Finish a sentence she has already started.** The bank holds half-phrases
she uses about herself. A line lands when it completes one she recognises
rather than introducing a new one.

**The line and the scroll stopper must be about the same thing.** A
strong line over an unrelated first frame kills the script in the first
second — the words say one thing, the picture says another, and she
resolves it by scrolling. The stopper shows the thing the line names,
already happening.

**Five variations means five different sentences.** The test is exact: **if
any two share their first four words, they are the same hook and you have
written one and padded it.** This applies to the control too — a variation
that opens like version 0 is not a variation. Same function, five genuinely
different ways of performing it — a different moment, a different failure, a different
person noticing, a different half-second of her life.

**Never open with a template.** These are banned outright, in any wording:
"If you're struggling with…", "If you're tired of…", "If you've ever…",
"POV:", "Nobody talks about…", "Here's why…", "The one thing…", "Stop
scrolling if…". They are the default shape of every ad in every category,
they signal an ad in the first two words, and none of them are how a person
starts a sentence. If a hook can be reused verbatim by a different brand in
a different category, it is a template.

**Prefer a behaviour over a condition.** A behaviour is how she talks about
her life. A condition is how a brand talks about her. Match the speaker's
own voice too — sentence length, vocabulary, rhythm, and any quirks the
teardown record shows. If she drops articles, the hook drops articles.

Write each hook in the format's own currency, per the placement plan: a
spoken line if the format speaks, a card if it does not, a frame if it has
neither.

Give me these four sections.

**1. THE FOUNDATIONAL HOOK**

The source's opening: its context, its structure, its awareness entry —
three lines, from the teardown record and the placement plan.

**2. VERSION 0 — THE CONTROL**

The source's opening, swiped straight, with only brand words changed. Show
the source line and ours side by side so the swap is visible, then its
scroll stopper — the source's own first frame, held. No citation
needed — it is the source's own line.

**3. THE FIVE VARIATIONS**

For each: the line · **the scroll stopper — what is happening in frame
one, concrete enough to shoot from the sentence alone** · the verbatim it
came from **quoted inside its original full sentence**, with its source ·
the axis it moves on against the foundational hook · what it assumes
about the speaker.

Quoting the whole original sentence is not decoration. A fragment can be
cited accurately and still be used to mean something the customer never
said — showing the sentence it lived in makes that visible on the page.

That last one matters. If the speaker cannot truthfully say it, the hook is
unusable no matter how good it is. Flag it plainly.

**4. LEDGER ROWS**

Six rows, append-ready for the hook ledger, matching its columns: date,
run label, verbatim spent, hook line, status — `control-ran` for version
0 if the chain proceeds on it, `offered` for the rest. The human updates
statuses after the pick; you never mark anything picked, testing or
validated.

**Do not recommend one.** The pick is a human decision, and a
recommendation on the page anchors it. Your job ends at six clean options —
if one of them is obviously strongest, the human will see it too.

Write no other part of the script.
