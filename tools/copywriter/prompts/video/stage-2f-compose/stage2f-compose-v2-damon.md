## The two rules that govern everything

**1 · The construct is BRAND-FREE.** The avatar, the product, the offer and
the research are here so you know what is TRUE and what the beats have to be
able to carry. None of them may appear in a mandate. Every brand-specific fact
goes into an INJECTION SLOT and is filled by a later stage. A construct that
names the product has done the next stage's job badly and its own job not at
all.

**2 · The framework is a PLAN, not a law.** Its order is the intended order.
The market state outranks it: a section the chosen awareness level does not
call for is **dropped**, and you print one line saying which section you
dropped and which rule dropped it. Equally, a section the level demands and the
framework omits is **added**, with the same one line. The sections list is a
vocabulary — you may not rename a section, and you may not invent one.

## The rules under those

- **Agnostic.** No brand, product, person, platform, model or room name
  anywhere in your answer.
- **No gendered generic pronoun.** The viewer, the avatar, the speaker, they.
- **No scene length as a number.** A beat runs as long as what it does takes.
  Say what fixes it, never how many seconds.
- **A picture is not a spoken line.** Where a phase shows something, say what
  is in frame — never a stage direction to a person, and never a caption. The
  words are the script architecture's job; the picture is this one's.
- **Never coin a name.** Sections, techniques, awareness levels, sophistication
  stages and the format all come from the material above, spelled exactly as it
  spells them. A name you made up is a name nothing downstream can find.
- **Cite or leave a slot.** A claim about what this audience says, believes or
  has already tried is either carried by a row in the language or the research —
  quoted, with who said it — or it is written `[UNFILLED: <what is missing>]`.
  An empty bank is a fact about the bank, never a licence to write the sentence
  yourself.
- **Read the language, do not transplant it.** Every row was true of a
  particular person in a particular moment and none of it was written for this
  asset. Keep the truth, drop the frame. A row flagged `ATTRIBUTION ASSUMED` is
  weaker evidence and a paid panel is not a customer speaking.

## What to write

Give me these seven sections, with these headings, in this order.

**1. FORMAT**

What kind of asset this is, in one line, naming the format exactly as the
format profile names it.

**2. THE STRUCTURAL SKELETON**

A table: `Phase | Section | Technique | Function | Picture | Shows | Fixed position? | Fixed or flexes?`

One row per phase, in the order the composed asset plays. **Section** is the
section id from the framework, spelled as the sections vocabulary spells it.
**Technique** is one of the seven, spelled as the techniques list spells it.
For **fixed position**, say whether the phase must stay in this order and why —
a phase whose job depends on what the viewer has already accepted is fixed.
For **fixed or flexes**, say whether its length is fixed by what the phase does
or whether it stretches and compresses with total runtime. No seconds, no
percentages.

**Picture** and **Shows** are the two columns that decide what the piece
looks like, and they exist because of a fault found on 2026-09-19: a whole
construct came through with every phase spoken straight to the lens, and the
brief written from it had nothing to cut to. So every phase declares one of
these three in **Picture**:

| **Picture** | What it means |
|---|---|
| `to camera` | the speaker says this phase to the lens, and the picture is them |
| `B-roll` | nobody speaks on screen; the phase is a picture and the voice runs over it |
| `to camera + B-roll` | a pair: the speaker says it, then a second scene shows it, the voice running unbroken across both |

**Shows** is what the B-roll is a picture OF — written generically, the way
every other mandate here is written: the thing in the hand, the row of things
already tried, the surface being worked on, the two states side by side, the
place the behaviour happens. `—` where **Picture** is `to camera`.

**The phases that SHOW default to B-roll**, and a construct that makes one of
them `to camera` says in one line why:

- **proof** — a result is seen, not asserted;
- **product** — the thing itself, in frame, from its own reference;
- **failed-solutions** — the row of things already tried is a picture, and a
  list of them spoken is the same beat with nothing to look at;
- **unique-mechanism** — the demonstration IS the mechanism. A mechanism
  described by a talking head is a claim; a mechanism watched happening is
  the argument this whole framework exists to make.

A phase whose **Picture** is `to camera + B-roll` produces TWO scenes
downstream, not a scene with a note in it. The brief stage has no way to
invent a picture the construct never asked for, and it has been told never to
try — so a phase that does not declare one gets a talking head.

Under the table, the drop/add line or lines required by rule 2 — one line each,
naming the section and the rule. If nothing was dropped or added, say so in one
line.

**3. THE ELEMENT INVENTORY**

The core section. Number every discrete element. One element is one thing to
build. For each, give all four of these as separate labelled lines:

- **ELEMENT** — a name for the thing.
- **MANDATE** — what must be true. Written generically, executable without a
  judgment call, numbers where numbers apply. No brand, product or subject.
- **SCENE SHAPE** — what this beat looks and feels like, taken from the
  technique's own scene shape in the material above: what is on screen, what the
  emotion does, and what changes between the start of the beat and its end.
  Open it with the element's **Picture** — `to camera`, `B-roll` or
  `to camera + B-roll` — and where it is not `to camera`, say what is in
  frame instead of a face: the hands, the thing, the surface, the two states.
  The skeleton's row and this line must agree.
- **INJECTION SLOT** — what brand-side material fills this: customer language,
  product truth, the offer, identity/character, or FIXED if the element is
  structural and nothing gets injected.

**4. THE SCRIPT ARCHITECTURE**

The script as a slot-based template, phase by phase, in the skeleton's order.
Write each beat as a fill-in line with its slots marked in brackets — for
example: "If you [SPECIFIC BEHAVIOUR THE AUDIENCE RECOGNISES], do not [ACTION]
because [REASON THAT REVERSES THE EXPECTATION]."

Under each beat, four lines and no more:

- **Section** — the section id this beat is holding.
- **Technique** — the one technique it runs, and which of that technique's
  named sub-methods.
- **Job** — what this beat does, in one line.
- **Leans on** — the language or research rows this beat is built from, quoted
  with who said it and where it came from, or `[UNFILLED: …]`.

**5. SOPHISTICATION SIGNATURE**

Which stage-move this asset runs, named from the five stages supplied above and
spelled exactly as that list spells it: the plain claim · the claim enlarged ·
a mechanism · the mechanism enlarged · identification only.

**Take it from the market state, which has already decided it — do not
re-derive it here.** Print it with the phase that carries it. If the framework
that was chosen cannot run the signature the market state names — an
identification-only framework against a market that needs a mechanism, say —
print both, say where they disagree, and let a human settle it. Never quietly
pick one.

Then one line, and it is why this section exists: **what the signature makes
load-bearing.** A mechanism asset's load-bearing part is the explanation, so a
build that keeps the pacing and drops the how has built nothing. A
claim-enlargement asset's load-bearing part is the scale of the claim itself.
An identification asset carries neither, and its load-bearing part is who the
viewer is being shown as. Section 6 must agree with this line; if it does not,
one of the two is wrong and you say so here.

**5b. WHAT THE PIECE LOOKS LIKE**

Two lines, no more. First: the alternation this construct runs — which phases
are to camera, which are B-roll, and where the pairs are, read off the
skeleton rather than decided again here. Second: the one thing this format
must be seen doing, from the format profile's own line about what carries the
weight. A construct whose skeleton is `to camera` end to end writes that here
in plain words, and says which rule above allowed it.

**6. LOAD-BEARING**

The elements that carry the performance — change them and the asset stops
working. One line of why for each. The format profile names what carries the
weight in this shape; agree with it or say where you do not.

**7. SWAPPABLE**

The elements that can change freely per brand at no cost. One line of why for
each.
