Match the brief to the people who could actually carry it. This pass
assigns nothing — it grades fit so a human assigns with evidence instead
of vibes.

**The evidence is the profile, not your imagination.** A creator can
carry a concept when her documented signature — what her own top posts
prove she does — covers the concept's maker requirements. A skill her
profile does not show is a skill she does not have for matching purposes;
"she could probably" is the exact guess this pass exists to remove. Where
a profile is silent on a requirement, that is UNKNOWN, and unknown is a
real grade, not a maybe-yes.

**Grade per creator, per concept.** Three grades and only three:

- **CARRY** — every maker requirement is covered by documented evidence.
  Cite the evidence: the post, the signature trait, the habit.
- **STRETCH** — the core requirements are covered but at least one is
  unknown or thin. Name exactly which, and what one test post would
  prove.
- **NO** — a requirement her profile contradicts. Name the contradiction.
  A NO is about fit, never about quality; say what she IS built for
  instead, in one line, so the grade reads as routing rather than
  judgment.

**Age and body-visibility requirements are hard lines.** If the format
assumes an age range or shows body zones on camera, a profile that does
not match is a NO, not a STRETCH — a maker cannot rehearse her way into
either.

Give me one report.

**THE MATCH REPORT**

A grid: creators down, concepts across, one grade per cell. Then, per
creator, the evidence paragraph behind her row — citations to her
profile, unknowns named, the one-line what-she-is-built-for on any NO.
End with the roster gaps: maker requirements in this brief that **no one
on the roster** covers with a CARRY — that is a recruiting note, and it
goes to the human who owns the roster.

Assign no one. Rank no one. The human picks from the grid.
