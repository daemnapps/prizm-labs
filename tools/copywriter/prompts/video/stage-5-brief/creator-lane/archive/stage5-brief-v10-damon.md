# WHAT THIS DOCUMENT IS

A shot list she can film from. Nothing else.

Everything upstream — the teardown, the spec, the injection, the four stage-4
passes, the audit — was our working material. **None of it appears here.** She
is not reviewing our process; she is filming.

## What goes in

Exactly four things, and nothing else:

1. **The source video, as a link**, at the top of each concept. One line. She
   can watch it herself in ten seconds, which tells her more than a paragraph
   about it ever will.
2. **The script** — every scene, in order.
3. **A timestamp on every scene** — its span in the finished video, and the
   reference point in her own footage.
4. **A picture slot on every scene**, which a later pass fills with a
   generated frame.

## What is cut — do not write any of it

Cut it entirely. Do not summarise it, do not compress it into a line, do not
move it to an appendix.

- **Why one choice was made over another.** No "what we changed", no "why it
  works", no "what we cut and why". She did not ask and it is not her decision.
- **The objection, and any map back to the objection bank.** Never name a bank,
  a ledger, a spec or a stage in this document.
- **The line-in-her-voice trace** — which phrase came from where in the
  language bank.
- **The removals** — what was taken out of the source, whose names were
  stripped, how many claims were dropped.
- **Runtime accounting.** No budget, no seconds-remaining, no beat count.
- **Audit flags and claim warnings.** If a claim cannot be made, the line
  simply is not in the script. She never sees a warning about a line she was
  never given.
- **Unresolved slots.** A slot that could not be filled means the scene is not
  in the brief. Never ship a blank for her to guess at.
- **Assembly notes** — anything addressed to whoever cuts the video, generates
  frames, or checks them. That is our business and belongs in our own files.

If a section would only make sense to someone who had read the upstream
material, it does not belong in this document.

## The two things that DO stay

These are not process notes; they are the difference between a usable take and
an unusable one.

- **What she may not say**, as a short flat list. No explanation of where each
  rule came from.
- **The guarantee, word for word**, if the script has her say it.

Keep both tight. A list, not an essay.

# THE SHAPE

```
# <Concept name>

**Watch first:** <link to the source video>

**Scene 1 · 0:00–0:04**

**On screen** — what the camera sees: framing, what she is doing, what is in
her hands, the light, and what she is wearing.

**She says** —
> the spoken line, verbatim

**How** — the delivery note, one line.

*Reference: her reel at 0:41 — same framing, nothing new to look at.*

![Scene 1](—)

**Scene 2 · 0:04–0:13**
…
```

Rules on that block, all four load-bearing:

1. **`**Scene N · <start>–<end>**` opens every scene.** Bold, the word Scene,
   the number, a middle dot, the span. Not a heading, not a table row.
2. **Every scene ends with a `*Reference:*` line naming a timestamp in her own
   footage** — the moment showing this shot, or the nearest to it. Add *same
   framing, nothing new to look at* when her footage already has it.
3. **`![Scene N](—)` sits under the reference line on every scene.** That is
   the picture slot. Leave the dash; a later pass replaces it.
4. **The On screen paragraph states what she is wearing, in full, every time**
   — never "as before", never omitted.

A concept starts with a `# ` heading; scene numbers restart at 1 in each.

# IF THE BRIEF IS UNASSIGNED

When no creator is attached — swipe research with nobody to address — write it
to no one. Never invent a name, never say "you" as though addressing a person
we have not signed, and never reference habits, past posts or a style we have
no evidence for. Use *the creator* throughout. Every `*Reference:*` line reads
`*Reference: none — no creator footage for this brief.*`

# TONE

Write it the way you would hand a page to someone about to start filming in ten
minutes. Short sentences. No preamble, no thanks, no sign-off, no explanation
of the document itself. Start at the first concept.
