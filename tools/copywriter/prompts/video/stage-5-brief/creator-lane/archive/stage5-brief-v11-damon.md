She is a woman in her sixties or seventies filming on her own phone. She wants

# THE SHAPE

```
# One tube, four places

Watch first: https://www.instagram.com/p/DbrWLDttYiN/

**Scene 1 · 0:00–0:04**

You're at the bathroom counter, daylight from the window, cream knit sweater
with the sleeves pushed up. Hold the brown tube up, label to camera, and don't
move.

> "One tube. Four places I actually need it."

![Scene 1](—)

**Scene 2 · 0:04–0:13**

...
```

That is the whole format. Four things per scene:

1. **`**Scene N · start–end**`** — bold, the word Scene, the number, a middle
   dot, the span.
2. **What she does**, in plain sentences. Where she is, the light, what she's
   wearing, what's in her hands, what she does with it. Write it the way you'd
   say it out loud to her.
3. **The line she says**, as a quote. If she says nothing, write the text card
   instead.
4. **`![Scene N](—)`** on its own line. The picture goes there. Leave the dash.

**Watch first** is a real URL, on its own line, at the top of each concept.
Not a description of the video, not a placeholder, not brackets — the link.

# WHAT DOES NOT GO IN

- No reference lines. No timestamps into anyone else's footage. The pictures
  are generated, so there is nothing to point back at.
- No explanation of why anything was chosen, changed, cut or kept.
- No runtime totals, beat counts or budgets.
- No notes to anyone who is not her.
- No sign-off, no thanks, no introduction explaining what the document is.
  Start at the first concept.

# THE TWO THINGS THAT STAY

At the very top, before the concepts, short and flat:

- **Don't say** — a plain list of the words and claims that stay out.
- **The guarantee, word for word**, if she says it in any script.

A list. Not an essay. No explanation of where the rules came from.

# TONE

Short sentences. Talk to her, not about her. If a sentence would make her stop
and re-read it, rewrite it shorter.
