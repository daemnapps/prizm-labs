Turn these into the one document the maker receives. Everything upstream was

Four rules govern everything below.

**Nothing unresolved crosses into the maker's document.** If a script still
carries a `[SLOT: …]`, either the fact exists and you fill it, or the line is
cut. A maker who meets an unfilled slot will skip the line or invent
something — both are worse than not having the line. Every slot you cut gets
listed in the internal section so nobody thinks it was handled.

**The route decides whether this is a script or a direction.** When the maker
is a contracted creator, the brief carries **concepts, hook options and story
beats — never a script to read.** Her own words are the reason she works;
handing her lines makes her sound like she is reading an ad, and most
contracts say the content stays hers. When the maker is the brand itself — a
founder, a studio, a generated asset — the brief carries **the script as
written**, because there is no voice to preserve but our own.

**Every concept names the source it came from and the habit it uses.** The
maker does not see the teardown, but she should be able to tell this was
built for her rather than sent to everyone. Name which of her own posts the
structure came from, and which of her habits each concept leans on. This is
also what stops one brief going out repeatedly with the names changed.

**The maker must never have to ask a question.** If she would have to guess a
length, a shot, a product, or what she is allowed to say, the brief has
failed and the fix is in the brief, not in her.

Give me these two documents.

**DOCUMENT ONE — THE BRIEF**

What the maker receives. Write it to her, warmly, in plain language. No
jargon from this process appears anywhere in it — no stages, no lanes, no
awareness rungs, no move numbers.

Open with a short note to her by name: how many videos, that these are built
around her own content and style, and — if she is a creator — that these are
directions rather than scripts and her own wording is wanted.

Then the requirements every video shares: format and orientation, length
range, file delivery, music and rights constraints, anything the platform or
the agreement demands.

Then the brand direction that applies across all of them, and the offer
direction: what may be said about the offer, the exact guarantee wording, and
when to use it. Keep it short — this is context, not instruction.

Then one section per concept, up to the number of videos owed. Each carries:

- **A name** for the concept, so it can be referred to in one word
- **Where it came from** — which of her own posts, and the habit it uses
- **The core idea** in two or three sentences
- **The opening** — the control version first, then two or three alternatives
  she can choose between, in her own delivery mode
- **The beats** — what happens in order, as direction rather than dialogue if
  she is a creator, as script if she is not
- **Must-have shots** — what has to be in frame for this to work
- **What to say about the product** — the true things, in her words
- **Suggested length**

Close with anything she needs to know that is neither brand nor concept —
who to send questions to, what happens next.

**DOCUMENT TWO — INTERNAL**

Never sent to the maker. What the team needs to check the work and to make
the next brief better.

- **Concept provenance** — each concept traced to its source format and to the
  post of hers it was built from
- **Slots cut** — every fact that was missing, what was removed, who owns it
- **Flags** — every rule the scripts brushed against, every claim carried
  without substantiation
- **Rejected** — any concept you considered and dropped, and why. This is the
  most useful part of this document for whoever writes the next one.
- **To enrich her profile** — what this round teaches about her that belongs
  in the creator file once results come back
