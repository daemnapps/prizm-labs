Five rules govern everything below.

**Nothing unresolved crosses into the maker's document.** If a script still
carries a `[SLOT: …]`, either the fact exists **in one of the files above**
and you fill it from there, or the line is cut. A maker who meets an
unfilled slot will skip the line or invent something — both are worse than
not having the line. Every slot you cut gets listed in the internal section
so nobody thinks it was handled.

There is no third option. **Generalising a slot is filling it.** Turning
`[SLOT: current promotion]` into "the bundle deal" or "today's sale" has
announced a promotion nobody approved, in the one document that goes to
someone who will say it out loud. If the fact is not in a file, the line is
cut — say so in the internal document and move on.

**This document adds no facts.** Every claim in the maker's document traces
to a supplied file, and the brief is the last place where an invention is
still cheap to catch. Watch for the ones that arrive as flavour rather than
as claims: a country or heritage the product file never mentions, a named
method or ritual, an ingredient count, a manufacturing story, a founder
detail, a scientific mechanism stated more confidently than the file states
it. Those read as harmless colour on the page and become claims the moment
the maker says them to camera. If it is not in a file, it does not appear —
not in a concept name, not in a hook option, not in an aside.

And a phrase appearing in a file is not automatically ours to use. The
language bank records what customers say **and what they were shown not to
say** — a phrase sitting inside a sentence that reports its absence
("neither described it as X unprompted", "nobody used the word X") is
evidence against X, not a source for it. Read the sentence around any
phrase before lifting it: if that sentence is telling you the phrase
failed, was rejected, or never came up, the phrase is banned rather than
available, and a concept built on it is built on the file's opposite.

Prices come from the offer file exactly as written. Where the same product
sells at different prices on different surfaces, say which surface each
price belongs to rather than choosing one, and never let two numbers for
the same thing appear in one document without that explanation.

**The route decides whether this is a script or a direction.** When the maker
is a contracted creator, the brief carries **concepts, hook options and story
beats — never a script to read.** Her own words are the reason she works;
handing her lines makes her sound like she is reading an ad, and most
contracts say the content stays hers. When the maker is the brand itself — a
founder, a studio, a generated asset — the brief carries **the script as
written**, because there is no voice to preserve but our own.

**Every concept names the source it came from and the habit it uses.** The
maker does not see the teardown, but she should be able to tell this was
built for her rather than sent to everyone. Name which of her own posts the
structure came from, and which of her habits each concept leans on. This is
also what stops one brief going out repeatedly with the names changed.

**The maker must never have to ask a question.** If she would have to guess a
length, a shot, a product, or what she is allowed to say, the brief has
failed and the fix is in the brief, not in her.

**DOCUMENT ONE — THE BRIEF**

What the maker receives. Write it to her, warmly, in plain language. No
jargon from this process appears anywhere in it — no stages, no lanes, no
awareness rungs, no move numbers, no cell talk.

Open with a short note to her by name: how many videos, that these are built
around her own content and style, and — if she is a creator — that these are
directions rather than scripts and her own wording is wanted.

Then the requirements every video shares: format and orientation, length
range, file delivery, music and rights constraints, anything the platform or
the agreement demands.

Then the brand direction that applies across all of them, and the offer
direction: what may be said about the offer, the exact guarantee wording, and
when to use it. Keep it short — this is context, not instruction.

Then one section per concept, up to the number of videos owed. Each carries:

- **A name** for the concept, so it can be referred to in one word
- **Where it came from** — which of her own posts, and the habit it uses
- **The core idea** in two or three sentences
- **The openings** — the control first, then **every approved variation from
  the hook set, none dropped and none ranked**: each with its line and its
  first-frame moment (the scroll stopper), in her own delivery mode. These
  are filmed as alternate openings on the same video — the body does not
  change between them — and the market decides which runs. If the set says
  a variation needs a particular shot emphasis, that note rides with that
  variation, not with the video.
- **The beats** — what happens in order, as direction rather than dialogue if
  she is a creator, as script if she is not
- **Must-have shots** — what has to be in frame for this to work
- **What to say about the product** — the true things, in her words
- **Suggested length**

Close with anything she needs to know that is neither brand nor concept —
who to send questions to, what happens next.

**DOCUMENT TWO — INTERNAL**

Never sent to the maker. What the team needs to check the work and to make
the next brief better.

- **Claim trace** — write this section **before** you finalise the maker's
  document, and let it decide what survives. List every proper noun, place
  or nationality, named method or ritual, ingredient, mechanism, number and
  price that appears anywhere in the maker's document — concept titles and
  hook options included — and against each one name the file it came from.
  A row you cannot name a file for is not a flag to raise later: **delete
  that claim from the maker's document now, then write the row as
  `<claim> — REMOVED, no source`.** One row per claim, no grouping.
- **The test matrix** — per concept: each opening as its own cell, control
  marked, so whoever launches knows exactly how many cuts exist and what
  distinguishes them. Statuses live in the hook ledger, not here.
- **Concept provenance** — each concept traced to its source format and to the
  post of hers it was built from
- **Slots cut** — every fact that was missing, what was removed, who owns it
- **Flags** — every rule the scripts brushed against, every claim carried
  without substantiation
- **Rejected** — any concept you considered and dropped, and why. This is the
  most useful part of this document for whoever writes the next one.
- **To enrich her profile** — what this round teaches about her that belongs
  in the creator file once results come back
