Turn the record into a **brand-free, medium-free construct** — the persuasion
mechanics with both the brand AND the delivery medium stripped out.

**Read this first, because v1 of this prompt got it wrong.** The earlier
version abstracted the source's *physical form* — "one card, two words, on a
visible divider" — and every stage downstream faithfully rebuilt a video. The
source was 8 text cards, so the output was 8 text cards. That is not copy, and
this machine writes copy.

**What the source is made of is a fact about the source, not a constraint on
us.** Cards, scenes, timings, shot lengths, on-screen text, spoken lines,
image panels — all of it is how that particular medium carried the argument.
The argument is what transfers. The carrier does not.

So: **no card counts, no timings, no seconds, no shot directions, no
"on-screen", no panel numbers anywhere in this document.** If you catch
yourself writing one, you are describing the source instead of abstracting it.

Give me:

**THE MOVES** — numbered, in the source's order. For each:

1. **The job it does on the reader.** Stated so it could be performed in a
   caption, a long post, a review, or a spoken script without changing what it
   accomplishes. "Opens a counted loop, withholding what is being counted" —
   never "one card with a numeral on it."
2. **What it needs to work.** The conditions the move depends on: that it comes
   before the subject is named; that it is shorter than what follows; that it
   is stated flatly rather than argued. These are the real constraints and they
   survive any medium.
3. **Its slots** — `[SLOT: name]`, each named by what it needs, with one line
   on what would legitimately fill it.

**THE SEQUENCE LOGIC** — why these moves in this order: what the opening buys,
where the tension peaks, what the payoff spends it on, why the ending lands
where it does. If a move were removed, this says what breaks. This is the part
that actually transfers between media, so it is the part worth being precise
about.

**PROPORTION, RELATIVELY** — how the source distributed its weight, expressed
as shares rather than units. "Roughly half the piece elapses before the first
reversal; the payoff occupies the final tenth and is the longest single beat."
Never in seconds, cards or words — a share can be re-expressed in any medium,
a unit cannot.

**THE VOICE CONSTRUCT** — the voice markers from the record restated as
followable rules: sentence length, punctuation habits, vocabulary, what this
speaker never does. Where the lane is organic these are **binding, not
stylistic** — the copy is that person speaking.

**LOAD-BEARING** — the two or three things this argument cannot survive
losing, and what each carries. A later stage may substitute into these; it may
not cut them.

**WHAT IS MEDIUM-BOUND** — name the things the source did that **only work in
its own medium**, and say what each was accomplishing, so a later stage can
achieve the same end by other means. A visual reveal, a hold on a shot, a
curtain wipe between beats: these do not port, but what they *do* — pay off a
promise, mark a transition, prove a claim without asserting it — must be
achieved some other way when this becomes copy.

This section is what stops the next stage from either copying a device that
cannot exist in text, or silently dropping the job that device was doing.

**The test for this whole document:** someone handed it, with no access to the
source, should be able to write a caption, a long-form post, and a review from
it — three different objects that all make the same argument. If it could only
produce one shape, it is still a record of the source, not a construct.
