# The copy channel map — the master board

Generated surface: channel-map.json + format-bank.json + library counts.
This mirror lists the specs; the artifact is the readable board.

**16 formats, four channels.** Copywriting is one brain — research, real language, DR structure. Every format is the same brain wearing a different shape. A channel decides the reading context; a format is a data row inside it; the words always come from the same place. Workflows activate a slice of the brain — never a separate copywriter.

## Paid social — Interrupted, skeptical, mid-scroll

The reader did not ask for this. The copy buys attention it wasn't given.

### Primary text / caption
- **What:** The words above the creative in the feed.
- **Length:** 50–120 words
- **Form:** Real sentences. First line stands alone, then short paragraphs, one idea each, blank line between.
- **Opens with:** a line that works with the creative not yet seen
- **Carries the offer:** optional
- **Reads as:** something a person typed, not something a brand approved
- **Variations:** 3
- **Provenance:** proven — copy machine `caption` row; 216 swiped examples in library

### Headline
- **What:** The bolded line under the creative — the ad's one claim.
- **Length:** 4–8 words
- **Form:** One line. A claim, a contrast, or a named enemy. Title case or sentence case, never all caps.
- **Opens with:** the noun or the tension — never the brand name
- **Carries the offer:** rarely
- **Reads as:** the thing you'd repeat to a friend verbatim
- **Variations:** 2
- **Provenance:** evidenced — 15 real AdPlexity titles in library ('Collagen creams can't absorb. This serum can.')

### Description
- **What:** The small line under the headline. Most people never read it.
- **Length:** 2–5 words
- **Form:** A fragment. Reassurance or mechanism, compressed.
- **Opens with:** whatever the headline left unsaid
- **Carries the offer:** guarantee-shorthand only
- **Reads as:** a whispered aside
- **Variations:** 2
- **Provenance:** proven — copy machine defaults (2 at 3 words)

### Angle
- **What:** The copy block's framing — the bet a brand makes, carried by many creatives. Not an ad; the idea ads are cut from.
- **Length:** 1–2 sentences
- **Form:** One framing statement a stranger could brief ten creatives from.
- **Opens with:** the belief being installed or reversed
- **Carries the offer:** never
- **Reads as:** the strategy, said out loud
- **Requires:** a heat signal before imitation — live creatives and variant count, per METHOD.md
- **Variations:** 1
- **Provenance:** evidenced — 223 swiped angles across four brands, ranked by heat

### Static / video overlay
- **What:** Words ON the creative — the message that survives sound-off.
- **Length:** 3–12 words per card
- **Form:** Cards or captions burned into frame. Each card one beat. The set tells the whole story without audio.
- **Opens with:** the scroll-stopper — shown and said at once
- **Carries the offer:** only on the final card
- **Reads as:** what the video is saying, not a summary of it
- **Variations:** 2
- **Provenance:** evidenced — 46 overlay sets extracted from teardowns; music-only sources carry the whole script this way

### Video ad script
- **What:** The spoken words of the ad, beat by beat.
- **Length:** 30–90 seconds
- **Form:** Beats against the source's structure — hook, problem, mechanism, proof, close. Written to be spoken, not read.
- **Opens with:** the source's own opening move, injected with our language
- **Carries the offer:** in the close, exactly as the offer file words it
- **Reads as:** a person talking, whose sentences you could interrupt
- **Requires:** a teardown record — scripts are rebuilt from structure, never freestyled
- **Variations:** 6
- **Provenance:** proven — the video-teardown chain end to end (hooks v11, close v6)

## Organic social — Chosen, curious, native

The reader chose to be here. Copy that smells like an ad dies instantly.

### Creator caption
- **What:** The post's own words under an organic video or image.
- **Length:** 5–80 words
- **Form:** The creator's register exactly — fragments allowed, emoji where they'd use them, hashtags only if they would.
- **Opens with:** the line that makes the video make sense
- **Carries the offer:** never
- **Reads as:** the person who filmed it, typing on their phone
- **Requires:** the creator's own voice evidence — captions are theirs, not the brand's
- **Variations:** 2
- **Provenance:** evidenced — 360 real captions in library with views/likes signals

### Hook line / overlay
- **What:** The first-frame words — the two-second stop decision.
- **Length:** 3–10 words
- **Form:** One line on frame one. A moment shown, not a category claimed.
- **Opens with:** the formation the material calls for — free, but sourced from real language
- **Carries the offer:** never
- **Reads as:** how a person starts a sentence, never how an ad does
- **Requires:** a verbatim seam — every hook traces to a real row or it gets cut
- **Variations:** 6
- **Provenance:** proven — video-teardown 4b (entry test, four-word test, photograph test)

### Organic post (short / long)
- **What:** A story post with no offer — short-form and long-form per the machine's rows.
- **Length:** 25–350 words
- **Form:** Short: a few hard sentences. Long: full prose that earns its middle.
- **Opens with:** short: the sharpest line. long: a promise the length will be worth it
- **Carries the offer:** never on organic; late and soft when repurposed paid
- **Reads as:** a post someone wrote because they wanted to
- **Variations:** 3
- **Provenance:** proven — copy machine short-form + long-form rows

### Organic video script
- **What:** Spoken words for a native post — the ORGANIC lane's rebuild.
- **Length:** 20–90 seconds
- **Form:** The source's own structure held until the product must enter; commercial structure built, never inherited.
- **Opens with:** whatever the source's first two seconds did to stop someone
- **Carries the offer:** one aside at most — the lane dies if it sells
- **Reads as:** the creator's own post, with our subject swapped in
- **Requires:** a teardown record with lane ORGANIC
- **Variations:** 6
- **Provenance:** proven — video-teardown ORGANIC lane (expansion stage gated in)

## Email — Permitted, private, one-to-one

The reader let us in. The inbox is personal space — copy reads as written to one person.

### Campaign email
- **What:** A one-off send to a segment.
- **Length:** 40–200 words visible
- **Form:** One idea per send. Image-built or text-built per the brand's pattern; the copy carries even inside image alts.
- **Opens with:** a first line that pays off the subject within one scroll
- **Carries the offer:** usually — but the treatment (story/proof/offer) is the concept brief's call
- **Reads as:** written to one person who already let us in
- **Requires:** a concept brief and a segment — the email machine's contract
- **Variations:** 1
- **Provenance:** proven — email machine; 309 shipped <brand> sends classified in library

### Flow email
- **What:** A triggered send — welcome, abandon, post-purchase, winback.
- **Length:** 40–180 words visible
- **Form:** Same craft as campaign, but the trigger IS the context — never re-explain what the trigger already proves.
- **Opens with:** the moment that fired it, acknowledged in one line
- **Carries the offer:** per flow position — never in the first welcome
- **Reads as:** a reply to something the reader just did
- **Variations:** 1
- **Provenance:** drafted — flow sends sit inside the <brand> scrape, not yet split out

### Subject + preview
- **What:** The open decision — two fragments doing one job.
- **Length:** 3–9 words each
- **Form:** Subject stops the scan; preview completes or twists it. Never restate one in the other.
- **Opens with:** curiosity or named value — never the brand name
- **Carries the offer:** only when the offer IS the news
- **Reads as:** a text from someone you know
- **Variations:** 2
- **Provenance:** evidenced — 309 subject+preview pairs indexed in the send catalogue

## Owned pages — Arrived, evaluating, deciding

The reader clicked through. Copy's job shifts from attention to conviction.

### Landing page / advertorial
- **What:** The click's destination — attention is spent, conviction is the job.
- **Length:** 800–4000 words
- **Form:** Sections in an argument order: promise → problem owned → mechanism → proof → offer → objections → close. Ad-to-page scent held from the first headline.
- **Opens with:** the ad's promise, restated bigger — never a cold brand header
- **Carries the offer:** yes, staged — price appears after the mechanism, never before
- **Reads as:** an editorial that happens to end in a decision
- **Requires:** the angle that sent the click — page and ad are one argument
- **Variations:** 1
- **Provenance:** evidenced — 47 swiped pages captured (incl. a 21,490px advertorial), 4 of ours

### Quiz
- **What:** Question-led conviction — the reader tells us the argument to make.
- **Length:** 5–12 questions
- **Form:** Questions a person answers about themselves, each narrowing the recommendation; result page personalizes the pitch.
- **Opens with:** a question about THEM, not about the product
- **Carries the offer:** on the result page only
- **Reads as:** a diagnosis, not a funnel
- **Variations:** 1
- **Provenance:** proven — quiz-funnel skill + 1 shipped <brand> quiz; <competitor> runs quiz.<competitor>.com as its whole front door

### PDP
- **What:** The product's own page — where every funnel eventually lands.
- **Length:** 150–600 words above reviews
- **Form:** Name, one-line promise, benefit bullets in the customer's words, mechanism, ritual, guarantee exactly as the offer file words it.
- **Opens with:** the dominant problem word the avatar actually uses
- **Carries the offer:** yes — the canonical price, never a channel-specific one
- **Reads as:** the calm, complete answer after the ad's excitement
- **Variations:** 1
- **Provenance:** drafted — brand product files exist; no PDP spec has been exercised by a machine yet
