Step 1, worked through

What an email is for

Four wells. Each one does something different to the relationship, and the reason to keep them apart is that only one of them asks for anything. Email, SMS and subscription are how a community gets built — they remind people you are here, that you care, and that you are real. A calendar that only asks is a channel that decays.

One test settles every email: Whose material is this email made of? There is exactly one answer, which is what stops the categories bleeding into each other.

Promotional

Made of our offerstocked

A discount, a launch, a restock, a deadline. The material is the offer itself.

The offer bank, and the promo budget that says how many weeks a year may be one of these.

offer-bank per lane · 6.7k brotherhood, 5.3k two-oh

The only category that asks for money. Every one spent here is paid for by the other four.

Educational

Made of our expertisestocked

The skin, the problem, the mechanism — described the way THEY describe it, never in category language.

The language banks and the objection bank — 14,237 rows of real customer sentences, mined from 974 survey responses collected since April 2023 and still coming in.

14,237 language rows · 35k objection bank · 11k offer bank

An education email that ends with a link is still educational. The test is what it is MADE of, not whether it links.

Not categories — treatments

These cut across all five, and mistaking them for categories is what caused the overlap. A treatment has no material of its own — that is the tell.

Humour

Entertainment is not a category — it is how a category is played. It has no material of its own, which is the giveaway. Ask what a funny email is made of and the answer is always one of the five.

A question

Research — a poll, a survey, a vote. Also a mechanic rather than a category: asking about a product is promotional, asking about their skin is educational. Worth doing far more (2 sends in 308) but not a category of its own.

No ask

An email with deliberately nothing to click. A treatment, and a load-bearing one — it is what proves the other four are not a wind-up.

Proof

Photos, numbers, results. Attaches to any category rather than being one.

The engine

How a month gets composed

This is the difference between a dynamic calendar and a list of dates someone filled in. Four rules, applied every cycle.

Order, not ratio

How many emails ask is the low-level question. The real one is where a type sits in the arc — and whether that specific thing needs a follow-up. An ask that stands alone has nothing behind it; a question with no answer email was extraction. The arc governs order.

To build
Cadence is derived, never set

There is no target number of sends and no fixed share that may ask. What goes out is decided by measured responsiveness per segment and per avatar, and by what a send earns. Frequency is an output of the data, not an input to the plan.

To build
Coverage

The calendar tracks which cells of the space above have been cold longest — which sub-avatar has not been spoken to, which pain point has not been addressed, which awareness level is being skipped — and fills toward the gaps rather than repeating last month.

To build
Freshness

Nothing is proposed that the ledger records as spent. 308 subject lines are already on it, with their preview text.

Partly
Fit

A slot only reaches a segment whose language bank can actually carry it. Today that is two real banks per lane, not nine — so the calendar must not promise a granularity the language cannot deliver.

0 · Triage

stage0-triage-v1-damon.md
Today is: {today}
Here is the source: {source}
Here is where it came from: {source_reference}
Here are the formats this lane knows: {formats}

Four answers. Everything downstream binds to them, so be decisive and say
where you were unsure rather than hedging in the answer itself.

**LANE:** `ALREADY AN AD` or `ORGANIC`

The test is **not polish**. Every marketing email is polished. The test is
whether this email was **built to sell inside itself** — whether it carries
the argument that makes the case, or whether it is content with a link at the
end of it.

A sale, a launch, an abandoned-cart, a last-chance: `ALREADY AN AD`. A
newsletter, a how-it-works, a founder's note, a customer's story told for its
own sake: `ORGANIC`.

When genuinely between, answer `ORGANIC`. Building structure that turns out
redundant costs less than assuming structure that was never there.

**FORMAT:** one key from the list above.

What the source **is**, not what we might turn it into. If nothing fits,
answer `unlisted` and describe the shape in one line — never force it into
the nearest key, because every stage after this binds to the key.

**VOICE:** whose mouth this comes out of, and the binding that follows.

Name the speaker, then state the rules a later stage has to obey to keep
sounding like them. Where the lane is organic these are binding, not
stylistic.

**SENDER:** the from-line, and what it licenses.

Not metadata — part of the argument. Answer with which of these it is:

- `BRAND` — the company is speaking
- `PERSON` — a named human is speaking, and they are identifiable
- `PERSON-AT-BRAND` — a named human, speaking for the company
- `SUPPORT` — an operational address; a different kind of trust entirely

Then one line on what that sender **may claim**. A named identifiable human
can say "I used it for three weeks" and stand behind it. A brand cannot. This
line is what stops a later stage writing a first-person claim nobody can own.

Then, briefly:

- **ABOUT** — what it is about
- **DOING** — what it is doing to the reader
- **LENGTH** — roughly how many phone screens it runs
- **IDENTIFIABLE** — is the speaker a real, named, traceable person: yes or no

Give me the four labelled answers first, then the brief lines. Nothing else.

1 · Read

stage1-read-v1-damon.md
Today is: {today}
Here is what triage decided: {triage}
Here is the source: {source}

An objective record of this email. **Observation only.** You are not saying
what would work better, what is weak, or what we should do — a later stage
does that and it needs a clean record to do it from.

If the source is a teardown record from another lane rather than an email,
read it as it stands. Do not redo it, do not contradict it, and do not
translate it into email terms — stage 2 does that. Note what it does not
cover and move on.

Give me:

**THE ENVELOPE**

- From-name and the shape of the address
- **Subject line, quoted exactly.** Character count.
- **Preview text, quoted exactly.** If there is none, say `no preview text` —
  that is a finding, not a blank.
- What the pair does together: whether the preview completes the subject,
  repeats it, or changes the subject entirely

**THE BLOCKS, IN ORDER**

Numbered, in the order they appear down the scroll. For each: the type
(copy / image / button / review / product / divider / PS / footer), and its
content **quoted, never paraphrased**. Copy blocks quoted in full. Image
blocks described by what is in the frame and what it is doing.

The opening line of body copy gets quoted in full and called out — it is the
line the preview text was buying.

**THE CTA CENSUS**

Every button and every inline link: what it says, where it falls in the scroll,
and where it goes. Then the count, and whether the first one comes before or
after the argument has been made.

**THE WEIGHT**

Roughly how much of the email is image and how much is text. How many phone
screens it runs. Where the fold falls — what a reader sees before scrolling at
all.

**THE OFFER**

Where it appears, in what words, exactly. Where it conspicuously does not
appear. If there is no offer, say so.

**THE PROOF**

Every specific: a number, a duration, a named person, a review, a study, a
before-and-after. Quoted. Unspecific claims are not proof and do not go here.

**VOICE MARKERS**

Sentence length and rhythm. Punctuation habits. Vocabulary — the words this
speaker reaches for and the register they sit in. Contractions or not. What
this speaker **never** does.

Where the lane is organic this is the most load-bearing section in the
document; everything downstream depends on it being precise rather than
flattering.

**WHAT IT NEVER DOES**

The things this email conspicuously avoids — a claim it could have made and
didn't, a beat it skipped, a word it stayed away from. Absences are structure
too, and they are the part a replication most often loses.

2 · Spec

stage2-spec-v1-damon.md
Today is: {today}
Here is what triage decided: {triage}
Here is the record of the source: {record}

Turn the record into a **brand-free, medium-free construct** — the persuasion
mechanics with both the brand and the delivery medium stripped out.

**Read this first, because the copy lane's v1 got it wrong and cost a rebuild.**
The earlier version abstracted the source's *physical form* — "one card, two
words" — and every stage downstream faithfully rebuilt the source's medium
instead of making the argument. The same trap here is subject-line lengths,
block counts, hero images and button placements.

**What the source is made of is a fact about the source, not a constraint on
us.** Blocks, buttons, hero images, subject lines, scroll positions — that is
how an inbox carried the argument. The argument is what transfers.

So: **no block counts, no button positions, no character limits, no "hero", no
"above the fold" anywhere in this document.** If you catch yourself writing
one, you are describing the source instead of abstracting it.

Give me:

**THE MOVES** — numbered, in the source's order. For each:

1. **The job it does on the reader.** Stated so it could be performed in an
   email, a caption, a video or a landing page without changing what it
   accomplishes. "Names a cost the reader is already paying and has stopped
   noticing" — never "a headline over a lifestyle image."
2. **What it needs to work.** The conditions the move depends on: that it
   comes before the product is named; that it is shorter than what follows;
   that it is stated flatly rather than argued. These survive any medium.
3. **Its slots** — `[SLOT: name]`, each named by what it needs, with one line
   on what would legitimately fill it.

**THE SEQUENCE LOGIC** — why these moves in this order: what the opening buys,
where the tension peaks, what the payoff spends it on, why the ending lands
where it does. If a move were removed, this says what breaks. This is the part
that actually transfers, so it is the part worth being precise about.

**PROPORTION, RELATIVELY** — how the source distributed its weight, as shares
rather than units. "Roughly a third elapses before the product is named; the
proof beat is the longest single stretch." Never in blocks, screens or words —
a share can be re-expressed in any medium, a unit cannot.

**THE VOICE CONSTRUCT** — the voice markers from the record restated as
followable rules: sentence length, punctuation habits, vocabulary, what this
speaker never does. Where the lane is organic these are **binding, not
stylistic**.

**LOAD-BEARING** — the two or three things this argument cannot survive
losing, and what each carries. A later stage may substitute into these; it may
not cut them.

**WHAT IS INBOX-BOUND** — name the things the source did that **only work in
an inbox**, and say what each was accomplishing, so a later stage can achieve
the same end by other means.

A subject line buys the open. A preview text buys the first line. A hero image
has to survive being blocked. A button is a decision made without reading. Name
the job, not the device — and note plainly that nothing downstream may treat
"it had a hero image" as a structural requirement. The job is required; the
device is one way of doing it.

**The test for this whole document:** someone handed it, with no access to the
source, should be able to write an email, a caption and a landing page from it
— three different objects making the same argument. If it could only produce
one shape, it is still a record, not a construct.

An organic source was not made to sell. It has attention and it has credibility

3. **Proof.** Gate: is there a claim here a stranger has no reason to believe?
