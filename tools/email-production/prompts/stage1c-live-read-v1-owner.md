A moment is not a label — it is a week with specific things happening in it,
and the reader is already inside that week. An email that names the moment
generically ("football season is back") is barely better than one that ignores
it. An email that names **what is happening the day after it lands** is the
one the reader answers.

This runs for every send. Where no dated moment was scheduled, the live week
is still live: what the category is arguing about, what the weather is doing
in this market, what just changed for this audience.

## What you produce

**1 · THE WEEK, IN FACTS**

Every fact the research supports, each on its own line, each with its source.
Dates and times as the reader would say them. Nothing here may be something
you know rather than something the research shows — this section is the reason
the email can be specific, so a wrong line here is worse than no line.

**2 · THE ONE ODD THING**

The single most talkable detail of that week — the thing a person would
actually mention to a friend. Not the biggest event: the strangest true one.
If nothing in the week is odd, say so; do not manufacture one.

**3 · THE BAIT**

Two or three ways this email invites the reader to engage with the moment
itself, on top of whatever it is selling. A reply-worthy question, a pick, a
side to take, a thing to watch for. Rules:

- It must be answerable by a person who has not bought anything.
- It must be about the moment, not about the product. Bait that is really a
  product question is not bait.
- It must sit alongside the offer, never replace it — the send still does its
  job.
- Name where in the construct it goes, and say what a reply looks like.

**4 · WHO CAN BE NAMED**

Only from the notable-customers file above. If it holds nobody whose world
matches this moment, say that plainly and write `no name available — the copy
argues without one`. Never reach for a person from a neighbouring world and
hope the reader does not notice — that is exactly the failure this stage was
built after.

**5 · DO NOT CLAIM**

Everything the research did NOT establish, listed, so the later stages cannot
drift into it: results not yet played, numbers nobody checked, a person's
opinion, anything dated after this send goes out.

## Walls

- **Nothing invented, ever.** A fact with no source is not a fact. If the
  research came back thin, produce a thin sheet and say it is thin.
- **The reader lives in the market above, not in yours.** A moment that does
  not reach this market is not a moment; say so.
- **This is not copy.** Write no lines, no subjects, no headlines. You are
  handing the writing stages a week they can stand in.
