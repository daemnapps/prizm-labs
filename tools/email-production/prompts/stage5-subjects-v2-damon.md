**THE UNIT IS A PAIR. ALWAYS.**

A subject line never ships alone. Every version below is a **subject line and
the preview text that runs under it**, written together as one object.

- The **subject** buys the open.
- The **preview** buys the first line of the body. It is not a summary of the
  subject and it is not a repeat of it. It is the second half of a two-part
  opening, and the two are read in one glance.
- The preview must not be the opening line of the body copy. A reader who has
  already read that line has been given the first sentence twice.

A version that ships a subject without its preview is incomplete and does not
count toward the number owed.

**VERSION 0 — THE CONTROL, SWIPED STRAIGHT**

The source's own subject line and its own preview text, substituted and
nothing else. Same shape, same length, same rhythm, our nouns in place of
theirs.

This is the control and **it may not be improved.** If it reads plainer than
the variations, that is the point — it is the version whose performance we
already have evidence for, and a control that has been quietly upgraded
measures nothing. Write it, label it VERSION 0, and leave it alone.

If the source had no preview text, VERSION 0 has none either, and you say so.
Adding one is an improvement, and improvements are what the variations are
for.

**THE VARIATIONS**

Then {subject_count} more pairs, each doing a genuinely different job — not

Draw them from what the source and the brand context actually contain:

- A customer's own words, quoted, where the language bank or context carries

- The objection said out loud before the reader can think it

**Rules that bind every one of them:**

- **The voice rules are absolute.** If the lane is organic, every pair is that
  named person writing. A subject that sounds like a brand — even a great one
  — is disqualified, and no amount of expected performance argues it back in.
- **The sender's ceiling binds.** A first-person subject line only exists if
  the sender file says someone can own it.
- **Never spend a subject on a claim the files do not carry.** A subject line
  is the most-seen line we write; an unsupported one is the most expensive
  place to put a claim we cannot stand behind.
- **Never reuse ground the ledger records as spent.** If a line or an angle is
  on the ledger, that ground is used, and building on it again is sending the
  same email twice with new words. Say which you avoided and why.
- **No manufactured urgency.** No deadline, no countdown, no scarcity that the
  offer file does not carry.
- **No fake-personal devices.** `Re:`, `Fwd:`, a false reply, an invented
  first name, a fake typo. They buy one open and cost the relationship.

**THE HEADLINE RULE.** A subject line's only job is to force the reading of
the preview and the first line — not to sell, not to carry the offer. For
version 0 and every variation: name the awareness level triage gave this
send, quote that level's `must_not`, and say whether the subject breaks it.
Naming the product, the price, or the direct claim too early for the level
it is actually entering on is a defect in the subject, not a style choice —
a subject that fails this test is rewritten before it is offered, not
offered with a note.
