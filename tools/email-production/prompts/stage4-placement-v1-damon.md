**Decide. Write nothing.** No copy, no lines, no subject lines. This stage
produces a plan the next stages have to obey, and a stage that starts writing
here starts deciding by taste instead of by structure.

Four decisions.

**1 · WHERE THE PRODUCT ENTERS**

The source's own position is the default. Give it as a share of the piece —
"after the second move, roughly a third in" — never as a block number.

**Moving it earlier needs a stated reason**, and "it is an email and people
skim" is not one. The source's position is evidence; a preference is not. If
you move it, say what in the construct or the offer makes the earlier position
work, and say what you expect to lose.

**2 · THE SCROLL BUDGET**

How long this email runs, in phone screens, taken from the source's own
length and its proportions.

**Room is never bought by compressing a source beat.** If everything the
construct requires will not fit the source's length, do not quietly trim — say
by how much it overruns, in screens, and which move is the pressure. An
overrun is escalated with a number, not absorbed.

Then the shares: roughly what proportion goes to the opening, the argument,
the proof, the close.

**3 · THE CTA PLAN**

How many buttons, and where each falls.

The source's count is the default. **Adding a CTA needs a reason** — an email
that asks three times when the source asked once is a different email and
usually a worse one.

For each: where it falls in the scroll, and whether it comes before or after
the argument has been made. A button above the argument is a legitimate move
for a reader who is already sold and a wasted one otherwise; say which you
think this is and why.

Then: whether the copy links inline as well, or only through buttons. The
source's habit is the default here too.

**4 · THE IMAGE PLAN**

How many image blocks, and **what each one is for** — the job it does, in the
construct's terms.

An image block with no stated job is not planned, and it does not go in the
plan. "Visual break" is not a job.

Every image here becomes a job for the image lane, so state each one as
something that lane could actually build: what has to be true in the frame for
this to do its job. Do not describe the picture — that is the image lane's
stage 3, not this one.

**5 · THE OBJECTION AT THE FLINCH**

From the objection bank: the one objection this reader will hit, and where in
the scroll they hit it. Name the objection and the position. Stage 7 answers
it; this stage only says where the flinch is.

Give me the five decisions, labelled, each with its reason. Nothing else.
