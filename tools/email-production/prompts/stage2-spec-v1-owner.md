## THE THIRD STRIP IS NEW, AND IT IS THE POINT

RULED 2026-09-14 (the owner: *"the format — obviously it's taking the inspiration,
but then there is no real uniqueness to it. For the email we were sending, it
was basically just rewriting that existing email."*)

Two strips were not enough. A construct that removed the brand and the medium
but kept the source's ARGUMENT gave the next stage a finished case to re-skin,
and re-skinning is what it did. The new email said what the old email said,
with our nouns in it.

**So the subject matter goes too.** What the source argued is a fact about the
source. What transfers is HOW it argued: the order of the moves, the job each
one does on the reader, what has to be true for that job to land, and where the
weight sits.

The difference, concretely:

- *carries across* — "names a cost the reader is already paying and has stopped
  noticing, before anything is offered"
- *does NOT carry across* — "names the cost of buying razors every month"

The first is a move. The second is the source's argument wearing the move as a
coat. **Write the first. Never the second.** If a line of this document would
stop being true for an email about something completely different, it is
subject matter and it does not belong here.

The argument this construct will carry is decided elsewhere — the calendar
chose it, and the next stage is handed it. Your job is to hand that stage a
SHAPE strong enough to hold an argument it has never seen.

**Read this first, because the copy lane's v1 got it wrong and cost a rebuild.**
The earlier version abstracted the source's *physical form* — "one card, two
words" — and every stage downstream faithfully rebuilt the source's medium
instead of making the argument. The same trap here is subject-line lengths,
block counts, hero images and button placements.

**What the source is made of is a fact about the source, not a constraint on
us.** Blocks, buttons, hero images, subject lines, scroll positions — that is
how an inbox carried the argument. The argument is what transfers.

So: **no block counts, no button positions, no character limits, no "hero", no
"above the fold" anywhere in this document.** If you catch yourself writing
one, you are describing the source instead of abstracting it.

Give me:

**THE MOVES** — numbered, in the source's order. For each:

1. **The job it does on the reader.** Stated so it could be performed in an
   email, a caption, a video or a landing page without changing what it
   accomplishes. "Names a cost the reader is already paying and has stopped
   noticing" — never "a headline over a lifestyle image."
2. **What it needs to work.** The conditions the move depends on: that it
   comes before the product is named; that it is shorter than what follows;
   that it is stated flatly rather than argued. These survive any medium.
3. **Its slots** — `[SLOT: name]`, each named by what it needs, with one line
   on what would legitimately fill it.

**THE SEQUENCE LOGIC** — why these moves in this order: what the opening buys,
where the tension peaks, what the payoff spends it on, why the ending lands
where it does. If a move were removed, this says what breaks. This is the part
that actually transfers, so it is the part worth being precise about.

**PROPORTION, RELATIVELY** — how the source distributed its weight, as shares
rather than units. "Roughly a third elapses before the product is named; the
proof beat is the longest single stretch." Never in blocks, screens or words —
a share can be re-expressed in any medium, a unit cannot.

**THE VOICE CONSTRUCT** — the voice markers from the record restated as
followable rules: sentence length, punctuation habits, vocabulary, what this
speaker never does. Where the lane is organic these are **binding, not
stylistic**.

**LOAD-BEARING** — the two or three things this argument cannot survive
losing, and what each carries. A later stage may substitute into these; it may
not cut them.

**WHAT IS INBOX-BOUND** — name the things the source did that **only work in
an inbox**, and say what each was accomplishing, so a later stage can achieve
the same end by other means.

A subject line buys the open. A preview text buys the first line. A hero image
has to survive being blocked. A button is a decision made without reading. Name
the job, not the device — and note plainly that nothing downstream may treat
"it had a hero image" as a structural requirement. The job is required; the
device is one way of doing it.

**The test for this whole document — two parts, and the second is the new one:**

1. Someone handed it, with no access to the source, should be able to write an
   email, a caption and a landing page from it — three different objects
   running the same mechanics. If it could only produce one shape, it is still
   a record, not a construct.
2. **They should not be able to tell you what the source was about.** If
   reading this document reveals the source's topic, its product, its season or
   its occasion, the subject matter is still in here and the next stage will
   rebuild the source. Go back and take it out.
