# Design step 0 · the moment world

PRODUCT TRUTH IS LAW: every product appears exactly as its product file describes it (its container, colour,
proportion and how it is held); never call it or draw it as anything else.

THE EXAMPLES BELOW ARE INVENTED. They show the shape of an answer, never its content. Every scene,
object, format and feeling you write comes from the INPUTS: this reader's culture map, her profile, and
the research on this moment. If an input does not show it, it does not go in.

A send on a moment (a holiday, a sale weekend, a season) has to feel like that moment for THIS reader,
the way the brand's Halloween emails became a horror poster, a barbershop at midnight and a coffin box
(the owner, 2026-10-17: "what is this woman experiencing on Black Friday? She's shopping, she's looking for
gifts, go deep into the research, understand what this person is actually doing … think through the
process of unique elements to really showcase this period of time"). You are writing the moment's world
once, so every email in that moment draws from it.

## WRITE THESE SECTIONS, in this order, plain words

## HER {moment} — hour by hour
Where she is and what she is doing across the moment, in order (the night before, the day, the days
after), 8 to 12 lines, each one a concrete scene with where it comes from in the inputs.

## THE OBJECTS
15 to 25 real objects from her version of this moment that a picture could hold (the list on the fridge,
the flyer, the gift bag, the remote on the arm of the chair). Each with its source line.

## THE FORMATS SHE WATCHES AND SHARES
The post and video formats of this moment in her feeds and on her TV, each with its source and size.

## WHAT SHE FEELS
The pulls and tensions of the moment for her (buying for everyone but herself, the photos, the
overload), each with its source.

## CONCEPT SEEDS
8 to 12 concepts an email could be built as. Each is ONE line in this exact shape:
`seed name — the borrowed format or scene — how the product enters it (shown, never listed) — source`
A seed borrows a format or a scene she knows, never a real person's face or name. The villain is the
problem, never her. Products are SHOWN inside the scene (in her gift bag, on her list, under the tree),
never as a plain product shot.

## THE BLOW-OUT (key visuals)
A moment email opens on a KEY VISUAL, not a snapshot: a designed picture made to stop the scroll, the
way the brand's Halloween became an 80s horror movie poster with the product as the hero, and its
gift set became five spooky product pictures (the owner, 2026-10-17: "the design and images themselves need
to feel like a blow out too"). Write 6 to 8 key-visual directions for this moment, each ONE line in this
shape:
`key visual name — the borrowed format at full scale (a movie poster, a magazine cover, a flyer's front
page, a TV title card, a gift reveal) — how the real product is the STAR of it — the moment's colours
and light (dramatic, celebratory, maximal within the brand) — the title type the format carries, if any`
Then 4 to 6 GIFT PICTURES: the real products made into this moment's objects (wrapped, tagged, in the
bag, under the tree, in the stocking), each as its own picture.

## PICTURE DIRECTION
For the supporting story pictures (not the key visual): the light, the palette, the props and the places
that make a picture read as this moment for her, and what never appears (the generic version of this
moment every other brand sends).

## THE GENERIC VERSION, BANNED
The tropes every other brand uses for this moment (for a sale weekend: percentage-off badges, SALE
stamps, clip-art shopping bags, countdown clocks with no real deadline). None of them may appear.

Invented example of ONE seed line, for the SHAPE only:
