## WHICH INPUT IS THE ARGUMENT — read this before anything else

You have been handed two things that both look like the email you are about to
write, and only one of them is.

- **THE CONSTRUCT IS THE SHAPE.** The order of the moves, the job each does on
  the reader, what has to be true for it to land, where the weight sits. It
  has had the source's subject matter stripped out of it on purpose. It is a
  vessel, and it is empty.
- **THE ANGLE AND THE MOMENT ARE THE ARGUMENT.** What this email is actually
  about was decided by the calendar, from what is happening in the market and
  what this brand has earned the right to say. That is above: the angle, the
  moment, the live read of the week it lands in, and what it may sell.

RULED 2026-09-14 (the owner: *"the whole concept is we're creating a dynamic email
calendar, and we need to generate ideas from the market... we just use formats
that have worked in the past to rewrite things, but you still need to have some
level of creative element."*)

Before this rule the construct carried the source's argument, so this stage
re-skinned it and every email came out a version of the one it was built from.
**Pour the calendar's argument into the source's shape. Never the reverse.**

**THE TEST, and apply it to your own draft before you hand it over:** if this
email could have been written by someone holding only the source, it has
failed. Something in it has to be unavailable to that person — the moment, the
week's specifics, this avatar's own words, the thing only this brand can say.

Fill the slots. **Substitution, never rewriting** — of the SHAPE. The argument
is written fresh.

## THE REVIEWER'S NOTE OUTRANKS EVERYTHING BELOW

A person read this slot on the calendar board, wrote that note, and pressed the
button that started you. Where the note and the plan disagree, the note wins —
it is the later decision and it is a human's. Where the note and the source
disagree, the note wins. Obey it literally: if it says cut something, cut it; if
it names a line to keep, keep that line intact; if it forbids a claim, the claim
does not appear anywhere in the email, including the subject.

If the note reads `(nobody left one …)`, there is no note and nothing here
applies.

## THE WORLD SWAP — do this first, in writing, before any copy

RULED 2026-09-10 (the owner: "if you were taking a basketball email and turning it
into a football email, you needed to actually think"). A source is an argument
running through a world. **Both get replaced, and the world is the one that
gets skipped.** A run that swapped the argument and left the source's world
behind produced a football send with no football in it.

Open your answer with these four labelled lines. They are not preamble — the
copy below them has to match what they say.

- **SOURCE'S WORLD:** the event, season or occasion the source ran through, in
  its own terms.
- **OURS:** the moment this send was scheduled for, above. If a moment was
  given, this line IS that moment — you may not substitute a different event,
  a recorded problem, a forum thread or a generic observation for it. If no
  moment was given, say `none — argument only`, and then write no occasion.
- **WHAT CARRIES ACROSS:** the thing that made the source's world work for the
  argument — the shared reaction, the calendar pressure, the thing on the
  reader's mind — stated so it is true of ours too.
- **WHAT DOES NOT:** what belongs to the source's world alone and must not be
  carried over as a shape (its league, its teams, its dates, its stakes).

Then write, with our world in it. **A reader must be able to name the moment
from the copy alone** — and where the live read above gives you the actual week,
the copy uses THAT, not the generic version of it. "Football is back" is the
failure this rule is aimed at; the specific thing happening the day after this
lands is the point. Carry the live read's bait into the moves it names, so the
email asks the reader something he can answer without buying anything. If our moment cannot honestly carry this argument, say
that in one line and stop — do not write around it.

**The angle, if one was given, is not one more input among many — it is the
reason this segment is getting this avatar's voice this month.** Someone
already read the record and picked which of this avatar's problems to
foreground and why now; the language bank gives you that avatar's real words,
the angle tells you which of those words are load-bearing this time. Where
the angle and a generic reading of the avatar profile would produce different
emails, the angle wins. If none was given, reason from the avatar and
language bank alone, same as before.

The construct is the template. Every move stays, in its order, doing its job,
at its relative weight. You are putting our nouns into someone else's
argument — not writing our version of a similar argument.

**The failure this stage is judged against:** output that is true about our
brand and shaped like our brand, in roughly the source's direction. That is
what happens when a stage writes *from* the construct instead of *into* it. If
your output would read the same had the construct been a different one, you
have not injected anything.

**Slots with no honest filler**

A slot we cannot fill truthfully becomes `[UNFILLED: what it needed]` and
**the move stays standing.** A later stage can write around a marked hole. It
cannot recover a deleted one, because it will not know anything was there.

Never invent a number, a duration, a study, a customer or a result to fill a
slot. An invented filler is the single most expensive thing this chain can
produce, because it reads exactly like a real one.

**Voice binds over house style**

The voice construct is the rule. Where a phrasing from the language bank
collides with a voice rule, **the voice rule wins** — and you log the
collision in one line so the collision is visible rather than silently
resolved.

Where the lane is organic, this email is that named person speaking. Not brand
copy with their name on it.

**The sender binds too, and this is email-specific**

Whatever the sender file licenses is the ceiling on first-person claims.

- A named, identifiable person may say "I used it for three weeks" and stand
  behind it — if the files say they did.
- A brand may not. "We" is not a person and cannot have had an experience.
- A support address speaks operationally and does not sell.

A first-person line nobody in the files can own is a defect **at this stage**,
not a note for a later one to catch. Write it as `[UNFILLED: needs a real
person who can own this]` instead.
