# Where the machine stops and a person starts

**RULED (the owner, 2026-08-29).** The machine does not finish an email. It gets it
to a point a person can take over, and that point is different for two kinds of
email.

## The machine's half

Teardown → read → spec → inject → place → subject lines → close → **and then it
stops.**

What comes out is a **wireframe**: the argument, the copy, the blocks in order,
the image jobs described, the subject lines to choose from. Not a finished
email — a thing a person picks up.

## Design-focused emails → the graphic designer

She gets the wireframe and the copy that goes in it, and she does the part that
needs taste: **puts the images in, fine-tunes, tweaks.**

**It is mostly images.** The formats are already defined, so she is not
designing a layout from nothing — she is filling a known shape with the right
pictures and adjusting until it looks right.

Where AI helps here, and it is not "AI does the design": the Figma files become
design files the machine can work from, and the machine **selects the output**
for the format and the email in hand. She reviews everything.

## Written emails → the copywriter

No design. The machine writes it, and **a human reads it so it sounds human.**
That is the whole job and it is not a small one.

## Why it is drawn here

We are not at the point where AI does all of it, and taste is the reason. A
machine that hands over a wireframe and a set of image jobs is more useful than
one that hands over a finished email nobody trusts.
