Three tops, one pick

She asks strangers which of three tops to wear to her granddaughter's birthday party. She never once mentions her arms. Then she comes back, tells you she picked the sleeveless one, and shows you the thing that made that possible.

The opening — shoot all six

Control

Film: Medium shot, phone at chest height on the dresser, mirror and open closet behind you, warm lamps on. You are already wearing the long-sleeved cardigan, both hands up and moving, gesturing at the camera before the first word lands.

Say: "Need your help. Three tops. One granddaughter turning six next week in my backyard in the sun. I don't know what to wear. I need you to help me."

Variation 1 — "All long sleeves"

Film: Your closet rail, filled, shot from two feet away. Your hand pushes hangers along it left to right, fast, one after another — every single garment a long sleeve. The hand stops. The rail is still full. Cut to your face at two seconds.

Say: "Need your help. Three tops, all long sleeves. One granddaughter turning six next week in my backyard in the sun. I don't know what to wear. I need you to help me."

Variation 2 — "Out in the sun"

Film: Outside in your backyard, in the sun, at the hour the party would actually happen. Phone propped on a garden table. Three tops laid flat over the back of a patio chair beside you. Your forearms are bare in the light and the camera is close enough to see them. You are holding one top up against your chest, mid-comparison, when the first word lands.

Say: "Need your help. Three tops. One granddaughter turning six next week in my backyard in the sun. I don't know what to wear. I need you to help me."

Variation 3 — "Still"

Film: Standing at the open closet with the cardigan half on — one arm through the sleeve, the other sleeve hanging empty. The arm that is out is bare and spotted, the arm that is in is already covered. You are not putting it on and not taking it off. You are stopped.

Say: "Need your help. Three tops. Still. One granddaughter turning six next week in my backyard in the sun. I don't know what to wear. I need you to help me."

Variation 4 — "And a bottle of leg makeup"

Film: Three tops laid out flat on the bed, and on the bed beside them, upright and obvious, the bottle of body makeup — the one thing in the row that isn't clothing. Slow push in along the row, ending on the bottle. You step into frame at two seconds.

Say: "Need your help. Three tops and a bottle of leg makeup. One granddaughter turning six next week in my backyard in the sun. I don't know what to wear. I need you to help me."

Variation 5 — "Help"

Film: You are already talking when the video starts. First frame catches you mid-word, mouth open, one hand raised, half-turned away from the camera as if you just reached over and hit record without stopping the sentence. No settling, no clean start. Do not trim the first half-second clean — the messy in-point is the shot.

Say: "Need your help. Three tops. One granddaughter turning six next week in my backyard in the sun and I don't know what to wear I need you to help me."

The script, straight through

Every line in order, nothing else. The shot list above says how to film them; this is just what to say.

Need your help. Three tops. One granddaughter turning six next week in my backyard in the sun. I don't know what to wear. I need you to help me.

Is the cardigan I wore to her party last year. It's long, it's sleeved, it's hot. I just kept it hanging there 'cause I knew the party was coming and I thought I'd need to cover my arms.

This actually came out of the back of my closet. Got the elbow sleeve going, it's the safe one. It's not covered up, so I tried halfway.

Then there's this one, which I have had probably longer than she's been alive. It's sleeveless. Weather-wise, it's good.

Here you go. Three decisions. You help me decide. Thank you very much.

I picked number three.

And I've been using this on my arms twice a week since. On damp skin, not under the water. It clears the old spotted skin off, and it changes what comes in behind it. That's the part everything else I tried never did.

I'm not gonna tell you how fast. Mine took months. But if it doesn't fade, you get your money back — so it's my problem, not yours.
