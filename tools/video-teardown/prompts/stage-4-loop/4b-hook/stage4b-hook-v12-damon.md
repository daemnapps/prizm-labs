**A hook is a line and a scroll stopper, together.** The line is what the speaker
hears or reads; the scroll stopper is the first-frame visual event that
arrests the thumb before a single word lands. Neither is optional and
neither is the other's caption — they are the same thought in two
channels, show and tell at once. "Selfie walk-and-talk at the entrance"
is a camera setup, not a scroll stopper. A scroll stopper is a thing
*happening*: an act mid-motion, an object where it should not be, a
texture close enough to feel, a before mid-transformation. If the frame
would read as ordinary with the sound off, it stops nobody. Take the
visual vocabulary from what the source already does where it serves;
build inside the format's world where it does not.

**The spec already did the abstraction — read it, do not redo it.** The
replication spec names the opening phase, its duration and its fixed
bounds in the structural skeleton; the element inventory carries the
opening element's mandate; the script architecture names the premise and
its job. **That is the foundational hook, and it is read out of the spec,
cited, never re-derived from the teardown.** The teardown record is for
what was in frame; the reading supplies the awareness entry. If
the spec is missing any of this, that is a stage-2 defect — flag it and
stop rather than deriving around it.

**The reading names the opening beat by timestamp.** Rewrite that

**One brief, one discrete problem.** The reading and the baseline

**Version 0 is the straight swipe, and it is the control.**

Take the source's opening beat exactly as it is and change only the words

stopper is the source's own first frame, held.

This is the version that already performed, wearing our name. Every variation
is a bet against it in the same test, and if none of them beat it that is
a real finding rather than a failure. **Label it clearly as the control
and never improve it.**

Then write five variations. The rest of this prompt is about those.

Five rules govern what replaces the source's words:

**Every hook traces to a verbatim.** Pull the moment from the language bank
and the avatar — a behaviour, a time of day, an object, something done
with the hands. The strongest hooks name a private moment the avatar thought nobody
had noticed. A hook that cannot cite its source is invented and gets cut,
however well it reads. Reaching for a phrase that is not in the bank is the
signal you are writing at the avatar instead of from them.

**Finish a sentence the avatar has already started.** The bank holds
half-phrases they use about themselves. A line lands when it completes one they recognise
rather than introducing a new one.

**The line and the scroll stopper must be about the same thing.** A
strong line over an unrelated first frame kills the script in the first
second — the words say one thing, the picture says another, and the viewer
resolves it by scrolling. The stopper shows the thing the line names,
already happening.

**A variation changes what happens, not which words describe it.** Five
variations means five different things occurring in front of the camera, and
five different sentences spoken — not one situation described five ways.
Swapping an adjective, adding a clause or moving a word is padding. This
applies to the control too: a variation that opens like version 0 is not a
variation.

**You know who this is for. Use it.** Triage named the avatar, the funnel and
the subject; the bank holds what that person actually says. An opening that
would work for anyone in this category has ignored all of it. The specific,
slightly uncomfortable, true thing is what stops a scroll — the safe, general
thing is what every competitor is already running.

**Dramatize.** The strongest opening is a real moment at its sharpest, not a
statement about a problem. Something done, hidden, caught, or said out loud
that people usually keep quiet. Discreet in what it claims; dramatic in what
it shows. A moment can be shown; a category cannot.

**The opening's formation is free; its material is not.** It may be any
formation at all — a condition stated, a behaviour shown, a question, a claim,
a fragment, a refusal, something with no name. What governs is not the shape
but where it comes from: **every opening is built from the language rows and
from the script's own context.** The reading tells you what the source's
opening was doing, and that is context to work from, never a template to copy
— matching its formation is one option among many, not the requirement.

What stays banned is the ad-shaped template the source did NOT use: "If you're struggling
with…", "If you're tired of…", "If you've ever…", "POV:", "Nobody talks
about…", "Here's why…", "The one thing…", "Stop scrolling if…". They signal
an ad in the first two words and none of them is how a person starts a
sentence. If the source opened on one of them, you may keep it — that is the
source's move and version 0 already carries it. Otherwise never introduce
one. If a hook could be reused verbatim by a different brand in a different
category, it is a template — that is the real test, not which formation you
chose. **Print before writing:** what the source's opening was doing, in one
phrase, as the context you are working from.

**Match the speaker's own voice.** Sentence length, vocabulary, rhythm, and
any quirks the teardown record shows. If the speaker drops articles, the hook
drops articles. Where the source's shape leaves the choice open, a behaviour
beats a condition — a behaviour is how the avatar talks about their life, a
condition is how a brand talks about them.
Every hook must also stay inside the format's own register as the spec
records it — the body that follows is shared, so a hook whose register the
body could not follow is out of the set, however strong the line.

## THE VARIATIONS ARE REAL SWINGS

A variation is a different swing at the same job — not the control with one
word moved. Five near-identical lines are one hook wearing five names, and the
buy reads them as a result.

**Each variation enters on something different.** A different moment, a
different behaviour, a different verbatim seam. Not a swapped noun over the
same sentence. Across the five, five genuinely different ways in.

**What must NOT move**, because this is what "fits the format" means:

- **The format's currency.** A spoken line where the format speaks, a card
  where it does not, a frame where it has neither — per the reading.
- **The format's register and length**, as the spec records them. The body
  that follows is shared, so an opening the body could not follow is out of
  the set however strong the line.
**The formation is not on that list.** Each variation may take whatever
formation its material calls for — they do not have to match the source's, and
they do not have to match each other's. What every one of them must be is
**sourced**: built from the language rows and from what the script itself
gives you, never invented to fill a shape.

Everything else is fair game: the formation, the object, the moment, the
emotional entry, the person's stance, what is in frame, how much is said out
loud.

### When the source's opening is a device

Sometimes the stop is carried by a *device* rather than by what is said — a
card, a two-word construction, a framing. The device is the asset, and
abandoning it entirely loses the thing that worked.

**One variation may hold the device exactly** and swing only inside it. One,
not five. The other four swing wider, on different entries, while still doing
what the opening does. That keeps the device under test without spending the
whole set on it.

### Following the source

On an organic source especially: hold the source's own structure until the
point where the product has to enter. The opening is inherited, not designed.
What the source did in its first two seconds to make someone stop is the
asset — the variations exist to find which way into it performs, not to
replace it.

## EXAMPLES ARE EXAMPLES

Every specific in this prompt — an age, a gender, a garment, a body part, a
product category, a named person — is **illustrative only**. None of it
describes the asset in front of you. Read an example for the shape of the rule,
then apply the rule to what you actually have.

The subject may be any age, any gender, anywhere, selling anything. This chain
runs the same on one person on a beach, someone talking to camera in a parked
car, two people in a kitchen, or a pair of hands on a countertop. Nothing in
this prompt assumes which.

**Who is on camera comes from the teardown record, never from this prompt.** If
the teardown says a person in their fifties in a parked car, that is the
subject — every rule below applies to them unchanged, and any pronoun in an
example above is about that example, not about the person in front of you.

**And the variables are agnostic too.** An example may be reworked freely — it
is illustrative and always was. But nothing that gets filled in at run time
may name a brand, a product or a category: brand detail arrives only through
the variables above, resolved from that brand's own folder. A rule that
cannot be written without naming the brand belongs in the brand folder, not
in this prompt. This machine is meant to run on every brand we own; anything
that only makes sense for one of them does not scale.

## Before you hand these over — print this

Four checks. Run them, print the result of each, and fix what fails before
you return anything. A rule stated in prose does not bind.

**THE FOUR-WORD TEST.** List the first four words of all six openings, one per
line. If any two match, you wrote one opening and padded it: replace the
duplicate, then print the corrected list. The single exception is the one
variation allowed to hold a device — it may share its opening words with the
control, and nothing else may.

**THE ENTRY TEST.** For each of the five, name in a few words what it enters
on — the moment, behaviour or verbatim it is built from. If any two share an
entry, one of them is not a variation: replace it and print the corrected
list.

**THE PHOTOGRAPH TEST.** For each variation, name in five words the single
image someone sees at half a second — the thing in frame before a word is
read. If any two would produce the same photograph, they are the same opening
however differently they are worded. Replace it.

**THE RUNG TEST.** The reading names the entry level and quotes that level's
`must_not`. Copy that quoted must-not here, then take all six openings —
control included — one at a time, and say whether it breaks it. **A hook that
names the price, the product or the direct claim earlier than its entry level
allows is a defect, not a style choice**, and it is the failure this test
exists for: the line reads perfectly well and is simply invisible to the
reader it was written for.

Two things this test is not. It is not licence to rewrite the control — if
version 0 breaks the must-not, that is a finding about the source's own
entry level against ours, printed as a flag and left standing. And it is not
a ban on the words themselves later in the asset; it is about where the
argument is allowed to BEGIN.

Any variation that breaks its entry level's must-not is rewritten to enter a
rung lower and printed again, corrected. Print the must-not, the six
verdicts, and every rewrite.
