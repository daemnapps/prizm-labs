Write the opening. Six versions — a control and five variations. **All of
them ship to test.** The human gate on this pass is approval, not
selection: nothing is picked, nothing is ranked, and the body written
after this pass must carry any hook in the set. The variations are the

**A hook is a line and a scroll stopper, together.** The line is what the speaker
hears or reads; the scroll stopper is the first-frame visual event that
arrests the thumb before a single word lands. Neither is optional and
neither is the other's caption — they are the same thought in two
channels, show and tell at once. "Selfie walk-and-talk at the entrance"
is a camera setup, not a scroll stopper. A scroll stopper is a thing
*happening*: an act mid-motion, an object where it should not be, a
texture close enough to feel, a before mid-transformation. If the frame
would read as ordinary with the sound off, it stops nobody. Take the
visual vocabulary from what the source already does where it serves;
build inside the format's world where it does not.

**The spec already did the abstraction — read it, do not redo it.** The
replication spec names the opening phase, its duration and its fixed
bounds in the structural skeleton; the element inventory carries the
opening element's mandate; the script architecture names the premise and
its job. **That is the foundational hook, and it is read out of the spec,
cited, never re-derived from the teardown.** The teardown record is for
what was in frame; the placement plan supplies the awareness entry. If
the spec is missing any of this, that is a stage-2 defect — flag it and
stop rather than deriving around it.

**The placement plan names the opening beat by timestamp.** Rewrite that
beat and no other. Do not rewrite a later line because it looks more like a
hook.

**One brief, one discrete problem.** The placement plan and the baseline
pin the problem this asset solves. Every hook names that problem, in the
avatar's own dominant words for it — the words the research shows the avatar
uses most, not the category's default synonym. A strong research moment
that names a *different* problem is not a hook variation; it is a
different brief. Write no hook on it — route it to the BRIEF VARIATION
OPPORTUNITIES section, one line, and move on. Hooks that shift the
problem mid-set do not widen the test; they contaminate it.

**Version 0 is the straight swipe, and it is the control.**

Take the source's opening beat exactly as it is and change only the words
that must change — the brand, the product, the category. Nothing else.
Same sentence, same rhythm, same length, same punctuation. Its scroll
stopper is the source's own first frame, held.

This is the version that already performed, wearing our name. Every variation
is a bet against it in the same test, and if none of them beat it that is
a real finding rather than a failure. **Label it clearly as the control
and never improve it.**

Then write five variations. The rest of this prompt is about those.

**The ledger is spent ground.** Before writing, read the hook ledger. A
verbatim that already carries a hook there is **spent** — you do not
build another hook on it, however strong it is, unless the ledger marks
its hook `validated`. A validated hook returns only as itself — the
control of its own next test — never re-worded. The research is deeper
than its five most quotable lines, and reaching for a quote the ledger
shows twice already is the signal to mine a fresher seam: a body part
the scripts underweight, a behaviour nobody has used, a phrase from the
bank's edges. If the bank genuinely has nothing fresh left for this
format and this problem, say so plainly instead of repeating — that is a
real finding, and it routes to a research pass, not to a sixth cardigan.

Five rules govern what replaces the source's words:

**Every hook traces to a verbatim.** Pull the moment from the language bank
and the avatar — a behaviour, a time of day, an object, something done
with the hands. The strongest hooks name a private moment the avatar thought nobody
had noticed. A hook that cannot cite its source is invented and gets cut,
however well it reads. Reaching for a phrase that is not in the bank is the
signal you are writing at the avatar instead of from them.

**Finish a sentence the avatar has already started.** The bank holds
half-phrases they use about themselves. A line lands when it completes one they recognise
rather than introducing a new one.

**The line and the scroll stopper must be about the same thing.** A
strong line over an unrelated first frame kills the script in the first
second — the words say one thing, the picture says another, and the viewer
resolves it by scrolling. The stopper shows the thing the line names,
already happening.

**Five variations means five different sentences.** The test is exact: **if
any two share their first four words, they are the same hook and you have
written one and padded it.** This applies to the control too — a variation
that opens like version 0 is not a variation. Same function, five genuinely
different ways of performing it — a different moment, a different failure, a different
person noticing, a different half-second of a life.

**Never open with a template.** These are banned outright, in any wording:
"If you're struggling with…", "If you're tired of…", "If you've ever…",
"POV:", "Nobody talks about…", "Here's why…", "The one thing…", "Stop
scrolling if…". They are the default shape of every ad in every category,
they signal an ad in the first two words, and none of them are how a person
starts a sentence. If a hook can be reused verbatim by a different brand in
a different category, it is a template.

**Prefer a behaviour over a condition.** A behaviour is how the avatar talks
about their life. A condition is how a brand talks about them. Match the speaker's
own voice too — sentence length, vocabulary, rhythm, and any quirks the
teardown record shows. If the speaker drops articles, the hook drops articles.
Every hook must also stay inside the format's own register as the spec
records it — the body that follows is shared, so a hook whose register the
body could not follow is out of the set, however strong the line.

Write each hook in the format's own currency, per the placement plan: a
spoken line if the format speaks, a card if it does not, a frame if it has
neither.

## THE VARIATIONS ARE VARIATIONS OF THE CONTROL

Added at v5, and it overrides the four-words rule above wherever the two
disagree.

The four-words test was written to stop five near-identical lines being passed
off as a set. It does that. But applied to a control that is a swiped *device* —
a card, a framing, a two-word construction — it forces every variation off the
device, and the device is the thing that worked.

That is what went wrong on the <person> run. The source opened on a card reading
`At 68` over a woman on a beach towel. The control swiped it exactly. Then the
rule required that no variation open `At [number]`, so all five abandoned the
construction, and the set tested five different ideas instead of testing the
one idea five ways.

**So: a variation changes one thing about the control and holds everything
else.** Same construction, same beat, same length, same frame — one variable
moved. Across the set, five different variables.

On the <person> example that is: `At 68` / `Still 68` / `68 and out here` /
`At 68, no filter` / `68` alone on the frame. Same device, five tests of it.
Not five unrelated openings.

**What to vary, one per variation:**

- the number or noun that carries the dissonance
- the framing, holding the words exactly
- the one word that sets the register — *still*, *again*, *and*
- what is in frame beside the subject
- the punctuation, or its absence, which changes the read entirely

**What never varies across the set:** the construction, the beat length, and
the moment in the video. If those move, it is a different hook and belongs in a
different brief.

### The one case where the four-words rule still applies

When the control is a *line of dialogue* rather than a device — a spoken
sentence with its own content — five near-identical sentences genuinely are one
hook. There, keep the original test. The distinction is simple: **if you could
describe the control as a shape rather than a sentence, vary within the shape.
If the control's value is what it says, vary what it says.**

### Following the source

On an organic source especially: hold the source's own structure until the
point where the product has to enter. The opening is inherited, not designed.
Anything the source did in its first two seconds that made someone stop is the
asset — the variations exist to find which dial on it moves performance, not to
replace it.

## EXAMPLES ARE EXAMPLES

Every specific in this prompt — an age, a gender, a garment, a body part, a
product category, a named person — is **illustrative only**. None of it
describes the asset in front of you. Read an example for the shape of the rule,
then apply the rule to what you actually have.

The subject may be any age, any gender, anywhere, selling anything. This chain
runs the same on a woman on a beach, a man talking to camera in his car, two
people in a kitchen, or a pair of hands on a countertop. Nothing in this prompt
assumes which.

**Who is on camera comes from the teardown record, never from this prompt.** If
the teardown says a man in his fifties in a parked car, that is the subject —
every rule below applies to him unchanged, and any pronoun in an example above
is about that example, not about him.

**And the variables are agnostic too.** An example may be reworked freely — it
is illustrative and always was. But nothing that gets filled in at run time
may name a brand, a product or a category: brand detail arrives only through
the variables above, resolved from that brand's own folder. A rule that
cannot be written without naming the brand belongs in the brand folder, not
in this prompt. This machine is meant to run on every brand we own; anything
that only makes sense for one of them does not scale.
