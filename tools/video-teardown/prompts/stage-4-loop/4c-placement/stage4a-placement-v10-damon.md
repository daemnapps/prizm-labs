## THE VERDICT LINE — the first line of your output

Before anything else, print one line, exactly one of these three shapes:

```
PRODUCT ENTRY: 0:14 — the cut into the wide shot
PROBLEM ENTRY: 0:03 — "Brown spots out" over the wide shot
NO ENTRY: this format neither shows a product nor names a problem
```

Then the four sections.

**The question is not "where does the product go".** It is **"where does the
viewer cross from watching into recognising themselves"** — the entry onto the
awareness ladder. A product appearing is one way that happens. It is not the
only way, and on organic sources it is usually not the way.

- **`PRODUCT ENTRY`** — the product itself enters at that beat and the asset
  can carry it.
- **`PROBLEM ENTRY`** — the beat where the problem gets named, shown or
  admitted, with no product in frame. This is a full ad. The viewer has been
  stopped, has read the hook, and now sees their own problem stated. The
  selling continues from there, or hands to the destination. **Do not write
  `NO ENTRY` because a product cannot sit at that beat.**
- **`NO ENTRY`** — genuinely neither. The asset stops a scroll and names
  nothing. Rare. It becomes a hook asset that hands off.

**The mistake this line exists to prevent, in full.** On the <person> run this
pass was asked only about product placement. It answered, correctly, that no
beat could hold a product — the reveal's job is to say *this is fine*, and a
fix there contradicts it. But the injected version of that same beat reads
**"Brown spots out"**: the problem, named on screen, at 0:03, on the longest
hold in the piece, immediately after the hook has done its work. That is a
textbook problem entry and a strong setup. Reported as "no placement", it
nearly turned a workable ad into a hand-off.

So: look for the product beat. If there is none, look for the beat where the
problem is named — in the injected version, not the source. Only if neither
exists is it `NO ENTRY`.
