automatic.**

**The spec is the construct — read structure, never re-derive it.** The
replication spec already names the skeleton phase by phase, every
element's mandate, and where injections belong. Every beat you add names
the spec phase it lands in and the element mandate it serves; an added
beat that serves no mandate is decoration, and a beat placed by feel when
the spec names the slot is drift. The teardown record is reference for
scenes, rhythm and what was in frame — never for structure and never for
words.

**One body, many heads.** The hooks are the test cells; the body you
write is shared by all of them. Build it from the control opening, and
hold its register to the format's as the spec records it, not to any one
variation. Then check the fit: a hook the body cannot follow cleanly is a
compatibility defect — flag it, do not bend the body toward it.

Four rules govern everything below:

**The baseline is the only source of lines.** THE SCRIPT SO FAR copies
every baseline line **character for character** — every price, every name,
every piece of punctuation. Reaching past the baseline into the teardown
reintroduces the source's brand, numbers and category. The test is
mechanical: before you finish, compare every `[SOURCE]` row against the
baseline injection line it came from. **Any difference — one digit, one
word — means either the tag is wrong or the line is.**

**When the source is a hook, the body is your job and it is most of the
asset.** A short organic post makes no claim, names no product and carries
no offer — that is what a hook is. So "the source does not do this" is
never a reason for us not to do it. On a source like that the source can
only tell you how the asset *opens*; everything after the opening comes
from this pass, and four moves all answering "not needed" means an ad with
no product in it. That happened on 2026-08-26: a nine-second post came out
of this pass as an eight-second asset with nothing added but one card.

Hold the source's *structure* exactly. Do not hold its *silence*.

### What to build when the source has no product in it

A hook with no product is not a dead end — it is a starting rung, and the
body is the climb (Damon, 2026-08-26). Read what the hook already puts in
the viewer's head, then build up from it in this order:

1. **Name the problem the hook implies.** The hook shows a state; it never
   says what is wrong. "Brown spots, no leg makeup" is a person living with
   something. Say what that something costs her — plainly, in her words.
2. **Give it context.** Why it happens, how long it has gone on, what she
   already tried. This is the part that earns the rest: a viewer who has
   not agreed there is a problem will not care about a solution.
3. **Open the opportunity.** Something changed, or something exists that
   she did not know about. Not the product yet — the possibility of one.
4. **Bring the product in as the answer to the problem you just built,**
   described, not merely named.
5. **Close** — what to do, and the offer if the files hold one.

Each of those is beats, and beats are seconds. **Budget them.** A hook that
opens at problem-awareness and never climbs is an ad that asks for nothing.

Where a rung genuinely cannot be built from the supplied files — no proof
exists, no offer is approved — say so in the ledger and climb the ones that
can. Skipping the climb entirely is never the answer.

**The placement plan's runtime budget is a ceiling, not context.** Every
beat you add is priced in seconds against the seconds the budget reserved
for body additions, and the priced total must land inside it. If the
moves this video genuinely needs cannot fit, do not quietly overrun and
do not trim the source to make room — say so in the runtime ledger and
stop; a format that cannot carry the message inside its budget is a
finding for a human, not a problem you solve by making the video longer.
Length is earned one justified beat at a time.

**A move earns its way in or it stays out.** For each of the four moves,
first name the baseline line that already does that move's job for this
video. **If that line exists, the move is not needed — substitution
already delivered it, and adding it again is blanket insertion.** A move
that does earn its way in must state, in one line, why this construct
cannot carry the core message without it, naming the spec element it
serves. A strong format arriving from stage 3 may need one move, or
none — that is the machine working, not you underdelivering.

**No number you were not given appears anywhere in your output.** Not in
the script, not in your commentary, not in the moves table, and not in the
slots list — a slot that reads `needs the customer count (e.g. "over
100,000")` has just written the number it exists to avoid, and the example
is what gets copied into the brief. This covers customer counts,
percentages, ratings, review totals, years in business, and prices.

The test is mechanical, because the urge to illustrate is strong enough to
survive being told not to: **no entry in your SLOTS section may contain a
digit, a quotation mark, or the string `e.g.`** Name the *kind* of fact in
plain words — "the number of customers who have bought it" — and stop
there.

**You are injecting, not rewriting.** The construct's spine — its
opening, its sequence, its device, its rhythm — survives this pass intact.
Every added beat is connective tissue that carries the core message the
placement plan names. None reorganise what is already there. If you find
yourself moving the spec's phases around, stop: that is a different job
and it destroys the reason the format worked.

The four moves, each behind the earns-its-way-in gate above.

**Social proof.** Only if nothing in the baseline tells the viewer anyone else
bought this *and* the construct has a phase where a stranger's experience
belongs. Never invent a number. If none is substantiated, write
`[SLOT: substantiated customer count]` and carry on.

**Describe the product, do not just name it.** Only if the baseline
introduces the product without saying what it is. When the source's winner
was a service and ours is a product the viewer has never seen, this is usually
the one move that is genuinely needed — one beat, in the avatar's words,
naming what it is and how it differs from what they have already tried.

**Give every component a benefit tied to the buying reason.** Only if the
baseline names components without jobs. If the baseline's substituted
lines already carry the mechanism inside the source's own beats, this move
is done and a component rundown on top of it is padding.

**Fix the mechanics the source got wrong.** The borrowed format describes
a usage that belongs to its own product, not ours — frequency, method,
duration, what the product visibly does. Take the documented usage from
the product file exactly as written and correct every mechanic carried
across. This is the move most often genuinely needed, because the script
reads fine either way — check it even when every other move stays out.

Two things apply throughout. **The added beats must read as one continuous
thought**, not a format with a pitch bolted on — write the connective tissue
in the speaker's own rhythm, finishing sentences the language bank shows they
already starts. And **every message beat gets a shot**; a message with no
scene is a line the editor has to invent a picture for. Take shots from what
the source already does where you can.

Give me these seven sections.

**1. THE SCRIPT SO FAR**

Control opening through the end of the product section, beat by beat with
timings. A reader must be able to see the original still standing inside
it. Tag every beat, and the test is exact: **`[SOURCE]` means the words
are carried through unchanged from the baseline injection.** If you
altered so much as the usage instruction inside it, it is `[ADDED]`. Each
`[ADDED]` beat also names its spec phase and the element mandate it
serves.

**2. SCENE PER BEAT**

One line each: what is in frame.

**3. THE FOUR MOVES**

For each: the baseline line that already does its job, or the one-line
reason this construct needs it and the spec element it serves · what you

the line that proves it is a complete and correct answer.

**4. HOOK COMPATIBILITY**

One line per hook in the set, control included: does the body's first
source beat follow it cleanly — same problem, same register, no dangling
setup? `FITS` or `BREAKS` with the reason. A `BREAKS` is a flag for the
human, never a licence to rewrite the body or the hook.

**5. THE RUNTIME LEDGER**

Arithmetic, on the page: the source runtime, the budget's reservation for

**6. SLOTS**

Every `[SLOT: …]`, what fact would fill it, and who has that fact.

**7. WHAT THE SOURCE KEPT**

The construct's spine, phase by phase against the spec's skeleton. If any
phase was lost or moved, say so — that is a defect, not a choice.

## THE CONNECTIVE TISSUE HAS TO SOUND LIKE A PERSON

Added at v5. On an organic source there is no commercial spine to inherit, so
this pass writes the lines that carry a viewer from their world into the product.
Those written-from-nothing lines are the ones that come back sounding like an
advert read aloud, and one flat line is enough to make the whole take feel
staged.

The test: **read the line out loud in the speaker's register. If you would not say it to
someone standing in your kitchen, it does not go in.**

What that rules out, specifically:

- **Marketing connectives.** "That's when I discovered", "here's the thing",
  "little did I know", "and that's why", "the secret is", "game changer".
  Nobody says these. They are the seam showing.
- **The pivot sentence.** A line whose only job is to turn the video toward the
  product — "so when I found something that actually worked…". If the turn
  needs a sentence to explain itself, the turn is in the wrong place. Move it.
- **Explaining the joke.** On a comedy source, the funny beat lands and then a
  line arrives telling the viewer what it meant. Cut that line every time.
- **Stacked adjectives.** "Rich, creamy, luxurious." Real speech uses one, and
  usually a plain one.
- **Symmetry.** "Not just X, but Y." "It's not about A. It's about B." Written
  rhythm, not spoken rhythm.
- **Reading the product's own words back.** A benefit lifted off the product
  file, spoken as though the speaker thought of it.

What it sounds like instead:

- **Half-sentences and restarts.** People stop and change direction mid-thought.
  A line that reads slightly broken usually plays as real.
- **The specific over the general.** Not "my skin felt rough" — "the backs of
  my arms felt like a cat's tongue."
- **Understatement at the moment of the claim.** The strongest line in a real
  video is usually the flattest one.
- **The avatar's own words first.** The language bank is verbatim customer speech. Use a
  phrase from it rather than writing a new one that means the same thing.

**One check before this pass ends:** find the line where the video turns toward
the product. Read it aloud. If it sounds like an advert, rewrite it as the
thing the speaker would actually say, or cut it and let the turn happen visually.

## EXAMPLES ARE EXAMPLES

Every specific in this prompt — an age, a gender, a garment, a body part, a
product category, a named person — is **illustrative only**. None of it
describes the asset in front of you. Read an example for the shape of the rule,
then apply the rule to what you actually have.

The subject may be any age, any gender, anywhere, selling anything. This chain
runs the same on a woman on a beach, a man talking to camera in his car, two
people in a kitchen, or a pair of hands on a countertop. Nothing in this prompt
assumes which.

**Who is on camera comes from the teardown record, never from this prompt.** If
the teardown says a man in his fifties in a parked car, that is the subject —
every rule below applies to him unchanged, and any pronoun in an example above
is about that example, not about him.

**And the variables are agnostic too.** An example may be reworked freely — it
is illustrative and always was. But nothing that gets filled in at run time
may name a brand, a product or a category: brand detail arrives only through
the variables above, resolved from that brand's own folder. A rule that
cannot be written without naming the brand belongs in the brand folder, not
in this prompt. This machine is meant to run on every brand we own; anything
that only makes sense for one of them does not scale.
