**How these {n} were chosen** — the standing method, so you can trust the
set: her **entire page** was indexed, every post, not a recent window. The
posts were ranked by **views + likes + comments as a straight sum, no
weighting**, and the top {n} kept. That is her all-time best-performing
work by total engagement, which is the definition this process uses.

Two consequences to hold in mind:

- **These may be old.** All-time top means the dates can span years. If the
  set skews to one period, say so — a signature drawn from two-year-old
  work describes how she used to make videos, and that is worth flagging to
  whoever writes the brief.
- **Nothing here is filtered for relevance or format.** Whatever performed
  is in, including posts that are off-topic for our brand. Do not quietly
  drop one because it doesn't suit us; if a post is an outlier in kind
  rather than degree, name it and say what it tells us.

Work out this creator's **signature** — the way she actually makes videos,
consistent across her best work. We are about to brief her to shoot for our
brand, and the brief has to arrive so complete that she only has to
execute. Everything below exists to make that possible.

**Count, don't impress.** Every claim names how many of the {n} videos it
appears in, and which ones by rank. An element in 8 of 10 is a signature.
An element in 2 of 10 is a one-off, and saying otherwise makes the brief
wrong. Where you are unsure, say so rather than rounding up.

**Separate frequent from effective.** An element appearing in 9 of 10 is
consistent. An element appearing in her top 3 by engagement but nowhere
else is a different and more interesting thing. Never merge those two
findings — report each, and say which is which.

**Quote her.** Anything about how she talks is a verbatim quote from a
transcript, with the video it came from. Never a paraphrase, never a
characterisation. If you cannot quote it, do not claim it.

Return these seven sections.

**1. HER OPENING**

How she starts, consistently. The shape of the first three seconds across
all {n}, how much variation there is, and how long she takes before the
first cut. Quote every opening line verbatim in a list so the pattern is
visible rather than asserted.

**2. HER STRUCTURE**

The phase shape she reliably uses. A table:
`Phase | Appears in | Typical duration | Typical position`

"Appears in" is a count out of {n} plus the ranks. Only include phases
present in more than half. Then, in one line: the shortest version of her
structure that still works, and the longest she has run.

**3. HER VOICE**

The recurring language. Phrases she uses more than once across videos,
verbal tics, how she addresses the viewer, her register, her pace. Every
entry carries at least one verbatim quote and its video rank. Separate the
phrases that recur from the ones that are merely characteristic of a
single video.

**4. HER FRAME**

What is consistently true of the picture: camera position and distance,
setting, lighting, wardrobe, what she does with her hands, whether she
holds product and when, how she uses on-screen text. Count each.

**5. WHAT SHE NEVER DOES**

The consistent absences — devices, framings, or moves that appear in none
of the {n}. These matter as much as the presences: briefing her into
something she has never done is how a brief gets ignored or shot badly.

**6. THE BRIEFABLE FORMAT**

One template that combines the above into the format we would brief her
into: her opening shape, her phase structure with durations, her voice
rules, her frame. Write it as a fill-in template with bracketed slots,
the same way a replication spec's script architecture works — this is what
the brand injection stage will substitute into, so it must be a template
and not a description.

State plainly which parts of it are load-bearing to *her* — the things
that make it recognisably her work — and which are free to change.

**7. THE EVIDENCE TABLE**

A table: `Element | Videos it appears in | Count | In her top 3?`

One row per element named anywhere above, so every claim in this document
can be checked against the source without re-reading the specs.

Finally, if the {n} videos do not share enough to constitute a signature —
if she is genuinely inconsistent — say so directly and show the spread.
A creator with no signature is a real finding and a useful one; inventing
a pattern that isn't there produces a brief she cannot execute.
