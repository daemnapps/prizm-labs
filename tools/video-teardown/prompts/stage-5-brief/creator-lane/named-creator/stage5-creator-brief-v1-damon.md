**This is the CREATOR lane.** It exists for one situation only: the
formats in the stage-4 outputs were torn down **from this creator's own
top posts**, and the brief you write sends her structures back to her
wearing our brand — so the finished video reads as a post that came from
her page, because structurally it did. **Check before writing:** if any
stage-4 output traces to a source that is not one of her own posts, it
does not belong in this brief — set it aside and say so in the internal
document. A swipe-file format goes down the general lane instead.

Turn the rest into the one document she receives. Everything upstream was
working material; this is the only thing that leaves the building.

Five rules govern everything below.

**Nothing unresolved crosses into the maker's document.** If a script still
carries a `[SLOT: …]`, either the fact exists **in one of the files above**
and you fill it from there, or the line is cut. A maker who meets an
unfilled slot will skip the line or invent something — both are worse than
not having the line. Every slot you cut gets listed in the internal section
so nobody thinks it was handled.

There is no third option. **Generalising a slot is filling it.** Turning
`[SLOT: current promotion]` into "the bundle deal" or "today's sale" has
announced a promotion nobody approved, in the one document that goes to
someone who will say it out loud. If the fact is not in a file, the line is
cut — say so in the internal document and move on.

**This document adds no facts.** Every claim in the maker's document traces
to a supplied file, and the brief is the last place where an invention is
still cheap to catch. Watch for the ones that arrive as flavour rather than
as claims: a country or heritage the product file never mentions, a named
method or ritual, an ingredient count, a manufacturing story, a founder
detail, a scientific mechanism stated more confidently than the file states
it. If it is not in a file, it does not appear — not in a concept name, not
in a hook option, not in an aside.

And a phrase appearing in a file is not automatically ours to use. The
language bank records what customers say **and what they were shown not to
say** — a phrase sitting inside a sentence that reports its absence is
evidence against that phrase, not a source for it. Read the sentence around
any phrase before lifting it.

Prices come from the offer file exactly as written — character-exact for
the guarantee. Where the same product sells at different prices on
different surfaces, say which surface each price belongs to.

**Her voice is the product — the brief carries directions, never a script
to read.** Concepts, hook options and story beats; her own words are the
reason she works, and most contracts say the content stays hers. The one
exception is a line the rules require verbatim (the guarantee), and the
brief says exactly that.

**Every concept names her post it came from and the habit it uses.** Not
as decoration — this is the lane's whole premise. Name the actual post
(by its opening, date, or rank in her profile), what the teardown found
load-bearing in it, and which of her documented habits the concept leans
on. If you cannot name the post, the concept is in the wrong lane.

**She must never have to ask a question.** If she would have to guess a
length, a shot, a product, or what she is allowed to say, the brief has
failed and the fix is in the brief, not in her.

Give me these two documents.

What she receives. Written to her by name, warmly, in plain language. No
process jargon anywhere in it.

dropped and none ranked, each with its line and its first-frame moment

what to say about the product, the true things, in her words · suggested
length.
