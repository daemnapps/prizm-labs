# THIS IS A HOOK ASSET, NOT AN AD

Placement found no beat in this format that a product can enter. That is the
finding, and this brief obeys it.

So this is a shot list for **an opening that earns attention and hands off.**
It gets the scroll to stop and the viewer to click, comment or follow. The
selling happens somewhere else — the destination page, a second asset, the
creator's own reply in the comments.

**Do not invent a product moment.** No demonstration, no tube in frame, no
benefit line, no price, no guarantee, no call to buy. If you find yourself
writing one, you are writing the wrong document — the whole reason this lane
exists is that the format rejects those and the last brief added them anyway.

Most of what gets swiped is somebody's ad. This lane is for the other thing:
organic content that already knows how to stop a scroll, kept as what it is.

# THE SHAPE

```
# <what this opening is>

Watch first: <the source link exactly as given, or omit the line if NONE>

**Runs about <N> seconds.** Shoot it the length the source is. Do not stretch it.

## The opening — shoot all six

**Control** · "<the line or card>"
*Scroll stopper:* <the frame, copied from the hook set>

**Variation 1 — <name>** · "<the line or card>"
*Scroll stopper:* <the frame>

…all six, control first.

## The shots

**Scene 1 · 0:00–0:02**

<what she does: where she is, the light, what is in her hands>. **Wearing:**
<garment, colour, sleeves, hair, jewellery — in full, every scene>.

> "<what she says, or the card>"

![Scene 1](—)

## Where it hands off

<One line: what the viewer does next, and where the selling happens.>
```

# RULES

- **Length matches the source.** A seven-second source makes a seven-second
  asset. Never pad it toward ad length.
- **The scroll stoppers are copied from the hook set, not rewritten.** Same
  frame, same angle, same setting. That frame is the entire asset.
- **`Wearing:` on every scene, in full.** Never "as before".
- **`![Scene N](—)`** under every scene.
- **No offer block and no guarantee.** Nothing is sold here, so nothing needs
  claiming. The only list that stays is **Don't say**, kept short and flat.

# TONE

She is filming on her own phone. Short sentences, said to her. No preamble, no
sign-off. Start at the concept.

## EXAMPLES ARE EXAMPLES

Every specific in this prompt — an age, a gender, a garment, a body part, a
product category, a named person — is **illustrative only**. None of it
describes the asset in front of you. Read an example for the shape of the rule,
then apply the rule to what you actually have.

The subject may be any age, any gender, anywhere, selling anything. This chain
runs the same on a woman on a beach, a man talking to camera in his car, two
people in a kitchen, or a pair of hands on a countertop. Nothing in this prompt
assumes which.

**Who is on camera comes from the teardown record, never from this prompt.** If
the teardown says a man in his fifties in a parked car, that is the subject —
every rule below applies to him unchanged, and any pronoun in an example above
is about that example, not about him.

**And the variables are agnostic too.** An example may be reworked freely — it
is illustrative and always was. But nothing that gets filled in at run time
may name a brand, a product or a category: brand detail arrives only through
the variables above, resolved from that brand's own folder. A rule that
cannot be written without naming the brand belongs in the brand folder, not
in this prompt. This machine is meant to run on every brand we own; anything
that only makes sense for one of them does not scale.
