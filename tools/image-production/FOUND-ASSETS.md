convention the index follows, and it is a good one:

  CGI. Nothing generated ever goes in. If a render and this disagree, this wins.
- `2-frames` — stills pulled from the videos. Rebuildable, safe to delete.
- `3-model` — what the ads actually use. Clean renders and the 3D mesh.

## 6 · Duplicates between the two drives

1,059 of the 3,579 rows are flagged `duplicate_on_company_drive` — matched on
identical file name **and** identical byte size. They are almost entirely
`My Drive/Growth/<brand>`, which is a straight mirror of the company drive's
`brands/<brand>/products/`. Company `products/` holds 937 media files; 963
name+size pairs match across the two drives.

The flag is on every row in `found-assets.json`, so a copy job can simply skip
anything already true.

## 7 · Roughly how long a copy would take

- **Brand kit + all new product/static imagery — 546 images, 0.42 GB:**
  minutes. This is the copy that matters and it is almost free.
- **Brand kit alone — ~25 MB:** under a minute.
- **The full 109 GB including video:** hours, not minutes. Both ends are Google
  Drive network mounts, so every file is downloaded then re-uploaded; realistically
  a half-day to overnight run, and worth doing selectively rather than wholesale.

## 8 · What could not be reached

Nothing was blocked. Both mounts read cleanly and every <brand> folder was walked
to its full depth. Two limits worth naming:

- Sizes and dates come from the Drive mount's metadata; files are streamed, so
  nothing was opened or downloaded to verify contents. Classification is from
  folder and file names only, as instructed.
- `kind` is `unknown` for 427 rows. Most are finished statics, memes, tweet-cards
  and banners, plus the logo and design-element files — a logo is not a scene
  element and none of the allowed kinds fit it, so it was left `unknown` with the
  reason recorded in `why_kind` rather than forced into a category.
