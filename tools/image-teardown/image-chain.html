The Image Chain

Every step from a swiped ad to a finished draft, in run order — who runs it, what it writes, and whether any of it reaches the picture. Built to answer one question: what can be cut.

“You don’t even need to remake the image itself first… all the next prompt needs to do is go through the process of creating a brief for that image of what needs to be in the image. We then just write that prompt using the reference image. All this other stuff — the compositor, all this other crap — I really don’t think it matters.”

You’re right about the picture. Seven writing stages run before a single picture is made. They produce about 62,800 characters per swipe. Exactly one section of one stage reaches the generator — the plate prompt, 2,807 characters on the median brief. Everything else is read by the next stage, by the copy layer, or by a person.

And the quality lever was never the stage count. The four things that actually decide whether a draft comes back right are all attachments and file lookups, not writing passes. They’re listed further down.

The whole input

What the generator actually gets

Four things. That’s it. Nothing else from the sixty-three thousand characters is in the room.

1 · The swipe, as IMAGE 1

The original still. Reproduce its framing, pose, crop, angle, light and layout exactly; the person, the product and the words are the only things that change.

2 · The plate prompt

About 2,800 characters lifted out of the brief — the subject, the setting, the framing, the light, the negatives, and the crop line.

3 · Our real photographs

The clean tube from three angles, the range where the swipe shows several. Attached, never described — and stripped out when the frame has no hands in it.

4 · Facts read from files

Price, guarantee and discount bar out of the offer bank; the accent colour out of the brand palette. Looked up, not written.

Where the quality actually comes from

Four levers, none of them a stage

Ranked by what the runs on disk show. Each one changed the pass rate; no writing pass ever did.

1

The swipe goes in as a picture

For months it didn’t. The generator saw the cast, the product and a paragraph — and a paragraph cannot carry a composition.

before: “not close enough to the inspiration” · after: 20 of 21 passed
2

The right reference photos — and only those

A wrong attachment beats every word in the prompt. The model copies the reference rather than the swipe.

p187–p190: a skin photo on a person-free frame → all 12 rolls came back as a woman applying product, offer text gone · p186: stripping the in-hand photo took away the only correct grip we own
3

Offer, colour and product read out of files

Price, guarantee, discount bar, accent colour. Looked up from the brand’s own folders, so nothing is invented and nothing is dropped.

4:5 content inside a 9:16 frame

Content generated at 4:5 and taped into the tall frame, only the strips painted. Asking a model not to reflow does not work.

Troubleshooting

The three things that actually break

None of them is the number of stages.

References outrank words

Whatever photo is attached is what comes back. The rule the runs taught: attach our real thing when the change touches it, and strip it when the frame doesn’t contain it.

two incidents, both 24 Sep, both caught by the judge and both avoidable at attach time

Models redraw words every single roll

An image model regenerates rather than edits, so any text in frame is re-drawn — including text nobody asked to change. This is the one place a composite beats a generation, because a composite is exact and costs nothing.

“Centela” for Centella · “188 g” for 180 g · “4.52 OZ” for 3.53 OZ · a masked edit that passed the judge carried a doubled headline

A brief that never passes keeps rolling

Four rounds of three is up to twelve generations on one brief. Two briefs have never produced a passing roll; they ship flagged, which is the design, but they are also the bill.

p153 — every roll cloned her face or mangled the hands · p162 — the tube comes out flat-on with a smear the swipe never had

Correction to what I told you first

I said the compositor “isn’t switched on everywhere.” That was too vague, and the truth matters here: the half that clears the space is switched on. The half that sets the words was never wired. Full picture in the next section.

What the compositor actually is

It isn’t a third model. It’s typesetting.

Two models run in this chain and that doesn’t change: GPT Image 2.5 paints, Gemini reads and judges. The compositor is neither — it’s software on the Mac that sets type on a picture, the way a design program does.

What you guessed

“A step inside of the generation prompt saying, hey, composite this thing in this space.”

Half right, and the half that’s already built. The generation prompt does exactly that — it hands the model measured rectangles and says leave these empty.

The other half

Asking the model to place exact words is the version that never works — that’s what turns Centella into “Centela.” After the picture comes back, the words get set on top as real text, from the list the brief already wrote. Nothing is guessing. It’s typing.

Where the loop stops

Three pieces. Two are built and running. The third was never connected.

On image editing — my read

Yes, and it’s already built. The engine that does one-change-at-a-time edits with a two-picture judge exists and has been running since 22 September. It literally files its own work under the name image-edit — it just isn’t called that out loud anywhere.

So this is a naming and placement job, not a build: give it the name, and put it at the end of production where video editing sits. That’s the third folder above, and it costs nothing to stand up.

What the move actually costs

Clean

  • The tools already separate 16 / 19 along the seam — almost nothing is genuinely shared
  • Production has a folder already; it just isn’t where the drafting happens
  • No prompt changes, no model changes, nothing about how the work is done

The bill

  • 36 files elsewhere point at the old location and have to be repointed
