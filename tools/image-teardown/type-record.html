The Type Record

What the teardown captures about the lettering in a swipe, what survived into the brief, and the one thing that was written down and thrown away — now recorded, carried and sent.

“Things like the colour of the font, the style of the font. This is what I’m talking about: getting really clear on just the image itself in the teardown record.”

Colour is captured. Style is captured. Both are dropped before the picture is made. The teardown writes a full paragraph per type zone — the class, the width, the weight, the case, the cap height, the hex, the tracking, the leading, and sometimes the treatment. The brief’s type layer then keeps eight of those fields and throws the rest away, and the generator never sees any of it at all.

What changed today

Three prompt versions. Here they are versioned rather than edited, so the old ones stay readable and nothing changes under a run that already happened.

VersionWhat it now does
stage1 v3
the teardown		letterform is required on every text element, and explicitly is not the typeface. It records what a person would have to do to build the letters. Fourteen worked examples ship in the prompt. “Say what you see, never what is likely” — flat digital type is a real answer; leaving the field out is not.
stage6 v3
the brief		Carries letterform into the type layer word for word from the teardown, never written fresh. The words change when our copy goes in; the way they are made does not — that belongs to the format we swiped, not to the sentence. A run torn down before the field existed writes [NONE: not in the teardown] instead of inventing one.
stage7 v4
the draft		Replaces the plate-only prompt. Every line goes to the model verbatim with its position, size, colour, case, weight, alignment and exact line breaks, plus its own letterform — then one how the lettering is made clause for the whole ad.

What the generator used to get, and gets now

Before — one sentence, scraped out of the brief’s prose with a regex

THE WORDS, exactly: VITAMIN C, IS A SCAM, It
brightens your face then burns it. Spell every
word exactly as written.

This is how “2016” got invented, how “WEEK 1 / WEEK 3 / WEEK 6” got added to an ad that never asked for it, and how a competitor’s own quote got copied straight off a swipe.

The compositor is retired. The plate-only prompt — picture with no text, words typed on afterwards — is superseded. It could not do moulded clay, and it had its own defects besides: it stacked “2016” down the frame where the swipe reads “20 16” across.

One thing to know. All 66 existing briefs were torn down before this field existed, so they fall back to reading the swipe fresh at draft time. Any new teardown gets the real thing. The existing ones can be re-read on your word.

Run on a real ad — the result

One brief, one swipe, torn down fresh with the new prompts and drafted. Nothing else changed.

Before

The swipe is an illustration with a hand-drawn, hatched headline. The old draft came back as a photograph of a tube on a flat ground, in set type, with the call to action missing entirely. It is a competent ad and it is not this ad.

After

Both rolls come back drawn, in the swipe’s own line and shading. “face scrub” is hand-drawn and hatched like “eye balm”. The tube label is white extruded type with a hard outline, like the jar label. “Shop now” is there, in the same hand it was written in.

Three letterforms survived all three stages — the teardown read them off the picture, the brief carried them word for word, the generator was told each one: hand-drawn-textured headline, flat-digital body, extruded-3d label.

Two honest notes. Roll 2’s tube came back brown where the real product is cream — roll 1 is the closer of the two, and that is what the judge exists to catch. And the product is now drawn rather than photographed, because matching the swipe’s medium outranks everything else in the standard. That is correct here and it is a real tension worth knowing about: on an illustrated swipe, our product will be an illustration of itself.

Then the product came back wrong

the owner, on the draft above: “our tube is completely wrong with this final ad. The first tube was actually perfect. Make sure when we’re talking about the product, the product’s gotta be the product.”

He was right, and it was not the medium rule. The teardown had recorded the swipe’s jar label as three text zones — jar label text (main), (brand), (volume). The brief injected our ad copy into all three. The generator, now told to draw every word on the list, printed “Thai Botanical / Dark Spots & / Uneven Tone Face Scrub” across the front of our own tube — in place of “BRILLIANCE FACE SCRUB · Turmeric and Moringa”.

Stage 1 had forbidden this since August — “in-world text is not a type element” — and the teardown did it anyway, with the exact parenthetical note the rule forbids. So the fix went in at all three points where the mistake can be made or caught.

VersionWhat it now does
stage1 v5
the teardown		Forbids creating the zone at all, not just leaving its column blank. A zone named “jar label text” is a zone whose whole purpose is in-world text, and everything downstream treats a zone as something the ad sets. The label, wordmark and net weight are one object — the package.
stage6 v5
the brief		The type layer is the ad’s type. A product’s own label never enters it — not carried, not injected into, not renamed. Every element declares on_product, so a mistake is visible instead of silent.
stage7 v5
the draft		The product’s identity now outranks the medium rule, which used to outrank everything. The product photograph is the authority on shape, colour and label. No word from the word list is ever printed on the product.
gpt_drafts.py
enforcement		on_product is honoured in code, not only asked for in the prompt — a flagged line is dropped from the word list and the drop is printed. A brief carrying the flag is safe whatever the model decides.

Run again — the result

What the teardown did

Three jar label text zones became one product field. The label belongs to the package again.

What the brief did

Seven elements down to four. The three package lines are gone; all four survivors carry their letterform and their on_product flag.

What came back

The real cream tube with the real label — emblem, SKINCARE, BRILLIANCE FACE SCRUB, Turmeric and Moringa, and on roll 1 the net weight and “BE THE BEST YOU” in their right places.

Both halves hold at once now. An illustrated ad in the swipe’s own register — “face scrub” hand-drawn and hatched like “eye balm”, “Shop now” in the same hand — carrying our real product. That is the thing that was broken in both directions: the old draft had the right tube in the wrong style, the letterform draft had the right style with the wrong tube. Roll 1 is the better of the two — roll 2 dropped “BE THE BEST YOU” and the net weight off the label.
