Plan the direct-response transformation. Decide four things and write no
copy lines — everything downstream depends on these, and they are cheap to
change now and expensive to change later.

Three rules govern everything below:

**The lane sets the depth.** A source that is already a direct-response ad
has offer logic and objection handling to inherit, so the work is mostly
swaps. A source that reads as editorial or native has none of that, so the
added work is heavier — but heavier means *justified zone by zone*, never
doubled by default.

**The product enters where the frame already re-engages the eye.** Not at the
bottom by default. Find the stop the frame itself builds toward — the reading
path in the teardown record names the order the eye travels, and the product
belongs at the stop where attention is re-acquired rather than at the stop
where it leaves.

**Space decides how many rungs the frame can carry.** A single frame carries
far less than a video. One headline, one supporting line and one offer block
is a full frame; a fourth idea buys illegibility. An ad that cannot climb four
rungs in one frame stops at problem-awareness and earns the click.

Give me these seven sections.

**1. THE LANE**

Answer with exactly one of these three labels, spelled this way, and nothing
else: **ALREADY AN AD** · **EDITORIAL** · **ORGANIC**. Then one line of why.
Later passes key off this label, so an invented name for it breaks them.

- **ALREADY AN AD** — carries an offer block, a guarantee, a badge, or a
  price. Inherits offer logic.
- **EDITORIAL** — laid out to read as an article or a magazine page: kicker,
  serif headline, body-copy register, a CTA that reads as a link rather than
  a button. Inherits credibility borrowing, not offer logic.
- **ORGANIC** — reads as a native post with no commercial furniture at all.

This lane runs on one frame. If the image you were given is plainly one panel
of a sequence, say so in one line and lane it on what the panel itself does —
the chain still runs, on this frame alone.

**2. PRODUCT PLACEMENT**

The zone where the product enters, named and quoted from the teardown record,
and what makes it that zone.

If the frame has no such zone — the source never shows or names a product —
say so. Some formats make no promise and cannot carry a pitch; they are
openings that hand off to a landing page. That is a real finding for the
owner, and the chain still completes.

**3. THE HEADLINE ZONE**

The zone the opening pass rewrites, named and quoted from the teardown
record, with its position. On a static this is the whole hook, so name it
explicitly and name no other zone — the next pass must not have to guess
which words are the headline and which are support.

**4. THE RATIOS TO BUILD**

Which aspect ratios this concept ships in, and for each: the placement it
serves, and what changes about the layout at that ratio. Take the evidence
from the teardown record's frame spec and, where a set map is supplied, from
what the crop actually cost.

Name any zone that cannot survive a ratio. A frame whose proof is a wide
two-hand gesture does not become a 9:16 by cropping, and finding that out at
build time costs a re-shoot.

**5. AWARENESS**

Which rung she is on when this frame finds her, which rung it must reach, and
therefore how many rungs it has to carry.

**An upstream `[SLOT: …]` is still empty.** Where the baseline injection left
a slot, the fact behind it does not exist yet — do not describe the end rung
as if it did, and never restate a claim the injection deliberately slotted
out. This holds for every section of your output.

**6. THE FRAME BUDGET**

**This is a hard ceiling for every pass after you, so budget the whole
frame** — the passes downstream add a support line *and* an objection line
*and* an offer block, and a budget that covers only the headline guarantees
an unbuildable layout nobody chose. Give it as a small ledger, in percentages
and **characters**, never in seconds and **never in words**.

**Price everything in characters per line × lines, taken from the layout
system's measure and line count.** A word ceiling cannot be checked against a
layout: "15 to 25 words" says nothing about whether a block fits, because a
line holds characters, not words. The teardown measured the measure — carry it.
Measured cause, 2026-08-26: headlines priced at "9 words" overran a 30-character
line by five characters and nothing objected, because nothing downstream had a
number it could test against.

Every row below is written as `<zone> — <measure> chars × <lines> lines =
<total>`:

- the headline's budget, taken from the source headline's measured measure and
  line count at the source's type size;
- the support line's budget, and which zone it lands in;
- whether an objection line gets its own zone or rides inside an existing
  one, and its budget;
- whether the offer block is present, and the share of frame height it takes
  — and if the spec marks the source's closing element fixed or load-bearing,
  the offer arrives *with* that element, so its area is additional, not
  swapped in;
- the total share of frame height type may occupy before the image stops
  carrying the ad;
- the legibility floor: which zones must still read at 25% scale.

Reserve a zone's space only if the transformation plausibly needs it. Say
plainly if the budget cannot carry the rungs; a frame that cannot fit its
message at a legible size is a real answer, and it is yours to give, not the
later passes' to discover.

**7. THE DEVICE CHECK, AND THE RISK**

The format's central device — the thing the image does that makes it work,
not the thing the copy says — read against the objection bank.

A device can contradict a brand as hard as a sentence can, and it will not
show up as a claim. A frame that demonstrates the product on a body part the
brand does not sell for is a device problem, not a copy problem. Name every
collision, cite the file and rule it collides with, and say whether the
format survives without the device.

**Collisions are reported, never ruled on.** However many devices collide,
however load-bearing they are, your output is the report — the chain
completes every pass, the collisions ride forward in the flags, and the owner
decides what runs, what gets rewritten, and what dies. You do not stop the
chain, and you do not editorialize about whether the format deserves to run.

Close with **the risk**: the one thing most likely to go wrong in this
specific frame. A headline too long for its zone, a proof the crop destroys,
a device the brand cannot stand behind.

Write no copy lines. The next pass does that.
