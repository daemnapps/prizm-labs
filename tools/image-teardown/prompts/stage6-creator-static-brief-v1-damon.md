## The five rules

**Nothing unresolved crosses into the brief.** If the material above still
carries a `[SLOT: …]`, a `[MISSING: …]`, or an `[UNCLEAR: …]`, then either the
fact exists in one of the files supplied above and you fill it from there, or
**the line carrying it is cut.** A maker who meets a placeholder will either
skip the element or invent one, and both are worse than not having it.
Generalising a placeholder is filling it. If the fact is not in a file, the
line is cut — say so in the notes at the end and move on.

**The brief adds no facts.** Every claim traces to a file supplied above. No
heritage the product file never mentions, no ritual, no ingredient count, no
result the product has not been shown to deliver.

**The words are already decided.** The headline set and the injection carry
the copy, already written and measured. You do not rewrite, shorten or
"tighten" it. Reproduce it character for character. If a line will not fit
on the picture, say so in the notes — never fix it by editing the words.

**The brief reports; it does not decide.** Who is in the picture, what body
part it shows, whether the product is in frame — all of that was settled in
the injection and arrives decided. Do not re-derive it, do not raise a
conflict against it.

**The maker is a person, so the ask is one they can do.** A photograph a
person can take on a phone in a room they have: no impossible light, no
studio rig, no composite of two places, no prop the brand has not sent them.
If the source's picture needs something a person cannot do with a phone, say
what the nearest thing they CAN do is, and say it is a departure.

# THE CREATOR BRIEF

**What this is.** Two or three sentences, in plain words: what picture we are
asking for, what makes it work (the mechanism, taken from the spec's
load-bearing list — said in a way a person understands, not in the spec's
words), and why it is worth their time.

**Who it is for and what they already believe.** Two or three sentences, in
the avatar's terms: the person who will see this picture, and the thing they
already think that this picture answers.

**The shot.** Written to the maker, as instructions:

- **Where.** The room or place, in their world. What is in the background,
  what is on the surfaces. What should NOT be visible.
- **You.** What they are wearing — every garment, colour, fit — hair, nails,
  jewellery, taken from the injection. Where they are looking (at the lens,
  or where), their expression, and exactly what their hands are doing.
- **The product.** Whether it is in the picture at all (the injection
  decided). If it is: which hand, how it is held, which side faces the camera,
  whether the cap is on. If it is not: say so, so they do not add it.
- **The frame.** Portrait, 9:16. Where the camera is — phone at chest height,
  arm's length, straight on — in words a person can follow; how much of them
  is in frame (head and shoulders · waist up · full length); which side they
  sit on. Everything that has to be readable — face, hands, product, the
  words — stays in the middle 70% of the height; the top and bottom edges are
  background only.
- **The light.** Window light from the left, overhead bathroom light, golden
  hour — the real light they can get, named from what the teardown shows and
  said so a person can find it. Never a rig.
- **The feel.** One line: the register the picture has to carry (candid,
  staged, before/after, proof-shot), and the one thing that would make it
  read as an ad instead of a post.

**The words.** Every piece of text, verbatim, one per line, with its line
breaks shown — and for each, WHERE it lives:

- **On the picture** (they add it in the app, or we add it over their photo
  — say which): the headline, the support line, the offer. Give the words,
  which one is the headline, and roughly where on the picture it sits (top
  third · bottom third · over the product), in their terms.
- **In the caption**: the caption text, verbatim, if the format carries one.

Do not paraphrase and do not re-line-break. Where a line carries two colours,
say which words are which.

**Technique per element.** Per working zone — the headline, the proof, the
offer, whatever else carries real work — name the ONE technique from the
seven above doing that zone's job, one line each: the zone · the technique ·
the specific line or picture element carrying it. A zone with no traceable
technique says so plainly.

**The alternates.** The other headlines, verbatim, control first — none
dropped, none ranked, none reworded — each with the axis it moves on in a few
words. They are the same picture with the words reset; if any needs a
different shot, say what changes and keep it to one line.

**The offer, and the wording that cannot change.** What is being offered, in
the exact words the offer file uses. If a guarantee or a claim has fixed
wording, reproduce it exactly and say it is fixed.

**What not to do.** Plain terms: no other brand's product or packaging in
frame, no filter that changes skin, no result the product has not been shown
to deliver, no words other than the ones above — plus anything specific to
this picture that would make it wrong.

**How we will know it is right.** Three to five things a person can check by
looking, each a yes or no — at least one about the picture (is the product
the right way round; is the face where the brief says), at least one about
the words (are they exactly as written), and always: nothing that is another
brand is legible anywhere in frame.

**Where this came from.** One line naming the source by its own identifier
and where it was captured — for us, not for the maker; keep it to the id.

**Notes.** Every line cut because its fact was not in a file, and every
departure from the source that a phone forced. Short, at the end, so nobody
thinks it was handled.
