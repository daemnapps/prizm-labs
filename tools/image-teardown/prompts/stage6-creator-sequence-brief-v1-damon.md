**You are not given the language bank, and that is deliberate.** The copy
arrives already written and already measured.

Write the **creator sequence brief**: the pages a person reads before they
make this, slide by slide.

**What a sequence is, and is not.** The unit is a SLIDE, not a frame. There is
no runtime, no timing span, no `Hear:` line, no voice — nobody speaks. The
words on each picture carry the whole argument, and the reader decides at
every slide whether to swipe to the next. So every slide has two jobs and
only two: earn the next swipe, and carry its own line of the argument. The
close is built from the captions the reader has already read, never from a
script.

**Who reads this.** The maker is whoever the injection put in the picture —
any age, any gender. Not a designer: no image model, no plate, no template,
no layout block, none of those words. They want to know, per slide, what to
shoot and what it says.

**One thread, or it is not a sequence.** The teardown names what carries
across every slide — the person, the product, the palette, the framing. The
maker has to hold it too: same clothes, same room, same light, same distance
from the phone, across every slide, shot in one sitting. Say so once, up
front, and never contradict it per slide.

**No source link goes in this document.**

## The five rules

**Nothing unresolved crosses into the brief.** A `[SLOT: …]`, `[MISSING: …]`
or `[UNCLEAR: …]` is filled from a file supplied above, or the line carrying
it is cut and listed in the notes. Generalising a placeholder is filling it.

**The brief adds no facts.** Every claim traces to a file supplied above.

**The words are already decided.** The headline set and the injection carry
the copy. Reproduce it character for character, line breaks included. A line
that will not fit its slide is reported, never edited.

**The brief reports; it does not decide.** Who is in it, what it shows,
whether the product appears — settled in the injection. Do not re-derive.

**The maker is a person, so every slide is a photo a person can take** on a
phone, in one sitting, in a room they have. Where the source did something a
phone cannot, say the nearest thing they can do and mark it a departure.

# THE CREATOR SEQUENCE BRIEF

**What this is.** Two or three sentences: what sequence we are asking for,
how many slides, what the argument is from first slide to last, and the one
thing that makes it work (from the spec's load-bearing list, in plain words).

**Who it is for and what they already believe.** Two or three sentences in
the avatar's terms.

**Hold this on every slide.** One block, before the slides:

- **You.** Everything they wear and how they look — every garment, colour,
  fit; hair; nails; jewellery — from the injection. The same on every slide.
- **Where.** The room, what is in the background, what must not be visible.
  The same on every slide.
- **The phone.** Portrait, 9:16, the same distance and height on every slide
  (say which: chest height, arm's length, straight on) unless a slide says
  otherwise. Everything that must be read — face, hands, product, the words —
  stays inside the middle 70% of the height on every slide; the top and
  bottom edges are background only.
- **The light.** The one real light they can get, named from what the
  teardown shows, and the same on every slide.
- **The product.** Whether it appears, on which slides, and how it is held
  when it does (the injection decided).

**The slides.** One block per slide, in order, numbered — every slide,
never a range:

```
## Slide N

**Shoot:** what is in the picture on this slide, as an instruction — the
pose, where the hands are, what the product is doing, where they are
looking. One or two sentences. Where the slide repeats the one before,
say "as slide N-1, and …" and give only what changes.

**It says:** the words ON this slide, verbatim, with line breaks shown, and
roughly where they sit (top third · bottom third · over the product).
Say whether the maker types them in the app or we add them over the photo.

**Why they swipe:** the thing on this slide that earns the next one — an
unfinished sentence, a numbered step, a question, a reveal withheld. One
line. The last slide says what it asks for instead.
```

**The caption.** The post's caption, verbatim, if the format carries one —
and which slide's words it echoes.

**Technique per slide.** One line per slide: the slide · the ONE technique
from the seven above doing its work · the words or picture element carrying
it. A slide with no traceable technique says so.

**The alternates.** The other headlines from the set, verbatim, control
first, none dropped or reworded, each with its axis in a few words — and
which slide each would replace the words on.

**The offer, and the wording that cannot change.** In the offer file's exact
words; fixed wording reproduced exactly and marked fixed.

**What not to do.** No other brand in frame, no filter that changes skin, no
result the product has not been shown to deliver, no words other than the
ones above, no slide shot in a different room or outfit from the rest — plus
anything specific to this sequence.

**How we will know it is right.** Three to five yes-or-no checks: at least
one about the thread (same person, same room, same light on every slide), at
least one about the words (exactly as written, slide for slide), one about
the close (does the last slide ask for what the argument earned), and
always: nothing that is another brand is legible on any slide.

**Where this came from.** The source's own identifier — the id only.

**Notes.** Every cut line and every phone-forced departure, short, at the end.
