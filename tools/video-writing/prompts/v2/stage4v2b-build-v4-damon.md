## THE CONNECTIVE TISSUE HAS TO SOUND LIKE A PERSON

Added at v5. On an organic source there is no commercial spine to inherit, so
this pass writes the lines that carry a viewer from their world into the product.
Those written-from-nothing lines are the ones that come back sounding like an
advert read aloud, and one flat line is enough to make the whole take feel
staged.

The test: **read the line out loud in the speaker's register. If you would not say it to
someone standing in your kitchen, it does not go in.**

What that rules out, specifically:

- **Marketing connectives.** "That's when I discovered", "here's the thing",
  "little did I know", "and that's why", "the secret is", "game changer".
  Nobody says these. They are the seam showing.
- **The pivot sentence.** A line whose only job is to turn the video toward the
  product — "so when I found something that actually worked…". If the turn
  needs a sentence to explain itself, the turn is in the wrong place. Move it.
- **Explaining the joke.** On a comedy source, the funny beat lands and then a
  line arrives telling the viewer what it meant. Cut that line every time.
- **Stacked adjectives.** "Rich, creamy, luxurious." Real speech uses one, and
  usually a plain one.
- **Symmetry.** "Not just X, but Y." "It's not about A. It's about B." Written
  rhythm, not spoken rhythm.
- **Reading the product's own words back.** A benefit lifted off the product
  file, spoken as though the speaker thought of it.

What it sounds like instead:

- **Half-sentences and restarts.** People stop and change direction mid-thought.
  A line that reads slightly broken usually plays as real.
- **The specific over the general.** Not "my skin felt rough" — "the backs of
  my arms felt like a cat's tongue."
- **Understatement at the moment of the claim.** The strongest line in a real
  video is usually the flattest one.
- **The avatar's own words first.** The language bank is verbatim customer speech. Use a
  phrase from it rather than writing a new one that means the same thing.

**One check before this pass ends:** find the line where the video turns toward
the product. Read it aloud. If it sounds like an advert, rewrite it as the
thing the speaker would actually say, or cut it and let the turn happen visually.

Four checks. Run them, print the result of each, and fix what fails before
you return anything. The rules they enforce are already stated above, and
being stated is not enough — a rule only binds when its result is written
down.

**THE GUARANTEE COUNT.** (Paid lane only; on organic the count is zero — no
guarantee text at all.) Quote the guarantee sentence exactly as the offer
file records it. Then list **every** sentence in this script that promises
money back, refunds, or anything returned — in any wording, however
glancing. If the list has more than one entry, the extras are paraphrases:
delete them and print the corrected list.

A paraphrase drops the conditions the exact wording carries. Placed before
the real one it is worse than wrong — the viewer hears an unconditional
promise attached to the price, then hears it narrow as the speaker keeps
talking, which reads as the offer shrinking in front of them.

**THE ONE-TIMELINE CHECK.** Name the section that carries the timecodes.
Then confirm, section by section, that no other section carries any. If one
does, delete its times — do not reconcile the two, because reconciling
leaves two sets that agree today and drift on the next pass.

**THE SUPPLY CHECK.** (Paid lane only — on organic print `ORGANIC — no offer stack`
and confirm no bundle, dosing schedule, guarantee text or label-style statistic is
anywhere in the script.) Print the supply math line. Then quote the guarantee
beat and say whether it tells the viewer what to do, how often, for how long,
and with how much of the offer. If it does not, rewrite it and print it again.

**THE LAST-ASK CHECK.** Quote the last spoken line and the last on-screen line,
and the lane. Say whether each makes the lane's ask — paid: the click; organic:
comment the word, or follow. If either asks for anything else as the final
ask, rewrite it and print it again. Then confirm the product, its claims and
the close are in the piece whichever the lane.

Print every result. A check whose result is not written down did not
happen.

## EXAMPLES ARE EXAMPLES

Every specific in this prompt — an age, a gender, a garment, a body part, a
product category, a named person — is **illustrative only**. None of it
describes the asset in front of you. Read an example for the shape of the rule,
then apply the rule to what you actually have.

The subject may be any age, any gender, anywhere, selling anything. This chain
runs the same on one person on a beach, someone talking to camera in a parked
car, two people in a kitchen, or a pair of hands on a countertop. Nothing in
this prompt assumes which.

**Who is on camera comes from the teardown record, never from this prompt.** If
the teardown says a person in their fifties in a parked car, that is the
subject — every rule below applies to them unchanged, and any pronoun in an
example above is about that example, not about the person in front of you.

**And the variables are agnostic too.** An example may be reworked freely — it
is illustrative and always was. But nothing that gets filled in at run time
may name a brand, a product or a category: brand detail arrives only through
the variables above, resolved from that brand's own folder. A rule that
cannot be written without naming the brand belongs in the brand folder, not
in this prompt. This machine is meant to run on every brand we own; anything
that only makes sense for one of them does not scale.

## THE VIBE HOLDS

This is social media. We are technically making an ad, but it has to live in
the feed as content, and the source earned its numbers partly on how it
feels. The doctrine read above names that feel: its `HUMOR`, `DELIVERY
STYLE`, `REGISTER`, `PACING`, `REFERENCE WORLD` and `AVOID` lines.

Print, before anything else you write:

`THE VIBE: <one line — how the source feels to watch: funny or straight, and
in which register; the world it leans on; what it never does>`

Then hold it in every line you write:

1. **A funny source stays funny — and the close keeps proof and offer
   plain.** Where the source jokes, what you write keeps joking in the same
   register — new lines included. Keep: no joke on the proof or the offer. Allow: the twist beat may be the product doing its job, shown, not claimed. A plain beat with the proof always follows it.
   In this pass that means the proof at the doubt beat, the guarantee, the
   price and the ask are written plain, even when the source joked there; the
   laugh may run up to them, never through them.
2. **A straight source stays straight — unless the room plants the joke.**
   Straight is the default. A straight source stays straight unless the culture read or the culture map gives a receipt AND the piece is not a testimonial, a before/after, a founder story, a demonstration, or about pain, health or money.
   A straight source may take a planted joke only when it has a receipt: a meme, format or reference the culture read or the avatar's culture map shows this audience already laughs at. Name the receipt. A joke the writer invented is still a finding.
   Print each planted joke as `PLANTED JOKE: <the line> · <receipt, quoted
   from the culture read or the culture map>`. A planted joke never sits on the
   proof or the offer, and nothing on the culture map's off-limits line is
   planted.
3. **Every new line sounds like it came from the same video**: the same
   speaker's voice, the same reference world, nothing from the `AVOID` line.
   A line that reads like an ad dropped into the video is a defect, however
   true it is.
4. If the doctrine read says `not shown in the record` for a line, hold what
   the source's own transcript does.

**Feel versus structure.** Some `AVOID` lines are about feel (a too-clean
look, stacked superlatives, ad-voice, a sell in the opening seconds) and some
are about structure (no price, no proof beat, no mechanism, no offer). Feel
always holds. Structure gives way to the sections line: on a Variation video
run, a section the branch's sections line adds is written — in the source's
feel — even where the source avoided it. A price is still bound by the target
level's must-not.

**The mood and the one-of-a-kind line hold too.** The doctrine read's `MOOD:`
(the rhythm and where it shifts) and `WHAT MAKES IT UNIQUE:` (the combination
worth banking) are part of the feel: new lines keep the mood's rhythm, and the
unique combination is never smoothed away.
