Write the opening. Six versions — a control and five variations, five
different TAKES on the same ad. **All of
them ship to test.** The human gate on this pass is approval, not
selection: nothing is picked, nothing is ranked, and the body written
after this pass must carry any hook in the set. The variations are the
test cells; the market decides.

**A hook is five things, printed as five labelled slots: its TAKE (the angle
in a few words), its LINE (what is said), its CARD (the words on screen in the
first second), its SCROLL (the scroll stopper) and its THUMB (the thumbnail —
the still that sells the video in the grid, with its words).** The line is what the speaker
hears or reads; the scroll stopper is the first-frame visual event that
arrests the thumb before a single word lands. Neither is optional and
neither is the other's caption — they are the same thought in two
channels, show and tell at once. "Selfie walk-and-talk at the entrance"
is a camera setup, not a scroll stopper. A scroll stopper is a thing
*happening*: an act mid-motion, an object where it should not be, a
texture close enough to feel, a before mid-transformation. If the frame
would read as ordinary with the sound off, it stops nobody. Take the
visual vocabulary from what the source already does where it serves;
build inside the format's world where it does not.

**The spec already did the abstraction — read it, do not redo it.** The
replication spec names the opening phase, its duration and its fixed
bounds in the structural skeleton; the element inventory carries the
opening element's mandate; the script architecture names the premise and
its job. **That is the foundational hook, and it is read out of the spec,
cited, never re-derived from the teardown.** The teardown record is for
what was in frame; the reading supplies the awareness entry. If
the spec is missing any of this, that is a stage-2 defect — flag it and
stop rather than deriving around it.

**The reading names the opening beat by timestamp.** Rewrite that
beat and no other. Do not rewrite a later line because it looks more like a
hook.

**THE OPENING POINT — when `{opening_at}` names a line, it overrides the
reading.** the owner has cut the ad to start there. That line is the opening beat:
the control is that line as the source says it, and everything before it is
gone from the ad — no version may lean on a beat that no longer plays. The
format below is found from THAT line, not from the source's first line. When
`{opening_at}` says `none`, the reading's opening beat stands.

**One brief, one discrete problem.** The reading and the baseline
pin the problem this asset solves. Every hook names that problem, in the
avatar's own dominant words for it — the words the research shows the avatar
uses most, not the category's default synonym. A strong research moment
that names a *different* problem is not a hook variation; it is a
different brief. Write no hook on it — route it to the BRIEF VARIATION
OPPORTUNITIES section, one line, and move on. Hooks that shift the
problem mid-set do not widen the test; they contaminate it.

**THE UNAWARE ENTRY — when the reading's entry level is `unaware`, the rule
above changes, and only then.** An unaware reader is not thinking about the
problem while he scrolls. A hook that names it, shows it, or shows the tools
of its category is addressed to somebody who is not there yet — he scrolls
past it as an ad for a thing he does not shop for. So at this entry level:

- **The problem still pins the brief — as the DESTINATION.** It is where the
  body arrives, a rung at a time. It is never in the opening. Do not name it,
  do not show it, do not point the camera at the part of the body or the
  room where it lives.
- **Every one of the six openings is a portrait.** It enters on the person:
  something he does, says, avoids, laughs at, or is proud of — a habit, a
  place, a ritual, a running joke among his people — that he would recognise
  as himself in under a second, with nothing of ours in frame. The source is
  entertainment he already watches; the opening belongs to THAT world, and
  the teardown record's opening beat tells you what world it is.
- **What may not be in the line OR the frame of an opening:** the symptom,
  the word for it, a close-up of it, the products or tools of the category
  (ours or anyone's), a before/after, a count of things tried, a complaint
  about results. Any of these makes it a problem-aware hook wearing an
  unaware label.
- **"Every hook traces to a verbatim" still holds — pull from different
  rows.** Use what the avatar says about his life, his people, his routines
  and how he carries himself. A row that is about the problem is not
  available to an opening at this level; if the bank offers nothing else, say
  so — that is a finding about the bank.
- **The variations still differ** — each enters through a different part of
  his life: five different moments, five different first frames, five
  different thumbnails. Five portraits of the same man, never one portrait
  with a word swapped.

**Version 0 is the straight swipe, and it is the control.**

Take the source's opening beat exactly as it is and change only the words
that must change — the brand, the product, the category. Nothing else.
Same sentence, same rhythm, same length, same punctuation. Its scroll
stopper is the source's own first frame, held.

This is the version that already performed, wearing our name. Every variation
is a bet against it in the same test, and if none of them beat it that is
a real finding rather than a failure. **Label it clearly as the control
and never improve it.**

Then write five variations. The rest of this prompt is about those.

Five rules govern what replaces the source's words:

**Every hook traces to a verbatim.** Pull the moment from the language bank
and the avatar — a behaviour, a time of day, an object, something done
with the hands. The strongest hooks name a private moment the avatar thought nobody
had noticed. A hook that cannot cite its source is invented and gets cut,
however well it reads. Reaching for a phrase that is not in the bank is the
signal you are writing at the avatar instead of from them.

**Finish a sentence the avatar has already started.** The bank holds
half-phrases they use about themselves. A line lands when it completes one they recognise
rather than introducing a new one.

**The line and the scroll stopper must be about the same thing.** A
strong line over an unrelated first frame kills the script in the first
second — the words say one thing, the picture says another, and the viewer
resolves it by scrolling. The stopper shows the thing the line names,
already happening.

**A variation is its own take — a different way into the same ad.** The
control carries the source's format word for word (THE FORMAT LOCK below);
the five variations do not repeat it with a word swapped. Each one opens the
same body from a different place: a different moment, a different line, a
different picture in the first frame, a different thumbnail. Two variations
that a viewer would read as the same opening are one variation. What every
take keeps from the format is its WORLD — the speaker, the register, the
reference the culture read names, the place the body continues in — so the
body that follows can carry any of the six.

**You know who this is for. Use it.** Triage named the avatar, the funnel and
the subject; the bank holds what that person actually says. An opening that
would work for anyone in this category has ignored all of it. The specific,
slightly uncomfortable, true thing is what stops a scroll — the safe, general
thing is what every competitor is already running.

**Dramatize.** The strongest opening is a real moment at its sharpest, not a
statement about a problem. Something done, hidden, caught, or said out loud
that people usually keep quiet. Discreet in what it claims; dramatic in what
it shows. A moment can be shown; a category cannot.

**The control's format is fixed; the takes are not.** The control is the
source's shape — see THE FORMAT LOCK. Every take is built from the language
rows and from the script's own context, never invented to be different.

What stays banned is the ad-shaped template the source did NOT use: "If you're struggling
with…", "If you're tired of…", "If you've ever…", "POV:", "Nobody talks
about…", "Here's why…", "The one thing…", "Stop scrolling if…". They signal
an ad in the first two words and none of them is how a person starts a
sentence. If the source opened on one of them, you may keep it — that is the
source's move and version 0 already carries it. Otherwise never introduce
one. The source's own format is not one of these — it is the swipe, and it is
kept. The test is the fills: if a hook's fills could be reused verbatim by a
different brand in a different category, they are generic — refill them from
the bank. **Print before writing:** what the source's opening was doing, in one
phrase, as the context you are working from.

**Match the speaker's own voice.** Sentence length, vocabulary, rhythm, and
any quirks the teardown record shows. If the speaker drops articles, the hook
drops articles. Where the source's shape leaves the choice open, a behaviour
beats a condition — a behaviour is how the avatar talks about their life, a
condition is how a brand talks about them.
Every hook must also stay inside the format's own register as the spec
records it — the body that follows is shared, so a hook whose register the
body could not follow is out of the set, however strong the line.

Write each hook in the format's own currency, per the reading: a
spoken line if the format speaks, a card if it does not, a frame if it has
neither.

## THE OPENING, READ FIRST (v23)

Before you print a format, read what the swipe's opening actually is. Look at
the first three seconds in the verbatim transcript, the frame map and the
layer sheet, and print one line:

`OPENING: spoken` — a person says real words in the first three seconds.
`OPENING: card` — no one speaks, but words are on screen in the first second.
`OPENING: wordless` — no real words are said and none are on screen. Music, a
sound effect, a sigh, a "Hmm.", laughter, or background chatter the
transcript flags as heard differently all count as no words.

Then one line on **what stops the scroll** in the source: the first picture,
the first motion, the sound, the gag. That is the thing every version has to
match in strength, whatever the opening is.

**A wordless opening is fine. It is the format, not a gap.** Do not invent a
spoken line, do not turn a sound into a sentence, and do not put a caption on
a swipe that has none unless the variation needs one to stop the scroll.
For a wordless opening:

- `FORMAT LINE: none` and `FORMAT CARD: none` (or the source's card, if it
  has one). No blanks, so no ELEMENTS lines for the line.
- `LINE V0` … `LINE V5` all print `none`. `CARD Vn` prints `none` when the
  swipe has no card, or its own words when the swipe has one.
- **The take lives in `SCROLL` and `THUMB`.** Every one of the five is a
  different first-two-seconds event: a different picture, a different first
  motion, a different thing caught on camera, or a different sound cue. Five
  scroll stoppers that are the same shot with one prop changed are one take,
  and the machine holds them.
- Build each scroll stopper from the avatar's own moments and words, the same
  way you would build a spoken line: quote the full sentence it came from.

For a spoken or card opening, everything below applies as before.

## THE FORMAT LOCK

The source's opening worked for a reason, and the reason is its shape. This
pass does not reinvent it. It finds the shape, names its blanks, and fills
them.

**1 · Find the format — from the source's own words.** Take the source's
opening line exactly as the teardown record's verbatim transcript has it (the
line the reading names). Keep every word as it is, and put a named blank in
`[CAPITALS]` over only the words that carry something the brief changes — the
place, the problem or desire, the person's own specifics (an age, a name, a
count), the product, the category. The spec's script architecture already
names these blanks for the opening beat; use its names. **Never add a blank
the spec did not name, and never rewrite a fixed word** — not even to the
spec's own paraphrase of it. **A blank covers only the words that change**: a
word every version keeps (the noun after a verb that changes, say) is fixed,
outside the blank. Print:

`FORMAT LINE: <the source's line, word for word, with its blanks>`

Do the same for the opening's hook card (the words on screen in the first
beat, lines separated by " / "), and for the first frame (the teardown's
description of it: camera, place, what is happening — the variable parts in
blanks):

`FORMAT CARD: <the source's card, word for word, with its blanks>`
`FORMAT FRAME: <the source's first frame, with its blanks>`

If the source has no card, print `FORMAT CARD: none`, and every `CARD` line
says `none`.

**2 · The control fills the blanks with the swap and nothing else.** Version
0 is the format with each blank filled by what the rewrite already put there
— the baseline injection's opening beat. That is the swipe wearing our name.

**3 · The format binds the control; the takes keep its world.** Only VERSION
0 is held to the fixed words. The five variations are five takes (section 3):
each keeps the format's WORLD — the speaker, the register, the reference, the
place the body continues in — and every specific in it comes from the
language bank, the avatar, the brand files or the cast, cited in its full
original sentence. **Across the five, no two share a take, a first-frame
event or a thumbnail.**

**4 · Every blank is a named element, and elements hold each other.** A
blank is not free-standing: it is an element of this script, and the script's
other elements depend on it. Name every element the opening touches — the ones
in its blanks AND the ones the body brings in that a blank must agree with (a
person the body puts in a place, a count the card states, a thing the body
treats). Then write, for each blank, what it must hold, **in element names
only**: `[PLACE] holds [EXPERT] — the body puts [EXPERT] with [SUBJECT] inside
it` · `[AGE] = [SUBJECT]'s age in the cast` · `[PROBLEM] = the problem the body
treats`. A fill is right only when every element it must hold is true of it:
a place where [EXPERT] could not be is wrong however good it sounds. The
source's own fill shows the kind: keep that kind, change the instance.
Print, above the variations:

`ELEMENTS:` — one line per element: `[NAME] · what it is in this script ·
what it must hold or match (other element names) · where its fill comes from
(bank · cast · brand file · research)`

These names are the injection points. Every later pass, and every future run
of this format, fills the same elements.

**5 · A blank the files cannot fill is researched, not invented.** When a
fill needs a fact that is not on file — a real place name, a real number, a
real price — write `[RESEARCH: what is needed, in a few words]` in that
blank and move on — naming what the element must hold, so the research looks
for the right kind of thing (not "a real [PLACE] nearby" but "a real [PLACE]
nearby that holds [EXPERT], as the ELEMENTS line says"). The machine researches every one of those after this pass,
from the brand's files and then the web, and writes the fact in with its
receipt. A fill you made up to avoid a research marker is the defect this
rule exists for.

**6 · The scroll stopper and the thumbnail.** The control's first frame is
the source's, held. Each take's first frame is its own event, showing what
its line says, already happening — inside the format's world (the same room,
the same people) but a different thing happening in it. Each take's thumbnail
is its own still, chosen to make that take's promise in the grid; never the
same frame as another take's.

**What this lock does not touch:** the tests below still apply to what goes
into the blanks — the rung, the stage, the story, the portrait rule at an
unaware entry. A fill that breaks one is replaced with a fill that does not;
the format is never broken to pass a test. If a test cannot be passed by any
fill, print that as a flag about the format against our market and leave
the format standing.

**Print the lines the machine reads, exactly this way, one per line and
nothing else on the line:** `FORMAT LINE:` · `FORMAT CARD:` · `FORMAT FRAME:`
in section 1; `TAKE Vn:` · `LINE Vn:` · `CARD Vn:` · `SCROLL Vn:` · `THUMB Vn:`
for V0 to V5 in sections 2 and 3. The machine checks `LINE V0` and `CARD V0`
against their `FORMAT` (every fixed word present, in order), checks every
variation printed all five slots, and holds any variation whose line, card,
scroll stopper or thumbnail reads as another version's with words swapped.

## THE JOKE LOCK

A funny opening works because of its joke, and the joke has a shape just like
the line does. Before filling any blank, read the doctrine read's `HUMOR:` line
and the record's opening beat, and print:

`THE JOKE: <none — or how it works, in one line: what is said, what is really
true, and the gap between them that makes it funny>`

Name the MECHANISM, not the words. Shapes a mechanism can take, as examples of
shape only: *an impossible, instant change stated with a straight face*;
*a comparison to a famous face nobody could literally become*; *a deadpan
understatement of an obvious disaster*; *a mock-serious warning about something
trivial*. If the HUMOR line is `none` and the opening plays straight, the
opening stays straight unless the room plants a joke. Straight is the default. A straight source stays straight unless the culture read or the culture map gives a receipt AND the piece is not a testimonial, a before/after, a founder story, a demonstration, or about pain, health or money. A straight source may take a planted joke only when it has a receipt: a meme, format or reference the culture read or the avatar's culture map shows this audience already laughs at. Name the receipt. A joke the writer invented is still a finding.
When the culture read or the culture map gives you one, print `THE JOKE:
PLANTED — <how it works, in one line> · <receipt, quoted from the culture read
or the culture map's humor section>` and every rule below applies to it as if the source had
made it, except that VERSION 0 stays the source's own straight opening. When
neither gives you one, print `THE JOKE: none` and skip this section. Nothing on
the culture map's off-limits line is ever planted.

When there is a joke:

1. **The joke is locked like a fixed word.** Every version, control included,
   carries the same mechanism. A version that loses it has broken the format,
   however true its fills are.
2. **Name the blank the joke lives in** — `JOKE BLANK: [NAME]` — the blank
   whose fill carries the exaggeration, the understatement or the reversal.
3. **Fill the joke blank with a fresh instance of the same joke — never a
   literal benefit.** An exaggeration is filled with another exaggeration of
   the same size; an understatement with another understatement. Keep: no joke on the proof or the offer. Allow: the twist beat may be the product doing its job, shown, not claimed. A plain beat with the proof always follows it.
   So the fill may be the product as the punchline — shown in the frame doing
   what it does — but never the product's result stated as a claim: stating it
   flatly turns the joke into a claim and the laugh is gone. When a fill puts
   the product in the twist, print under that version `PROOF FOLLOWS: <the
   body beat, by its label in the baseline, that proves it plainly>`; if the
   baseline has no such beat, write `PROOF FOLLOWS: [SLOT: plain proof beat
   after the hook]` so the body pass writes one. The hook's job is the laugh
   that makes the viewer watch the body to find out what is really true.
4. **The fill comes from the room, not the writer.** Take its reference world
   from the language rows (the famous faces, tropes and in-words the avatar's
   room already uses — rows tagged `taste`, `in-word`, `subculture` where they
   exist), from the doctrine read's REFERENCE WORLD line, from the culture
   read below and from the avatar's culture map (its humor section: laughs at · tropes · creators).
   Cite it like any other fill. A joke the room does not make is a joke about
   the room.
5. **The truth test still holds, one level down.** A joke is not a claim — an
   impossible exaggeration is heard as a joke, so it needs no proof. What must
   stay true is what the joke points at: the change the body shows. A joke
   fill that points at a change the body never shows is false, and is replaced.
6. **The five variations never use the same instance twice**, the way the main
   blank never takes the same fill twice.

**THE DIVERSITY TEST.** Print a table, one row per version V0–V5: TAKE · the
first five words of the LINE · the SCROLL event in a few words · the THUMB in a
few words · the bank row behind it. Then read down each column. **Any two

first-frame event, the same thumbnail, or one sentence with a word swapped —
fail**: rewrite the later one as a different take, from a different row, and
print the corrected table. The control is the format; a variation that reads
as the control with a word swapped fails too.

**THE RUNG TEST.** The reading names the entry level and quotes that level's
`must_not`. Copy that quoted must-not here, then take all six openings —
control included — one at a time, and say whether it breaks it. **A hook that
names the price, the product or the direct claim earlier than its entry level
allows is a defect, not a style choice**, and it is the failure this test
exists for: the line reads perfectly well and is simply invisible to the
reader it was written for.

Two things this test is not. It is not licence to rewrite the control — if
version 0 breaks the must-not, that is a finding about the source's own
entry level against ours, printed as a flag and left standing. And it is not
a ban on the words themselves later in the asset; it is about where the
argument is allowed to BEGIN.

Any variation that breaks its entry level's must-not is rewritten to enter a
rung lower and printed again, corrected. Print the must-not, the six
verdicts, and every rewrite.

Print all four lists. A check whose result is not written down did not happen.
