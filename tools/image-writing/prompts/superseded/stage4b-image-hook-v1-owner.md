Write the headline. Six versions — a control and five variations. **All of
them ship to test.** The human gate on this pass is approval, not selection:
nothing is picked, nothing is ranked, and the frame built after this pass
must carry any headline in the set at the same type size in the same zone.

**A hook is a line and an image concept, together.** On a static there is no
first second and no sound — the line and the picture land simultaneously, and
the picture usually lands first. The line is what she reads; the image
concept is the frozen moment that arrests the thumb before a single word is
read. Neither is optional and neither is the other's caption.

"An older woman in a rustic kitchen" is a casting note, not an image concept.
An image concept is a **moment stopped mid-action**: a hand mid-application,
an object where it should not be, a texture close enough to feel, two states
of the same skin in one frame. If the frame would read as a stock photo, it
stops nobody. Take the visual vocabulary from what the source already does
where it serves; build inside the format's world where it does not.

**The image concept must be shootable from the sentence alone.** Whoever
builds it — a photographer, a designer, a generator — gets your sentence and
nothing else. Name the subject, the action, the body part, the props in the
sharp plane, and where the eyes go.

**The spec already did the abstraction — read it, do not redo it.** The
replication spec names the headline zone, its area and its fixed bounds in
the layout skeleton; the element inventory carries the headline element's
mandate; the copy architecture names the premise and its job; the image
mandate says whether the frame demonstrates or asserts. **That is the
foundational hook, and it is read out of the spec, cited, never re-derived
from the teardown.** The teardown record is for what was in frame; the
placement plan supplies the awareness entry and the word ceiling. If the spec
is missing any of this, that is a stage-2 defect — flag it and stop rather
than deriving around it.

**The placement plan names the headline zone and its word ceiling.** Rewrite
that zone and no other, and stay inside the ceiling. Do not rewrite the
support line because it looks more like a hook. A headline three words over
ceiling is not a headline, it is a layout change.

**One brief, one discrete problem.** The placement plan and the baseline pin
the problem this frame solves. Every headline names that problem, in the
avatar's own dominant words for it — the words the research shows she uses
most, not the category's default synonym. A strong research moment that names
a *different* problem is not a headline variation; it is a different brief.
Write no headline on it — route it to the BRIEF VARIATION OPPORTUNITIES
section, one line, and move on. Headlines that shift the problem mid-set do
not widen the test; they contaminate it.

**Version 0 is the straight swipe, and it is the control.**

Take the source's headline exactly as it is and change only the words that
must change — the brand, the product, the category. Same sentence, same
rhythm, same length, same punctuation, same case. Its image concept is the
source's own photograph, held.

This is the version that already performed, wearing our name. Every variation
is a bet against it in the same test, and if none of them beat it that is a
real finding rather than a failure. **Label it clearly as the control and
never improve it.**

Then write five variations. The rest of this prompt is about those.

**The ledger is spent ground.** Before writing, read the hook ledger. A
verbatim that already carries a hook there is **spent** — you do not build
another hook on it, however strong it is, unless the ledger marks its hook
`validated`. A validated hook returns only as itself — the control of its own
next test — never re-worded. If the bank genuinely has nothing fresh left for
this format and this problem, say so plainly instead of repeating — that is a
real finding, and it routes to a research pass.

**The job of this headline is to earn the click.** The test on every line is
the same — **would she tap this?** A line that is accurate, on-brand,
well-measured and gives her no reason to move her thumb has failed.

**What earns the click depends on where the format sits, and the format tells
you.** Do not assume the answer.

- A format that opens on a problem earns the click by naming the problem
  better than she can. The product is barely in it; everything the ad has to
  say happens after the tap.
- **A format that closes earns the click by selling the product** — the offer,
  the saving, the scarcity, the guarantee, the reason to act now. On a
  bottom-funnel swipe the product, the price and the terms belong in the
  headline, and writing around them because "the page will handle it" throws
  away the exact thing that format was built to do.
- Most formats sit somewhere between, and the source's own copy architecture is
  the evidence. Read what the source put in this zone and what job the spec
  says that zone does. If the source's headline carried a discount, ours
  carries a discount. If it carried a symptom, ours carries a symptom.

**Match the register to the rows you were given.** The language layer tags
every row with where in the funnel it was said. Problem language from a
prospect is the raw material for a top-of-funnel hook; a buying criterion or a
why-bought from a customer is the raw material for a closing one. Building a
closing headline out of prospect problem-language, or an opening one out of
purchase reasons, is using the right words in the wrong place.

**The rows you are given are real sentences real people said.** They arrive
queried from the language layer for this stage, each carrying who said it, what
kind of thing it is, and where it came from. Use their words; do not paraphrase
them into ours. **A refunding customer and a paid panellist are not equal
evidence** — the provenance on a row is part of the row, and a claim built on a
weak source is a weak claim.

**The format's copy architecture is the mould.** The replication spec records
this zone's sentence shape as a template — the source's own structure with its
specifics lifted out. Every version fills that same mould. A headline that
abandons the shape is not a variation of this format; it is a different ad.

**Six headlines, and all six are different from each other.** Not six
rewordings of one idea. Before you offer the set, read it back: if two of them
could be swapped without a reader noticing, one of them is wasted and gets
replaced.

**Every variation is priced in characters against the measure, and one that
does not fit is not a variation.**

The layout system measured this zone: so many characters per line, so many
lines. That is the frame the words have to live in. State, for every variation:

- **each line's character count**, counted, including spaces and punctuation;
- **the measure it is being checked against**, carried from the budget;
- **fits** or **OVER by N** — and a variation marked OVER is rewritten before
  it is offered, not offered with a note.

Do not report word counts. A word count cannot be checked against a layout.
Measured cause, 2026-08-26: five headlines were offered at "9 words" each; two
of them overran the measured 30-character line, one by five characters, and the
overrun only surfaced when the compositor shrank the type to fit. The words were
good. The frame was never told.

**Keep the source's line breaks.** Where the source headline breaks across two
lines, every variation breaks in the same place, and each line is counted
separately against the measure. A variation that re-breaks the same words
somewhere else has changed the layout, not the copy.

Five rules govern what replaces the source's words:

**Every headline traces to a verbatim.** Pull the moment from the language
bank and the avatar — a behaviour, a time of day, an object, a thing she does
with her hands. The strongest headlines name a private moment she thought
nobody had noticed. A headline that cannot cite its source is invented and
gets cut, however well it reads.

**Finish a sentence she has already started.** The bank holds half-phrases
she uses about herself. A line lands when it completes one she recognises
rather than introducing a new one.

**The line and the image concept must be about the same thing.** A strong
line over an unrelated picture kills the ad on contact — the words say one
thing, the picture says another, and she resolves it by scrolling. The
picture shows the thing the line names, already happening.

**Five variations means five different sentences.** The test is exact: **if
any two share their first four words, they are the same headline and you have
written one and padded it.** This applies to the control too. Same function,
five genuinely different ways of performing it.

**Never open with a template.** These are banned outright, in any wording:
"If you're struggling with…", "If you're tired of…", "If you've ever…",
"POV:", "Nobody talks about…", "Here's why…", "The one thing…", "Stop
scrolling if…", "This is your sign…". They signal an ad in the first two
words. If a headline can be reused verbatim by a different brand in a
different category, it is a template.

**Prefer a behaviour over a condition.** A behaviour is how she talks about
her life. A condition is how a brand talks about her. Every headline must
also stay inside the format's own register as the spec records it — the frame
is shared, so a headline whose register the layout could not carry is out of
the set, however strong the line.
