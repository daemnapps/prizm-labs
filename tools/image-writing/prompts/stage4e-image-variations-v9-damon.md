## Each variation is ONE change off the approved picture

The approved picture is a print of a 3D set: the swipe's scene built at
true sizes, with its camera and light, printed into a photo, the words set
on top. The machine that makes the five sends the approved picture itself
as the reference and asks for one change to it. Everything you do not name
stays exactly as it is: the person, the pose, the crop, the light, the set,
the product, the type. So write each picture change as a change to the set:
the object, its real size in centimetres, where it sits, what it is made
of. Written that way it can be made as an edit today and built into the set
and printed again, which keeps sizes, contact and light true. Never ask for
a size or a light as an effect ("bigger", "more dramatic light"). Our
product is never redrawn: more of it means more of its real build. So:

- **One idea per variation.** A new headline, and the one picture change that
  makes that headline true, is one idea. A new headline and an unrelated new
  setting is two, and it is not allowed.
- **Small and visible.** The change has to be something a viewer sees at a
  glance (where something sits, how many, what the person holds, what the
  person wears, which prop of the concept's world stands where), not a grade,
  a lens or a crop. Those are the same picture. On an offer or seasonal ad
  the moment's palette on the set's surfaces is visible and counts.
- **The format does not move.** Every change sits inside the spec's
  swappable list. Nothing the spec marks load-bearing changes.
- **Defensible.** Every headline is a variation of the approved headline
  (below), built from the headline set and the language bank, and every
  picture change comes from something in the files above: a worry the language
  bank ranks, a detail the brand step already put in the picture, a use the
  product file supports. Nothing nobody's words justify.

## How to build the five

## The joke stays

When the culture read says FUNNY, or THE JOKE IN THE WORDS names a double meaning, the joke is the ad. Every variation
keeps it: the load-bearing word or phrase (its KEEP lines) stays in every headline, and each variation works a different
angle of that same joke: another reading the room would make, or one of its SAME-KIND JOKES pointed at our problem.
A variation that swaps the joke word for a plain description of the problem has killed the ad. Never explain the joke.

## The concept stays (stage 3 v13, the owner 2026-10-28)

The approved picture carries one concept, named in the brand step's THE CONCEPT block: a world from the avatar's
culture map crossed with the moment, dressing the swipe. All five stay inside that one world. A picture change
takes another prop or surface from the same concept's world, at its true size, in the place of the thing it
replaces; a headline is that concept talking, with one spice device (cite its row on the LINE). A variation that
brings in a second concept is two ideas and is not allowed. The moment's name printed on a prop in the set (the
sale flag, a shop sign) stays exactly as it is in every variation: it is never a headline, and no variation adds
words to a prop. When the brand step has no THE CONCEPT block (a run made before stage 3 v13), this section does
not apply.

## WHEN THE APPROVED AD IS A SALE, EVERY VARIATION IS THE SALE (the owner, 2026-10-28)

The headline stage holds this rule; the variations hold it too. the owner, 2026-10-28, on an offer ad that came back
about the problem: *"it doesn't make sense, it has to do with a warehouse sale, not blaming the razor."*

When the approved ad is an offer ad (a sale, a clearance, a deal, a price, a "we made too many", a seasonal event),
the sale IS the idea of all five. Each variation keeps the approved words' shape and changes ONE thing about the
SALE: the sale's reason in the swipe's own premise, the event's name, the product's name, the offer line as the run's
order states it, the deadline, or the call to action. Never put the problem's name where the sale's word stands, never
turn an offer ad into a problem ad, and never write a price, a code or an offer the run's order does not state word for
word. The problem-aware rule below does NOT apply to an offer ad. A picture change on an offer ad changes something the
sale is about (how much product, how it is shown, the moment's props), never the problem on skin, unless the approved
picture itself shows skin.

**The approved picture is what was made.** The baseline injection you are handed may open with THE APPROVED PICTURE:
its swaps and words win over the injection's first idea wherever they differ. Every `swap` row you write replaces a
row of the swap list you are handed, by its exact `from`, and describes the approved picture's own things.

## The headlines: the approved headline's shape, problem-aware, read cold

the owner, 2026-10-27, on a batch whose five headlines did not follow the approved
one: *"some of these sentences don't follow the structure of the initial
headline"* and *"we can use a fragment of one but it still needs to read
properly. like 8 years of what??? covering up what? 12 steps of what? dark
spots? brown spots, my spots. these are clear. this is a problem aware
creative, headlines need to be problem aware."*

The five are variations of the APPROVED headline above, the words actually on
the approved picture, not of the swipe's line and not of the headline set's
control. So every variation headline:

1. **The headline is a format, and you only fill its blanks.** This is the
   video hook step's rule (the owner, 2026-09-25: *"There's a reason the hook
   works, so we don't want to reinvent it. All we want to do is adjust its
   variables a bit … all other variations of this headline need to follow that
   format, and we just inject words from our language bank"*; 2026-10-27:
   *"look at the headline process for video because that's pretty dialed in,
   we need to leverage the same process"*). Print the approved headline as a
   FORMAT: its own words, with the slots replaced by named blanks in square
   brackets (`[PROBLEM]`, `[PAIR]`). Every word outside a blank is a fixed word
   and stays, in order, in every variation. Give each blank an ELEMENTS line:
   what it must hold, in element names only. Each variation fills the blanks,
   and only the blanks, from the avatar's own words: a row of the language bank or the
   spice (cite its id), cut to a fragment if you need to. A blank that needs a
   fact nobody's words give is `[RESEARCH: what to find]`, filled with the
   source it came from. Creativity goes into what fills the blanks, never into
   the fixed words.
2. **Names the problem, every time.** This ad is problem-aware: the reader
   knows the problem and the headline says it in the avatar's own word for it, the way
   the language bank and the avatar say it (`DARK SPOTS`, `BROWN SPOTS`,
   `MY SPOTS`). A slot that does not name the problem never stands where the
   problem goes: `8 YEARS?` fails, `8 YEARS OF DARK SPOTS?` passes; `12 STEPS?`
   fails, `12 STEPS FOR 3 SPOTS?` passes; `COVERED UP?` fails.
3. **Reads properly, cold.** Material comes from the headline set and the
   language bank, and a customer's words may be cut to a fragment, but the
   whole headline reads as one sentence a person would say, and a stranger who
   has never seen the ad knows what it is about on one read. A fragment that
   only makes sense to someone who read the original post (`DOING JULY
   AGAIN`) fails. Never keep a word from a quote you are not using.

Before you write each row, check its headline against those three and fix it
until it passes all three. Then each one is a row:

- **words**: every caption zone on the approved picture, in the order the
  brand step lists them, one string per zone, with ` / ` where the line
  breaks on the ad. Only the headline's zone takes the new headline; every
  other zone is copied word for word from the brand step. Written exactly as
  it will be set: the same case, no long dashes.
- **picture**: the one change to the picture that makes this headline true,
  in one plain sentence, as it will read after "THE ONE CHANGE to the
  approved picture:". Say what is different, not what stays. Leave it out
  only when the new words are true of the approved picture as it is; that row
  costs nothing, because the approved picture just gets the new words.

A row changes the person only when its headline cannot be true of the person
in the approved picture. Then it also carries:

- **people**: `made-up` (a new person, not a banked cast member) or `cast`.
- **person**: `who`, `age`, `look`, `wardrobe`, each in plain words, the way
  a casting note says it. The wardrobe stays the approved picture's unless
  the headline is about it.
- **swap**: one row, `{"from": ..., "to": ..., "kind": "drawn"}` (or `cast`
  with the cast member's name), whose `from` is copied character for
  character from the swap list above, so it replaces that row.

Use `stays_drop` only when the change undoes a line the swap list keeps
(copy that line exactly). Use no other keys.

**ids** are `v1` to `v5`. **label** is `Headline <n> · <three to five words
saying what it is>`.
