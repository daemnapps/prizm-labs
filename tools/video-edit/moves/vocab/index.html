Cuts

How one shot hands over to the next, with no effect in between.

AI SLOP · 0:27.9 — one shot straight into the next

Hard cut

One shot stops and the next starts instantly, with nothing in between.

you'd say“Hard cut from the falls straight into the next shot, no fade.”

AI SLOP · 0:32.7 — stone him into stone him

Match cut — shape

The cut lands so a shape in one shot sits in the exact same spot as a similar shape in the next.

you'd say“Match cut the stone him into the stone him — same size, same spot.”

AI SLOP · 0:24.7 — robe into silk shirt, same move

Match cut — motion

A movement that starts in one shot carries on in the same direction and speed in the next.

you'd say“Swap the outfit mid-move so the gesture carries straight through.”

AI SLOP · 0:06.8 — purple iris into purple galaxy

Match cut — colour

The last colour of one shot is the first colour of the next, so the eye doesn't feel the jump.

you'd say“Colour-match out of the purple iris into the purple galaxy.”

AI SLOP · 0:35.1 — skips ahead on the kick

Jump cut

Cutting inside the same shot so it skips ahead (or back) in time, and the picture visibly jumps.

you'd say“Jump-cut him on the kick — skip a beat of the shot.”

AI SLOP · 0:12.9 — calm river into the tongue

Smash cut

A sudden cut from one mood to the opposite — calm to loud, or loud to silent — with no warning.

you'd say“Smash cut from the quiet river to his face pulling a tongue at the lens.”

DEMO, NOT IN THE VIDEO · the court's sound starts before the picture cuts

J-cut

You hear the next shot before you see it: its sound starts early, under the current picture.

you'd say“J-cut it — let the crowd noise come in before we cut to the court.”

DEMO, NOT IN THE VIDEO · the falls' sound runs on over the court

L-cut

The sound of a shot keeps going after the picture has already cut away to the next one.

you'd say“L-cut the falls — keep their sound running over the court.”

DEMO, NOT IN THE VIDEO · main shot, a close shot of his hands, back

Cutaway

A short shot of something else (what he's looking at, his hands, the place) dropped in, before returning to the main shot.

you'd say“Give me a cutaway to his hands, then back to the wide.”

AI SLOP · 0:10.1–12.9 — the city burst

Insert / B-roll burst

Short extra shots dropped in to add texture — details, places, objects — often a fast run of them.

you'd say“Hit me with a B-roll burst — quick shots of the city, one per beat.”

AI SLOP · 0:19.8 / 0:20.6 — cuts on the kick

Cut on the beat

Every cut lands exactly on a beat in the music, so you feel the edit in your body.

you'd say“Cut on the kick — every change lands on the beat.”

AI SLOP · 0:19.0 — 4 frames of a persona (a picture, not white)

Flash frame

A single frame of white (or colour) between two shots — a blink of light on a hit.

you'd say“Flash the president in for four frames on the kick.”

Transitions

Moves that carry you from one shot or place into another.

DEMO, NOT IN THE VIDEO · the monument whips to the falls

Whip pan

The camera swings sideways so fast it blurs, and comes out of the blur somewhere new.

you'd say“Whip pan off the monument and land on the falls.”

AI SLOP · 0:03.6 — into one point, out of his eye

Zoom transition

The camera zooms into or out of something (a screen, an eye) and that carries you into the next picture.

you'd say“Suck everything into one point and zoom out of his eye.”

AI SLOP · 0:16.6 — lands close, then settles

Punch-in

The frame jumps a little closer in one hard step, usually on a beat, then settles.

you'd say“Land every cut punched in, then let it settle.”

AI SLOP · 0:18.1 — the helmet fills the lens

Object wipe

Something passes across the lens and fills the frame, and when it clears we're in a new shot.

you'd say“Fly the helmet into the lens and wipe us onto the monument.”

AI SLOP · 0:08.5 — the koi, space into the pond

Object hand-off

An object leaves one shot through the edge of the frame and arrives in the next shot, so it carries you across.

you'd say“Hand the koi off — out of space and into the pond.”

AI SLOP · 0:33.3 — stone becomes him

Morph

One shape or face melts smoothly into another, with no cut.

you'd say“Morph the stone statue into him, flesh coming through the stone.”

AI SLOP · 0:06.0 — space in the iris

Iris transition

The picture opens or closes through a circle — here, the eye's iris opens into another world.

you'd say“Push into his eye and open the iris into space.”

AI SLOP · 0:41.6 — the screen dissolves into frame one

Dissolve

One shot fades out while the next fades in on top of it, so for a moment you see both.

you'd say“Dissolve the phone screen into the opening frame.”

AI SLOP · 0:06.0–6.8 — speeds up into the cut

Speed-ramp transition

The shot speeds up hard into the cut, and the next shot comes in fast and slows down.

you'd say“Ramp into the cut and ease out the other side.”

DEMO, NOT IN THE VIDEO · the cut hides in the black of his sunglasses

Invisible cut

The cut is hidden in a moment where the frame goes fully dark or blocked, so it looks like one continuous shot.

you'd say“Hide the cut in the black of his sunglasses so it feels like one take.”

Time

Playing with how fast, which way and how often time runs.

AI SLOP · 0:03.6–6.8 — Goja, −3.2× to 5.1×

Speed ramp

The speed changes inside one shot: fast, then suddenly slow on the key moment, then fast again.

you'd say“Ramp the Goja shot — backwards, forwards, stop, go.”

DEMO, NOT IN THE VIDEO · the forehand at real speed, then ¼ speed

Slow motion

Time runs slower than real life so you can see every detail of a moment.

you'd say“Put the forehand in slow-mo so you see the ball leave the racket.”

AI SLOP · 0:29.5–32.7 — bullet time

Time freeze / bullet time

Time almost stops, and the camera keeps moving around the frozen moment.

you'd say“Freeze him in stone and let the camera drift round him — bullet time.”

AI SLOP · 0:26.3–29.5 — played backwards

Reverse

The footage plays backwards.

you'd say“Reverse the falls so the water climbs back up.”

AI SLOP · 0:42.3 → 0:00 — the seam

Seamless loop

The last frame hands straight back into the first, so it plays forever with no visible seam.

you'd say“Make the end hand back into the start so it loops forever.”

DEMO, NOT IN THE VIDEO · the same three frames, four times over

Stutter

The same few frames repeat quickly, so the picture looks like it's skipping or stamping.

you'd say“Stutter three frames of him on the kick.”

DEMO, NOT IN THE VIDEO · fading copies of him trail behind

Echo / trails

A moving thing leaves fading copies of itself behind it.

you'd say“Give him an echo trail as he dances.”

Motion

How things move inside the frame: the feel of the movement itself.

AI SLOP · 0:18.2–19.7 — the temple, lens to lens

Keyframe

A point where you pin down where something is at a certain moment; the computer fills in the movement between the pins.

you'd say“Pin the temple at the lens at both ends and let it travel between.”

DEMO, NOT IN THE VIDEO · the headphones at one speed: evenly spaced dots

Linear

Movement at one constant speed from start to stop — feels robotic.

you'd say“It feels linear — give it some ease.”

AI SLOP · 0:29.3 — the bag speeds up and leaves

Ease-in

Starts slow and speeds up — like something being pulled away.

you'd say“Ease-in so the bag accelerates out at the lens.”

AI SLOP · 0:23.0 — the basket slams in and settles

Ease-out

Starts fast and slows down to a stop — like something landing.

you'd say“Ease-out as the basket lands in the shot.”

DEMO, NOT IN THE VIDEO · the headphones ease in and out: dots bunch at the ends

Ease-in-out

Starts slow, speeds up in the middle, slows down at the end — the most natural feel.

you'd say“Ease-in-out on the headphones so they don't jerk.”

AI SLOP · 0:21.5 — the racket winds back

Anticipation

A small move in the opposite direction just before the main move, like winding up before a throw.

you'd say“Give it anticipation — the racket drops back before it swings.”

DEMO, NOT IN THE VIDEO · the headphones pop past full size, then settle

Overshoot

Something goes a bit past where it should stop, then settles back — it feels bouncy and alive.

you'd say“Pop the headphones in with a little overshoot.”

DEMO, NOT IN THE VIDEO · the headphones stop; the swing carries on

Follow-through

When the main thing stops, the loose parts (hair, a strap, a tail) keep moving for a moment.

you'd say“Let the headphones keep swinging after they stop.”

AI SLOP · 0:11.7 / 12.4 — the disco ball smears

Motion blur

Fast movement smears into a streak, the way a real camera sees speed.

you'd say“More smear on the disco ball so it feels fast.”

AI SLOP · 0:30.2 — railing vs the falls

Parallax

Near things slide past faster than far things, which makes a flat picture feel deep.

you'd say“As the camera drifts, the railing should slide faster than the falls.”

DEMO, NOT IN THE VIDEO · the frame jolts on the hit

Camera shake

The picture jolts as if the camera took the hit.

you'd say“Shake the camera on the hit.”

AI SLOP · 0:35.9 — the objects circle him

Orbit

The camera circles around the subject while he stays in the middle.

you'd say“Orbit him while the objects float.”

Tracking & compositing

Putting things into footage so they look like they were always there.

AI SLOP · 0:13.3 — the headphones ride his selfie

Motion tracking / match-move

The software follows a point in real footage so a 3D object can be stuck to it and move with it.

you'd say“Track the shot so the headphones ride his selfie like they're really there.”

DEMO, NOT IN THE VIDEO · the footage, his matte, him on a new background

Rotoscoping / matte

Cutting a person or object out of the footage, frame by frame; the cut-out shape is called the matte.

you'd say“Roto him out so the objects can go behind him.”

DEMO, NOT IN THE VIDEO · him on green, then the green keyed out

Keying / chroma key

Removing one flat colour (usually green) from the picture so you can put anything behind.

you'd say“Put him on green and key it out later.”

AI SLOP · 0:18.3 — before the layers (left), finished (right)

Compositing / layering

Stacking separate layers — footage, 3D objects, text — into one finished picture.

you'd say“Composite the 3D layer over the footage.”

DEMO, NOT IN THE VIDEO · the koi passes behind him

Occlusion

When something closer blocks something further away — getting it right is what makes a 3D object feel really there.

you'd say“The koi should pass behind him, not over him — fix the occlusion.”

DEMO, NOT IN THE VIDEO · before and graded, wiping across

Colour grade / colour match

Adjusting colour and contrast for a mood, or so different shots and layers look like they belong together.

you'd say“Grade it warmer, with more punch.”

AI SLOP · 0:39.1 — only the carousel is sharp

Depth of field / rack focus

How much of the picture is sharp; a rack focus moves the sharp spot from one thing to another.

you'd say“Shallow focus — only the carousel sharp, the lights behind melting.”

Sound-led

Picture decisions made by the music.

AI SLOP · 0:23.0 — ball at the lens on the bar

Hit point

The exact moment in the music a picture event must land on — a hit, a drop, a snare.

you'd say“The ball has to reach the lens right on the hit point.”

AI SLOP · 0:26.3 — new bar, new shot

Downbeat

The first and strongest beat of each bar — the "ONE" in 1-2-3-4.

you'd say“Land the big change on the downbeat, not the 3.”

AI SLOP · 0:15.1 / 15.5 / 15.7 — on the kicks

Sync

Picture and sound lined up so they happen at exactly the same moment.

you'd say“Every skip and every flash lands on a kick — keep it in sync.”

DEMO, NOT IN THE VIDEO · the words on screen carry it with no sound

Sound-off design

Making the video work even with the sound off — captions, clear visuals, the story told in pictures.

you'd say“It has to read sound-off — put the words on screen.”

Camera

Where the camera is and how it travels.

AI SLOP · 0:40.1 — the 3D camera travels back

Dolly in / dolly out

The camera physically moves closer or further — near things grow faster than far ones, unlike a zoom.

you'd say“Dolly back off the carousel, don't zoom — let the table open up.”

AI SLOP · 0:05.6 — into the eye

Push-in

A slow, steady move toward the subject that builds tension or focus.

you'd say“Slow push-in on his eye.”

AI SLOP · 0:39.1–41.6 — it was a toy

Pull-back reveal

The camera pulls back to show something we couldn't see — often that the world was smaller or different than we thought.

you'd say“Pull back to reveal the whole thing was a toy carousel.”

AI SLOP · 0:16.6 — riding pillion

POV

Point of view: the camera is someone's eyes, so we see what he sees.

you'd say“Give me a POV off the back of the moto — road rushing at us.”

AI SLOP · 0:23.0 — the footbridge

Locked-off

The camera doesn't move at all — only the subject and the world move inside the frame.

you'd say“Locked-off on the footbridge — only he changes.”
