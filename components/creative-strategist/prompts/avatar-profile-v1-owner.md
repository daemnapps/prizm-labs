# Avatar profile — step 2 of the creative strategist (v1)

Turns the rows the research gatherer filed into the profile a brief is written
from. Brand-agnostic: every `{slot}` is filled from the brand folder at run time.

---

DO NOT INVENT. Every line you write about this person comes from a row below,
quoted exactly, with its link. If the rows don't show something, write
`NOT IN THE ROWS` under that heading. A short honest profile beats a full
made-up one. Never use an example from another brand, product or avatar.

You are writing the profile of one sub-avatar: **{sub_name}**, inside the core
avatar **{avatar_name}**, for the brand **{brand_name}**, which sells
**{product_name}** for **{problem}**.

What we already know about the core avatar (do not repeat it, only say where
this sub-avatar is different):

{core_profile}

The sub-avatar card as it stands:

{sub_card}

The rows (each one is a real post or comment, with its room and link):

{rows}

Write these sections, in this order. Quotes are copied exactly, with the link
after each one.

## 1. Who she is
Age (only ages the rows state, with the count), where the problem shows up,
how long she's had it. One short paragraph, then the receipts.

## 2. How she says it
The 10 phrases she uses for the problem, ranked by how many rows use them,
with the count. Her words, not the category's words.

## 3. What she has tried, and what it cost her
Every product, treatment and routine she names, with how many rows name it,
and any money, time or pain she attaches to it.

## 4. What she believes
Things she takes as true about the problem and its fixes (right or wrong).
These are what an ad opens on.

## 5. Objections
What stops her buying, in her words, ranked by count. Mark each one: does the
product answer it (yes / no / not in the rows).

## 6. The transformation she wants
What life looks like when it's fixed, in her words. Settings, moments, who
notices.

## 7. Affinity groups
- Adjacent categories she buys in (count each)
- Brands and products she names (count each)
- Where she buys (stores, channels, professionals)

## 8. What this means for ads
Three to five plain lines: the opening she'd stop for, the objection to answer
first, the proof she'd believe. Each line points to the rows it rests on.

## 9. Gaps
What the rows don't cover that a brief will need, and where to pull it from
next (reviews, comment sections, a named room).
