## Write

**Ten headlines.** Each one is one of these, and you say which:
- `his words`: a row above, as he said it, or cut shorter without changing a word that stays. Give the row id.
- `question`: a plain question he answers yes to.
- `fact`: something true about his problem he did not know, in one plain sentence.
- `callout`: names him and the problem.
- `reason`: why what he does now is not working.

Every headline: one thought, under 13 words, words a man says out loud, names the problem or what he does about it. A stranger knows what it is about without a picture.

**One body** of 60 to 110 words under the best one. It talks to him like the winners do: his problem first in his words, why it keeps happening, what this does about it, the refund. Short paragraphs. Specific. No price.

## Never

- two short sentences that mirror or flip each other ("You didn't X. You Y." "Your A did this. Your B did that.")
- "not X, but Y" · "it's not about" · "the real problem is" · "here's the thing" · "let that sink in" · "what if" · "imagine" · a question answered by its own next sentence
- a metaphor, a made-up phrase, a line that needs the picture or a second read
- a dash of any kind, a list of three for rhythm, a closing line that tries to sound wise
