# Creative controls: the real names (practitioner and craft side)

Scope: practitioner, craft and advertising-research vocabularies for ad "dials" in copy, video and static images. The psychology side (valence/arousal, discrete emotions, incongruity, benign violation, vividness, persuasion knowledge) is deliberately not covered here.

Confidence tags used in every table:
- **[V]** read in a page or paper text I actually opened this session.
- **[S]** seen only in a search-result summary or a secondary source (blog, vendor, student paper). Real term, wording of the definition not checked against the original.
- **[M]** from my own background knowledge, not retrieved. Treat as a lead to verify, not a citation.

Medium column: **C** = copy, **V** = video, **I** = static image.

---

## A. Master glossary

### A1. Goldenberg, Mazursky and Solomon: "creativity templates" (ad-creativity research, 1999)

The 1999 paper (Marketing Science 18(3), 333-351) found that 6 templates with 16 versions classify 89% of 200 award-winning print ads, and that people trained in the templates make ads rated as more creative. Full paper text read. The list below is the real list; the six-name list some summaries give (Extreme Consequences, Inversion, Absurd Alternative, Extreme Effort...) is a later book's re-cut, see the last row of this block.

Paper: https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/3384/fundamental.pdf ; abstract: https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ormksc/v18y1999i3p333-351.html

|---|------|------------------|--------|-----|------|
| 1 | Creativity template | An abstract, repeatable pattern that successful ads share; usable as an ideation recipe | ad research | C V I | V |
| 2 | **Pictorial Analogy** (template I) | Bring a symbol that carries the message into the product's space | ad research | I V | V |
| 3 | **Replacement** (version of Pictorial Analogy) | The product, or one of its traits, replaces a symbol that already carries the message | ad research | I V | V |
| 4 | Extreme analogy (version) | Same as above but the symbol is pushed to an extreme instead of just transplanted | ad research | I V | V |

### B3. Controls the owner has not named yet (highest-value additions)

1. **Relevance link (D5).** The research says creativity needs divergence AND relevance. The absurdity, specificity and valence dials have no control that stops weird from drifting away from the product. This is the safety rail.
2. **Inference load (D3) and anchorage.** How much the viewer must work out, and whether words steer the reading. Picture-swap difficulty lives here.
3. **Polish vs native (D15).** Biggest practitioner dial in paid social.
4. **Hook / pattern interrupt (D25)** with its pair, **hold**, i.e. what keeps people after the hook.
5. **Delivery axes (D8 to D11)**: dry-to-enthusiastic, formal, irreverent. Tone is four axes in the best-known framework, not one "humor" knob.
6. **Irony depth (D12)** and **cringe (D13)**: separate from funny; they are how internet-native humor works.
7. **Status (D14)**: who is higher up in the scene, speaker vs audience.
8. **Pacing (D16), shot scale (D17), camera instability (D18), eye contact (D19)**: all video dials with names in film language.
9. **Visual density (D22), scale distortion (D23), uncanniness (D21), degradation (D24)**: image dials.
10. **Borrowedness (D26)**: how recognizable the borrowed source is; research says metaphor/pun effects fade when viewers lack the cultural knowledge.

Fix to consider on the owner's own words: "absurdity" and "specificity" are real; "emotion/valence/intensity" belong to the psychology agent; "dramatisation" is closest to executional frameworks (lecture vs drama, textbook, unverified) and to Goldenberg's "consequences" and "extreme situation" templates.

## C. The picture swap: established names

**The operation.** Keep a source picture; replace its meaning-carrying objects with our own objects chosen because they look like what they replace.

| Name | Who | What it covers | Fit to picture swap | Source | Conf |
|------|-----|----------------|---------------------|--------|------|
| **Replacement version of the Pictorial Analogy template** | Goldenberg, Mazursky, Solomon (1999) | The product or one of its traits replaces a symbol that already carries the message | Closest textbook name for the whole operation. Extreme analogy version = push the swapped symbol to an extreme | https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/3384/fundamental.pdf | V |
| **Replacement (visual structure)** | Phillips and McQuarrie (2004) | One thing absent; viewer infers it from the stand-in | The stand-in carries the idea; paired with connection or comparison as the meaning operation | https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1131387/full | V |
| Replacement subtypes: **source without target context / source in target context / target in source context** | van Enschot et al. (2022) | Where the borrowed object and the product sit relative to each other's setting | My reading (inference, not from the paper): keeping THEIR scene and dropping OUR product into it is "target in source context"; our object placed in our own scene is "source in target context"; our object alone is "source without target context". Third was rated most fluent and pleasing | https://research.tilburguniversity.edu/en/publications/refining-replacements-validating-a-revised-typology-of-visual-met/ | V (subtypes); inference flagged |
| **Contextual metaphor** | Forceville (2008) | Target placed where the scene forces you to think of the absent source | Same family: the scene does the work, the swapped object is the target | https://pure.uva.nl/ws/files/4111507/74907_Forceville_2008_in_PhillipsMcQuarrie_DARE_version_2_.pdf | V |

| **Visual rhyme** | McQuarrie and Mick (1999) | Two different things pictured with similar form | This is exactly "by visual rhyme": the swapped object must share shape/pose/colour with what it replaces. **Swap sub-controls that fall out of the research**
- **Rhyme fidelity**: how closely shape, pose, colour and framing of our object match what it replaced (visual rhyme, graphic match).
- **Swap scope**: one object (contextual/substitution) vs several vs everything (becomes pastiche or parody).
- **Which object holds the meaning**: the "symbol" (Goldenberg), the "source" (Forceville and van Enschot).
- **Anchorage**: whether words name the link (Barthes) or the swap must be solved unaided (inference load, D3).
- **Recognizability of the source picture**: effects of tropes faded for viewers lacking the cultural knowledge (McQuarrie and Mick 1999). Source familiarity is a dial, with risk at both ends.
- **Do not deform the product**: hybrid and integrated treatments can make a product look damaged (Forceville).
