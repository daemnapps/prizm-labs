## THE MONTH HAS TO SELL — this is a floor, not a ceiling

RULED 2026-09-14 (the owner: *"every single month for every single brand, we should
have 20% ask, 80% the rest. Really, every week should have an offer. I don't see
a reason to not have offers every single week."*)

- **About one send in five is an ask.** Count them: a send whose type sits in
  the `ask` well. Four of twenty, three of sixteen. Under that and the month
  does not pay for itself; far over it and the list burns.
- **Every week carries at least one offer.** Not one a month, not one when a
  holiday happens to fall — every week. A week with nothing to buy is a week
  the brand chose not to earn.
- **The 20% is the ASK ITSELF.** Its warm-up and its cool-down are not asks,
  so a full arc puts one send in the 20% and two in the 80%. Building the arc
  does not use up the budget.
- **A holiday is not the only reason to sell.** Before this rule the only asks
  in a month were the ones a holiday anchored, so a month with no holiday in
  it sold nothing. A cold ask-type, a product nobody has been told about, a
  bundle that has never run — each is reason enough.

**AN OFFER IS NOT A DISCOUNT** (the owner, same ruling: *"it's not even that these
offers are discounts or anything like that. They're just literally being more
straightforward so we make money."*). An offer is the thing the brand sells,
named plainly, with a price and a reason to buy it now. The bank is a list of
products at their real prices — most of them carry no discount at all, and
carrying one is the exception a human rules, never something you reach for
because a send is an ask.

So an ask is not a sale. It is a send that stops hinting: this is the product,
this is what it costs, here is why it is for you. A month of five gentle
educational sends and one coupon is worse than a month that simply says what it
sells, and the second one is what this rule is asking for.

## EVERY SEND MAKES ITS OWN CASE

RULED 2026-09-14. The cells stage handed you a STANDING angle per avatar — the
problem that person is living with all month. That is a starting point, not an
answer. Until this rule, every send to a cell inherited that one line, so a
month of 35 emails carried 13 arguments and four of them were the same email
written four times.

**So every move you propose carries its own `variants`, and each one names the
angle THIS send makes.** Same avatar, same underlying problem, different case:

- the standing angle — *"they already tried the shelf and it came back anyway"*
- a `mechanism` send's angle — *"the reason it came back is the step they
  stopped doing, not the product they stopped using"*
- a `proof-drop` send's angle — *"the people it worked for did the boring thing
  for six weeks; here is what that looked like"*
- a `bundle` send's angle — *"the two they are missing are the two that make
  the one they have work"*

One problem, four arguments, four emails worth opening.

**The rules on angles:**

- **No two sends carry the same angle.** Not to the same segment, not to a
  different one. If you cannot make a different case, you do not need another
  send — propose something else or leave the day empty.
- **An angle names the case, not the topic.** "Back to school" is a topic.
  "The marks he sees in every photo are the ones that were there in June" is an
  angle.
- **It has to fit the type.** A `guarantee` send and a `myth-correction` send
  cannot argue the same way; the type says what job the send is doing.
- **It stays inside the avatar's world** — the standing angle tells you what
  that world is, and the language bank is where its words come from.
- **A dated angle sits inside its moment's window**, exactly as the cells stage
  was told. The code drops a stale one and records the break.

For every send you propose, name a **preferred date** inside the month. Code
resolves collisions — it will not put two sends to one segment on the same
day, and it keeps the anchored days clear for their arcs.

**The rules that bind every move:**

- **Dates are real.** A moment may only be used inside its window as printed
  in the skeleton. A moment not in the skeleton is not in this month — using
  it is a rule break, not a creative choice.
- **Every ask carrying an offer arrives as a full arc** — warm-up, ask,
  cool-down — to the same segment (RULED 2026-08-31). Code completes any arc
  you leave half-built, so err toward naming the moment.
- **The type comes from the catalogue** — never invented, never forced. A
  cold or never-sent type serving the need beats the habitual one; cite the
  coldness line when that is the reason.
- **Offers come from the bank, product keys from the products list** — a
  key, or the exact word `none`. Never a sentence.
- **Every why cites** — a finding id, a moment key from the skeleton, or a
  coldness line. An uncited why is a hunch and the checker will flag it.
- There is **no send budget and no cap** (RULED 2026-09-01). Volume is the
  result of what is happening this month, not a constraint on it.
- Leave at least two slots `HELD OPEN — live` across the month for what
  cannot be planned.
- **Never invent.** Nothing enters that is not in the material above. A move
  that needs something missing is marked `[UNFILLED: what it needed]`.
- **Every move names its technique.** From the seven above — the one doing
  the actual persuasive work of this send, not a label chosen to fill the
  field. A `guarantee` send is usually redefinition or intensification's
  guarantee-as-summary; a `myth-correction` send is usually redefinition; say
  which and why in `why` when it is not obvious from the type.
