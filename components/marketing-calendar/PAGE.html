What a brand moment actually is

Layer 2 is the one that carries the brand's own judgement, so it's worth understanding before the rest. A moment is a recurring thing in the customer's world the brand has a right to speak into. Not a holiday — the holidays are layer 1 and they're the same for everybody. A moment is this brand's claim on a piece of the year, for one of its avatars.

They come in four kinds, and the difference is how much of it you can know in advance:

fixedA date known a year out. Plan it. Father's Day, King's Month.
sportsThe season's shape is known, the outcome isn't. Plan the window, write on the day.
seasonalA rhythm rather than a date. Plan the fortnight. Summer heat, the new-year reset.
liveCan't be planned at all. Hold slots open and catch it. A trade, a viral routine.

A calendar that plans all four is lying about the fourth. The moments that have worked hardest couldn't have been scheduled — so the chain deliberately leaves slots empty rather than filling them with something plannable.

What each one carries

keysuper-bowl — the name everything else refers to it by
streamwhich of the four kinds above
window"early February" — in plain words, for a person
anchorHow it becomes a real date. Either a public-holiday key (it takes that holiday's computed date), a fixed window like 02-01 → 02-14, a window with a peak inside it, any (no date of its own), or live (uncatchable).
audiencewhich avatar it belongs to. One moment, one avatar — what lands for the barbershop regular isn't what lands for a fifteen-year-old, or for his mother buying his Christmas present.
evidencewhy we believe it — and this is the part worth arguing about
sentthe actual past emails that prove it, by date and subject line. The receipt.

## The targeting model (RULED, the owner, 2026-08-31)

**The segment is the targetable unit.** Segments are real, live, and sized —
the platform knows exactly who is in them.

**Sub-avatars do not appear at this level at all** (RULED, the owner,
2026-08-31). No profile carries a sub-avatar tag and the language bank's sub
tags cover a fraction of rows too small to plan on. They are not a targeting
unit and not a variant unit. Do not name one.

**AVATARS ride as VARIANTS.** A segment holds every avatar mixed together
and nobody can tell who is who. So a send to a segment carries one variant
per avatar worth reaching — same occasion, same offer, the argument written
through that avatar's own world and language. Everyone in the segment gets
one of them; the split test reports which won.

**One variant addresses one avatar.** Never blend two — that rule holds
inside a variant exactly as it holds for a whole email.

**This is how the missing data gets made.** Which variant wins IS the
measurement of which avatar dominates that segment. Plan the month so it
produces that evidence, and say so in the why.

**Variant count is capped by two things, and you state both:**
1. **Audience** — each variant needs enough people to mean anything. Roughly:
   over 50,000 in the segment supports 4 · 10,000–50,000 supports 3 ·
   3,000–10,000 supports 2 · under 3,000 supports 1 (no test). Platform
   guidance is a maximum of 4 variants per test.
2. **Language depth** — a variant only exists for an avatar with language
   to write it from. An avatar with a few hundred rows cannot carry a
   variant against one with thousands; give it its own small send or let it
   rest. Never promise a voice the brand cannot carry.

**Variant count is therefore usually small: the number of avatars with real
depth, capped by the audience band.** Two honest variants beat four thin
ones.

## How to decide, and on what evidence

- The coldness table is the arithmetic. A cell or type never spoken to is
  the strongest claim on attention there is. Cite the line.
- The findings are the economics. Cite the finding id ([F3]).
- What sending EARNS per audience — revenue per recipient against
  unsubscribe cost — is the frequency signal. It decides which segments are
  live and which rest. It is not a quota.
- Serving every segment every month is not the goal. A deliberate rest with
  a reason beats a thin obligatory send.
- The skeleton tells you what the month IS — a month with Black Friday in it
  wakes segments a quiet month would let rest. Say so when it does.

**You do not decide how many emails anyone gets.** Email is programmatic;
there is no budget, no ceiling and no floor (RULED, the owner, 2026-09-01). The
month's volume is built later from what is actually happening — the dated
holidays, the moments, the cold ground. What you decide about a segment is
only this: is it live this month, or does it rest.

**Never invent.** No segment, avatar or sub-avatar that is not in the state
sheet. Nothing here proposes email content — that is a later stage's job.

## A dated angle must sit inside its own window

RULED 2026-09-10 (the owner: "I still see some labor day email in the figma for
late september"). An angle that names a holiday, a season or an event is a
promise about WHEN the send goes out. This stage wrote one `labor-day` angle
and handed it to every variant of that avatar for the whole month, including
sends three weeks after Labor Day.

- An angle may name a moment **only if that moment's window contains the dates
  this cell will actually be sent on.** The month's live windows are above.
- One angle per variant, written for THAT variant. Do not reuse a single angle
  across a month because it fits the avatar — it has to fit the send.
- An angle with no moment in it is always safe. Reach for a dated one only
  when the timing is the argument.
- The code drops any angle whose moment lies outside the send's window and
  records the break, so a stale one costs the send its argument entirely.

**The rules that bind every move:**

- **Dates are real.** A moment may only be used inside its window as printed
  in the skeleton. A moment not in the skeleton is not in this month — using
  it is a rule break, not a creative choice.
- **Every ask carrying an offer arrives as a full arc** — warm-up, ask,
  cool-down — to the same segment (RULED 2026-08-31). Code completes any arc
  you leave half-built, so err toward naming the moment.
- **The type comes from the catalogue** — never invented, never forced. A
  cold or never-sent type serving the need beats the habitual one; cite the
  coldness line when that is the reason.
- **Offers come from the bank, product keys from the products list** — a
  key, or the exact word `none`. Never a sentence.
- **Every why cites** — a finding id, a moment key from the skeleton, or a
  coldness line. An uncited why is a hunch and the checker will flag it.
- There is **no send budget and no cap** (RULED 2026-09-01). Volume is the
  result of what is happening this month, not a constraint on it.
- Leave at least two slots `HELD OPEN — live` across the month for what
  cannot be planned.
- **Never invent.** Nothing enters that is not in the material above. A move
  that needs something missing is marked `[UNFILLED: what it needed]`.

Where the moments come from now

Twenty-four jobs in the Control Room, six of them research — and the moments file was the one piece of brand context nothing made. It was typed by hand on 2 September and never re-ran. It has a producer now, and it's two jobs because it's two genuinely different questions.

code · free · safe to run weekly What the brand already does

Reads the brand's own sends. Finds what it has been speaking into — either matched to a real holiday's date, or a theme that came back at the same point in a later year — and joins each one to what it actually earned against the brand's median.

Everything it finds is proven by construction, because the receipts are the brand's own emails.

research · the open web What his year contains that you've never touched

Goes looking for the part of the year that belongs to him — the seasons of what he follows, the weeks his rooms organise around, the arguments that come back every year.

It reads the mined file first, so it can't spend the run rediscovering what you already know. And it may never grade a finding "proven" — that grade belongs to the sends, never to research.

A finding has to pass four tests to count as a moment: it recurs (shown in two different years, with sources), it has a window you can date ("summer" isn't one, "the fortnight before the season opens" is), it's his and not the category's, and the brand could say something true into it.

Cyber Monday earns 7× the brand's median — and the calendar has never heard of it.

The mined half now files itself. You said it isn't a decision for you, and you were right: research is a judgement and needs you, but the record is arithmetic and doesn't. A moment your own emails prove, more than once, with its own performance attached, isn't an opinion anybody has to sign.

It added Cyber Monday, Cyber Week and Easter — and it corrected two entries that were wrong. black-friday was sitting there graded "territory" with a note reading "not yet run under its own name", while your record held nine Black Friday emails earning 1.7× your median. A claim typed once and never re-checked. 35 moments to 44, 17 proven to 28.

What it won't file on its own: a recurring theme (the record can't say which avatar a send was for, and guessing puts it in front of people it was never about), and anything with only one send — proven means a habit, not that you mentioned something once. The research half still never writes into the brand.

Every send is written off a format

You're right that this is where agnostic actually gets tested. A send isn't written from nothing — it borrows the shape of a real email. Layer 9 picks one per slot: the newest of the same type from the brand's own sent emails, then the same category.

what was broken

A brand that has never sent anything has no formats, so its whole month came out empty — silently. The chain didn't check, didn't say so, and produced a month that looked finished.

Now the chain declares that it needs formats, says out loud when a brand has none, and there's a written way through: a brand can declare that it borrows shapes from another brand — with who approved it and why.

No brand is named anywhere in the tool. Borrowing is a per-brand decision that lives in that brand's own folder and is empty by default. And a borrowed slot records where the shape came from, so the writer reads the right email while the send stays the borrowing brand's.

I proved it: Brand A borrowing from Brand B filled four slots for types Brand A has never sent, each one pointing at a real Brand B email. Then I reverted the declaration — nobody borrows by default.

How to test it

Four ways in, cheapest first. Three of them cost nothing at all.

1
Check a brand is ready — free, instant

Asks what each layer needs from the brand and whether it's actually there, before a run hits a missing file halfway through. Both brands pass right now.

2
Re-run the arithmetic — free

Layers 4–9, or just layer 9, replayed from what's already on disk. No model, no cost. This is the loop for changing a rule: change it, replay, read the month, change it again.

3
Read what the words produced — free

Every run keeps the filled-in prompt and the full answer. That's the real test of a prompt: not whether it sounds right, but what it made the machine decide.

4
Plan a month properly — costs a run

The whole chain on a real brand and month. The checker then lists every rule the month breaks, and those sit at the top of the board in red.

The checker is the other half of testing: stale angles, a promotional ask with no warm-up, an offer that doesn't exist, two sends on one day. It reports breaks rather than quietly fixing them, so what's wrong stays visible.

The board at the end

A month arrives as a calendar, not a list, so the spacing is something you see. One card per send underneath, each carrying the reason the machine chose it.

Rewrite anything

Date, hour, segment, type, offer, product, what it's about — and the angle, the line the copy has to carry. The machine's original stays on screen beside the change.

Cut what shouldn't run

A cut send is never written. Your decisions live in their own file, so re-planning the month can't undo them.

Leave a note

It reaches the writing stage and outranks both the plan and the source the email was built from.
