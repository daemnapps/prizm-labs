# Mechanics — how the asset converts

Bound as `{mechanics}`. Source: Research page of 2 Nov 2026 on betting products and retail use of the same mechanics, with published studies and rule texts. Numbers below are as published; a figure marked second-hand was not read at its origin.

**Borrow the mechanic, never the bet. The customer always receives what they paid for, and no money is ever at risk on chance. Each mechanic serves the outcome the customer came for: it shows them getting closer, gives a true reason to act now, or recognizes what they have done.**

## How to use these

- One asset, one lead mechanic, because one clear point gets the response. A second may support it. More than two is a defect.
- Pick measured mechanics first. An unproven mechanic runs as a test with a holdout, read on its metric, and is promoted or cut on the numbers.
- Every mechanic names the number it moves: cost per click, cost per conversion, order value, upsell take rate, subscription rate, repeat rate or email revenue.
- Every fact a mechanic shows (a count, a deadline, a stock level, a price, a tier) comes from live data. An unfilled fact drops the mechanic; it is never estimated.
- Every asset passes every gate below. A failed gate is fixed before the asset moves on.

## Gates — yes or no, every asset

`subscription` and `chance` apply to every asset that sells or awards anything, whatever the mechanic.

| Gate | Ask | Pass | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| `chance` | Is a prize picked by chance tied to a purchase? | No. Everyone in the offer gets the same item, or free entry has equal chance at the same prizes. | Lottery test: prize, chance, consideration. 18 U.S.C. 1302; People v. Miller (N.Y. 1936). |
| `prize-filing` | Does a chance promotion carry over $5,000 in prizes? | No, or filed and bonded before start. Thirty days in New York, seven in Florida. | N.Y. GBL 369-e; Fla. Stat. 849.094. |
| `true-data` | Does every count, stock level, member number and rank come from live data? | Yes. | 16 CFR 465.8 fake social-influence indicators; FTC Act section 5. |
| `real-limit` | Does the offer truly end or sell out as stated, with no reset? | Yes. | FTC dark patterns staff report, 15 Sep 2022; NY AG Fareportal. |
| `price` | Is any former price one regularly offered, and is the shown price what is paid? | Yes. | 16 CFR 233; California SB 478. |
| `reviews` | Are reviews and customer stories real, with paid or given connections disclosed, and rewards not tied to sentiment? | Yes. | 16 CFR 255.5; 16 CFR 465.2 and 465.4. |
| `claims` | Is every result claim backed by evidence, within cosmetic wording, with typical results disclosed? | Yes. | 16 CFR 255.2; FTC health claims guidance, Dec 2022; intended-use test. |
| `no-risk-free` | Does the guarantee state exactly what comes back and how? | Yes, and the word risk-free is not used. | NY AG consumer alert, 10 Feb 2022; state betting rules on the same word. |
| `subscription` | Are recurring terms stated before purchase, with express consent and a simple way to stop? | Yes. | ROSCA, 15 U.S.C. 8403. |
| `permission` | Did the customer opt in to this channel, with stop honored? | Yes. | TCPA for texts; platform sending rules. |
| `platform` | For paid social and social shops, does the asset avoid spins, wheels, raffles and sweepstakes, before-and-after comparison where barred, statements of physical inferiority, and implied personal attributes? | Yes. | Current ad and shop policies, read on the day of launch. |
