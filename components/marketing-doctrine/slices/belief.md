# Belief — the one doubt this ad closes

Bound as `{belief_doubts}`. Source: the doctrine component; refs are line pointers into the book, for verification only.

The doubt that stops this person believing this solves their version of the problem. Desire, awareness and market stage can all be right and the message still fails here. An ad closes ONE doubt, with the proof built for that doubt. Proof aimed at no doubt closes none.

| Doubt | What they say | The proof that closes it |
|---|---|---|
| `does-it-work` | I don't believe this works; nothing does. | demonstration, the cause explained, a before and after of the mechanism itself |
| `for-me` | It might work for someone, not for someone like me. | proof from a person who matches: same skin or hair, same age, same failed history, same starting point |
| `worth-it` | Even if it works, it's not worth the money, time or hassle. | what the old way costs, how easy it is, the guarantee |
| `trust-you` | I don't trust who is telling me. | a third party, a trusted voice from their own group, the founder's story, real customers |

## The claim

The claim (also called the angle) is one sentence a customer could agree or disagree with. It is the read-out of the layers above it, never a separate brainstorm. It is one desire, spoken at one awareness level, pitched for one market stage, closing one doubt, through one mechanism. The claim belongs to the brand and can be aimed at any avatar; the avatar, sub-avatar and demographic decide how it is said. Only the owner signs a claim.

## A chosen group

A market is not all of one piece. Two groups can share one wide appeal and need different mechanisms and different proof; a chosen group changes the headline, the identification, the mechanism and the proof. [[L2015-2040]]
