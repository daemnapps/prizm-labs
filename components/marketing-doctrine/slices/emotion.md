# The emotional map — what each beat makes the viewer feel

Bound as `{emotion}`. Source: the doctrine component; refs are line pointers into the book, for verification only.

The emotional map — what the viewer should FEEL at each beat of a piece, before anything decides what they see or hear. The sections say what an ad argues, the techniques how the argument is built, the delivery dials how it is performed; the map says what each beat must make the viewer feel, where the feeling turns, where it peaks and what it leaves them with. A director plans a commercial this way: the feeling first, the picture second.

**Read off the swipe.** The map is READ OFF THE SWIPE, never invented. The swipe already proved which feelings, in which order, hold this audience; our version keeps its map — the same feeling at each beat, the same turn, the same peak, the same ending — and only the words and the product change. A feeling moved, dropped or added is a structure change, exactly like a moved scene. Where the swipe itself breaks a rule below, the swipe wins: keep it and note it, never fix it.

**Shown, never said.** A feeling is SHOWN, never said. It arrives through a face, a sound, a frame, a cut or a held silence — never through a line that names it. A script that tells the viewer how to feel has given up on making them feel it.

## 1. The rules

- **read-off-the-swipe** — Every map starts as the swipe's own map, read beat by beat off the teardown and the frame map, with the moment on screen that makes each feeling. Ours keeps it. _(craft)_
- **one-feeling-per-beat** — Each beat names ONE feeling from this palette and one intensity. Two feelings in one beat is two beats or one muddle. _(craft)_
- **every-beat-moves** — Each beat changes the feeling — its kind or its intensity. A beat that leaves it where the last one left it is a transition: merge it or cut it. _(research — mckee-1997)_
- **one-peak** — Exactly one beat is the peak (intensity 5), and it sits on the product working or on the payoff. _(research — fredrickson-kahneman-1993)_
- **contrast-makes-the-turn** — The relief is only as big as the low before it. Keep the low as deep as the swipe made it — softening it weakens the turn. _(research — reagan-2016)_
- **negatives-resolve** — Every negative feeling raised is paid off before the end, by what the product actually does. Never end at the low; never make the viewer the joke. _(craft)_
- **claims-sit-calm** — A claim, a price and a guarantee land in a plain beat — trust or calm — never inside the peak or a laugh. _(craft)_
- **receipted-to-the-room** — The low and the desire are the avatar's own, in the room's own words; a feeling the writer imagines for them is a finding. _(schwartz [L3253-3872])_
- **emotion-builds-memory** — Feeling is not decoration: emotionally led advertising builds more long-term effect than purely rational advertising. The map is load-bearing. _(research — binet-field-2013)_

## 2. The five points every map names

- **The open** (`open`) — The feeling of the first one to three seconds — what stops the scroll. The open is curiosity, surprise or recognition. A piece that opens on calm, trust or desire has nothing yet to earn them with. _(research — teixeira-2012)_
- **The low** (`low`) — The deepest negative beat — where the problem is felt most. The low is the avatar's own pain, in the room's own words. Not every piece has one; a piece that does must resolve it before the end. _(research — reagan-2016)_
- **The turn** (`turn`) — The one beat where the feeling flips — problem to relief, doubt to 'oh'. The turn is one beat, not a stretch. It is only as strong as the contrast into it: a turn with no low before it lands flat. _(research — mckee-1997)_
- **The peak** (`peak`) — The single strongest feeling in the piece — intensity 5. Viewers remember the peak and the end, not the average. The peak sits on the product working or on the payoff — never on a claim, a price or a disclaimer. _(research — fredrickson-kahneman-1993)_
- **The end** (`end`) — The feeling the last beat leaves — the one attached to the brand. The end is a positive feeling the product really delivers. Never end at the low. _(research — fredrickson-kahneman-1993)_

## 3. The feelings — the only words a beat's feeling is spelled in

| Feeling | Valence | What the viewer feels | What on screen makes it |
|---|---|---|---|
| `curiosity` | neutral | An open question the viewer wants closed — 'wait, what is that?' | Something unexplained or out of place, an action cut before it finishes, a claim with the reason held back. |
| `surprise` | neutral | A jolt — the expected thing does not happen. | A pattern set up and broken, an unexpected person or place, a sudden cut, sound or reveal. |
| `recognition` | neutral | 'That's me' — the viewer sees their own life, habit or moment on screen. | A specific, true-to-life detail of the avatar's day, said or done the way they do it. |
| `frustration` | negative | The itch of the problem — annoyance at something that keeps not working. | The failed attempt shown, the sigh, the thing thrown down, the same struggle repeated. |
| `unease` | negative | Tension — a quiet worry that something is wrong or about to go wrong. | A held close-up, a pause before an answer, a detail noticed that should not be there, sound dropping out. |
| `empathy` | negative | Feeling for the person on screen — 'oh, I know how that feels.' | A real, unperformed moment of the person struggling or exposed, held long enough to land. |
| `amusement` | positive | A laugh or a smirk. | Whatever the humor dial and the room's own references allow — never over a claim. |
| `relief` | positive | The exhale — the problem finally lets go. | The struggle stops, the shoulders drop, the easy version of the moment that was hard a beat ago. |
| `hope` | positive | 'Maybe this could work for me.' | Someone like the viewer trying it, a first small change, the mechanism making sense. |
| `desire` | positive | 'I want that' — wanting the life, the look or the result on screen. | The after shown at its best: the person at ease, the result in use, the product handled with care. |
| `trust` | positive | Belief — 'this is real, these people are straight with me.' | Proof shown plainly: a real result, a real person, a demonstration with nothing hidden, a calm unhurried read. |
| `belonging` | positive | Pride of membership — 'people like me do this.' | The avatar's own people, references and world; the product as part of who they already are. |
| `satisfaction` | positive | The click of something working exactly right. | A clean demonstration, a perfect fit, a before-and-after that resolves, a sound that lands. |
| `urgency` | neutral | 'I should do this now.' | A real deadline or scarcity from the offer file, the cost of waiting shown, the pace picking up into the close. |
| `calm` | positive | Settled ease — nothing is pushing. | Slow, steady shots, soft light, room tone, an unhurried voice. |

## 4. Intensity

- **1** — A trace — present, not felt.
- **2** — Noticed — the viewer registers it.
- **3** — Felt — it shows on the viewer's face.
- **4** — Strong — the moment they would tell someone about.
- **5** — The peak — the single strongest moment of the piece. Exactly one beat carries a 5.

## 5. The arcs — the shape of the whole piece

- **Dip and rise** (`dip-and-rise`) — recognition → frustration or unease (the low) → turn → relief or satisfaction → desire. Fall into the problem, climb out with the product. The commonest shape in direct response. Frameworks: `pas`, `bab`, `story-testimonial`, `mechanism-led`. _(research — reagan-2016)_
- **Steady climb** (`steady-climb`) — curiosity → hope → desire → urgency. No low: each beat raises the feeling a step until the close. Frameworks: `aida`, `direct-offer`. _(craft)_
- **Set-up and reveal** (`reveal`) — calm or recognition → surprise (the turn) → amusement or satisfaction. A normal moment broken by something unexpected; the reveal is the peak. Frameworks: `mechanism-led`. _(craft)_
- **Held warmth** (`held-warmth`) — recognition → belonging, held and deepened. No problem and no turn; the piece stays in the viewer's world and lets them feel at home in it. Frameworks: `identification-only`. _(craft)_
- **Contrast** (`contrast`) — frustration (them) ↔ satisfaction (us), alternating, ending on us. Two feelings cut against each other; the side the piece ends on wins. Frameworks: `us-vs-them`. _(craft)_

## 6. What carries a feeling — the director's levers

- **Performance** (`performance`) — The face and body — a real reaction, a glance, an exhale. Carries more than any line.
- **Framing and distance** (`framing`) — Close is intimate and 'this is me'; wide is watching someone else; selfie framing is a friend talking.
- **Camera movement** (`camera-move`) — Handheld is raw and present; locked-off is calm and certain; a push-in raises intensity.
- **Light and colour** (`light-colour`) — Cool, flat, dim for the low; warm, soft, open for the after — the change in light can carry the turn alone.
- **Sound and music** (`sound`) — Music sets the feeling before the picture does; a sound dropping out makes tension, a sound landing makes satisfaction.
- **Cut rhythm** (`cut-rhythm`) — Fast cuts build energy or anxiety; a held shot lets a feeling land.
- **The held beat** (`silence`) — A pause before the answer or after the reveal — the space the feeling happens in.
- **Words and voice** (`words`) — How the line is said, never what it names. The voice carries the feeling; the words carry the meaning.

## 8. Receipts

- `fredrickson-kahneman-1993` — Fredrickson, B. L. & Kahneman, D. (1993). Duration neglect in retrospective evaluations of affective episodes. Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 65(1), 45–55. People judge an experience by its most intense moment and its end, not its length or average — the peak-end rule.
- `teixeira-2012` — Teixeira, T., Wedel, M. & Pieters, R. (2012). Emotion-induced engagement in internet video advertisements. Journal of Marketing Research, 49(2), 144–159. Measured on viewers' faces: surprise concentrates attention and joy keeps people watching — the open has to earn the next second.
- `reagan-2016` — Reagan, A. J., Mitchell, L., Kiley, D., Danforth, C. M. & Dodds, P. S. (2016). The emotional arcs of stories are dominated by six basic shapes. EPJ Data Science, 5, 31. Stories follow a few emotional shapes; the fall-then-rise ('man in a hole') is among the most common.
- `mckee-1997` — McKee, R. (1997). Story: Substance, Structure, Style and the Principles of Screenwriting. ReganBooks. A scene turns a value — the viewer leaves it feeling differently than they entered; a scene that does not turn is a non-event.
- `binet-field-2013` — Binet, L. & Field, P. (2013). The Long and the Short of It. IPA. Across the IPA effectiveness databank, emotional campaigns outperform rational ones on long-term business effects.
