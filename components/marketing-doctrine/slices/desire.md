# Desire — the one the ad runs on

Bound as `{desire_dimensions}`. Source: the doctrine component; refs are line pointers into the book, for verification only.

Copy never manufactures a want. It finds a want enough people already carry and channels it onto one product. Ride an existing mass desire and the spend is amplified; fight one and the ad dies. [L312-320] [L347-348] [L364-382]

## The three dimensions — a lead desire must win all three

| Dimension | What it measures | The test | Ref |
|---|---|---|---|
| Urgency | How hard the want demands satisfaction today, not eventually. | Would this audience act on it this week if a route opened? | [L438-447] |
| Staying power | How well the want resists being satisfied once — whether it returns. | After one purchase, is the want gone or back tomorrow? | [L438-447] |
| Scope | How many people inside the market carry the want at all. | Is this a whole-market want or one slice of it? | [L438-447] |

**One desire leads.** A product touches several desires; exactly ONE leads the ad, and the headline picks it. Every later decision inherits that choice, so getting it wrong sinks everything downstream. [L448-459]

## Physical vs functional product

The physical product is what the thing is made of and never the sell. The functional product is what it DOES, and that is the only thing that sells. [L479-529]

Physical facts are allowed back in for exactly five jobs:

- justify the price
- document the quality claim
- promise durability
- sharpen the mental picture
- supply a fresh believability mechanism

**Dominant trait.** Every product carries one dominant trait that should summarise it in the headline; the remaining traits blend in underneath, never alongside. [L1967-1990]

## Where desire comes from

- **permanent-forces** — Mass instinct and long-unsolved problems — the wants that do not move. [L383-426]
- **forces-of-change** — Trend cycles and mass imitation working through a population — the wants that are moving right now. [L383-426]
