# Sophistication — the five stages

Bound as `{sophistication_stages}`. Source: the doctrine component; refs are line pointers into the book, for verification only.

A separate question from awareness: how many competing products has this market already been pitched? The answer decides what the ad must lead with, independent of what the audience knows about itself. [L1305-1649]

| Stage | The market has already heard | The ad must lead with | Ref |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. First in the market (`stage-1`) | Nothing — no one has made this claim here before. | The plain claim, stated simply and with no embellishment. | [L1315-1360] |
| 2. The claim is in the market (`stage-2`) | The plain claim, now from competitors. | The same claim, enlarged to the edge of what is still believable. | [L1361-1404] |
| 3. Claims exhausted (`stage-3`) | Claims pushed to their limit, and it discounts about half of what it hears. | A NEW MECHANISM — how the result happens, not what the result is. The mechanism leads the opening. | [L1405-1470] |
| 4. The mechanism is in the market (`stage-4`) | A mechanism, from a competitor. | The same mechanism, enlarged, made easier, faster or more complete. | [L1471-1521] |
| 5. Exhausted field (`stage-5`) | Every claim and every mechanism, and believes none of them. | Drop claim and mechanism both and sell on identification alone — the same move as the Unaware level. | [L1522-1649] |

**Category reset.** A genuine category reset — a new form of the product, not a new claim about the old one — restarts the clock at stage 1, and the plain claim becomes the right lead again. Opening a demographic that was never addressed does the same on identification alone. [L1542-1649]
