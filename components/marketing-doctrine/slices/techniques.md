# The seven techniques

Bound as `{techniques}`. Source: the doctrine component; refs are line pointers into the book, for verification only.

Body copy works three levers: desire (what is wanted), identification (roles and self-image), belief (what is already accepted). These seven are how all three get built. [L2166-2240]

## Intensification (`intensification`) [L2244-3250]

Keep generating fresh, concrete pictures of the desire already fulfilled — never the same picture twice; vary the angle so repetition reinforces instead of tiring.

Sub-methods:

1. Blunt, fully detailed direct description of the product or the result [L2336]
2. Put the claim into action — show the mechanism operating [L2448]
3. Bring the viewer in — narrate the first-use moment as lived [L2510]
4. Turn the claim into a test the viewer can run and verify alone [L2541]
5. Stretch the benefit across time — weeks and months, not one moment [L2603]
6. Bring in an audience — testimonials and case histories of ordinary people [L2646]
7. Bring in experts, and show the surprise on their side [L2693]
8. Compare and contrast against the old way to prove superiority [L2723]
9. Picture the dark side too — before and after, the cost of doing nothing [L2761]
10. Show how easy the benefit is to obtain [L2821]
11. Use metaphor, analogy and personification [L2847]
12. Summarise before closing — the catalog, horizontal (more applications) or vertical (desire piled on desire) [L2880]
13. Turn the guarantee itself into the closing summary of every promise made [L3013]

**Across a campaign.** Across a series, hold ONE dominant image and vary only its disguise, so every asset deepens the same picture. [L3066-3249]

- **When:** Building desire anywhere in the body, heaviest at the open and at the close.
- **Owning stage:** 4d-expansion, 4b-hook, 4e-close
- **Research question:** Every distinct way this audience describes living the fulfilled desire — different settings, timeframes and onlookers — so each beat is a new angle rather than a repeat.
- **Scene shape:** One fresh fulfilment moment per scene; emotion builds anticipation into awe; the delta is the same desire at escalating vividness.

## Identification (`identification`) [L3253-3872]

Build a saleable personality into the product by attaching roles the audience wants to be seen playing. The product then does double duty: physical satisfaction plus a badge of identity.

Sub-methods:

1. Character roles — adjectives, who the person IS [L3253-3400]
2. Achievement roles — nouns, the status or position attained [L3253-3400]

Method:

1. Find the product's existing primary image in the audience's mind first [L3401-3560]
2. If that image is favourable, intensify it — stack several expressions of the same trait [L3561-3700]
3. If it is neutral or negative, do not contradict it — use it as the base and bridge to better images layered on top; never demand an identity jump the audience cannot make [L3701-3872]

- **When:** Parity products, price-insensitive categories, and anywhere physical performance no longer separates the product from the field.
- **Owning stage:** 4d-expansion, 1b-audience
- **Research question:** Which character and achievement roles this audience already claims in its own self-descriptions, and what the product's current unprompted image is — favourable, neutral or negative.
- **Scene shape:** The product appears as a visible marker inside a life moment; emotion is pride or recognition, ideally witnessed by someone else in frame; the delta runs from unlabelled self to clearly identified self.

## Gradualization (`gradualization`) [L3876-4539]

Belief is structural, not only a matter of content. A claim's strength equals its own content plus how far the copy has already prepared the audience to accept it — so build a chain of acceptances from a fact already believed, each step earning the next.

Sub-methods:

1. Inclusion question — a question the intended audience answers yes to, starting a habit of agreement [L4359]
2. Detailed identification — symptoms described specifically enough to be recognised as lived [L4377]
3. Contradiction of a present false belief — used only with strong authority behind it [L4402]
4. The language of logic — phrasing that signals reasoning and carries conviction by association [L4424]
5. Syllogistic thinking — a real chain, A causes B and B causes C, that makes the product's working feel inevitable [L4485]
6. Other belief forms — contingency phrasing, repeated-proof echo, promise and proof alternation, paragraph parallelism [L4519]

- **When:** Whenever the real claim would be unbelievable stated cold — start where belief already exists and walk forward.
- **Owning stage:** 4d-expansion, 4b-hook, 4e-close, 5-brief
- **Research question:** The one fact or complaint this audience already accepts as true, and the shortest path of accepted steps from there to the actual claim.
- **Scene shape:** A short sequence of small-truth beats before the reveal; each beat lands as recognition; the delta is incremental and never a jump.

## Redefinition (`redefinition`) [L4544-4894]

Give the product, or the problem, a new definition that removes an objection before it is consciously raised.

Sub-methods:

1. Simplification — for products that read as too complicated: relabel the intimidating process in familiar terms [L4595]
2. Escalation — for products with too narrow an appeal: connect the small thing to a larger outcome, or show the need is constant rather than occasional [L4760]
3. Price reduction — for products that read as too expensive: change the comparison anchor rather than defend the number [L4823]
4. The direct flip — turn a liability into the proof of an asset with a single idea [L4569]

- **When:** A specific known objection is blocking the sale: too hard, too small a deal, or too pricey.
- **Owning stage:** 4e-close, 4d-expansion
- **Research question:** The exact objection wording that appears in reviews and comments, and the real anchor this product is being compared against.
- **Scene shape:** The feared frame appears on screen and is relabelled inside the same beat; emotion is relief; the delta runs feared-big to actually-small.

## Mechanization (`mechanization`) [L4896-5132]

Verbal proof of HOW the product delivers — reason-why copy answering the silent question of how the thing works.

Sub-methods:

1. Name it only — the market already understands and accepts the mechanism, so label it and compete on price or features [L4969]
2. Describe it — the market does not know it yet: promise first, then explain the mechanism in selling language [L4988]
3. Feature it — the market is saturated with parity claims, so the mechanism itself becomes the lead claim, the new way [L5046]

**A price cut needs its own mechanism.** A price cut needs a mechanism of its own — a believable reason the price is lower — or the discount itself is not believed. [L5079-5131]

- **When:** Whenever how-does-it-work or why-is-it-this-price is the live objection; depth scales with how familiar the category already is.
- **Owning stage:** 4d-expansion, 4b-hook
- **Research question:** Whether this audience already accepts a mechanism for the category — so it need only be named — or does not, so it must be described or featured; and what the real point of mechanical difference is.
- **Scene shape:** A clear visual demonstration of the mechanism working, inside-the-product or before-and-after; emotion is the click of understanding; the delta runs unexplained result to visible cause and effect.

## Concentration (`concentration`) [L5136-5432]

Destroy the alternate ways of satisfying the desire — a direct attack on the competing option or on the status quo.

Sub-methods:

1. Point-by-point bad and good contrast — parallel structure, each weakness answered immediately by the matching strength [L5219-5313]
2. Before-and-after time sequence — narrate the familiar failure pattern of the old approach, then the same sequence succeeding [L5316-5416]

**Rule.** Never attack a weakness without supplying the fix for that exact weakness in the same breath, or the attack reads as bias.

- **When:** The budget is smaller than the incumbent's, or most of the audience is already loyal to an alternative that has to be cracked first. Skip it when the category is already dominated — do not hand a competitor free attention.
- **Owning stage:** 4d-expansion, 4e-close
- **Research question:** Which specific alternatives this audience names and what their actual, sourced weaknesses are — never invented ones — plus the failed-solution stories the audience tells about itself.
- **Scene shape:** The alternative fails at a relatable moment, immediately followed by the same moment succeeding; frustration into relief, back to back; the delta is the same task with the opposite outcome, repeated a few beats.

## Camouflage (`camouflage`) [L5436-5661]

Borrow believability from the medium the ad lives in. Editorial and organic content is trusted more than advertising voice, so minimise the gear change between the two.

Sub-methods:

1. Format — match the host medium's real layout, headline type, sub-heads and illustration style [L5474-5542]
2. Phraseology — adopt the host medium's stock language and tone [L5544-5574]
3. Register — understatement (plain, short, adjective-light, self-deprecating) or deadly sincerity (admit a real limitation openly, so the claim that follows lands harder) [L5575-5661]

- **When:** Scepticism toward advertising voice is the main barrier, or a native placement is available.
- **Owning stage:** 3-injection, 4c-placement
- **Research question:** What genuinely top-performing organic content in this exact placement looks like structurally — pacing, captions, camera — rather than what native is assumed to look like.
- **Scene shape:** Shot and voiced exactly like the platform's own organic top content — handheld, plain talk, imperfect; it reads as a person saying something; the delta runs perceived-as-ad to perceived-as-content with the same claim underneath.
