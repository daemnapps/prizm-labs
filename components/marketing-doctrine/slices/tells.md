# The tells — what makes work read as machine-made

Bound as `{tells}`. Source: the doctrine component; refs are line pointers into the book, for verification only.

The tells — what makes copy, a voice, a picture, a clip or an edit read as machine-made, how to spot each one, and what a human maker would do instead. Every layer of this doctrine says what to make; this one says what gives it away. A tell is not a style choice: viewers punish work they read as AI-made, and the gap between how advertisers and consumers feel about it is widening.

**The master rule.** Generators drift to the AVERAGE — the average face, the average sentence, the average kitchen, the average camera move. Every tell is the average showing through. The fix is never 'make it look less AI'; it is always a SPECIFIC detail taken from something real: the swipe, the room's own words, a real reference photo, the brand's own footage. Specific reads as human. Average reads as machine.

**How a tell is held.** A `hard` tell is caught by the machine with no model and is removed before the work moves on. A `judge` tell is read by a person or a judge prompt against this list and named with its id. Where the swipe itself does the thing — the source speaks in that phrase, shoots that way — it is the swipe's and stays: a tell is something WE added.

**Why it matters.** Consumers already feel worse about AI-made ads than advertisers think, and the gap is growing; a tell costs trust before the message is heard. _(research — iab-2026)_

## Words — The script, on-screen text and captions.

### W1 · Machine vocabulary (machine-checked)

- **Spot it:** Words generators reach for and people rarely say out loud.
- **Why it reads as machine:** These words rose sharply in text after language models arrived; readers have learned to hear them as machine-made.
- **Instead:** Say the plain thing the room would say. Quote the language bank.
- **Source:** research — kobak-2024

### W2 · Ad-template phrases (machine-checked)

- **Spot it:** Stock lines every generated ad reuses.
- **Why it reads as machine:** A line the viewer has heard in a hundred ads tells them this is one more ad, and a generated one.
- **Instead:** Replace with the swipe's own line shape filled from the bank, or cut it.
- **Source:** craft

### W3 · Exclamation stacking (machine-checked)

- **Spot it:** Two or more exclamation marks in a row, or more than one in a single line.
- **Why it reads as machine:** Manufactured energy; people show excitement in the voice, not the punctuation.
- **Instead:** Full stops. Let the delivery carry the energy.
- **Source:** craft

### W4 · The tidy triple (judged)

- **Spot it:** Three parallel adjectives or clauses in a row — fast, easy and effective.
- **Why it reads as machine:** Generators default to the rule of three; real speech is lopsided.
- **Instead:** Keep the one that is true and specific. Drop the other two.
- **Source:** craft

### W5 · The generic claim (judged)

- **Spot it:** A benefit that would fit any product in the category — 'feel your best', 'glowing skin', 'results you can see'.
- **Why it reads as machine:** The average claim; it is the clearest sign nobody looked at this product or this customer.
- **Instead:** The product file's specific fact, or the customer's own words about the specific moment.
- **Source:** craft

### W6 · Over-explaining (judged)

- **Spot it:** A line that tells the viewer what they just saw or how to feel about it.
- **Why it reads as machine:** Generators narrate; people let the picture land.
- **Instead:** Cut the line. If the picture does not carry it, fix the picture.
- **Source:** craft

## Voice — How the words are read.

### V1 · Even prosody (judged)

- **Spot it:** Every sentence the same pitch shape and speed, no breath, no restart, no filler.
- **Why it reads as machine:** Real speech is uneven: it speeds up, trails off, restarts once.
- **Instead:** The spoken slice's register recipe — fragments, one restart, fillers at the start of a thought — and the voice settings that allow variation.
- **Source:** craft

### V2 · The announcer lift (judged)

- **Spot it:** The voice rises into a sell on the product name or the offer.
- **Why it reads as machine:** Nobody talks to a friend like that; the lift says 'ad' before the words do.
- **Instead:** Read the product line flat, the way the rest of the paragraph is read.
- **Source:** craft

## Picture — Every still: cast sheets, frames, plates.

### P1 · Plastic skin (judged)

- **Spot it:** Skin with no pores, no texture, an even sheen; waxy or airbrushed.
- **Why it reads as machine:** The commonest picture tell; generators smooth skin toward an average.
- **Instead:** Real reference, the realism block (visible pores, fine lines, matte skin), the subject's own detail crops.
- **Source:** craft

### P2 · The average face (judged)

- **Spot it:** A face that is perfectly symmetrical, flawless and forgettable — model-pretty with nothing particular about it.
- **Why it reads as machine:** Generated faces drift toward the average face, which is why they read as trustworthy and anonymous at once.
- **Instead:** Build from a real reference and keep its particulars — the asymmetry, the mark, the uneven brow.
- **Source:** research — nightingale-farid-2022

### P3 · Studio light everywhere (judged)

- **Spot it:** Even, glowing, shadowless light in a place that would never have it — a bathroom lit like a product shoot.
- **Why it reads as machine:** Generators light for beauty, not for the place; real rooms have one window and a bad ceiling light.
- **Instead:** Light from the world block: where the window is, what the lamp does, the shadow it throws.
- **Source:** craft

### P4 · The sterile room (judged)

- **Spot it:** A spotless, empty, catalogue room with nothing personal in it.
- **Why it reads as machine:** The average room; real places have clutter, wear and someone's life in them.
- **Instead:** The swipe's room inventory, sharper — the believable clutter, the worn surface.
- **Source:** craft

### P5 · Dead centre, perfect pose (judged)

- **Spot it:** Subject centred, square to camera, smiling, holding the product up.
- **Why it reads as machine:** The stock-photo pose; nobody holds a bottle beside their face in real life.
- **Instead:** The swipe's framing and its real gesture — caught mid-action, off-centre where the source was.
- **Source:** craft

### P6 · New-out-of-the-box wardrobe (judged)

- **Spot it:** Clothes with no creases, no wear, a perfect fit; hair with no stray strand.
- **Why it reads as machine:** Generated clothes are ironed by default; real clothes have been worn.
- **Instead:** Name the wear in the look: creases, a pushed-up sleeve, hair as it actually sits.
- **Source:** craft

### P7 · The grade (judged)

- **Spot it:** Teal-and-orange, oversaturated, HDR glow, fake shallow focus on a phone shot.
- **Why it reads as machine:** A 'cinematic' default laid over a format that was shot on a phone.
- **Instead:** The camera block's realness dial — no cinematic grading — taken from the swipe.
- **Source:** craft

### P8 · Broken detail (judged)

- **Spot it:** Wrong fingers or hands, garbled text, a label that changes, impossible reflections or shadows, jewellery melting into skin.
- **Why it reads as machine:** The detail a viewer's eye catches in a second, and the one that ends trust in everything else.
- **Instead:** Type is a layer, never generated; the product only from its real packshot; regenerate the frame, never ship it.
- **Source:** craft

## Motion — Every generated clip.

### M1 · The floating camera (judged)

- **Spot it:** A perfectly smooth, slow glide or push-in on a shot that should be handheld or locked off.
- **Why it reads as machine:** The generator's default move; no phone and no person moves like that.
- **Instead:** The swipe's own camera: handheld with its shake, or locked off and still.
- **Source:** craft

### M2 · Slow drift and morph (judged)

- **Spot it:** Everything moving a little too slowly; faces, hands, products or backgrounds that shift shape across the clip.
- **Why it reads as machine:** Temporal drift is the signature failure of generated video.
- **Instead:** Shorter clips, first and last frame pinned, the product from its packshot; a morphing clip is remade, never shipped.
- **Source:** craft

### M3 · Wrong physics (judged)

- **Spot it:** Liquid, cloth, hair or skin that moves wrong; contact with no weight; a gesture that never quite lands.
- **Why it reads as machine:** The body knows physics before the mind does.
- **Instead:** Beat-by-beat directions with the real action from the swipe's frame map.
- **Source:** craft

### M4 · The dead stare (judged)

- **Spot it:** Unblinking eyes, a fixed smile, lip-sync drifting off the words, a background that does not move.
- **Why it reads as machine:** Faces are what people read hardest; a still face in a moving clip is uncanny.
- **Instead:** Line parity on every talking clip; a reaction beat on the face; motion QC names it.
- **Source:** craft

## Edit and structure — How the piece is cut and shaped.

### E1 · Metronome cuts (judged)

- **Spot it:** Every shot the same length; every scene the same shape.
- **Why it reads as machine:** A machine cuts on a grid; an editor cuts on the feeling.
- **Instead:** Cut to the emotional map: fast where the feeling builds, held where it lands.
- **Source:** craft

### E2 · Too clean (judged)

- **Spot it:** No imperfection anywhere — no stumble, no off-frame moment, no real-world noise.
- **Why it reads as machine:** Polish is the tell in a feed of phone footage; perfection reads as fake.
- **Instead:** Keep the anti-perfection budget: the swipe's own roughness, carried on purpose.
- **Source:** craft

### E3 · The music swell (judged)

- **Spot it:** Stock music that rises on the product reveal and resolves on the call to action.
- **Why it reads as machine:** The template's emotional shortcut — the viewer hears the formula.
- **Instead:** The swipe's sound: its own bed, its drop-outs, its silence.
- **Source:** craft

## Concept — The idea itself — the first, obvious, average idea.

### C1 · The first idea (judged)

- **Spot it:** The obvious version anyone would write first — the generic setting, the generic person, the generic problem.
- **Why it reads as machine:** A generator's first answer is the average answer by construction.
- **Instead:** Start from the swipe and the room, not from the category; change one thing on purpose.
- **Source:** craft

### C2 · No specific human detail (judged)

- **Spot it:** Nothing in the piece that only this audience would recognise — no in-joke, no real habit, no particular object.
- **Why it reads as machine:** Specific detail is the strongest signal a person made this for a person.
- **Instead:** One detail from the culture read or the culture map, named with its receipt.
- **Source:** craft
