# Who it is for — avatar, sub-avatar, demographic

Bound as `{people_layers}`. Source: the doctrine component; refs are line pointers into the book, for verification only.

The one home of who an ad speaks to. Every chain reads these through the strategist's who reader; no prompt restates them.

| Layer | What it is | When it is a new one | The test |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Avatar** | a whole person: one group of people most likely to buy, described so exactly that they think "that's literally me". Built from one desire, then filled in order: desire, experiences, emotions, behaviors, culture. Demographics come last and sit in their own layer. | the desire or the problem changes. A new problem on the same person is a new avatar, because it has its own experiences, emotions and behaviors (dark marks need not come from acne; ingrown hairs do not mean acne; someone with acne may be a teen or grown). The problem is the avatar's label, never the whole of it. | Would a person with this problem, and none of the other problems, still be who this speaks to? |
| **Sub-avatar** | that same whole person plus ONE narrowing element: an experience, a situation, a product they tried, a feeling, or where on the body it is. It sharpens the same five parts; it never starts a new person. | that one factor changes the argument (bumps on the back of a shaved head, or on the neck of someone with hair) | Swap what they have been through. If the ad breaks, it is a sub-avatar. |
| **Demographic** | age, gender, race, language, culture: the face, the words and the place | never a new problem or argument. It fits any avatar and any sub-avatar, one demographic per piece. | Swap race, age or language. If the ad still works, it is a demographic. Hair or skin that causes the problem belongs to the avatar or sub-avatar, not to race. |
