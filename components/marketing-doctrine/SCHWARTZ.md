## 1. Desire — the only force the ad rides

Copy does not create wanting. It finds a want that enough people already carry
privately and channels it onto one product — the phrase used for that is "the
public spread of a private want" [L312-320, L347-348]. Copy that fights an
existing mass desire dies; copy that rides one gets amplified, because the
force was already there and the spend only aims it [L364-382].

Two things feed a market's wanting, and both are worth inventorying before a
run: the permanent forces (instinct, and problems technology has not solved
yet) and the forces of change (trend cycles, education and imitation moving
through a population) [L383-426].

Any candidate desire is judged on three dimensions [L438-447]:

| Dimension | What it measures |
|---|---|
| Urgency | How hard the want demands satisfaction today |
| Staying power | Whether it returns after being satisfied once |
| Scope | How much of the market carries it at all |

A product touches several desires; **exactly one leads the ad**, and the
opening line is where that choice shows. Get it wrong and everything
downstream is wrong with it [L448-459].

Then the split that governs injection: the **physical product** is what the
thing is made of and is never the sell; the **functional product** is what it
does, and that is the only thing that sells. Physical facts come back in for
five jobs only — justify price, document quality, promise durability, sharpen
the picture, supply a fresh believability mechanism [L479-529]. Alongside it:
every product has one **dominant trait** that should summarise it up front,
with the other traits blended in underneath [L1967-1990].

## 2. Awareness — where the ad is allowed to begin

Five levels, named as the book names them, each with what the opening must do
and what it must not [L667-972]:

1. **Most Aware** — knows the product, wants it, has not bought. State the
   product and the deal. Explain nothing.
2. **Product-Aware** — knows it, is not sold. Do one of seven jobs on the
   claim already in the market: reinforce, sharpen, extend, document, new
   mechanism, remove a limitation, reposition [L696-710]. Do not reintroduce
   the category from scratch.
3. **Solution-Aware** — carries the desire, does not know a product delivers
   it. Crystallise the desire or its solution in one line, then prove it.
   Do not mention price.
4. **Problem-Aware** — feels the need, has not connected it to a category.
   Name the problem directly. Do not assume a solution is known.
5. **Unaware** — does not know the want exists, or will not admit it. Open on
   pure identification — a portrait the audience recognises. **Do not mention
   price, the product name, or the direct claim** [L917-935].

Every level sits behind a wall from the one next to it: a level-1 opening is
invisible to a level-3 market, and a level-3 opening is stale to a level-1
market [L884-896]. The level decides where the argument may *start* — at the
product, at the desire, or at the problem [L462-473] — and start is not
destination: copy opens where the audience stands and walks it forward
[L2064-2069].

This is the one framework already load-bearing in our chain (the 4a read names
an entry and an exit rung). What the doctrine adds is the **names** and the
explicit **must-not** per rung, which turns a style opinion into a check: an
opening aimed at an unaware audience that names the product is a defect.

→ Slice: `slices/awareness.md`, bound as `{awareness_levels}`.

## 3. Sophistication — what the market has already been sold

A separate question from awareness, and an independent one [L1305-1312]: how
many competing products has this market already been pitched? Five stages,
each with its own move [L1315-1649]:

| Stage | Has already heard | Leads with |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Nothing | The plain claim, stated simply |
| 2 | The plain claim, from competitors | The same claim, enlarged to the edge of belief |
| 3 | Claims at their limit, half-discounted | A NEW MECHANISM — how, not what |
| 4 | A mechanism, from a competitor | The same mechanism, enlarged or improved |
| 5 | Everything, and believes none of it | Identification alone — drop claim and mechanism |

Worked through two whole categories end to end, plus two side cases: opening a
demographic nobody had addressed, on identification alone, and a genuine
category reset — a new *form* of the product, not a new claim about the old
one — which restarts the clock at stage 1 [L1542-1649].

The practical consequence for our chain: a claim borrowed from a stage-2 swipe
reads as behind the market once the category has moved to stage 3, and at
stage 3 or above the mechanism has to **lead**, not appear halfway down.

→ Slice: `slices/sophistication.md`, bound as `{sophistication_stages}`.

## 4. The opening line

The headline has one job: force the next line to be read [L621-645]. It does
not sell and does not carry the offer — the single exception is a Most Aware
audience being shown a price cut. It is not a claim either: it is the entry
point set by awareness plus sophistication, and at levels 3 to 5 it may not
mention the product at all [L603-619, L917-935].

Once the content is fixed — the desire plus the entry rung — dramatising it is
a **separate craft**: measure it, compare it, ask it as a question, state it as
a paradox, and the rest of the 38 [L1673-1846]. These are phrasings of an idea
already chosen. They never substitute for choosing one.

Our hook spec (line + scroll-stopper + verbatim + source + axis) already agrees
with the job description. What the doctrine adds is the **rung test**: check
every hook against its entry level's must-not list before judging its craft.

## 5. The seven techniques — what builds the body

Body copy works three levers: desire (what is wanted), identification (roles
and self-image), and belief (what is already accepted) [L2166-2240]. The seven
techniques are how all three get built.

**Intensification** [L2244-3250] — keep making fresh, concrete pictures of the
desire already fulfilled, never the same picture twice. Thirteen ways, as
numbered: direct description [L2336]; the claim in action [L2448]; bring the
viewer in [L2510]; make it a test [L2541]; stretch it across time [L2603];
bring in an audience [L2646]; bring in experts and their surprise [L2693];
compare against the old way [L2723]; picture the dark side [L2761]; show how
easy it is [L2821]; metaphor and analogy [L2847]; the catalog summary,
horizontal or vertical [L2880]; the guarantee as the closing summary of every
promise [L3013]. Across a campaign: one dominant image, varied only in
disguise [L3066-3249].

**Identification** [L3253-3872] — build a saleable personality into the product
by attaching roles the audience wants to be seen playing: character roles
(adjectives, who I am) and achievement roles (nouns, what I have reached). Find
the product's existing image first. Favourable → intensify it. Neutral or
negative → do not contradict it; bridge from it. Never demand an identity jump
the audience cannot make. This is the technique for parity products.

**Gradualization** [L3876-4539] — belief is structural. A claim's strength is
its own content plus how far the copy has already prepared the audience to
accept it, so build a chain of acceptances from something already believed. Six
devices: the inclusion question [L4359]; detailed identification [L4377];
contradiction of a false belief, with authority [L4402]; the language of logic
[L4424]; real syllogistic chains [L4485]; and the other belief forms —
contingency phrasing, repeated proof, promise-and-proof alternation, paragraph
parallelism [L4519].

**Redefinition** [L4544-4894] — remove an objection by redefining the product
or the problem, before it is consciously raised. Three drawbacks, three
answers: simplification for too-complicated [L4595]; escalation for
too-narrow — connect the small thing to a bigger outcome, or show the need is
constant [L4760]; price reduction for too-expensive — change the comparison
anchor rather than defend the number [L4823]. Plus the direct flip: turn a
liability into the proof of a strength with one idea [L4569].

**Mechanization** [L4896-5132] — verbal proof of HOW, the "reason why" copy
that answers the silent question. Three levels, scaled to the market's
sophistication: name it [L4969], describe it [L4988], or feature it as the lead
claim [L5046]. A price cut needs its own mechanism, or the discount is not
believed [L5079-5131].

**Concentration** [L5136-5432] — destroy the alternative ways of satisfying the
desire. Two shapes: point-by-point bad/good contrast in parallel structure
[L5219-5313], and the before/after time sequence — the familiar failure
pattern, then the same sequence succeeding [L5316-5416]. The rule: never attack
a weakness without supplying the fix for that exact weakness in the same
breath. Skip the technique entirely when the category is already dominated.

**Camouflage** [L5436-5661] — borrow believability from the medium. Three
methods: format [L5474-5542], phraseology [L5544-5574], and register —
understatement (plain, short, adjective-light) or deadly sincerity, where a
real limitation is admitted openly so the claim that follows lands harder
[L5575-5661]. This is the native placement, in the doctrine's own terms.

→ Slice: `slices/techniques.md`, bound as `{techniques}`. Each technique
carries its own owning stage, research question and scene shape there.

### The sections, and what builds each

The list in `frameworks.json` is the list — never one it does not carry, never
a renamed one. The mapping, from the techniques above: hook = intensification
(the single sharpest image) + camouflage; problem = intensification (detailed
symptoms) + gradualization (the inclusion question); failed solutions =
concentration; root cause = redefinition + gradualization; unique mechanism =
mechanization, at the level the sophistication stage demands; solution =
intensification + identification; product = redefinition (the price anchor) +
identification; offer = intensification #13 (the guarantee) + redefinition;
call to action = gradualization's conclusion, where acting is the only step
left.

**Six more, added 2026-09-18 on the owner's ask** — the nine above came from
memory, and the ask was what a fundamental direct-response reading still
leaves out. These
are the answer, and any of them can be struck: identification = the call-out
that names the viewer to themselves before any problem (the unaware opening
[L873-972]); agitation = the cost of leaving it alone, the black side of the
picture [L2761]; proof = the evidence beat placed where doubt peaks
[L5694-5808]; transformation = the fulfilled desire pictured across time
[L2510, L2603]; objections = the flinch answered and redefined before the ask
[L4544-4894]; urgency = why now, real and receipted, never invented scarcity.

Note that **mechanism** is the doctrine's own word for that section — it is
the one place the book hands us an exact label, so we use it and not a synonym
[L1405-1521].

## 6. Mood, proof, and the close

**Mood** [L6260-6361] — word choice and sentence rhythm set tone deliberately
for the writer and invisibly for the audience. If the trick words get noticed,
the copy has failed and gets rewritten. Rhythm carries mood independently of
content, which for us is VO pacing and cut pacing, not only vocabulary.

**Verification** [L5694-5808] — three things that are commonly confused:
mechanization is logical demonstration and needs no outside facts;
documentation is any external proof; **verification is the placement** of that
documentation where acceptance is highest. Proof lands where the audience
already, unconsciously, demands it — not front-loaded because it is proof. And
proof copy is written as selling copy: dramatic, specific, short.

**Offer and close** — not a chapter of its own. The guarantee is the final
intensification beat, restating every promise inside the risk reversal
[L3013-3065]; price is always set against a bigger anchor, never stated bare
[L4823-4886]; a discount carries its own believable reason [L5079-5131]; and
the ask is the conclusion of the accepted-step chain, the only step left
[L4485-4518].

→ Slices: `slices/mood.md` `{mood}`, `slices/verification.md` `{verification}`,
`slices/offer-close.md` `{offer_close}`.

## 7. Research — where the words come from

Before motivation research existed, the method was personal digging, reading,
talking — and guessing [L1909-1910]. The market is treated as a running
conversation: ideas go out as ads, reactions come back, and a sensitive writer
turns that feedback into an explicit research question [L1932-1965]. Three
worked examples show the move — each one turns a vague puzzle into a question
narrow enough to answer.

Two rules ride with it. A research finding is **a direction, not a headline**:
it says where not to waste time and roughly where the answer sits, and turning
it into copy is still the same creative work [L1922-1931]. And the moment to
fire one is mechanical, not optional — any awareness or sophistication judgment
call, or an injection that cannot tell whether a claim will land, is a research
question, never a guess.

Our sixteen questions, each mapped to the language-bank tags that answer it and
the stages it feeds, are in `slices/research-questions.md`, bound as
`{research_questions}`.

## What this doctrine does not settle

- Nothing about scene *length* — the book has no opinion on it and neither does
  this component.
- The section list is the owner's, not the book's: the book names the hook, the
  problem and the mechanism, and the rest are ours, built from the techniques
  above. That gap is stated rather than papered over. The six added on
  2026-09-18 are marked in `frameworks.json` with an `added` line so it stays
  obvious which came from memory and which from the reading.
- Everything here is content, not craft. Choosing the desire, the rung and the
  stage tells a writer what to say. It never writes the line.

## The sections are a vocabulary, not a sequence

The sections in the list (hook · problem · failed solutions · root cause ·
unique mechanism · solution · product · offer · call to action ·
identification · agitation · proof · transformation · objections · urgency)
name what a part of an asset is DOING. Any ad carries any subset, in any order, with repeats, and the framework
the source follows — PAS, AIDA, story, mechanism-led, a direct offer — is read
off the asset and named, never imposed. The awareness level decides which
sections an asset needs at all [L667-972]: most-aware goes straight to the
offer; unaware opens on identification before a problem is named. The point of
labelling is that every beat is known for the job it holds, so a brief can be
fleshed out and the writing can get creative systematically. The crosswalk from
the common frameworks to the list lives in `frameworks.json` and renders into
`slices/sections.md`.
