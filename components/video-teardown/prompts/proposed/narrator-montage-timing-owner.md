# Narrator montage: time budget per part (proposed)

Status: proposed, not in use. Written for `components/video-production/formats/narrator-montage/FORMAT.md`, "What it is". If the owner accepts it, the column and the notes below go into that table; until then FORMAT.md is unchanged.

| Part | What is on screen | What is heard | Time budget (share of runtime · at 40 s) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | whatever the chosen hook is (a slot) | the hook's own sound | about 30% · 11–12 s |
| Body | short shots: the product, people using it, how it feels | one narrator, one continuous read | about 55% · 21–23 s |
| Proof | a real customer's before and after | the narrator | about 5% · 2–3 s, held: the slowest shot in the piece |
| Offer and ask | the product, the offer, the ask | the narrator | about 10% · 4–5 s |

- The budgets are a guide for the plan and the writing pass. A part that needs more seconds takes them from another part, inside the planned runtime, where it can. When the runtime cannot give them, the writing pass's own length rule stands: the overshoot goes in FLAGS with how much, and the owner decides whether to cut a move or accept the longer cut; `plan.md` says how many seconds.
- Where they come from: the first swipe carried on this format runs 40.4 s: hook 0:00–0:12.1, body 0:12.1–0:36.0, offer and ask 0:36.0–0:40.4. It has no proof shot; the proof share is the hold its first brief added (about 2 s). A longer piece keeps the shares.
- The proof is the payoff, so it gets the longest single hold even though its part is short; link shots in the body get the shortest.
- Every part changes something the viewer can see (the stakes, the evidence or who is winning) at least once every 15 seconds.
- The first run writes the measured part times into `LEARNINGS.md`; the budgets change only through that entry and the owner's yes.
