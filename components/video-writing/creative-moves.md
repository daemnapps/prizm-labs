# Creative moves bank

Real moves, taken from real high-performing swipes, for making the root cause, mechanism, proof, failed solutions, objections, offer, guarantee, call to action and the turn into the problem as alive as the story beats.
Read by the video writing line. Every example is the swipe's own words, word for word, at the path given; `T` numbers are the lines in that transcript. Headings use the ids in `components/marketing-doctrine/frameworks.json`.

## root-cause

- **Name**: The rule-out list
- **What it does**: Crosses off every usual suspect, one short line each, so the real cause lands alone.
- **How to use it**: List the three things she already blames, deny each in its own short sentence, then name the real cause in one word.
- **Real example**: "This is not sun damage. This is not age. This is not bad skin care. This is estrogen." — @shenmuhealer Instagram, `runs/video-teardown/_work/fmt-shenmu-dbd9fxkijjf/transcript.json` T7, 729K views.

- **Name**: The wrong-problem swap
- **What it does**: Moves the fault from a character flaw to a body process.
- **How to use it**: Say it in two lines: it is not a [willpower word] problem, it is a [body word] problem.
- **Real example**: "Your gut isn't a discipline problem. It's a hormone problem." — Mars Men paid ad, written ad text, `components/swipe-paid/marsmen/blocks/79_fix-your-hormones-lose-the-gut.md`, views not on file; 32 ad variants in the block.

- **Name**: The household picture
- **What it does**: Makes an invisible process something you can see on a kitchen table.
- **How to use it**: Compare the part of the body to a common object, then show the size mismatch with a second object.
- **Real example**: "Think of your skin like a tennis net." — Frøya paid ad, written ad text, `components/swipe-paid/froya/blocks/03_harvard-study-we-ve-been-applying-anti-aging-creams.md`, views not on file; 204 ad variants in the block.

- **Name**: The small worker with one job
- **What it does**: Turns a chemistry word into a character.
- **How to use it**: Introduce the hidden cause as one thing in the body, give it its one job, and let that job do the harm.
- **Real example**: "There's an enzyme in your body called aromatase. Its job is to convert testosterone into estrogen." — Mars Men paid ad, written ad text, `components/swipe-paid/marsmen/blocks/26_block-estrogen-burn-chest-fat.md`, views not on file; 4,719 ad variants in the block.

- **Name**: The cause told through a friend
- **What it does**: Lets a person discover the cause, so nobody gets lectured.
- **How to use it**: Tell the cause as what happened to someone you know, then drop it in as one thing you would say to a friend.
- **Real example**: "My boy used to drop like 300 a bottle. Sharpest dude I know, but by the time we'd link up at night, couldn't smell a thing on him. And he had no idea why. Cause here's the thing, it was never the scent, it's the skin." — Penrose paid video ad 156440932, `runs/video-teardown/_work/156440932/transcript.json` T2–T3, views not on file; 2 ad variants.

- **Name**: Take the blame off her
- **What it does**: Relief comes first, and the viewer keeps listening to whoever cleared her.
- **How to use it**: Close the cause with a line that hands the fault to the object, not the person.
- **Real example**: "If your foundation makes you look older by the afternoon, it's not your face. It's the bottle." — Brand C paid ad, written ad text, `components/swipe-paid/Brand C/blocks/24_1-5m-women-over-40-switched-to-this-foundation.md`, views not on file; 13 ad variants in the block.

- **Name**: The test at the table
- **What it does**: The viewer proves the cause on her own body while she watches.
- **How to use it**: Give one small physical test she can do right now with her own hand, then read the result to her.
- **Real example**: "Let me just ask you one question. Is it firm or does it fold? Pinch the skin on the back of your hand right now." — Aesun skit ad, Instagram, `runs/video-teardown/_work/en-aesunnutrition-dcuazyssla/transcript.json` T5, 18K views.

- **Name**: Then versus now
- **What it does**: A family comparison shows the cause is new and came from outside.
- **How to use it**: Compare the viewer to a parent or grandparent at the same age, then name what changed.
- **Real example**: "Your grandfather had more testosterone at 60 than you do at 35." — Mars Men paid ad, written ad text, `components/swipe-paid/marsmen/blocks/01_50-off-free-gifts.md`, views not on file; 2,411 ad variants in the block.

## unique-mechanism

- **Name**: The everyday analogy
- **What it does**: One familiar object explains the whole thing in five words.
- **How to use it**: Replace the science sentence with "this is like a [thing she already buys] for your [body part]".
- **Real example**: "This is like an energy drink for the skin." — @midlife.nursing TikTok, `runs/video-teardown/_work/tts-midlife-nursing-025118/transcript.json` T6, 26.1M views.

- **Name**: Every ingredient gets one verb
- **What it does**: A dull ingredient list becomes a team where each player has a job.
- **How to use it**: Name each ingredient once and give it one plain verb, no adjectives, in one breath.
- **Real example**: "The berberine holds your sugar flat, cinnamon softens the spike, chromium burns it off, and gymnema kills the cravings." — @dr.rafaelvega Instagram, `runs/video-teardown/_work/sq-01-grandma-house-face-x-dcmj1t8ntdz/transcript.json` T8, 1.2M views.

- **Name**: The stranger who explains it
- **What it does**: The mechanism comes out of a chance meeting, not out of the brand.
- **How to use it**: Put the explanation in the mouth of someone the narrator bumps into, and keep it to one idea.
- **Real example**: "You see, our hair follicles aren’t actually dead, they are just dormant" — Spartan paid ad, written ad text, `components/swipe-paid/sprtn/blocks/14_spartan-us.md`, views not on file; 22 ad variants in the block.

- **Name**: Point at it while it works
- **What it does**: The product shows the mechanism, the words only point.
- **How to use it**: Tell the viewer exactly where to look, and tie that detail to the result on screen.
- **Real example**: "Look closely at that tube, because that's a huge part of why you're getting that separation." — Blushe live TV-shop demo, Instagram, `runs/video-teardown/_work/fmt-tv-shop-dd7gvdvprzl/transcript.json` T5, views not on file.

- **Name**: The mechanism as a fight
- **What it does**: The ingredient has an enemy, so the explanation has a plot.
- **How to use it**: Say what the ingredient attacks, using a crime or fight verb.
- **Real example**: "Tongkat Ali blocks the enzyme stealing your T" — Mars Men paid ad, written ad text, `components/swipe-paid/marsmen/blocks/01_50-off-free-gifts.md`, views not on file; 2,411 ad variants in the block.

- **Name**: Built on what she already does
- **What it does**: The mechanism fits a habit she already has, so nothing new gets asked of her.
- **How to use it**: Name the habit in her day and slot the product inside it.
- **Real example**: "You already shower. You're already standing there with wet hair." — Spartan paid ad, written ad text, `components/swipe-paid/sprtn/blocks/13_spartan-us.md`, views not on file; 27 ad variants in the block.

- **Name**: The kitchen-table experiment
- **What it does**: The maker doubts the idea out loud, and the viewer roots for it.
- **How to use it**: Tell how it was made as a question, then list the doubts, ending on one blunt word.
- **Real example**: "So we asked ourselves, what would happen if instead we paired this magical superfood with nature's most nutrient-dense sweetener, organic dates? Would it taste any good? Would it still feel like chocolate? Or would it suck?" — Conscious Bar paid video ad, `runs/video-teardown/_work/conscious-never-candy/transcript.json` T11–T13, views not on file; 1 ad variant.

- **Name**: The survival story of the ingredient
- **What it does**: Where it grew explains why it is strong.
- **How to use it**: Tell the hard place the ingredient comes from, then say that hardship is the strength.
- **Real example**: "These plants grow where winters are brutal, so they pack in dense nutrients to survive, and that potency literally revives your face." — Frøya paid ad, written ad text, `components/swipe-paid/froya/blocks/12_everyone-asks-if-i-ve-had-work-done.md`, views not on file; 22 ad variants in the block.

## proof

- **Name**: The spouse who notices first
- **What it does**: Someone with no reason to flatter delivers the result, and the narrator denies it.
- **How to use it**: Have a husband or wife notice in a passing moment, then have the narrator wave it off.
- **Real example**: "Somewhere around the two-month mark my wife runs her hand through my hair and goes "there's more here than there used to be." I told her she was seeing things." — Spartan paid ad, written ad text, `components/swipe-paid/sprtn/blocks/22_spartan-us.md`, views not on file; 15 ad variants in the block.

- **Name**: It spreads through the circle
- **What it does**: Proof climbs from one person to everyone he knows.
- **How to use it**: Line up the people who noticed in order, each with one small reaction.
- **Real example**: "Then the guys at coffee started with it. Then my son came by, took one look, and got that smug grin like he'd been right all along." — Spartan paid ad, written ad text, `components/swipe-paid/sprtn/blocks/22_spartan-us.md`, views not on file; 15 ad variants in the block.

- **Name**: Half and half, live
- **What it does**: One side treated, one side not, nothing to hide behind.
- **How to use it**: Treat one side only, say it is being done live, and tell the viewer to watch.
- **Real example**: "We're doing this completely live, one eye, one coat of blushe. Watch what happens." — Blushe live TV-shop demo, Instagram, `runs/video-teardown/_work/fmt-tv-shop-dd7gvdvprzl/transcript.json` T1, views not on file.

- **Name**: The gross evidence
- **What it does**: What came out is more believable than what went on.
- **How to use it**: Show what the product pulled out, then give the honest reaction, disgust and delight together.
- **Real example**: "That's oil and blackheads that it's pulled out from places that I didn't even know were clogged. I'm equal parts grossed out and obsessed." — @patchfacelabs Instagram, `runs/video-teardown/_work/fs-patchfacelabs/transcript.json` T1, 2.6M views.

- **Name**: The almost-quit week
- **What it does**: A dated diary with one low point reads as true.
- **How to use it**: Log it by week, and put in the week you nearly stopped and what kept you going.
- **Real example**: "Around week 6. This is where I almost quit, because your eyes lie to you when you look every day. The photos didn't lie though." — Spartan paid ad, written ad text, `components/swipe-paid/sprtn/blocks/10_18-000-men-made-the-switch.md`, views not on file; 19 ad variants in the block.

- **Name**: Admit what is left
- **What it does**: A small remaining flaw makes the big result believable.
- **How to use it**: State what has not gone away before you state what has.
- **Real example**: "even though I do still have some marks, these literally used to be active flare-ups and now they are completely flat, which I think is a huge improvement." — @mcjerbear TikTok, `runs/video-teardown/_work/tts-mcjerbear-009869/transcript.json` T9–T10, 6.7M views.

- **Name**: Counted by what they did next
- **What it does**: A repeat purchase beats any percentage.
- **How to use it**: Prove it with a buying behaviour: which bottle they are on, and what they said before they started.
- **Real example**: "Men who wrote the whole category off after retinol burned them are on their third bottle." — Brand E Lab paid video ad 154995621 (the transcript hears the name as "Cerlena"), `runs/video-teardown/_work/test-chain-one-person/transcript.json` T20, views not on file; 351 ad variants in its block.

- **Name**: The complaint that is really praise
- **What it does**: A fake grievance slips the result past the viewer's guard.
- **How to use it**: Open as a complaint, then deny the bad reasons by listing the good results as the things it is not about.
- **Real example**: "No, it's not even because my husband said I look like I did when we first met because my nasolabial folds are no longer drooping." — @kinnnyb TikTok, `runs/video-teardown/_work/tts-kinnnyb-716558/transcript.json` T1, 7.7M views.

## failed-solutions

- **Name**: The receipt total
- **What it does**: One dollar figure says how long she has been at this.
- **How to use it**: Add up what was spent and say the figure in the narrator's own embarrassment.
- **Real example**: "I literally spent $700 this year alone trying to lighten them" — @storieguinn TikTok, `runs/video-teardown/_work/tts-storieguinn-292429/transcript.json` T3, 5.1M views.

- **Name**: The drawer tour
- **What it does**: Failed products become a physical place she recognises.
- **How to use it**: Walk through the drawer or cabinet and give each item one line on why it failed.
- **Real example**: "I had a drawer full of hairloss products I didn’t trust anymore. An oil that made my hair look greasy. A serum I used for three months and still couldn’t tell if anything changed." — Spartan paid ad, written ad text, `components/swipe-paid/sprtn/blocks/06_18-000-men-made-the-switch.md`, views not on file; 24 ad variants in the block.

- **Name**: Name it and catch the feeling
- **What it does**: Admits why the old product seemed to work, then breaks it.
- **How to use it**: Grant what she feels when she uses it, then turn that exact feeling into the flaw.
- **Real example**: "I know they feel like they're doing something in the mirror. That's exactly the problem." — Brand E Lab paid video ad 154995621 (the transcript hears the name as "Cerlena"), `runs/video-teardown/_work/test-chain-one-person/transcript.json` T3–T4, views not on file; 351 ad variants in its block.

- **Name**: The question and the one-line kill
- **What it does**: Clears a whole category in five seconds.
- **How to use it**: Ask the category as a one-word question, answer with one flat reason it never gets there.
- **Real example**: "Collagen powders? Broken down in digestion before your skin ever sees them." — Brand C paid ad, written ad text, `components/swipe-paid/Brand C/blocks/03_collagen-creams-can-t-absorb-this-serum-can.md`, views not on file; 239 ad variants in the block.

- **Name**: The hour it fails
- **What it does**: Failure pinned to a time of day she has lived.
- **How to use it**: Give the time it looks fine and the time it stops, then describe what she sees.
- **Real example**: "Looked fine at 8am. By 3pm it was sitting in my smile lines, turning orange, and making me look older than my bare skin." — Brand C paid ad, written ad text, `components/swipe-paid/Brand C/blocks/24_1-5m-women-over-40-switched-to-this-foundation.md`, views not on file; 13 ad variants in the block.

- **Name**: The expert sold her more
- **What it does**: The professional route is shown as a bill, not as a mistake.
- **How to use it**: Tell the appointment as a scene, and end on a price comparison from her own life.
- **Real example**: "Then she handed me a 12-step routine that cost me more than my monthly rent." — Frøya paid ad, written ad text, `components/swipe-paid/froya/blocks/06_your-skincare-is-making-you-age-faster.md`, views not on file; 35 ad variants in the block.

- **Name**: She did not quit, she got tired
- **What it does**: Says the feeling under the failures, and it reads like being understood.
- **How to use it**: After the list of failures, name the feeling in two short sentences, without a product in them.
- **Real example**: "You don’t stop caring. You just get tired of being sold hope." — Spartan paid ad, written ad text, `components/swipe-paid/sprtn/blocks/06_18-000-men-made-the-switch.md`, views not on file; 24 ad variants in the block.

- **Name**: The bottle under the sink
- **What it does**: The failure is real life getting in the way, not the viewer.
- **How to use it**: Show the routine dying in one plain sentence with a count of days and where the bottle ended up.
- **Real example**: "You do it for nine days, life gets busy, and the bottle ends up under the sink." — Spartan paid ad, written ad text, `components/swipe-paid/sprtn/blocks/13_spartan-us.md`, views not on file; 27 ad variants in the block.

## objections

- **Name**: Say the doubt first, and flip it
- **What it does**: Saying it first takes it away from the viewer and turns it into a reason.
- **How to use it**: Say the doubt in her words, then turn the same words into proof.
- **Real example**: "I know it sounds too simple, but that's exactly why the industry gate keeps it." — @wellness.with.gloria Instagram, `runs/video-teardown/_work/sq-01-grandma-house-face-x-dcraqytan5c/transcript.json` T9, 175K views.

- **Name**: Tell them what they expect you to say
- **What it does**: Plays against the viewer's guess, so the claim gets a hearing.
- **How to use it**: Name what she assumes you are about to say, refuse it, and state the plain fact.
- **Real example**: "Some of you are probably expecting me to call this out as BS, but I'm not going to, because it's real." — @dannyguo.md Instagram, `runs/video-teardown/_work/fmt-dannyguo-dscbuh9caej/transcript.json` T2, 4.1M views.

- **Name**: The friend asks the hard questions
- **What it does**: The objections come from a person, so they are not answered at the viewer.
- **How to use it**: Have the second speaker ask the rude questions out loud, one per line.
- **Real example**: "And it's legit? How much weight loss are we realistically talking about?" — Hims paid video ad, `runs/video-teardown/_work/dx-hims-04-wegovy/transcript.json` T5, views not on file.

- **Name**: I thought it was a waste of money
- **What it does**: The narrator starts as the doubter, so the viewer stands beside him.
- **How to use it**: Open as the person who did not want it, and say who pushed it on him.
- **Real example**: "My son bought me a hair shampoo and I'll be honest, I thought it was a waste of his money." — Spartan paid ad, written ad text, `components/swipe-paid/sprtn/blocks/22_spartan-us.md`, views not on file; 15 ad variants in the block.

- **Name**: Even I can manage this
- **What it does**: The effort objection is answered with a joke on the narrator.
- **How to use it**: Shrink the whole commitment to one sentence, then make fun of yourself.
- **Real example**: "You wash your hair with it instead of your regular one. That's the whole commitment. Even I can't mess that up." — Spartan paid ad, written ad text, `components/swipe-paid/sprtn/blocks/22_spartan-us.md`, views not on file; 15 ad variants in the block.

- **Name**: I use it on my own family
- **What it does**: Safety answered by what the speaker does at home, not by a claim.
- **How to use it**: Answer "is it safe" with what you do with your own people.
- **Real example**: "yes, it's safe in kids, I do it in mine once in a while." — @dannyguo.md Instagram, `runs/video-teardown/_work/fmt-dannyguo-dscbuh9caej/transcript.json` T8, 4.1M views.

- **Name**: Respect the reason they waited
- **What it does**: Agrees that holding back makes sense, then offers a smaller step.
- **How to use it**: State why the sensible person has not acted, in a "not because, because" pair.
- **Real example**: "Not because they don’t care about their hair. Because they do. They just don’t want their first move to be a prescription, a drug that messes with hormones, or something they feel stuck with forever." — Spartan paid ad, written ad text, `components/swipe-paid/sprtn/blocks/08_spartan-us.md`, views not on file; 24 ad variants in the block.

## offer

- **Name**: The bigger sacrifice
- **What it does**: Sets the price beside the drastic thing they were about to do.
- **How to use it**: Name the costly alternative with its real figure, then offer the small step before it.
- **Real example**: "Before you hand some guy $5,000 to rearrange your scalp in a back-alley clinic halfway around the world… maybe try washing your hair with the right shampoo first." — Spartan paid ad, written ad text, `components/swipe-paid/sprtn/blocks/23_spartan-us.md`, views not on file; 12 ad variants in the block.

- **Name**: The price, said flat
- **What it does**: Two numbers and one word, spoken like a tip to a friend.
- **How to use it**: Give your price and the old price in one short line, with no selling words.
- **Real example**: "You can get the best scents for 35 bucks a jar, not 300." — Penrose paid video ad 156440932, `runs/video-teardown/_work/156440932/transcript.json` T9, views not on file; 2 ad variants.

- **Name**: What the old one costs to make
- **What it does**: Turns the other brand's price into the scandal.
- **How to use it**: Reveal what the viewer really paid for, using the shelf price and the cost inside it.
- **Real example**: "the liquid inside that $200 bottle you just bought, it costs about $3 to make" — Penrose paid video ad 158667419, `runs/video-teardown/_work/158667419/transcript.json` T2, views not on file; 4 ad variants.

- **Name**: I overpaid so you don't have to
- **What it does**: The narrator warns her off full price, so the discount reads as advice.
- **How to use it**: Admit what you paid, say it was too much, and tell her to wait for the sale.
- **Real example**: "do not pay full price. I paid like $67, which was a little too much." — @millennialmagss TikTok, `runs/video-teardown/_work/tts-millennialmagss-269150/transcript.json` T7, 14.7M views.

- **Name**: Set it against what she pays now
- **What it does**: Compares the price to a bill she already has, not to nothing.
- **How to use it**: Price it against the thing she keeps paying for today.
- **Real example**: "two gummies a day that cost less than a month of antibiotics" — Nuora paid ad, written ad text, `components/swipe-paid/nuora/blocks/32_new-vaginal-odor-research.md`, views not on file; 17 ad variants in the block.

- **Name**: Say what the deal is not
- **What it does**: Knocks down the fine print she expects.
- **How to use it**: Name the two tricks she suspects, deny them, then state the term in plain words.
- **Real example**: "Not a first-order discount. Not a trial gimmick. Every single shipment - half price - as long as you stay subscribed." — Mars Men paid ad, written ad text, `components/swipe-paid/marsmen/blocks/54_50-off-for-life.md`, views not on file; 34 ad variants in the block.

- **Name**: Learn from my credit card
- **What it does**: The bundle sells through the narrator's own mistake.
- **How to use it**: Warn against buying the parts one at a time, and say you did it yourself.
- **Real example**: "Now, you can go out and grab these all up separately, but be prepared to run your credit card up. Just ask me how I know." — @kinnnyb TikTok, `runs/video-teardown/_work/tts-kinnnyb-716558/transcript.json` T4, 7.7M views.

- **Name**: Tell them to buy less
- **What it does**: Talking the viewer down from the big pack earns trust.
- **How to use it**: Say which part of the range she can skip and name the only pieces she needs.
- **Real example**: "Now, of course, you can get the whole bundle, but I'm gonna be real with you, you only need to use these two." — @ajlittle0902 TikTok, `runs/video-teardown/_work/tts-ajlittle0902-330846/transcript.json` T8, 9.1M views.

## offer (guarantee)

The framework has no separate guarantee section; it sits inside `offer`. Every line here is written ad text, not speech. The spoken transcripts only ever state the guarantee flat, so no spoken move came out of them.

- **Name**: Her own before photo as the test
- **What it does**: The guarantee is measured with the viewer's own evidence.
- **How to use it**: Name the exact picture or moment that decides if she gets her money back.
- **Real example**: "If your day-90 photos look like your day-zero photo, you get your money back." — Spartan paid ad, written ad text, `components/swipe-paid/sprtn/blocks/10_18-000-men-made-the-switch.md`, views not on file; 19 ad variants in the block.

- **Name**: The result or your money
- **What it does**: The promise and the refund fit in one breath.
- **How to use it**: Put the single visible result before the word "or", and nothing else.
- **Real example**: "Sharp jawline or your money back." — Mars Men paid ad, written ad text, `components/swipe-paid/marsmen/blocks/37_crush-cortisol-sharpen-your-jaw.md`, views not on file; 45 ad variants in the block.

- **Name**: Worst case, best case
- **What it does**: Lays out the bet so the downside looks empty.
- **How to use it**: Say the worst case in three words, then the best case in the viewer's own daily moment.
- **Real example**: "Worst case you lose nothing. Best case you stop feeling like a teenager every time you look in the mirror." — Brand D paid ad, written ad text, `components/swipe-paid/Brand D/blocks/07_redness-gone-in-2-weeks.md`, views not on file; 104 ad variants in the block.

- **Name**: The guarantee is why I bought

- **Name**: No argument

## call-to-action

- **Name**: If the cart is there, it is in stock
- **What it does**: The link itself becomes the news.
- **How to use it**: Tell her what seeing the button means, not just to press it.
- **Real example**: "So if you see that cart, it's in stock." — @midlife.nursing TikTok, `runs/video-teardown/_work/tts-midlife-nursing-025118/transcript.json` T17, 26.1M views.

- **Name**: Save it and check back
