# The creativity ladder

Five rungs. The rung says how far the piece may travel from its source. It
never says how far it may travel from the doctrine — the floor below is the
same on every rung.

## The floor (every rung, no exceptions)

1. **One discrete problem**, named the way the reader names it.
2. **One awareness entry**, by its id — the piece begins its argument only
   where that level allows.
3. **One mass desire** the piece walks into, in the reader's terms.
4. **The mechanism is carried by someone or something inside the piece** —
   never a lecture bolted onto the end.
5. **Proof only from the brand's files.** An invented proof is worse than none.
6. **It ends on the brand's close** exactly as the run's rulings state it.

## The rungs

### L1 — Straight swap (`swap`)
The source's script, beat for beat. Only the brand's words go in. Nothing
about the world, the cast or the plot changes.
*Use when:* the source is proven and the only question is whether our message
rides it.

### L2 — Remix (`remix`)
The source's structure stays — its beats, its timing, its turn. The world
changes: new setting, new cast, new situation, chosen because it is the
reader's world.
*Use when:* the structure works but the source's world belongs to someone else.

### L3 — Transplant (`transplant`)
A whole kind of entertainment is borrowed — a show, a film genre, an era of
TV, a music genre, a sport broadcast, a game. Its conventions carry the piece.
The brand is injected through the role map: hero (the reader), villain (the
problem, given a face), the move (the product as the show's big moment), the
voice (whoever the show lets explain — carries the mechanism), the replay
(where the show slows down — the mechanism scene), the verdict (how it ends —
the close).
*Use when:* the reader already loves a thing and the brand can live inside it.
Nostalgia lives here.

### L4 — Story (`story`)
An original plot. Characters who want something, an obstacle, a turn, stakes,
a payoff. The doctrine is the spine: the awareness entry is where the story
starts, the mass desire is what the hero wants, the mechanism is the turn,
the close is the resolution.
*Use when:* the reader needs to feel it before they will hear it.

### L5 — Auteur (`auteur`)
Concept first. A premise nobody in the category has run — a song, a short
film, a mockumentary, a genre mash, a formal trick. It may not look like an ad
until the last beat. The doctrine is invisible on the surface and fully
present underneath; the producer checks the floor exactly as hard as on L1.
*Use when:* the category is numb (sophistication stage 4–5) and only a new
experience gets through.
