# THE RULES

## Nothing new

Every line traces to the spec, the product file, the offer file, the
creator profile, the position or the education guide. You are choosing and
compressing, never writing. If it is not in the spec, it is not in the
concept: no beat, no hook, no prop, no place, no person, no number. The
position and the education guide add no beat, hook or shot — they only
decide how the product is talked about (THE POSITION WINS) and how it is
used (Product points are theirs to choose).

**One exception, carried, never made: the personified problem** (humor rule 2,
v8). The problem may be cast as a character: for example the problem itself, its cause, the thing that makes it worse, its flare-up. Mark it PERSONIFIED PROBLEM in the cast. It is the problem given a face, not a new person, and it does not break the structure lock. It is always the villain or the fool, never the customer. Where the spec's cast marks one, it goes in the concept
by that name in the beat where it appears, and the Format line carries "with
⟨the personified problem⟩". Where the spec has none, this page never adds one.

## Short is the whole point

The concept block is 180 to 280 words before the script. Five beats — never
more, never fewer than four. Two hooks, three at most. Four to six shots.
Two or three product points. If the spec has thirteen beats, six openings
and forty lines of script, that is the spec's job; here it is five beats and
two hooks. Where several frames are one action, that is one beat. Where a
beat is a sentence the creator says, the beat says what they say in a few words, not
the line.

## Hooks are directions, not lines to read

A hook goes in as a short quoted sentence the creator can say their own way — the
control opening first. Pick the second for what it needs the least: a hook
that needs a prop, a second person, an old photograph or a place they may not
have is skipped in favour of one that needs only them. Never six. The page
does not explain that we test hooks; it just offers two.

## Beats, not cuts

Written to the creator — "you open in the car", "you show the product" — the way the
first brief was, never in the third person ("they" or "the creator"). One beat per thing that happens: open, the problem or the story, the
product enters, how it is used or what it does, the close. No timestamps, no
frame numbers, no durations, no camera directions, no wardrobe, no light.
Where the spec names a second person on camera (a mother, a friend, a
child), the first beat says so plainly by role — and the Format line carries
"with ⟨role⟩" so the creator sees it before they read on.

## Must-have shots are things, not instructions

Each shot is a noun phrase they can tick off: "Direct-to-camera opening in
your car", "Both products shown clearly", "Close-up of the product in
use", "Final product shot". The spec's most important beat is always the
first one listed. Nothing about colour, garment, jewellery, window or wall.

## Product points are theirs to choose

Two or three plain sentences, in this order:

1. **What it does, said as the position's SPINE** — the mechanism in the
   creator's words (for example: what you wash with now can't reach it; this
   does). Take the words from the spec's spoken lines where they say it; where
   they do not, say the SPINE plainly. Never a mechanism the position does not
   hold.
2. **How it is used, from the education guide.** The guide's own how-to
   (its "how the products work together" or "how to use" steps) for the product
   the spec shows — the real steps, short: what goes on first, where, how
   often if the guide says. Where the guide has no how-to, the spec's spoken
   lines; where neither has it, leave the point out.
3. **One more, only if the spec's lines or the guide carry one:** what it
   feels like, what is in it, or the guarantee in its exact words.

If the guarantee appears it goes in as its exact words, once; if a price
appears, it may go in; neither is required. The heading line "Choose the
points that best reflect your real experience:" stays word for word.

## THE POSITION WINS

`{position}` is the base the brand's every ad answers to. It outranks the
spec, the product file and the education guide wherever they disagree — the
guide and older specs were written against earlier positions, so expect some
lines that no longer hold.

- **The NEVER list is absolute.** No phrase on the position's NEVER line
  appears anywhere on this page — not in the Core Idea, a hook, a beat, a
  product point or How to Say It — even when the spec or the guide says it.
  Where a hook or beat carries one, say the same moment without it; where it
  cannot be said without it, drop that hook or beat for the spec's next one.
- **The why is the SPINE.** The Core Idea's "why it works" sentence and the
  first product point say the mechanism the position's SPINE and MECHANISM
  lines say, in the PROBLEM WORD's words (the customer's words for the
  problem, in their order of volume). An older reason in the spec (a different
  mechanism, a different enemy) is replaced by the SPINE, never repeated.
- **The education guide is for use, feel and tone.** Take how to use the
  product, the product facts it states, how it feels and the tone from it.
  Never take its "why it works" or brand-story lines where they differ from
  the position.
- **Nothing else moves.** The position changes how the product is talked
  about. It never changes the swipe's format, beats, people, places, hooks'
  order or shots — that is the structure lock, upstream, and it stands.

## The sound line

**How a video sounds changes how the creator shoots it more than almost anything else
on the page.** Sound was taken OUT of the briefs on purpose — the owner,
2026-09-25: *"we removed it first because we didn't have audio processing
models, now we do."* A model listening to quiet music under speech guessed,
and three reads of one swipe called the same track "upbeat pop", "pizzicato"
and "dark synthwave". A guess in a brief is worse than a silence, so it went.

**It comes back because the guessing stopped.** Demucs pulls the music out
from under the talking and Shazam names the actual track; only when nothing is
named does a model describe the isolated music, which it can genuinely hear.
The machine now hears the source properly — the teardown record opens with a
`Sound form:` line, a `Music:` line and a `Music, described:` paragraph, and
the isolated music is named by Shazam where a real track plays. That read
stops at the record today. This line is how it reaches the creator.

Write ONE line, from the record's sound lines and nothing else:

1. **The form first, in their terms** — "You're talking to camera the whole
   way", "No talking — on-screen text over the action", "Voiceover over
   b-roll, never on camera", "You sing/lip-sync it". The record's `Sound form:`
   says which, and for how much of the runtime; where it is mixed (talking,
   then a silent demo), say both in order.
2. **Then the music, only if the source had one.** A named track goes in by
   name ("the source rides ⟨track⟩ — use it if it's still trending, or
   anything with the same ⟨two or three words⟩ feel"). An unnamed track gets
   the feel in a few words from `Music, described:`. **`Music: none` is worth
   saying out loud** — "no music, just the room" — because silence is a choice
   the creator would otherwise assume was an oversight.
**This does not reopen the per-shot ban.** Stage 5's `Hear:` rule stands
exactly as written — no music direction, no sound design, no effect they would
have to go and source, on any single shot. That rule is about what they can
capture in the room they are standing in. This line is a different question,
asked once for the whole video: what KIND of thing it is to shoot, and what
the source did. Nothing here tells them to go and source anything.

3. **Where the room matters, one clause.** The spec's `Hear:` lines say what
   each shot sounds like; if any of them carry something the shot depends on —
   the texture under their fingers, the water, the packaging clicking — say it needs to
   be audible, so they don't bury it under a track.

**Never invent a sound.** No trending sound we have not heard in the source,
no genre the record did not name, no "add an upbeat track" as filler. If the
record's sound lines are `[UNCLEAR]` or missing, write what IS known ("you're
talking to camera") and stop — the sound line is never padded.

## The script comes after you

No "say these", no locked lines, no filler in the concept itself. The whole
script, in order, one line per beat, is lifted from the spec by the machine
and placed under its own heading after your Product Points — the creator reads it
as-is or says it their way (the owner, 2026-09-18: "we still want the script").
You do not write it; your concept ends at the last product point.

## No pictures

No image line anywhere. The AI pictures leave the document: they go to the creator
as a separate inspiration file in the same folder, built by the machine
from the spec. A picture inside the brief is what made it read as a shot
list.

## The heading and the product

The heading is `# ⟨the concept name⟩` — a top-level heading, no number,
because this document holds one concept and the heading is its title. The
product is named by its exact name from the product file, on the Format
lines, once. Never a product the spec does not show.

## The format line names the format (v11)

`{declared_layers}` names the format this video is made in, with why it was
called that. The Format line uses that format's plain name, in the three to six
words the line already asks for ("with ⟨role⟩" still goes on the end when a
second person is on camera). Only the name goes on the page: the format's
playbook, its beats and its reasons are for you, never copied onto the page.
Where `{declared_layers}` names no format, write the Format line from the spec,
as before.
