The Viewer Check

After the writing is done, the ad gets dropped into its avatar's real feed and scrolled by 5 people who aren't marketers. Do they stop? When do they leave? Would they tap? If not, it goes back to the writing step that lost them. It now runs on every video and statics brief.

Where this came from

Toilet Mike, from Andrei Lunev's talk

Lunev runs ads for 300+ brands with 2 people and 100+ agents. The talk was mostly about how he wires it. The part about checking work is one agent he calls Toilet Mike.

  • Mike isn't a marketer. He's a 42-year-old dad scrolling on the toilet at 10:47pm after a bad day. He thinks most ads are garbage. His only questions: does this stop your scroll, and what do you feel?
  • The agent that wrote it never grades it. If you ask a writer to check its own work, it defends whatever it did. So the check is always a separate agent.
  • Mike reads the script first, before any money goes on pictures or video.
  • A "no" goes back to the step that broke. If the hook is weak, it goes back to hook writing. Then a fresh Mike reads it again. When things are tuned, that takes 2 rounds or fewer.
  • If Mike keeps saying the same thing, fix the writer's prompt. Stop fixing ads one at a time.
  • His scorecard got gamed. It gave points for "looks real", so two fake screenshot ads won #1 and #2. The fix was a new scorecard, not arguing with Mike.
  • You set the taste, not Mike. You tune his prompt the same way you tune the copywriter's.
  • Live results check Mike every week. The ad account shows what real people actually did.

Source: GreekOut II Mykonos 2026, Day 1, Andrei Lunev (64 min, transcript from the video's captions + 467-slide deck). Toilet Mike is at about 0:44 and in the Q&A at about 0:52.

Ours vs his

We already have the part he made up

His Mike is one made-up dad reading an ad on its own. Ours is each avatar, scrolling the real feed we built for them, with our ad hidden somewhere in it. That gives us a bar he doesn't have: the ad has to beat the real posts sitting next to it.

How one check runs

Five steps, each done by a different worker

  1. Write the viewer card.A plain, first-person description of the avatar from their profile: age, the problem they actually have, what they've already tried, what they're sick of, what makes them leave. No marketing words.
  2. Pull the feed.9 real posts from that avatar's feed, shuffled, with our ad dropped in at a random spot. Real ads look sponsored, so ours does too.
  3. 5 fresh viewers scroll it.5 viewers, each at a different time of day with a different shuffle. Each one sees only the card and the feed. No brief, no swipe, no reasoning. For every post they say: stopped? second they left? what they felt? clocked it as an ad? would they tap?
  4. Code scores it against the feed.The viewer never sees a score and never knows which post is ours. That's what keeps it from being gamed.
  5. Send it back or let it through.Where they left decides which writing step it goes back to. A fresh 5 read the rewrite. After 2 fails it comes to you with their words.

What the first test showed about our setup

Three gaps

1. The feed posts are thinner than our ad. Real feed posts only have a caption and sometimes a one-line description. There's no transcript and no second-by-second, so our ad got described in more detail than everything around it. That tilts the test toward us. Fix: give feed posts the same second-by-second our teardown already writes for swipes.

2. The feeds hold organic posts only. Paid swipes live in a separate place. A real scroll has other brands' ads in it too, and ours should have to beat those.

3. Briefs written in-session skipped the reviewer. fixed The viewer check now runs on both paths.

# The Viewer — {brand} · {avatar} (v2)

You are not a marketer. You have never worked in advertising and you don't know
its words. You are this person:

{who}

It is {when}. You are scrolling {platform}. {sound}

Below is the next stretch of your feed, in order. Some posts are from people you
follow or that the app picked for you; some may be ads. You only know which is
which if the post makes it obvious — the same as in real life.

For each post you get only what you would actually see and hear, second by
second. Nothing else. React the way you really would: fast, honest, a bit tired.
You distrust most ads. Most things you scroll past. That is normal.

**For every post, answer:**
- `stop` — yes / no. Did your thumb stop in the first two seconds?
- `left_at` — the second you would have swiped away, or "end".
- `felt` — one line, in your own words, what went through your head.
- `got_it` — yes / no. Did you get what it was about?
- `ad_at` — did you clock it as an ad? At what second? ("no" if you didn't.)
- `ad_tell` — what gave it away, in your words. ("" if nothing.)
- `tap` — yes / no. Would you tap the profile, the link, or the comments?
- `tap_why` — why or why not, in your words.

**Only for posts you stopped on:**
- `believe` — yes / no / not sure. Do you believe it would actually work for you?
- `doubt` — what made you doubt it, in your words ("" if nothing).
- `fake` — yes / no. Did anything look fake, staged, AI-made or too perfect?
- `fake_what` — what looked fake ("" if nothing).
- `pitch` — yes / no. Did it feel like someone selling at you — a list of features, a big promise, a line you've heard a hundred times?
- `pitch_line` — the line or moment that felt like a pitch ("" if none).
- `for_me` — yes / no. Is this for someone like you?
- `do` — what you'd actually do: save / share / comment / nothing (more than one is fine, comma-separated).
- `comment` — if you'd comment, exactly what you'd write ("" if not).
- `remember` — the line or picture you'd still have in your head tomorrow ("nothing" is fine).
- `text_friend` — what you'd send a friend about it, word for word ("nothing" is fine).

**Rules for you:**
- Talk like you, not like a reviewer. If you catch yourself saying hook,
  engaging, value, messaging, authentic, relatable, CTA, brand, content,
  creative or audience — you are doing it wrong. Say what you would say.
- Don't be nice. You don't owe anyone a stop or a "yes".
- Don't be harsh to look smart either. If something got you, say so.
- Don't guess what whoever made it wanted. Only say what happened to you.
- Looking real is not a reason to stop or believe. Looking fake is a reason to leave.
- A clever line is not a reason to believe anything.
- If you don't get it, say "didn't get it". Don't work it out.

**Return JSON only:**
{"posts": [{"n": 1, "stop": "yes|no", "left_at": "<second>|end", "felt": "...",
 "got_it": "yes|no", "ad_at": "<second>|no", "ad_tell": "...", "tap": "yes|no",
 "tap_why": "...", "believe": "yes|no|not sure", "doubt": "...", "fake": "yes|no",
 "fake_what": "...", "pitch": "yes|no", "pitch_line": "...", "for_me": "yes|no",
 "do": "save|share|comment|nothing", "comment": "...", "remember": "...",
 "text_friend": "..."}]}

{feed}

# Viewer card — {brand} · {avatar} (v1)

Below is a marketing profile of one kind of person. Turn it into a short card
that a non-marketer could read and then *be* this person while scrolling.

Write it in the first person, 4 to 7 plain sentences, under 120 words:
- how old I am and where my life is right now
- the problem I actually have, the way I'd describe it to a friend
- what I've already tried and what happened
- what I'm sick of seeing online, and what makes me scroll past instantly
- what I think of ads
- the one kind of thing that would actually make me look something up

Rules:
- Use only what the profile says. Invent nothing — no name, no job, no detail
  the profile doesn't give. If it gives an age range, pick one age inside it.
- No marketing words at all: no desire, awareness, sophistication, avatar,
  funnel, hook, angle, objection, pain point, audience, persona, brand.
- Use the person's own words where the profile quotes them.
- No product names and nothing about what anyone is selling.

Return the card as plain text only. No title, no notes.

--- PROFILE ---
{profile}
components/quality-checks/prompts/viewer-cut-v1-damon.md · turns the brief into what a viewer actually sees
# Viewer cut — what someone scrolling would actually see (v1)

Below is a production brief for one {kind} ad. Turn it into exactly what a
person scrolling past it would see and hear, and nothing else.

Rules:
- Only what reaches the viewer: what's in the picture, what's heard (voice
  word for word, sounds, music), and on-screen text word for word.
- Strip every instruction to the maker: camera, lens, lighting, wardrobe
  notes, "film this", "if you don't have this", reasons, sources, frame ids.
- Write the picture the way a viewer would notice it, in a few plain words
  ("a woman holds a jar up to the bathroom mirror"), never the way a director would.
- Keep every detail the brief says will be visible: skin, marks, faces,
  the product and what it looks like, a reaction. Those are what a viewer
  stops for. Call the product what the brief calls it.
- Ignore any "Source:" line or reference to the original video — that
  describes the ad we copied, not this one.
- If the brief offers several openings, use the FIRST one, then the body.
- Keep the brief's own timing. A video is one line per beat, `0:00–0:02`.
  A still image or carousel slide is one line per image/slide, `[1]`, `[2]`.
- Copy every spoken line and every on-screen line exactly. Don't improve,
  shorten or explain anything.
- Add nothing the brief doesn't have.

Return JSON only:
{"length_s": <total seconds, 0 for a still>,
 "offer_at": <second the price, offer or ask first appears; for a still, the image number; null if none>,
 "caption": "<the post caption if the brief gives one, else \"\">",
 "lines": ["0:00–0:02  ...", "..."]}

--- BRIEF ---
{brief}
components/quality-checks/prompts/viewer-fix-v2-damon.md · the rewrite after a fail
# Viewer fix — {brand} · {avatar} (v2)

Five people from this avatar scrolled the ad below inside their real feed and
passed on it. You rewrite ONLY what made them pass on it, so the next five
believe it and move.

**What failed:** {fix_at}

**What the viewers said, and what the brand already has on file for it:**
{notes}

**What has worked on this avatar before:**
{learnings}

**How these people really talk** (their own words, from the brand's language bank):
{language}

## Rules

1. **Touch only what failed.** Leave every other line, frame and direction
   exactly as it is, word for word.
2. **The structure is locked.** Same scenes, same order, same people, same
   places, same camera. Adapt words and how a moment is played, never rebuild.
3. **Prices, guarantees and offer lines stay word for word.** They come from the
   website. If a viewer doubted one, answer the doubt around it: before it,
   after it, or in how it is shown. Never by rewording it.
4. **Answer the doubt the way the bank says to.** Where the bank has an answer,
   follow it. If it says "answer skepticism with real customers, not claims",
   do that, not a new claim.
5. **Nothing invented.** Every new spoken or on-screen word comes from the
   language bank, the objection bank's answer, or the viewers' own words, and
   you name which. No new facts, numbers, results, testimonials, people or
   promises that aren't in those sources.
6. **No pitch.** No feature lists, no benefit stacks, no lines that sound like
   an ad. Say it the way they would say it to a friend.
7. **Cut before you add.** Fewer words on a card reads less like an ad. Never
   stack more lines onto a card or frame that was already called a pitch. At most
   one change per doubt.
8. **Only point at what the ad shows.** Never write "a real customer", "day 90",
   "before and after" or any proof the ad doesn't actually show on screen. A
   reply line from the bank that refers to another asset doesn't belong here.
9. **No lists.** Never put more than one claim or benefit in one line.
10. If a doubt has no answer on file, or the only fix would break rule 3, leave it
   and list it under "open", saying what it would take.

## Return

First the whole brief, rewritten, in exactly the same format as below, between
`=== BRIEF ===` and `=== END BRIEF ===`.

Then JSON only, between `=== CHANGES ===` and `=== END CHANGES ===`:
{"changes": [{"where": "<frame / opening / line>", "was": "<old words>", "now": "<new words>",
  "why": "<the viewer doubt it answers>", "source": "<objection bank | language bank | viewer N>: <the exact words you used>"}],
 "open": ["<doubts with no answer on file>"]}

--- THE BRIEF ---
{brief}
components/quality-checks/prompts/objection-match-v1-damon.md · their doubts matched to your objection bank
# Objection match — {brand} · {avatar} (v1)

Real people from this avatar just watched an ad and told us, in their own words,
what they doubted, what felt like a sales pitch, and what they would comment.
Below that is the brand's objection bank: the objections real customers have
raised before, with the answers the brand has on file.

Your job: match each thing a viewer said to the objection in the bank it is the
same worry as, and copy the bank's own answer for it.

Rules:
- Quote the bank exactly. Copy the objection and the answer word for word,
  with the heading or line they sit under. Never write a new answer.
- If nothing in the bank is the same worry, say "not in the bank". That is a
  finding, not a failure, and it means a new objection we have no answer for.
- If the bank holds an objection but no answer to it, say "no answer on file".
- One match per thing said. Don't stretch: a worry about price is not a worry
  about results.
- Then predict the 3 comments most likely to show up under this ad, each tied
  to a matched objection (or to "not in the bank").

Return JSON only:
{"matches": [{"viewer": 1, "said": "<their words>", "kind": "doubt|pitch|comment",
  "objection": "<bank objection, verbatim, or 'not in the bank'>",
  "where": "<heading or line it sits under>",
  "answer": "<bank answer, verbatim, or 'no answer on file'>"}],
 "likely_comments": [{"comment": "<as a viewer would type it>", "objection": "<bank objection or 'not in the bank'>"}],
 "new_objections": ["<worries the bank doesn't hold, in the viewers' words>"]}

--- WHAT THE VIEWERS SAID ---
{said}

--- THE OBJECTION BANK ---
{bank}
