Triage · approach for the team meeting · 2026-10-02 · draft, not ruled

One way to turn long lists into decisions, shown on two themes

Every list we keep runs through one triage engine. The engine does the same steps for every list. What differs (what an item is, how it's prioritised, who decides, what it links to) comes from that list's theme, so a new list needs a new theme, not a new engine. This page shows how it generally works, using the two themes we run today as real examples: components (the atlas) and operations (the metrics dashboards, then the P&L). More themes will follow later; they aren't designed yet. Today we review the approach and the two lists. Option 3 is ruled: the owner said yes. Then each theme owner spends two weeks running their own list and brings back what has to change.

Rule, from 2026-10-02: anything this triage process creates (a signal, an action, an issue, a proposed card) comes with its eight-part summary from the start. Nothing is created now and summarised later.

The engine names no person, no folder and no rule: not who decides, not how items group, not how they rank. Everything in the two right-hand columns lives in that theme's own file, which its owner changes without asking anyone.

Grouping and prioritising: each theme brings its own rules

Neither how items are grouped nor how actions are ranked is built into the engine. Each theme's file holds both; the engine only applies them.

Grouping: which items become one action

Components four rules, first match wins

  1. Close-out: issues that look already fixed go into one action to confirm and close (1 action).
  2. Shared fix: the same fix across many components, e.g. repoint moved paths, the README how-to link line (24 actions).
  3. Decision group: issues waiting on one specific ruling, grouped by that ruling (11 actions).
  4. Local batch: everything else, per component, batched for its owner in one sitting (47 actions).

Operations one rule

  1. Detector per brand: one action for each kind of signal in each brand (data health first, then movement, sell-out, patterns), so each action reaches one decider (5 actions).

Components group by fix because one change clears many issues. Operations group by brand and question because the person who answers differs by brand.

Prioritising: how actions are ranked

Nothing about priority is built into the engine. Each theme's file holds an ordered list: what makes an item P0, P1, P2 or P3, what counts as proof for a P0, how much each tier weighs, and whether age raises it. The engine only applies that list, and ranks each action by the weight of everything it clears.

Components by what the issue risks

  1. P0: touches a wall, or says one thing and does another on a component that runs. Needs proof of live use (a run in the last 30 days, a grant on the mini, or a person using it).
  2. P1: blocks or breaks something on a component in use (over 20 runs, 3 or more depending on it, or runs we can't see).
  3. P2: cleanup, batched by category.
  4. P3: parked: little use, few dependents.

Weights 8 · 4 · 2 · 1. Age would raise an item after 7 days, never past P1, but issues carry no open-since date yet.

Operations by money at stake a month

  1. P0: $10,000 or more. The dollar figure is its proof.
  2. P1: $2,000 or more, or a data-health problem that holds other signals.
  3. P2: $500 or more.
  4. P3: below $500.

Weights 8 · 4 · 2 · 1. Age raises a signal after 7 days, never past P1, counted from the first run it fired (needs the decision log).

Same tiers, different reasons: components rank by risk to running work, operations by dollars. A future theme brings its own list the same way; the weights happen to match today but are the theme's to change.

The decision log: every act, written once

We start here instead of a weekly digest. Each time someone acts on a triage item, it's written once, as one record, and never edited. The queue pages, the atlas, anyone's Claude and any later view all read from it. Asking "what changed in my components this week, and how do I undo it?" is a question to the log, not a separate message.

What every record holds

  • theme, item, act: which list, which item, what happened
  • who, when: the person who acted, and the time, from the clock
  • areas: the components, folders, brands or products it touches. This decides where it shows up
  • evidence, commit, test: where the proof is, and what guards it. A machine can check all three still hold
  • why: one plain line. The only free text, kept short on purpose
  • ruling: a pointer to the org's decision log, when a ruling allowed it
  • undoes: on an undo, the record it reverses

Today the record kind (ATL-28 (triage record kind)) has the first three acts and the fields act · item · who · evidence · words · forwarded_to. The rest is the proposed extension (open issue triage #14).

Where it lives

Records of kind triage on the shared records plane: append-only, every act kept (several acts on one item are all history). An item's open or closed state stays with the item itself; the log is the trail of how it got there.

Who reads it

The queue pages (an item's history, and anything already decided), the atlas ("closed by the team, and why"), and each area's context file, so anyone's Claude working in that component already knows what changed. That last one worked when tested from a workspace-root session, but not from one started inside the component folder.

Two different decision logs

The org's decision log (the-org/decision-log.md) holds rulings. This log holds acts on items. A decide record points at the ruling and never restates it, so the two can't drift apart.

On the triage list

Every item and action shows Related elsewhere: other themes' items on the same refs, open cards or tasks already handling it, later Slack threads. That stops re-filing, shows one fix that clears two themes, and tells the triage owner whom to ask.

In the component design

The engine gains one index of refs, built each run from every theme plus the reference sources, and one resolver per ref type. Packs only declare which refs they emit and which sources they look at. The decision log, context files and cross-theme links all use the index.

Declared beats guessed

Lessons already built in

A third list (red tests) ran through the same engine before this round and is set aside for now. It is not a theme here, but six of its lessons are built into both themes:

Cause ≠ what blocks it

Two fields. A ruling is never a cause; it's what an item waits on.

Items can close themselves

When the source's own next run clears them. Operations signals now work this way.

Age raises priority

After 7 days open, never past P1: a P0 still needs evidence.

Summaries stay on Claude

In a blind test GLM wrote plausible summaries with 8 factual errors to Claude's 2. GLM stays for test-only fixes.

Batches beat causes

A back-test showed real fixes land in batches by person, not only by shared cause: "can do it, in one batch" is a column for that reason.

Check the source's freshness

A reporter that carries old statuses forward, or a dashboard a week behind, misleads the queue. Data health comes first.

2 · How it works

What goes in, what runs it, what comes out

A sorting desk for any long list. It reads a theme's list through that theme's adapter, drops what is already fixed, checks every item carries its summary, names each cause and what blocks it, then groups items into actions by the theme's own grouping rules (components by fix, operations by detector and brand). It ranks actions by the theme's own priority list (neither grouping nor priority is built into the engine), says who decides, who can do it (the triage owner first) and who is affected, links each item to related work elsewhere, and logs every act. The engine names no person, path or priority rule: all of that comes from the theme pack.

3 · How to use it

Asking for it, what you need, what you get

What it does
takes one theme's list through its pack and adapter: drops what's already fixed, checks summaries, names each cause, groups items by the one action that clears them, ranks the actions, names who decides, who can do it and who is affected, and links related work. Out comes one queue page for the triage owner (proposed as a component; run by hand today).
Before you start
a theme pack (themes/<theme>.json) with every key filled or set to null with a reason, and an adapter that can read the list and tell whether an item still holds. A named theme owner and triage owner.
The command
today, by hand: python3 tools/metrics_insights.py writes the operations model; /usr/bin/python3 tools/triage_page.py <model> renders any theme's page. Proposed: /triage <theme>.
What you get
triage/queue/model-<theme>-<date>.json and its page: actions ranked, items clickable into summaries, prompts to paste into Claude.
When to stop and ask
an action that bundles unrelated causes, a P0 without live-use evidence, or a stale source (data health fails): the triage owner confirms before anything goes to an owner.

the team for the engine; the team for components / atlas; the team for operations.
