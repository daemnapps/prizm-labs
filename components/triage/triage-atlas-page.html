2 · How it works

What goes in, what runs it, what comes out

A sorting desk for any long list. It reads a theme's list through that theme's adapter, drops what is already fixed, checks every item carries its summary, names each cause and what blocks it, then groups items into actions by the theme's own grouping rules (components by fix, operations by detector and brand). It ranks actions by the theme's own priority list (neither grouping nor priority is built into the engine), says who decides, who can do it (the triage owner first) and who is affected, links each item to related work elsewhere, and logs every act. The engine names no person, path or priority rule: all of that comes from the theme pack.

3 · How to use it

Asking for it, what you need, what you get

What it does
takes one theme's list through its pack and adapter: drops what's already fixed, checks summaries, names each cause, groups items by the one action that clears them, ranks the actions, names who decides, who can do it and who is affected, and links related work. Out comes one queue page for the triage owner (proposed as a component; run by hand today).
Before you start
a theme pack (themes/<theme>.json) with every key filled or set to null with a reason, and an adapter that can read the list and tell whether an item still holds. A named theme owner and triage owner.
The command
today, by hand: python3 tools/metrics_insights.py writes the operations model; /usr/bin/python3 tools/triage_page.py <model> renders any theme's page. Proposed: /triage <theme>.
What you get
triage/queue/model-<theme>-<date>.json and its page: actions ranked, items clickable into summaries, prompts to paste into Claude.
When to stop and ask
an action that bundles unrelated causes, a P0 without live-use evidence, or a stale source (data health fails): the triage owner confirms before anything goes to an owner.
Confirm with
the team for the engine; the team for components / atlas; the team for operations.
