# Method — how this finds what they actually consume

**This is not an email project.** It feeds the entire marketing system —
email, paid ads, landing pages, organic, everything downstream. The email
system was simply the first consumer.

Which is why it lives beside the brand rather than inside a tool, and why
every finding has to be channel-neutral: a named entity, a shape, a real
sentence someone said. Those transfer to an ad, a landing page and an
organic post as readily as to an email.

**The ambition, stated plainly so the depth is not mistaken for thoroughness
for its own sake:** knowing a market this well that the collateral built from
it is recognised before it is read. Content people meet as something already
theirs, rather than something arriving at them. That is only reachable from
what people actually do — which is the method below, and the reason it refuses
to work from anybody's list.

## The error to avoid

**A seed list is a hypothesis, not research.** Write seeds for "barbershop" and
you harvest barbershop. Write them for NBA and hip-hop and you harvest those.
Both are guesses wearing a lab coat, and neither can ever produce *all* the
content and formats a person consumes — a list only contains what somebody
already thought of.

The first pass here made exactly that mistake twice: scoped to one topic, then
widened to three more topics. Still a list of guesses, just longer.

## The inversion

**Seeds are scaffolding. People are the data.**

1. **Seed just enough to find real people.** A handful of terms from the
   avatar's own profile, only to surface posts this person plausibly watches.
   The seeds do not need to be right or complete — they need to be
   *good enough to reach a comment section*.

2. **Take the people, not the posts.** Whoever showed up in those comments is
   demonstrably this avatar. That is the only qualification that matters, and
   it is evidence rather than assumption.

3. **Read their whole feed.** Everything those people make and engage with —
   unfiltered by any topic anyone chose. This is the ground truth.

4. **Let topic and format fall out as findings.** What this room actually
   watches is the *distribution* that comes back, ranked by how many separate
   people carry it. Nobody decides it in advance.

The test for the whole method: **if the output only contains what the seeds
described, it has told us nothing.** A real result surfaces things nobody on
this end named — and those are the valuable rows, because they are the ones you
could not have written into a brief yourself.

## Why this matters more than it sounds

the owner's proof: the emails that worked named athletes and acknowledged the city
people lived in. Neither of those is a skincare topic, and neither would appear
in any seed list built from a skincare brief. They came from knowing what these
people were *actually* into — which is a distribution, not a category.

The same holds for format. "Older women are watching microdramas" is a finding
about **shape**, and shape transfers: the same form becomes an ad and an email
series. There are many of these and a fixed list would find none of them, which
is why the shape vocabulary here is open and mints new names as it goes.

## What this changes about the build

- **Seeds get broad and cheap**, not careful and exhaustive. They are a net to
  reach people, and their quality barely matters.
- **The weight moves to the people pass** — more people, deeper into their
  feeds, and what they *consume* as well as what they post.
- **Formats are read across the whole library**, never per topic, because a
  shape that only appears in the slice you seeded is an artefact of the seed.
- **Every finding carries how many separate people are behind it.** One person's
  taste is not a room's.

## Two standing limits

**Aggregate only.** Clusters and named entities. Never a file about a person.

**Nothing invented.** A claim with no receipt does not go in — the standard the
`avatar-two` profile already sets for itself: every claim traces to a view count,
a like count, or a comment receipt.


## The system's job is to present opportunity, not to be right

**the owner, 2026-08-29 — and this overrides the instinct that produced the
section it replaced.**

The first affinity pass returned Minecraft, school, Barcelona, the World Cup
and Drake from a barbershop harvest. The reflex was to call that contamination
and tighten the filter until only the expected room came back.

**That reflex is the failure mode.** A system tuned to return what we expected
returns what we expected, reports success, and has told us nothing.

His example, kept because it is the clearest statement of what this is for:

> Take a viral Minecraft commentary video. Keep its hooks, its scroll
> stoppers, its whole shape. Transform it — the character has acne, finds a
> magical brush in the castle he built, ends up with clear skin, and now
> everybody wants to play at his castle.

That concept is unreachable from any brief anyone would write for a men's
skincare brand. It exists only because an off-target result was allowed to
surface.

### What this means for the build

**Maximise surface area. Do not narrow.** The system's job is to put as many
openings in front of a person as it can find. Choosing between them is a human
act requiring taste, and the machine has none.

- **Nothing is filtered for being off-avatar.** Distance from the expected room
  is a property to *record*, never a reason to drop.
- **Report findings in two bands, both wanted:** what confirms the room, and
  what is unexpected. The second band is where novel concepts live, and it
  should be the more interesting half of the page.
- **Format travels further than topic.** Minecraft commentary is not a
  skincare topic and never will be — but its shape transfers intact. This is
  why the shape vocabulary is open and mints new names as it goes.
- **Platform filler is still filler.** `fyp` and `trend` are not unexpected
  findings, they are noise. Removing noise is not the same as narrowing.

### What was true in the observation, and still is

A viral post's comment section genuinely is broader than its niche. That stays
worth knowing — but as a **label on the finding**, not a gate on it. Say where
a signal came from and how far it sits from the avatar. Then leave it in.

**The test, restated:** the method fails when it returns only what the seeds
described. It also fails when it returns only what we recognise.

## Where the breadth actually comes from — a note on selection, not a gate

Ranking harvested posts on engagement finds viral posts. **The comment section
of a viral post is the whole internet, not a room.**

Measured 2026-08-29: 120 commenters from a barbershop-seeded harvest were
followed out to their own feeds. What came back was Minecraft, school,
Barcelona, the World Cup, Drake and gym — plus platform filler in five of the
top seven rows. That is teenagers and international football. The avatar is a
US working man in his thirties who books a cut every fortnight.

The seeds were not the problem this time. **The selection was.** A 5.7M-view
post is popular *because it escaped its niche* — so by definition its
commenters are no longer the niche.

### How to use that, given the rule above

Harvest **both**, and label which is which:

- **Niche posts** (smaller, inside the room) surface people who confirm the
  avatar. That band grounds the profile.
- **Escaped posts** (viral, beyond the niche) surface people who are into
  something else entirely. That band is where the Minecraft concepts come
  from, and it is not a defect.

Weight a commenter by how many seeded posts they appear on — but as a
**confidence score printed beside them**, not a cut. One appearance is weak
evidence about the avatar and may still be a strong lead about a format.

**Engagement stays the right rank for CONTENT** — what a room watches is
genuinely what it watches most. It is only wrong for choosing who to follow.

The pass ran, the output was checked, and it did not match the avatar. The
right response was not to tighten it — it was to look at what it found.

### The two files this lands in

One affinity pass writes both bands. `affinity.md` carries claims about
THE ROOM — members only (commenters seen on 2+ seeded posts, selected
for specificity over reach). `opportunity.md` carries the entire wide
catch, receipted and labeled as not-avatar-claims — the band where the
Minecraft castles live. Coded in `affinity.py`; nothing the net catches
is thrown away.
