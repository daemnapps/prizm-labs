# Format read — the prompt as sent to Gemini (2026-10-05)

Adapted from `formats.py`'s own prompt (same library, same naming rules, same format.json shape) so it runs on Gemini through `tools/video-teardown/tools/gemini_breakdown.py --prompt-file`. Added one field, `what_happens`.

```text
Read one swiped video's structure. Work only from evidence — what you see and hear in the video. This is for a maker who will rebuild this STRUCTURE for a different brand, so the structure has to be right and honest.

The format library (id — name: mechanic):
- sticky-line-maker-demo — Sticky-line maker demo: One text line pinned for the ENTIRE runtime while one continuous handheld demonstration plays out. The line never changes, so nothing competes with the product; the viewer re-reads it while watching hands work. Face is cropped or incidental — the maker is a pair of hands, not a personality.
- objection-flip-collection — Objection-flip collection: A quoted objection or question sits on screen the whole time with a two-word answer under it. One beat of human reaction, then the rest of the runtime is the PRODUCT COLLECTION as the answer — many units, ordered, filling a shelf or surface. The answer is objects, never words.
- forbidden-tease-dark-reveal — Forbidden tease → dark reveal: Two acts with an environment switch at the seam. Act one: bright room, product hidden then unveiled, text warns you not to look ('Don't show this to a ___'). Act two: hard cut to darkness, where the product's whole value becomes visible, and the text switches from tease to a share command.
- word-per-beat-procedure — Word-per-beat procedure: A camera that never moves, held on the thing being treated. ONE WORD per beat builds a sentence across the whole video, one step of the procedure per word. The operator is hands only — or a swapped-in element standing in for them; the problem is the subject and stays in frame the entire time.
- filth-extraction-montage — Filth-extraction montage: The transformation is the DEBRIS, not the surface. One tool pulled across a filthy thing extracts a visible mat of gunk; then the same tool proves itself on three or four unrelated surfaces in quick succession; then it rinses clean. Often opens with a witness in frame reacting to how bad it is.
- locked-frame-escalating-caption — Locked-frame escalating caption: The camera never moves — one wide interior frame, product centred. Small quoted captions escalate beat by beat ('hate these' → 'you mean THIS one?' → 'THIS?' → 'this...?') while the product physically transforms further and further in the identical frame.
- character-sketch — Character sketch: One heightened costumed character carries the whole runtime. Wardrobe, hair and props do the satire before a word lands; dialogue finishes it. Zero on-screen text — the post caption carries the quoted punchline. A cast may react in cutaways and the character may stay in one confined location or roam between several; neither is load-bearing.
- running-caption-borrowed-cutaways — Running caption over borrowed cutaways: One continuous narration is chopped into per-beat caption fragments that never complete a sentence, and that running caption is the only thing holding together footage the creator did not shoot — someone else's clip, a published diagram, a screen recording, stock process footage. A small talking-head panel or cut-out is pinned somewhere in frame so the narrator never fully leaves.
- split-screen-mechanism-verdict — Split-screen mechanism verdict: The frame is permanently divided for most of the runtime — the evidence in one band, the presenter in the other — so the viewer sees the claim and the person making it at the same second. The captions run the argument in order: correction, name, mechanism, verdict. The composite drops away only at the end, for the call.
- day-count-self-test — Day-count self-test: The runtime is organised by a rising day counter stamped in the identical position over the identical tight framing of the same body area. Nothing changes between beats except the number and the body. Wardrobe changes at each stamp are the proof the days are real and separate.
- reaction-panel-repost — Reaction-panel repost: Nothing in the post was shot for the post. Someone else's clip carries the whole runtime while a locked reaction panel of the poster sits in the frame the entire time and is never cut to. The reactor's presence, not their performance, is what licenses the borrowed clip.
- pinned-line-acted-out — Pinned line acted out: One caption sets a premise and then the footage does nothing but perform it — literally, and further each beat. The line is pinned unchanged for the whole runtime (or lands once at the head and never returns); it is never completed in words. No product, no dialogue, no talking head — the body is the entire payload.
- deadpan-reaction-hold — Deadpan reaction hold: One frame is locked and simply held while a subject refuses to react. Everything else — a crowd, a second person, a label, the screen light — escalates around them, and the camera never cuts away or moves. The last third of the runtime is very often one unbroken close-up of a face doing nothing.
- wordless-pov-procedure — Wordless single-take procedure: One service delivered on one subject, in step order, with ZERO on-screen text anywhere and no cutaway, location change or graphic. The camera is close and handheld at the work's own level and reframes with the hands rather than sitting on a tripod. The payoff is physical — extracted debris, a peeled sheet held up to the lens, or the subject's involuntary reaction.
- unfinished-line-real-payoff — Unfinished line, real payoff: A caption defers its own answer — it trails off in an ellipsis, ends on an open colon, or is cut off mid-word — and is then pinned unchanged while wordless footage delivers the answer instead. The footage is one continuous unstaged take; the payoff is something real that happened, not something performed.
- labelled-cutaway-mechanism — Labelled cutaway mechanism: No person, no room, no product in hand — the whole runtime is one unmoving cutaway or cross-section with a fixed grid of labels that never change, and the only thing that animates is the named ingredient travelling below the surface while the named problem resolves. Where live footage is used, the labelled explainer graphic is pasted into the empty part of an otherwise unmoving frame.
- stunt-funnel-reveal — Stunt funnel reveal: The first act is an attention toll that has nothing to do with the offer — an absurd prop, an out-of-place stunt, a staged confrontation — carrying all the on-screen text and all the ugliness. Then a hard turn funnels it into the thing actually being sold, shot completely differently, carrying no text at all.
- pinned-headline-monologue — Pinned-headline monologue: One person, one seat, one room, one unbroken take to camera — and a single headline pinned in the top third that never changes for the entire runtime while rolling speech captions carry the argument underneath. No product, no cutaway, no demonstration, no second location.
- opening-line-self-demo — Self-demo on the operator's own body: The front camera is on the operator themselves, and their own face or body is both the demo surface and the payoff. One line opens the video — a craving line, a quoted comment, a claim — and either stays pinned or drops after the first beat; the rest is a wordless self-applied routine in one unchanging frame, ending on the surface visibly changed.
- era-stamped-upgrade-ladder — Era-stamped upgrade ladder: Every beat carries a year stamp in the same fixed position, and the objects change with it — the obsolete method first, then 'Vs...', then the current or future year held for the rest of the runtime with the product doing the same job. The footage is stitched from visibly different sources with no continuity between rungs.
- concern-list-proof-photos — Concern list with proof photos: The same composite unit repeats at every beat — a fixed frame plus one big label naming a single concern plus a real photo of that exact concern composited into it. The caption changes every cut, the frame does not, and each named thing is paid off by a picture of the actual thing rather than by a claim.
- wordless-pov-handoff-transformation — Wordless POV handoff transformation: A POV arm reaches out of the bottom of frame and hands the product to a stranger, and the next beat is that stranger using it on camera. Zero on-screen text and no creator face at any point; the run repeats with new strangers in new places, or the crowd around the one stranger reacts to the result.
- pinned-line-stranger-sighting — Pinned line, stranger sighting: One unchanging line sits on screen while a handheld phone catches the product out in a real public place — the same subject sighted again and again in different parts of one venue, or worn into real transactions where unpaid strangers react to it. The repetition or the stranger's laugh is the payoff; nothing is demonstrated.
- locked-macro-debris-lift — Locked-macro debris lift: One extreme-macro framing on a single site, absolutely locked, held for the whole runtime with no cuts and no second surface. The only thing that changes is debris emerging — blooming into liquid, or breaking free of the surface in the final beat. No witness, no rinse, no multi-surface run.
- found-clip-reaction-cut — Found clip, reaction cut: Someone else's footage runs full-frame with zero overlay for most of the runtime, then a hard cut puts the poster alone in a bare room for the rest of it. The reaction is withheld to the end rather than running alongside — the borrowed clip is never shared with a panel.
- captioned-steps-comment-gate — Captioned steps, comment gate: A complete recipe or routine is given away in full on camera — every ingredient named, every amount stated, nothing withheld — carried by per-beat spoken captions from a fixed frame, with occasional cutaways standing in for what the camera cannot show. Then the last two beats convert: a physical book or product is held to the lens behind a comment-to-get gate.
- word-per-beat-internal-reveal — Word-per-beat internal reveal: One bold word per beat builds a single sentence across a run of fully rendered shots of a body, with the working part painted in or lit up inside it. The camera reframes freely between beats — nothing is locked — and the payoff is an interior view no real camera could ever get.
- silent-build-to-placed-reveal — Silent build to withheld reveal: Sequential build steps in one location with zero on-screen text and no address to camera. The camera tightens monotonically as the runtime goes on — wide on the maker, then medium at the work, then hands-only, then macro. The retention device is the unfinished object: you cannot tell what it is until the last beat opens or places it.
- one-product-three-worlds — One product, unrelated worlds: The same product is demonstrated from scratch in three or four completely unrelated settings, on different bodies or in different homes, with no text doing any work at all. Each demo is self-contained; nothing carries over between them. The repetition itself is the argument.
- public-reaction-repeat-demo — Public reaction, repeat demo: Two acts, no on-screen text anywhere. Act one is a witnessed act in a real public place — the old painful way endured, or the product used in front of strangers who visibly react. Act two hard-cuts into a wordless repeat run: the same product applied on three or four separate bodies or zones, tight and fast.
- pinned-line-borrowed-intercut — Pinned line, borrowed intercut: One caption stays word-for-word identical across every beat while the picture alternates between the poster's own shot and footage from a visibly different second source. The maker's own frame is deliberately plain; the borrowed frames do all the punching, and the last third often lives entirely in the borrowed source.
- deadpan-caption-evidence-montage — Deadpan caption, evidence pile-up: One claim is stated once — pinned unchanged on screen or left in the post caption — and then never argued again. Everything after it is a pile-up of visibly unconnected instances of the same thing, cut one to a beat: different rooms, different people, different sources, no continuity and no cast reverse.
- caption-only-single-take — Caption-only single take: One subject held in one framing for the whole runtime with zero on-screen text and effectively no cuts. Nothing is explained, nothing is demonstrated, nothing is sold on screen — the post caption carries the only words. What holds the viewer is real behaviour left running long enough to become the whole payload.
- flat-label-damage-reaction — Flat label, damage, reaction: A flat label or graphic runs over the first half — a product name, a set of category labels — and then drops out exactly where the demonstration goes wrong. The payoff shot is the damage or the mess rather than the thing working, and the video ends on a hard cut to a locked deadpan face in another frame.
- subtitled-monologue-evidence-cutaways — Subtitled monologue with evidence cutaways: The speaker's own unchanging frame carries most of the runtime while one continuous narration is burned in as per-beat captions that never close a sentence; every few beats the frame is handed to the thing the speech has just named — an object held up beside the face, a full-frame macro insert, a borrowed diagram or a stack of screenshots pasted into the same frame. The speaker's camera never changes; the evidence is the only thing that ever moves. Where the borrowed material instead holds the full frame for most of the runtime and the creator is reduced to a pinned panel, that is running-caption-borrowed-cutaways, not this.
- subtitled-monologue-angle-cuts — Subtitled monologue, no headline: One speaker in one place for the whole runtime with nothing pinned over them — a rolling speech caption is the only graphic and it is mid-sentence at every beat. The frame is either absolutely locked or cut between two or three fixed angles of the same setup; there is no product, no cutaway, no evidence and no second location. Locked, angle-cut and hook-then-dropped are one structure: what defines it is that nothing is held across the runtime and nothing is ever shown.
- clause-chain-mechanism-demo — Clause chain over a self-shot mechanism: One spoken sentence is broken into a short clause per beat, and each clause is answered by a different kind of shot of the same claim — the problem on a real body, the debris tapped out against a dark surface, a macro of the working part, a rendered cutaway of what happens underneath. Nothing is pinned and nothing is borrowed: the creator shoots or commissions all of it, and the shot type changes on every clause.
- spoken-caption-applied-demo — Spoken-caption applied demo: The narrator is also the demo surface: the product goes onto their own face or body — by their own hand, or by someone else while the camera stays on them — in one continuous setup, and the text is their own transcribed speech. The captions bracket the runtime and drop out entirely through the application beats. Where the text is instead one line landed at the open and nothing after it, that is opening-line-self-demo.
- personified-mechanism-explainer — Personified mechanism explainer: Everything is rendered and everything is a character: the problem is given a face and a body, the active ingredient arrives as its own creature and acts on it, and the viewer watches something happen TO a character instead of hearing a claim. One word of the voiceover sits on screen per beat and the camera reframes freely between beats — wide, macro, a different world.
- transformation-hook-mechanism-explainer — Transformation hook, mechanism explainer: The outcome is settled in the opening beats — a two-panel before/after, a year-stamped then-and-now, a promise card, or the worst-looking frame offered first — and the whole rest of the runtime explains why it could be true, in rendered cutaways and labelled diagrams under a running claim caption. The hook furniture never returns after the opening block.
- word-per-beat-scene-chain — Word-per-beat scene chain: One narration is cut into a single word or a two-word fragment per beat, and every beat is a different world with nothing carried over — different room, different country, different cast, rendered or real. The only continuity is the sentence, which never finishes, and one recurring subject or product that survives from world to world.
- dropped-hook-roaming-monologue — Dropped hook, roaming monologue: One hook line lands on the opening beat and is gone from every frame after it; the rest is a person arguing straight to camera with no text, no product and no cutaway, while the framing roams — close, wide, low, a different corner of the same place, or a location that travels behind them. The performance is the entire payload.
- instruction-card-routine — Instruction-card routine: Authored overlay furniture runs the video — a numbered list of moves that never depletes, a hold-time badge, a labelled anatomical diagram naming what is being worked — riding over a body performing one item per beat in one unchanging room. The overlay is the product; the body underneath is only proof the card is real.
- captioned-screen-walkthrough — Captioned screen walkthrough: A hook card on one face beat, then the rest of the runtime is a phone held over a real monitor at an off-axis angle — glare, moiré, the room visible behind the lid — stepping through the tool one screen at a time under a clause of speech caption per beat, and closing on the machine's own output.
- logo-stamp-crowd-coverage — Logo stamp, crowd coverage: A small brand lockup stamps the opening beat and then drops for good; everything after it is handheld coverage of one real event from visibly different setups with no on-screen text, no demonstration and no address to camera. The product is worn, never held up or named, and the crowd around it does the vouching.
- pinned-line-reaction-cut — Pinned line, reaction cut: One setup line, usually ending on an open colon, is pinned word-for-word identical over borrowed spectacle footage for most of the runtime, and the last beat hard-cuts to one person's face in a completely different room under that same unchanged line. The footage is the illustration; the face is the answer the colon promised.
- locked-frame-screen-readout — Locked frame, screen readout: One absolutely locked frame with a person in it who never turns to camera or leaves their seat, a caption pinned unchanged for the entire runtime, and a screen inside the frame that is the only thing that changes — a document, a dashboard, a monitor rig — walked through section by section while the viewer reads it over their shoulder.
- multicam-monologue-crowd-cutaway — Multicam monologue with crowd cutaway: One continuous live take of a performer talking to a real room, covered from four or more camera positions — tight close, full-stage wide, audience reverse, from behind the crowd — with word-synced speech captions running under every angle and a cutaway to the audience reacting placed exactly where the line lands.
- operator-service-montage — Operator service montage: A professional performs a multi-step service ON A CLIENT, shot third-person, with each product labelled on screen as it is used. The operator is an authority with hands on someone else's face; the viewer watches as the person in the chair, not as the person filming. Opens on the trade's own known ritual (the clippers, the wash) before any product appears, which buys the procedure its credibility.
- stranger-answer-montage — Stranger answer montage: The creator states the question to camera, then approaches strangers in public and lets their unscripted answers carry the whole argument. Every word is burned in as rolling captions because the audio is street audio. The creator never makes the claim — the strangers make it for him, and the more reluctant or unpolished they sound, the better it works.

Naming rules, because a library only works if names repeat:
- A format is the STRUCTURE — camera behaviour, text behaviour, beat order, and what carries the persuasion. It is never the topic, the product, the room or the person.
- Prefer a library id. Take it if the structure matches even when the subject is completely different.
- Only coin `new:` when the structure genuinely differs from every library id AND every id listed above, and say in `why` which one it was closest to and what made it different.
- A coined name is 2-4 generic words about the mechanic, never about content.

Return ONLY one JSON object, exactly this shape, no extra keys, no markdown fence:
{"format_id": "<one id from the library, or new:<kebab-name> if none fits>",
 "confidence": "high|medium|low", "why": "<one sentence: the structural evidence that decided it>",
 "what_happens": "<one plain sentence a non-technical reader gets: who does what to what, and the payoff>",
 "onscreen_text": ["<the exact words on screen, in order; [] if none>"],
 "beats": [{"t": <seconds>, "shot": "<what the camera sees>", "text": "<on-screen text at that moment or null>"}],
 "carries_the_value": "<the one thing doing the persuading>",
 "replicable_without_talent": true|false,
 "higgsfield": {"image": "<a full image prompt for the single most important beat — framing, subject, wardrobe, room, light, camera look; leave {PRODUCT} and {SUBJECT} as literal placeholders, nothing invented>",
   "motion": "<the camera behaviour for that beat, one sentence>",
   "text_overlay": "<the on-screen text pattern to reuse, with {} slots>"}}
Say plainly wherever the video does not show something — never fill a gap with an invention.
```
