You are watching a training/teaching video about advertising — a marketer
explaining how they scale ads by iterating on winning creatives. This is NOT
an ad. Do not tear it down as an ad. Your job is to extract the OPERATING
DOCTRINE it teaches, so a team can run it as a process.

Ground every claim in what is actually said or shown. Never invent, never
smooth over. If something is stated but unclear, write `[UNCLEAR at MM:SS]`.
If you are inferring rather than quoting, mark it `(inferred)`. A confident
wrong rule is worse than a marked gap — these rules get built into real
prompts.

Return these sections, in this order, in Markdown.

**1. WHAT THIS VIDEO IS**
Speaker (who they present as, what authority they claim), format, length,
and the single sentence that states the video's core argument — quoted if
they say it outright.

**2. THE DOCTRINE — every rule taught**
A numbered list. One entry per distinct rule, principle, or claim about how
advertising works. Each entry is four labeled lines, in this order:

```
Rule: <the rule, stated as an imperative a team could follow>
Said at: <MM:SS> — "<the words they actually used, quoted>"
Reasoning given: <the justification THEY gave, not yours; "none given" if none>
Scope: <what it applies to — video ads, static ads, hooks, testing, budget,
        creative refresh — and any condition they attach>
```

Do not merge two rules into one entry. Do not drop a rule because it seems
obvious. If they state the same rule twice in different words, give it one
entry and list both timestamps.

**3. THE PROCESS, IN ORDER**
The actual sequence of steps they describe, as a numbered workflow. For each
step: what you do, what you need before you can do it, what it produces, and
the timestamp where it is described. If the video does not lay out a
complete process, say so plainly and give only the steps it does cover.

**4. DEFINITIONS AND THRESHOLDS**
Every term they define (e.g. "winning creative", "iteration", "concept",
"hook", "angle", "variation") with their definition in their words. Then
every number they name — spend thresholds, days, ratios, counts, percentages,
how many variants, how long to run something — each with its timestamp and
exactly what it governs. Write "no numbers given" if there are none.

**5. WHAT COUNTS AS A WINNER, AND WHAT YOU CHANGE**
Two sub-lists:
- **Winner criteria** — how they say you identify a creative worth scaling.
- **The change surface** — which elements they say you vary when iterating,
  and which they say you hold constant. Keep these two apart; the whole
  method lives in that distinction.

**6. STATIC / IMAGE ADS SPECIFICALLY**
Everything the video says that applies to image ads rather than video ads —
quoted, with timestamps. If the video is entirely about video ads and says
nothing about statics, write exactly: "Nothing said about static ads." Do
not extrapolate.

**7. EXAMPLES SHOWN**
Every real ad, screenshot, dashboard, or piece of creative shown on screen:
what it was, what point it was used to make, and the timestamp. Include any
metrics visible on screen, transcribed exactly as displayed.

**8. WHAT IT DOES NOT ANSWER**
Open questions a team would still have after watching — gaps in the method
as taught, not your opinions about the method.
