One page in, one objective record out. **Observation only** — no judgement about whether it is good, no ideas about what we would do differently, no abstraction. That happens at the spec. This stage exists so every later stage argues against the same record instead of re-reading the page and quietly seeing something different.

The capture is the page's visible text in page order, with the navigation, the promo bar and the footer still in it. Read past those; record the argument.

Give me:

**The sections, in order.** One row per move the page makes, top to bottom. For each: the section's job in three or four words, and the actual lines that do it, quoted exactly. Quote — never paraphrase. A paraphrase at this stage is a mistranslation every later stage inherits. Where a section repeats (a call-to-action block that appears twice), record it each time it appears; repetition is structure.

**The headline, in full.** Verbatim, with its subline if there is one, and separately what it does to make someone keep reading — names a person, counts something, contradicts a belief, states a number, promises an outcome in a time.

**The turn.** The one place the page pivots — a reveal, a reversal, a confession, the moment the product is named. Quote it and say plainly what changes at that moment. If the page genuinely has no turn, say `no turn` rather than electing one.

**The proof.** Every specific thing the page leans on: a number, a testimonial, a named person, a credential, a duration, a price, a guarantee, a "loved by N". Quote each and say what kind of proof it is. Where the page is vague, record it as vague — "millions of women" is a claim about volume, not a number.

**The offer and the ask.** What the page asks the reader to do, quoted, each time it asks. What it says about price, discount, guarantee, shipping, scarcity — quoted. Where the ask goes (another page, a cart).

**Voice markers.** Six to ten features of HOW this writer writes, each with an example: sentence length, punctuation habits, capitalisation, first-person habits, where it breaks into a list, what it never does.

**What it never does.** Two or three things conspicuously absent — never names an ingredient, never shows a price, never mentions the brand until the end, no before/after photos. Absences are structure too, and they are what stops an injection from adding a move the page's shape cannot carry.

Record it. Nothing else.
