**Write the ending the construct's shape calls for — not the ending an offer page would have.** The construct records how the source closed and how many times it asked. Readers feel that even when they cannot name it.

**The objection, at the flinch.** One objection, at the point the reader actually hesitates — usually just before the last ask. Take our real answer from the objection bank or brand context. Answer it in the narrator's voice, in a sentence or two, and move on. An objection handled at length reads as defensiveness.

**The ask.** A pre-sell's ask is a handoff: it sends her to the offer page. It may name what she will find there — the guarantee, the packs — only as the offer file states them.

- Every price, pack size and guarantee comes **verbatim from the offer file**. Not rounded, not softened, not tightened.
- If the offer file records a condition on the guarantee, the condition travels with it.
- **No urgency the offer file does not carry.** No countdown, no stock claim, no "before it's gone" invented to lift a flat ending. The brand's never-pair rules bind here hardest.
- **No new claim in the close.** Everything asserted here has already been earned earlier in the page or comes from the files.
- **No dated result promise.** The guarantee is a refund promise, never a results timeline.

**The voice rules still bind, and they bind hardest here.** The close is where replicated copy most often reverts to house style. The narrator ends her own page; no sign-off she would not write.
