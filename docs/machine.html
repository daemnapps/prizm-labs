Build log · June → September 2026

A competitor's video in. A brief you can shoot from out. Forty-eight minutes.

I started on June 1 with nothing that worked. By July 8 I had a winner. On July 28 we started building the whole operation into a system: swipe what works, tear it down, write the brief, make the ad, edit it, ship it. Today a full teardown takes 48 minutes of machine time with nobody sitting with it, and the whole thing runs for about $1,500 a month where agencies and a team cost about $20,000.

The part nobody shows you

It throws work away.

Volume is easy to fake. What isn't is a line that declines to ship, on a stated rule, and leaves the reasoning behind for a person to overrule.

Refused

On the way to seventy good ones. A frame that drifts off the product, invents a face, or misses its reference doesn't get a vote.

Refused

Why it scales

One person does the bulk. Everyone else works from it.

The bulk runs

Made once, reused

Swipes, teardowns, briefs and baseline assets are made in big runs. That's where the credits go.

The team

A light plan each

People work from finished components and briefs, so their adjustments cost a few credits, not a fresh generation.

Creators and clippers

Paid on results

They get the tools and the flows on their own accounts, and they're paid on performance. Fixed cost becomes performance plus tokens.

The real shift isn't that the work got faster. It's that production stopped being the constraint — the only thing between a swipe and a live ad now is how fast someone reviews it.
