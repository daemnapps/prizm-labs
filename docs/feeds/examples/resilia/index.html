What they're actually doing

Copy is a fixed asset. Creative is the variable. One block — “Looking to take control of your heart health naturally?” — carries 313 of their 496 creatives, and 308 of those are different videos. Nobody at Resilia is rewriting ad copy; they are manufacturing footage.

What makes them worth an afternoon is how the footage gets made. Every creative sampled is generated: Pixar-grade character animation, transparent-body anatomy renders, photoreal doctors who do not exist, illustrated transformation panels. No filmed footage anywhere in the library. Seventeen finished ads a day, off four repeatable prompts.

Behind the ads sits a second operation the ads never touch. Their 496 creatives point at seven product pages. The other 222 pages on the site — 128 advertorials, 39 prelanders, 18 listicles, a quiz — are coded for Google, AppLovin and native. Two lanes, one catalogue, and they do not blend.

Structural findings

The creative is 100% synthetic. Not stylised — generated. Pixar-grade character animation, transparent-body anatomy renders, photoreal doctors who do not exist. No filmed footage, no UGC, no customer, across all 496. This is a competitor running an AI ad production line at roughly seventeen finished ads a day.

Copy is a fixed asset, creative is the variable — the same model Smooche runs. Eight blocks, two of them carrying 85% of the volume. Two independent competitors on the same operating system means it is the category's, not one operator's quirk.

Two lanes that never meet. Meta ads go straight to a product page — no advertorial, no prelander, no story. The 229-page factory is coded for Google, AppLovin and native.

229 live pages, 194 of them structurally distinct. 128 advertorials, 39 prelanders, 18 listicles, 11 product pages, 5 quiz screens, 3 review pages, 11 blog posts. This is not one template cloned — it is a page written per angle.

The listicle is a slot skeleton, rewritten per audience. Six 11-reasons-* pages share an identical eleven-slot structure with zero sentences shared word-for-word. Smooche reused the same eight bullets across four pages; Resilia rewrites every slot for kidney, heart, alcohol, hidden invaders, health-conscious. The better version of the same idea — and the labelled-slot pattern our own chain already runs.

The slug is the routing table. Product (ooo, ebso, garlic, lym), audience (w40, m40, menopause, glp-1, 60), channel (-google, -ga, -applovin), variant (-cc, -comp, -ps, -ds, -v2). 73 of 229 pages carry an explicit channel code.

-cc is a close, not a rewrite. Diffing pa-weight against pa-weight-cc: four lines added at the foot — the product block and “TRY Resilia Risk-Free for 60 days”. They test the close by appending it.

Invented staff, labelled sponsored. Bylines recur: Jonathan Foster (24 pages), Emily Carter (10), Emily M. (8). 42 pages carry a “Sponsored Content” label and a “Based on Clinical Research” kicker. The quiz quotes “Dr. James Harris, Integrative Health Specialist”. Not one verifiable person in the funnel.

No Meta pixel exists in any page's HTML. All 229 load psnen.resilia.shop/load — a first-party proxy on their own subdomain. TikTok pixel on 193, GTM on 164.

Price is being tested systematically. Intelligems on all 229 pages, and the ad-facing product pages are AOV variants of the same SKU at different ladders — oregano-aov-v4 at $39.99/$79.99/$99.99 against the main page's $34.99/$54.99/$79.99.

Offer and stack

How the offer is built

  • Quantity breaks, not percentages: Buy One $39.99 · Buy 2 Get 1 Free $79.99 · Buy 3 Get 2 Free $119.99, anchored at $59.99 / $179.97 / $299.95. Same ladder on all three SKUs.
  • The ad-facing pages price differently on purpose. oregano-aov-v4 runs $39.99 / $79.99 / $99.99; the main oregano page runs $34.99 / $54.99 / $79.99.
  • The creative undercuts the site. Ad copy says “$29.99 instead of $59.99”; one static says $17. The page you land on says $34.99–$39.99.
  • The guarantee changes by page: 30-day across ~218 mentions, 60-day on 27, 90-day on 32. Three promises live at once.
  • Scarcity furniture: “SUMMER SALE — up to 70% off + free gifts”, resetting countdowns, “93% Sold” / “82% Sold” / “78% Sold” bars.
  • Social proof, unsourced and inconsistent: “4.9 by 75,000+ customers”, “over 1,000,000 orders shipped”, “130,000+ reviews”, “132,454 people helped by the quiz”.

What they run on

  • Shopify, with two page builders at once — GemPages on all 232 captured pages, Replo on 45.
  • Intelligems on every page — systematic price and offer testing.
  • Triple Whale attribution, Klaviyo, OptiMonk popups, Microsoft Clarity session recording: all 229 pages.
  • TikTok pixel on 193 pages, GTM on 164.
  • No fbq anywhere. Every page loads psnen.resilia.shop/load — a first-party proxy on their own subdomain.
  • Heyflow on exactly one page: the dispute survey.

  • The quiz as a router, not a lead magnet. No email gate; question one sorts the visitor into which product's story they get. Friction removed, routing kept.
