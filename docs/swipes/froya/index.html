What they're actually doing

One argument, nine mouths. The same Harvard-study copy — “We've Been Applying Anti-Aging Creams Wrong For 30 Years” — runs from the Frøya Organics page, from Anne's Skincare Blog, from Dr. Amy, from Nordic Wellness Secrets, from Dr. Susan. The product never appears in the hook. What appears is a study, a doctor, or a woman who found something.

The mechanic underneath is a single substitution: water is the enemy, oil is the answer. Every angle restates it — luxury creams are 70–90% water, water needs preservatives, preservatives do nothing for menopausal skin, so use a waterless Arctic balm instead. Botox, filler, retinol and peptides each get their own page arguing the same substitution against a different incumbent.

Creative is majority static (99 of 153) and very long-lived: six creatives have been running 494–589 days. This is not a testing operation. It is a small set of proven arguments, cloned across identities and left to run.

Evergreen, not iterative. Longest-running creatives: 589, 553, 552, 525, 521, 494 days. Six ads have been live over 16 months — the library is a vault of proven arguments, re-cloned rather than replaced.

Offer and stack

How the offer is built

  • Up to 40% off sitewide as the standing position, held in the header bar on every page.
  • A named discount as the quiz reward: “Claim Your $81 Discount” — a dollar figure, not a percentage, set against the Complete System bundle.
  • CLEAR50 clearance as the volume event: “10 Days of Clearance”, “the biggest markdown we've run all year”, run across 26 creatives.
  • Scarcity as apology: “WE ARE SORRY 💔 We honestly didn't expect this…” — the low-stock ad written as a brand apology rather than a countdown.
  • 60-day money-back guarantee, stated on the funnel pages and in the quiz flow.
  • Systems, not products: The Complete System for Mature Women's Skin and for Acne-Prone Skin are the units sold; single balms sit underneath them.

The stack, as captured

  • Shopify storefront with a heavily customised theme.
  • ConvertFlow for the funnel layer — quizzes and listicles are CRO-tool builds dropped onto pages (cf-quiz-option-label, cf-10-reasons-why, croevolution-listicle-v1).
  • Nine Facebook pages running ads into one store, four of them invented health personas.
  • An affiliate programme with its own pages (affiliate-program, froya-affiliate-faq) — a second acquisition channel beside paid.
  • Aggressive rate limiting on the storefront: 195 of 304 page requests were throttled on a normal-speed crawl.
  • A blog built for search: 73 recipe posts and 26 ingredient posts, all written to mature-skin queries.
