What they're actually doing

One paragraph, one promise, every ad. The body copy barely changes across 177 creatives: "Get VISIBLE body transformation in 28 DAYS with Chair Yoga! 1️⃣ Choose your goal and target areas; 2️⃣ Get a Personalised workout plan; 3️⃣ Track your progress; 4️⃣ See visible results in 4 weeks!" Only the workout name is swapped (Chair Yoga, Tai Chi Indoor Walking, Military Calisthenics, Calisthenics). The headline is either that line or "Start Transformation Now!". The copy is a label. The video does all the selling.

Structural findings

The top ad is a proven, scaled winner, not a test. 168090649 was first seen 3 February 2025 and is still live. 51,749 clones on 3,181 pages is the biggest replication number in any brand we have swept (Sprtn's best was 37). This is the closest thing to real proof of a winner this data can show.

The videos are long hook stacks, 60 to 95 seconds. Watched the top eight. Each one is a montage of 8 to 12 short scenes, each its own hook: a mother and daughter in a driveway ("to ask how old…"), a woman taping a "Printable Chair Yoga for Seniors" chart to the fridge ("Mom, here's your…", "is day one of"), a woman holding a loose-skin suit in the sea, "If you're plus size I beg you to try chair tai chi, just 7 minutes a day to go from XL to M in 8 weeks", "I'm on day 18 of my printable anti menopause chair yoga", and a "Tap to start / Tap to join now" end card. It plays like a feed of other people's ads cut together.

All AI, and they say so. Every frame carries a small "Contains AI-generated imagery for illustrative purposes" line. The women, the kitchens, the beaches and the before/after bodies are generated. Block 14 is a full Pixar-style 3D cartoon (a woman who "blamed myself every day" and a "somatic recovery plan"). Block 04 is AI couples on a beach ("Just 9 minutes a day… built for women 50 plus… click the link, get the app").

The printed chart is the product in the picture. They sell an app, but the ads show a paper grid of 28 days of chair poses on a fridge or a door. "I printed my yoga plan and use it whenever and wherever I want." It makes a subscription look like a thing you can hold, and it is a strong, simple visual anyone can reshoot.

The avatar is women 50+, over and over. "A woman over 60", "a woman who hasn't exercised in years", "in post-menopause", "balanced hormones", "Ok, ladies over 40", "Looking for women 50+ who haven't exercised in years". Menopause belly and loose skin are the pain. "No gym, no equipment, no witnesses" is the permission.

The statics are a challenge with a start date. "Keep it private until it's done: 28-day printable Military Calisthenics challenge", "Starting Tuesday, September 1st, join the Chair Yoga Challenge", "Tai Chi Chair Challenge – Day 1". A dated challenge gives a reason to start now without a discount.

Many fake-person pages, one funnel. 12 page names run the ads: Transform in 28 Days (73 incl. "by Lasta"), Emma Taylor (37), Warrior Workouts (29), Zoe Chang, Lina Chan, Apex Bodyweight, Mia Brooks, Hanna Lee. They all send to lasta-fit.com quizzes. The top ad runs under "Emma Taylor".

Big launch in August. 110 of the 177 were first seen in August 2026. Median creative has run 32 days; 16 have run 90+ days.

Offer and stack

How the offer is built

  • A subscription app, not a product. The first quiz screen says it plainly: "This quiz leads to a paid auto-renewing subscription plan."
  • The promise is a time box, "visible results in 28 days / 4 weeks", and a dress size, "XL to M in 8 weeks".
  • The effort is tiny and named: "7 minutes a day", "9 minutes, three times a week", "15 minutes a day", "all you need is a chair".
  • No discount in the ads. Urgency comes from a dated challenge ("Starting Tuesday, September 1st") and "Last call! Our printable Tai Chi chair plan is leaving for good."
  • Price not seen yet; it sits behind the quiz.

The stack

  • Quiz funnels on lasta-fit.com, one per workout type, language and gender, with a "v2" version being tested on two of them.
  • UTM tags are the raw Meta merge tags ({{campaign.name}}, {{ad.id}}), so most ads are clones pushed out in bulk; a few carry made-up campaign names like "campaign_Best_Deal_Bundle_632".
  • Ads run under many invented persona pages plus "Transform in 28 Days by Lasta" and "Warrior Workouts by Lasta".

What to take, and what to beat

Take

  • Stack the hooks inside one long video. 8 to 12 short scenes, each one a hook on its own, 60 to 95 seconds, ending on "Tap to start". Their one scaled winner is built this way.
  • Give the product a thing you can hold. The printed chart on the fridge turns an app into an object. Find our version of "the chart on the fridge".
  • Write one plain body and swap one word. Their copy is a label with the workout name swapped. The picture does the selling.
  • A dated challenge instead of a discount. "Starting Tuesday, join the challenge" gives the reason to act now.
  • Talk to one person. Women 50+, haven't exercised in years, menopause belly. Every scene says who it is for.

Beat

  • It is all AI and labelled as AI. Real women doing it on camera will read as more true next to this.
  • The fake-person pages are a risk. Emma Taylor, Zoe Chang and the rest are invented. One platform sweep and the page count drops.
  • The claims are big and loose. "XL to M in 8 weeks" from 7 minutes a day is the kind of line that gets ads pulled. A smaller, provable result lasts longer.
  • One ad carries the brand. 51,749 clones on one creative means if it dies, most of their spend has nothing tested behind it.
