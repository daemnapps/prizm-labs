The line

Four stations. One machine.

This is the operating model, not a product's playbook. The brand, the product, the avatar and the angle are inputs. The line — teardown, writing, production, edit — is the same every time.

  1. Teardown

    Read a proven video and write down exactly what is in it and why it works.

  2. Writing

    Put our brand, product and avatar into that proven structure: the brief.

  3. Production

    Cast, voice, pictures, clips — made from the brief, checked before credits are spent.

  4. Edit

    The cut, the words, the final check, then hook versions.

People make the calls; the machine does the labour. A person looks at the stops, where money would otherwise be spent badly. Everything else runs.

The principles

What the system stands on.

  1. Proof drives the calls

    We start from what already earned attention, measure what we make, and let winners decide what comes next.

  2. Swipe, strip, inject, asset

    Take a proven piece, strip it to structure, swap ours in, make the asset — with a machine, any brand.

  3. People call, the machine works

    A person looks at the stops, where money could be spent badly; everything else runs.

  4. One idea, one axis

    Every idea is an avatar, an angle, a channel or a format — never two.

  5. The level decides the start

    What the viewer already knows decides where the ad may begin and which sections it needs.

  6. Trust is earned in Shadow

    A stop runs on its own only after its check has agreed with a person, decision after decision; launch stays a press.

  7. Slots, not prose

    Prompts are built from labelled slots; an empty slot is a refusal, not a guess.

  8. One change at a time

    A note names one change and keeps everything else, so you know what moved.

  9. Restructure, don't shout

    When a rule is ignored, move it into a check or a field — never repeat it louder.

  10. Twice is a ruling

    A problem seen twice, or said as always/never, is registered once at the stage that causes it, and the whole line follows.

  11. Worlds before products

    Search what the avatar watches first, in his own words; a guessed list is only a guess.

  12. Reach first, structure carries it

    Pick proof by how far it travelled, then name the structure that carried it, never the topic.

Checklists

One page per role.

Checklist

Everyone

  • Name the station any piece is at before asking about it.
  • Sort every idea onto one axis: avatar, angle, channel or format.
  • File a near-copy as a variant under its entry, never as a new one.
  • Call the awareness level before choosing sections.
  • Check the opening against that level's must-not list.
  • Open the tab whose one line matches the job.
  • Approve only with a press on Decide or a stop page.
  • Read the mode of a stage before expecting it to stop.
  • Quote the ruling's V-number and Damon's words, not memory.
  • Fill every labelled slot; an empty slot is a refusal.
  • Write one change per note, and end with 'keep everything else'.
  • Restructure an ignored rule into a check; never shout it.
  • Log a problem in the owner's words and count its sightings.
  • Raise a ruling at the stage that causes it, after two sightings or an always/never.
  • Give every Overrule on Calls one written reason.
  • Keep a winner's body and change one part for the next variation.

Checklist

Swiper

  • One avatar per plan; two avatars are two plans
  • Worlds listed from the feeds, biggest first, with post counts
  • At least one search per top world: coverage gate passed
  • Searches in the avatar's own plain words; no product names, no filler tags
  • Problem searches added on top of the world searches, never instead
  • Plan and dollar cap read; nothing pulled before go
  • Off-avatar finds kept and labelled, not filtered out
  • Views checked against the creator's following; shares per 1,000 views read
  • Post watched a few times before any AI breakdown
  • One-line why written in beats: the opening, the build, who carries it
  • Ads ranked on variants and run time, not on spend the library doesn't show
  • Seasonal promos held out; near-identical headlines grouped as one family
  • Every save labelled: scroll stopper or person
  • Person saved as a casting note; a real face is never copied
  • Format, style or cast test run on every save
  • Swipe in the pool as all three, format empty; six handed over with numbers, no ranking

Checklist

Creative strategist

  • Every idea on exactly one axis: avatar, angle, channel or format
  • Sub-avatar placed under its avatar; never its own angle
  • Style-vs-format test run on anything visual
  • Angle picked from the bank; nothing coined; new ideas marked proposed
  • Awareness level called, with the line that shows it
  • Sections picked for that level; opening checked against its must-not
  • One-line concept: avatar, angle, format, level
  • Proven body kept; control hook straight off the swipe, never improved
  • Hook versions: each a different opening on the same body
  • Variations: one lever each; a new concept is 3+ moves away
  • Script read out loud; choppy goes back with one named note
  • Voice sounds like the person delivering it
  • Every problem line as bold and specific as the swipe; nothing sterile
  • Viewer check passed before production spends
  • Control cut approved or one change named; launch pressed by me only
  • Results read at spend checkpoints; winners pick the next hooks and bodies; switches set in Settings

Checklist

Producer

  • Modes: every stage and stop checked in Settings before the batch starts.
  • Batch: started from approved briefs only, each card with an owner and priority.
  • Queue: every piece's lane and stop known.
  • Cast: banked person used if the role is banked; new people built from a real reference.
  • Cast sheet: same face, age, marks and build in every panel; no product, no text.
  • Look: one picture per outfit; face unchanged from the master.
  • Voice: from a real reference; sounds like the person; one bad line remade, not the whole read.
  • Set: our room, empty, in the presenter's culture; none of the swipe's flags or signs.
  • Props: made once, at real size, banked; the product is never a prop.
  • Pictures: six hard fails checked on every first frame, counted not glanced.
  • Pictures: product and words left out, laid on later from the real photo.
  • Pictures: raw skin, one tone, the swipe's light and grain.
  • Fixes: one change per edit; regenerate only when the base is wrong.
  • Clips: each beside its swipe moment; lips, object, room, person checked.
  • Tries: one; another only on failure; three at most.
  • Brand: every element BANKED or TO MAKE; brand words and numbers checked out loud.

Checklist

Editor

  • Swipe: open beside the cut the whole time.
  • Hook: words on frame one, no fade; hook line done by three seconds.
  • Scroll stopper: under the opening words only.
  • Sections: the swipe's lengths and shot count; never cut mid-sentence.
  • Breaths and held beats: kept; dead air cut.
  • Warning flags: every one looked at, fixed or kept on purpose.
  • Broken takes: sent back with one note, never covered.
  • Remakes: only the broken part, voice, picture or clip.
  • Voice: no clipping at line ends.
  • Music and effects: only if the swipe or brief calls for them, whole length.
  • Captions: off faces and mouths, one style, only the swipe's words.
  • Words: safe titles, bleeped curses, prices from the offer file, claims kept bold.
  • Final check: every word whole, lips on every word, by eye; batch counted.
  • Copy and name: read, parsed, on one card.
  • Hook versions and cutdowns: only after the control cut's OK; same body.
  • Thumbnails: a clean frame, its own words, off faces.
